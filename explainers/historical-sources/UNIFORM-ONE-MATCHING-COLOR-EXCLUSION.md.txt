## 1. Binary rigidity with an explicit additional cubic hypothesis

Work over C in the commutative physical-site-square-zero algebra, with
Q^[j]=Q^j/j!, and haf(empty)=1. Let V consist of m>=3 disjoint pairs

    I_i={i0,i1},       i=1,...,m,       |V|=2m.

Consider the ACTUAL diagonal binary quadratic

    Q=sum_i a_i h_(i0) h_(i1)+sum_(u<v) B[u,v] b_u b_v,
    a_i!=0,       B=B^T,       B[u,u]=0,
    Q^[m]=tau_h h^V+tau_b b^V,       tau_h tau_b!=0.       (1)

Thus the ENTIRE h graph is the displayed perfect matching; the b graph
and all its complex weights are initially arbitrary. In particular
tau_h=product_i a_i and tau_b=haf(B). For each physical root p in I_i,
let p* be its partner and define its two original root rows and core

    Z_p=a_i h_(p*),       Y_p=sum_(v!=p) B[p,v] b_v,
    R_p=Q restricted off p.

Assume in addition the WHOLE mixed cubic identities

    Y_p^2 Z_p R_p^[m-2]=0               for EVERY p.      (2)

This means every binary coefficient of this actual row/core response
is zero. It is a separate assumption, NOT a consequence asserted for
arbitrary binary sources satisfying (1).

**Binary rigidity theorem.** Under (1)--(2), the entire b graph is also
a perfect matching. No scalar cofactor inverse, positivity, source
minimum, higher response degree, or actual contraction is required.

**Full-source consequence.** At every even n>=6, a full ordinary complex
ternary source cannot have an entire monochromatic graph that is a
perfect matching. Section 6 checks the source-relative hypotheses and
the final already-reviewed obstruction. Section 7 gives a further
all-order bound on the number of isolated matching components.

## 2. Proper paired zeros and one b edge per quotient link

For J a nonempty proper subset of the pair indices, write V_J for the
union of its pairs. The coefficient in (1) with b on V_J and h elsewhere
is the exact product

    (product_(i notin J) a_i) haf(B[V_J])=0.

The h matching on the complement is forced. Thus

    haf(B[V_J])=0       for every nonempty proper J.      (3)

In particular B[i0,i1]=0 for every i. The entire b graph has no edge
internal to any h pair. All cancellations inside the hafnian in (3)
are retained; the proof will isolate a term only after inducedness.

Fix p in I_i and another pair I_j={u,v}. In (2), take the word b at
exactly u,v and h at every other retained site. The two Y_p factors
must fill u,v in the two possible orders, and Z_p fills p*. The
remaining h matching consists of every pair except I_i,I_j. Its exact
coefficient, with RAW rows and the DIVIDED residual power, is

    0=2 B[p,u] B[p,v] a_i product_(k notin {i,j}) a_k.    (4)

Every displayed anchor weight is nonzero. Therefore a physical vertex
has at most one b neighbor in any other h pair. Applying this to all
roots makes each 2-by-2 crossing block B[I_i,I_j] a partial matching:
at most one nonzero entry in each row and each column.

Because m>=3, the union of two pairs is proper. Equation (3) on those
four sites, using the absence of internal b edges, gives

    B[i0,j0] B[i1,j1]+B[i0,j1] B[i1,j0]=0.               (5)

The partial-matching pattern permits at most one of these products
to be nonzero. Hence the block contains at most ONE nonzero b edge.
This is exactly the one-color rectangle consequence needed below,
derived here from (1)--(2) without a separate cofactor hypothesis.
The properness of two pairs is where n>4 is used.

## 3. Physical port labels and the shortest-cycle graph fact

Define a simple quotient graph G on the h pairs: i--j is an edge
precisely when B[I_i,I_j] has its one nonzero edge. At each endpoint
i of this quotient edge, label the incidence by the physical port
0 or 1 used by that actual b edge. The two endpoint labels need not
agree. Equality of labels is compared ONLY at the same quotient vertex.

A corner of a simple quotient cycle is called aligned if its two
incidences use the SAME physical port. It is unaligned when the two
ports differ. A cycle with every corner unaligned is exactly a set
of its actual b edges matching both physical vertices of every pair
on that cycle once.

Since haf(B)!=0, there is at least one supported b perfect matching
whose edge-weight product is nonzero. Its projection to the h pairs
has degree two at every vertex. There are no loops, by (3), and no
doubled edges, by (5). Its components are therefore simple cycles of
length at least three, with EVERY corner unaligned. In particular
G contains a simple cycle with at most one aligned corner. This does
not assume the quotient is connected or that all matching terms survive.

Choose a SHORTEST such cycle C. It is induced. Here is the complete
elementary proof, valid for arbitrary local incidence labels. If uv
were a chord, it would split C into two shorter simple cycles C1,C2.
All nonendpoint corners are inherited once. At an endpoint with
different old labels, the chord label can match at most one of them,
so at most one of the new corners is aligned. At an endpoint with
equal old labels, at most two are aligned. Consequently

    align(C1)+align(C2) <= align(C)+2 <= 3.               (6)

One shorter cycle would have at most one aligned corner, contradicting
minimality. Thus C is induced. This reuses the elementary chord-count
mechanism in the existing common-anchor work. It needs neither Yeo's
theorem nor connected-cycle sums, graph enumeration, or tensor ranks.

## 4. Zero aligned corners cannot occur on a proper induced cycle

Suppose C has ell vertices J and zero aligned corners. Inducedness
and the one-edge-per-link result imply that B[V_J] has exactly the
ell actual cycle edges, with no internal-pair edges and no chords.
Its unaligned corners make these edges a perfect matching of V_J.
It is the UNIQUE matching on those sites, with nonzero weight product

    haf(B[V_J])=product_(e in C) B[e]!=0.                 (7)

If J is proper, this contradicts (3). Here B[e] denotes the scalar
weight of the unique physical b edge underlying the quotient edge.
No induced restriction is treated as a full source; only its literal
hafnian appears. Edges incident to outside pairs cannot contribute
to that restriction.

## 5. One aligned corner gives a forbidden WHOLE cubic coefficient

Suppose C has exactly one aligned corner, at pair I_i. Its two actual
cycle edges meet the same physical endpoint p of I_i; write p* for
the other endpoint. Let ell=|J|>=3. In the original p response (2),
choose the following word on V without p:

    b on V_J without I_i;
    h on p* and on every pair outside J.                 (8)

This uses BOTH colors even if J is the whole pair set: p* is h and
the other ell-1 pairs are b. Only the two neighbors of p on C can
receive the two Y_p monomers. Any b neighbor outside J is forbidden
by the requested h coordinate there. There are no other b neighbors
inside J because C is induced and each quotient link has one b edge.
The monomers therefore have exactly TWO ordered placements, and
Z_p has its unique placement at p*.

At every other cycle pair the two cycle edges use opposite physical
ports. After the two b monomers are placed, the remaining ell-2
cycle edges form the unique b matching of the other requested b sites.
All exterior h sites are filled by their original forced anchor edges.
No c edge exists in this binary calculation, and no outside b edge or
outside row placement survives the word (8). The complete coefficient is

    [word (8)] Y_p^2 Z_p R_p^[m-2]
      =2 a_i (product_(e in C) B[e])
                         (product_(k notin J) a_k) != 0. (9)

The residual matching uses ell-2 b edges and m-ell h edges, exactly
m-2 edges in total. The factor two is from the ordered raw Y_p rows;
the divided power contributes one copy of this residual matching.
All displayed factors are nonzero, including when the exterior product
is empty. Equation (9) contradicts the WHOLE identity (2).

Thus no induced cycle with one aligned corner can occur, at any size.
The shortest cycle of Section 3 must instead have zero aligned corners
and, by Section 4, span EVERY quotient vertex. Because it is induced,
G is exactly that cycle: there are no other quotient vertices or edges.
Its actual b edges are a perfect matching by their unaligned ports,
and there are no further b edges anywhere. This proves binary rigidity.

## 6. Application to one entire color of a full ternary source

Let an original full ordinary ternary source on n=2m>=6 have nonzero
pure target amplitudes and suppose one ENTIRE color-h graph is a
perfect matching. The reviewed global-edge theorem makes all its
edge blocks diagonal in the original target bases. Projection at every
site to h and another color b gives the actual binary quadratic (1).

At every original root, the three original rows belong to the same
odd core and have three independent nonzero pure responses. The
reviewed higher-response theorem says that the entire binary projection
of each higher odd product of these rows vanishes. Its cubic instance
with two original b rows and one original h row projects EXACTLY to
(2), with core exponent m-2. Projection preserves physical products
and divided powers. No theorem for arbitrary binary sources is invoked
to obtain this extra identity.

Binary rigidity therefore makes the whole b graph a perfect matching.
Apply the same argument with the third color c in place of b; its
whole graph is a perfect matching as well. The physical union of three
matching graphs has degree at most three at every vertex, even if
some color edges share a physical pair. This contradicts the reviewed
[two-high-vertices theorem](UNIFORM-TWO-HIGH-VERTICES-NECESSARY.md),
which requires at least two physical vertices of degree at least five
in every full ordinary ternary source at n>=6.

Hence no full ternary source at n>=6 has an entire matching color.
There is no induction on order, entry-minimum assumption, source
replacement, or use of H6/H8/H10 certificates in this argument.
At n=4 the two-pair set in (5) is the full set, so that zero equation
is unavailable; the familiar three matching colors of K4 remain allowed.

## 7. A uniform bound on the number of isolated color edges

Combining this theorem with existing scalar structure gives the
following useful consequence, observed independently by `/root`.
Every color of a hypothetical full source at n>=6 must have a nonmatching
component. Each component has even order and nonzero pure hafnian,
because the whole pure hafnian factors over components. The reviewed
scalar degree theorem gives minimum degree at least three in any
nonmatching component. A component on four vertices would therefore
be K4, whose vertices all have scalar degree three. This is excluded
by the reviewed cubic-triangle theorem. Thus every color has at least
SIX vertices lying in nonmatching components, and consequently at most

    (n-6)/2 isolated matching edges.                     (10)

This does not force a matching color in an arbitrary source, exclude
all nonmatching components, or furnish an ordinary-source descent.
General coverage of those components remains the unresolved step.
