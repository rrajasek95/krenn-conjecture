# Actual inverse cofactors exclude even one-vertex attachments and articulation points

**Status.** Independently audited all-order research, 2026-09-20.
[Complete independent audit PASS](../unaudited-codex-diagonal-continuation-2026-09-20/SCALAR-EVEN-LOBE-AND-ARTICULATION-EXCLUSION-NEW-SOURCE-AUDIT.md);
the parent read the complete source and audit and accepted their stated scope.
Frozen review objects are unchanged. The general conjecture remains open;
formal certification status is unchanged.

## 1. Scalar hypotheses and statements

Let `M` be any complex symmetric zero-diagonal matrix of positive even
order. Its support graph has edge `uv` exactly when `M[u,v]!=0`.
Let `C(M)` be its ACTUAL deleted-pair hafnian cofactor matrix:

    C(M)[u,v]=haf(M restricted off u,v) for u!=v,
    C(M)[u,u]=0,
    tau=haf(M)!=0,
    M C(M)=tau I.                                         (1)

Hafnians are unsigned perfect-matching sums. Empty hafnians are one;
odd-cardinality sets have no perfect matching. These are mutually
compatible cofactors of `M`, not separately chosen inverse entries.

**Even-lobe theorem.** Let the vertex set be partitioned into nonempty
even sets `E,B`. If all edges between `E` and `B` are incident to a
single vertex `p` in `B`, there are in fact no edges between the sets.

**Articulation corollary.** Every connected component of the support of
`M` is either an isolated edge or has at least four vertices and no
articulation vertex. Every bridge is therefore an isolated edge.

In a full ordinary ternary source, the reviewed
[global edge theorem](UNIFORM-GLOBAL-EDGE-MONOCHROMATICITY-AND-COFACTOR-INVERSE.md)
supplies (1) for every original color matrix, regardless of density,
support minima, or complex phases. Thus the statements apply separately
to all three original color graphs. Combining them with the reviewed
[scalar degree-two exclusion](UNIFORM-SCALAR-PENCIL-STAR-INVARIANCE-AND-DEGREE-STRUCTURE.md)
makes every nonmatching color component 2-vertex-connected with minimum
degree at least three. No cross-color hypothesis is used in this lemma.

## 2. Exact block cofactors of a single-vertex attachment

Write `P=M[E,E]`, `H=M[B,B]`, let `e_p` be the coordinate column of `p`
inside `B`, and put `v_u=M[u,p]` for `u` in `E`. Then

    M = [[ P,       v e_p^T ],
         [ e_p v^T, H       ]].                           (2)

Put `alpha=haf(P)`, `beta=haf(H)`, `C_E=C(P)`, and `C_B=C(H)`.
These smaller cofactor matrices have their diagonals set to zero.
No inverse identity is assumed for either smaller matrix.

Every matching uses at most one crossing edge, because all such edges
occupy `p`. But the number of crossing edges in a perfect matching has
the parity of the size of `E`, which is even. Hence it uses none and

    tau=alpha beta.                                      (3)

Since `tau!=0`, both `alpha` and `beta` are nonzero. The same parity
argument after deleting two vertices in just one block gives

    C(M)[E,E]=beta C_E,
    C(M)[B,B]=alpha C_B.                                 (4)

This includes the defined diagonal zeros on both sides of each equality.

Delete instead `u` in `E` and `r` in `B`. If `r=p`, the two remaining
odd blocks cannot be matched, so the cofactor is zero. If `r!=p`, a
contributing matching uses exactly one crossing edge `p,s`, with
`s` in `E\{u}`. Removing that edge leaves independent matchings of
`E\{u,s}` and `B\{p,r}`. Summing over `s` proves

    C(M)[u,r]
      = (sum_s C_E[u,s] v_s) C_B[p,r].

For `r=p`, the same formula is zero by `C_B[p,p]=0`. Therefore the
complete crossing block is

    C(M)[E,B]=(C_E v)(C_B e_p)^T,
    C(M)[B,E]=(C_B e_p)(C_E v)^T.                         (5)

All displayed signs are positive because these are hafnian expansions.
The proof permits arbitrary dense internal blocks, arbitrary zeros,
and cancellations within every smaller hafnian. No termwise
nonvanishing of a smaller cofactor was used.

## 3. The two identical matrix terms force the attachment to vanish

Compute the `E,E` block of `M C(M)` using (2), (4), and (5):

    (M C(M))[E,E]
       = beta P C_E + v e_p^T C_B e_p (C_E v)^T
       = beta P C_E,                                    (6)

because `C_B[p,p]=0` by definition. Equation (1) and (3) now imply

    P C_E=alpha I_E.                                    (7)

Thus `C_E` is invertible. This is deduced from the original matrix
identity; it was not assumed as a property of an arbitrary restriction.

Next compute row `p`, columns `E`, of the same product:

    (M C(M))[p,E]
       = beta v^T C_E + (H C_B)[p,p] v^T C_E
       = 2 beta v^T C_E.                                (8)

For the last equality, the ordinary hafnian expansion of `H` at `p`
gives `(H C_B)[p,p]=haf(H)=beta`. This diagonal identity is true for
every scalar `H`; no full equation `H C_B=beta I` is needed.
The first term in (8) comes from retained vertices in `E`, and the
second from retained vertices in `B`. They have the same sign and
are both present, rather than a single term with a missing factor two.

The target entry in (1) is zero since `p` is outside `E`. Over C,
`2 beta!=0`, so (8) gives `v^T C_E=0`. Invertibility from (7) forces
`v=0`, proving the even-lobe theorem. This argument also works over any
field of characteristic different from two under the scalar hypotheses.
No such extension of the preceding full-source global theorem is claimed.

## 4. Application to all connected color components

First, every connected component `K` of the support has even cardinality
and nonzero `haf(M[K,K])`, because a full perfect matching cannot cross
support components and the total hafnian is nonzero. Let `gamma` be the
product of the hafnians of all other components, so `gamma!=0`.
Then the actual cofactor restricted to `K,K` is
`gamma C(M[K,K])`. Restricting (1) and dividing by `gamma` shows that
the component matrix itself satisfies the same scalar hypotheses.

Suppose a connected component `K` has an articulation vertex `p`.
Every perfect matching of `K` must match `p` into exactly one component
of `K\{p}`. That component has odd cardinality, and every other component
has even cardinality. Since a perfect matching exists, there is exactly
one odd component. Since `p` is an articulation, there is at least one
other, nonempty even component `E`.

All edges from this `E` to `B=K\E` are incident to `p`. Both `E` and
`B` have even cardinality, so the even-lobe theorem forces all such edges
to vanish. This contradicts connectedness of `K`. Thus no articulation
vertex exists. Components have positive even order, so a two-vertex
component is one nonzero matching edge; every other component has order
at least four and is 2-vertex-connected. A bridge in a connected graph
of at least three vertices has an articulation endpoint, proving the
stated bridge corollary as well.

## 5. Exact controls and remaining scope

The companion checker verifies the unconditional block identities
(3)--(6) and (8) on exact signed matrices, including dense internal
blocks. It also checks matched internal blocks joined through one
vertex: their hafnian remains nonzero, while every chosen nonzero
attachment gives the predicted nonzero off-diagonal inverse-cofactor
residual. These are convention checks, not a graph census or a proof
by extrapolation. The general proof is the parity factorization and
two-term identity above.

This theorem excludes all one-vertex separators in nonmatching color
components. It does not exclude two-vertex separators, give compatible
bipartitions for different colors, produce a low-degree vertex, or force
components to be matchings. The already reviewed scalar cubic conference
example is a nonmatching connected component consistent with the lemma.
Consequently this necessary separator restriction does not close the
remaining general weighted diagonal-source problem.
