# Actual cofactor rows have degree one or at least three

**Status.** New analytic source by `/root/routing_author`, 2026-09-22.
The scalar lemma was independently reconstructed in messages by `/root`
and `/root/global_bridge`; complete source audit and root acceptance
remain pending. The consequences below hold at arbitrary order under
their explicit hypotheses. They do not resolve the general conjecture.
No canonical or previously frozen source is changed.

## 1. A dual degree lemma for ACTUAL hafnian cofactors

Let M be a symmetric zero-diagonal matrix on an even set V, |V|>=2,
over C. Put tau=haf(M), define C[p,p]=0, and, for distinct p,q, put

    C[p,q]=haf M[V without {p,q}].                       (1)

Assume tau!=0 and the actual scalar identity

    M C=tau I.                                         (2)

Symmetry also gives C M=tau I. Every row of C is nonzero.

**Dual degree lemma.** No row of C has exactly two nonzero entries.
Moreover, its p-th row has exactly one nonzero entry if and only if
p belongs to an isolated two-vertex component of the support of M.
Consequently, at a vertex of a nonmatching M component, the actual
cofactor row has at least THREE nonzero entries.

Suppose instead that the only nonzero entries of row p are
C[p,q]=a and C[p,r]=b, with q!=r and ab!=0. This requires |V|>=4.
The p-th row of C M gives, for every w!=p,

    a M[q,w]+b M[r,w]=0.                               (3)

In particular M[q,r]=0. For each w in E=V without {p,q,r}, set

    K_w=haf M[V without {p,q,r,w}],

using haf(empty)=1 at |V|=4. Expand the ACTUAL cofactor a at its
retained vertex r, and the ACTUAL cofactor b at its retained vertex q:

    a=sum_(w in E) M[r,w] K_w,
    b=sum_(w in E) M[q,w] K_w.                         (4)

Multiplying (3) by K_w and summing gives 0=ab+ba=2ab,
contradicting ab!=0 in characteristic zero. The common residual sums
in (4) are unchanged whole hafnians; no termwise noncancellation or
support-to-amplitude inference is used.

If row p of C is supported only at q, the p-th row of C M shows
that q has exactly one M neighbor, namely p. The reviewed
[scalar leaf lemma](../unaudited-codex-higher-common-power-bridge-2026-09-05/UNIFORM-SCALAR-PENCIL-STAR-INVARIANCE-AND-DEGREE-STRUCTURE.md#5-independent-scalar-lemma-leaves-are-isolated-edges-degree-two-is-impossible)
then makes p--q an isolated M edge. Conversely, on such an isolated
edge every C[p,w] with w!=q is zero because q is left isolated.
The identity (2) makes C[p,q] nonzero. This proves both directions.

This lemma does NOT identify C as the actual cofactor matrix of its
own inverse, and it does not claim that the supports of M and C
are equal. Only (1)--(2) are used.

## 2. Four far vertices wherever a complementary color is nonmatching

Now assume a full ordinary ternary source on even n>=6, in its
reviewed original diagonal form H,B,C. To avoid confusing the color
C with a cofactor, write K_B,K_C for the ACTUAL cofactor matrices of
B,C. All three color hafnians are nonzero and each color satisfies
(2), by the reviewed
[global diagonal and inverse theorem](../unaudited-codex-higher-common-power-bridge-2026-09-05/UNIFORM-GLOBAL-EDGE-MONOCHROMATICITY-AND-COFACTOR-INVERSE.md).
Define the far set in the physical support of H by

    F_H(p)={v: dist_H(p,v)>2},                         (5)

where distance between different H components is infinity. The
reviewed [closed-neighborhood theorem](../unaudited-codex-higher-common-power-bridge-2026-09-05/UNIFORM-CLOSED-NEIGHBORHOOD-ISOTROPY-AND-COLOR-DEGREE-BOUND.md)
gives the actual row-support inclusions

    supp K_B[p,*], supp K_C[p,*] subset F_H(p).         (6)

The reviewed [private-edge lemma](PRIVATE-COFACTOR-ACTIVE-EDGES-AND-STRICT-COMPONENT-BOUNDARY-DENSITY-NEW-SOURCE.md)
supplies a C edge p--r with C[p,r] K_C[p,r]!=0.
Thus r is in F_H(p). The original mixed word with c only on p,r
and b everywhere else gives

    C[p,r] K_B[p,r]=0, hence K_B[p,r]=0.                (7)

If p lies in a nonmatching B component, Section 1 makes the support
of K_B[p,*] have at least three elements. Those elements lie in
F_H(p), while (7) supplies the ADDITIONAL element r. Therefore

    p nonmatching in B or C  =>  |F_H(p)|>=4.          (8)

In particular, if |F_H(p)|<=3, then p belongs to an isolated B edge
p--q and an isolated C edge p--r. Their partners are distinct by
privacy. Equivalently, the only vertices that can have fewer than
four far H sites are degree-two vertices of the physical pair union
Q_BC, with one isolated edge from each color. The existing private
edge argument also gives |F_H(p)|>=2 at every vertex.

If Q_BC is 3-vertex-connected, no such degree-two vertex exists, so

    Q_BC 3-connected => |F_H(p)|>=4 for EVERY p.        (9)

The reviewed [pair-connectivity theorem](SCALAR-ODD-TWO-TERMINAL-DECOUPLING-AND-CONNECTED-COLOR-PAIR-CONNECTIVITY-NEW-SOURCE.md)
makes Q_BC 3-connected whenever B or C is spanning connected.
No spanning-connectivity assumption is used in the local bound (8).

Both complementary colors must have a nonmatching component, by the
reviewed [one-matching-color exclusion](../unaudited-codex-higher-common-power-bridge-2026-09-05/UNIFORM-ONE-MATCHING-COLOR-EXCLUSION.md).
Such a component has at least six vertices: its minimum degree is
at least three, its order is even because its local hafnian is
nonzero, and the only four-vertex possibility is excluded by the
reviewed [cubic-triangle theorem](../unaudited-codex-higher-common-power-bridge-2026-09-05/UNIFORM-CUBIC-SCALAR-TRIANGLE-EXCLUSION.md).
Hence, in EACH H graph, at least six vertices satisfy (8). There are
at least n+6 unordered pairs at H distance greater than two, since
sum_p |F_H(p)|>=2n+12. This count does not assert that those far
pairs support any particular color.

There is also a simultaneous version of (6): the actual cofactor
row of any fixed color is supported in the INTERSECTION of the far
sets for BOTH other colors. At a nonmatching vertex of that color,
this intersection therefore has at least three elements. This follows
by applying the same closed-neighborhood theorem twice.

## 3. Two isolated complementary edges have nonadjacent partners

For the same full source with n>=6, suppose B has isolated edge
p--q and C has isolated edge p--r. These are private edges, so q!=r
and H[p,q]=H[p,r]=0. Choose any H neighbor u of p; one exists because
haf(H)!=0. The reviewed
[odd-routing theorem](ZERO-BINARY-PAIR-COFACTOR-ODD-ROUTING-IDENTITY-AND-ALTERNATING-CYCLE-WITNESS-NEW-SOURCE.md)
gives odd sets I,J partitioning V without {p,u}, with

    haf B[I+p] haf B[J+u] haf C[J+p] haf C[I+u]!=0.     (10)

The isolated edges force q in I and r in J. In the notation of the
reviewed [four-site rectangle theorem](THIRD-COLOR-EDGE-ODD-ROUTING-FOUR-SITE-RANK-RECTANGLE-NEW-SOURCE.md),

    X={x in I: haf B[I without x]!=0}={q},
    Y={y in J: haf C[J without y]!=0}={r}.              (11)

Indeed deletion of x!=q leaves q isolated, while the deletion of q
is nonzero by (10) and B[p,q]!=0; the C argument is identical.
The exact four-site coefficient therefore gives

    H[p,u] H[q,r]+H[p,q] H[u,r]+H[p,r] H[u,q]=0.

Thus H[q,r]=0. Also B[q,r]=0 and C[q,r]=0 by the isolated edges.
The two partners are consequently NONADJACENT in the entire physical
support graph. This applies, in particular, whenever |F_H(p)|<=3.
It uses a mixed four-site word, so n>=6 is essential: the allowed
n=4 three-matching source is outside this claim.

The conclusion is local. It does not make q or r an isolated H
vertex, nor does it rule out the two-vertex cut through q,r in Q_BC.

## 4. Short-cycle consequences and exact scope

If p belongs to no H triangle and no H four-cycle, its closed
radius-two ball has size 1+sum_(u in N_H(p)) deg_H(u). Thus (8)--(9)
give, whenever p is nonmatching in B or C, or Q_BC is 3-connected,

    sum_(u in N_H(p)) deg_H(u)<=n-5.                   (12)

For a nonmatching H component D of minimum degree delta>=3 and
girth at least five, this sharpens the earlier component argument
to

    n>=delta^2+5.                                     (13)

For if n<=delta^2+4, every p in D has a radius-two ball of at least
1+delta^2 vertices, hence at most three far sites. Section 2 makes
every vertex of D isolated-edge in both B,C. A nonmatching B
component must then lie outside D and needs at least six vertices,
whereas |D|>=1+delta^2 leaves at most three exterior vertices.
This contradiction proves (13). Since n is even, the bound is
delta^2+5 for odd delta and delta^2+6 for even delta. It strengthens
the previous integer bound at even delta, for example to n>=22
at delta=4; at delta=3 it retains n>=14.

As a small conditional consequence, let n=12, let Q_BC be
3-connected, and let D be a cubic H component. The reviewed cubic
triangle theorem makes D triangle-free. At every p in D, (9) gives
a radius-two ball of size at most eight. After p and its three
neighbors, at most four distinct vertices can receive its six
nonbacktracking two-step walks. If t_w is the number of such walks
ending at w, then sum_w t_w=6, at most four t_w are nonzero, and
each t_w<=3. Distinct pairs of those walks ending at one w give
distinct four-cycles through p. Their count is
sum_w binom(t_w,2)>=sum_(w:t_w>0)(t_w-1)>=2. Thus EVERY vertex of D lies
on at least TWO distinct H four-cycles under these hypotheses.
No arbitrary twelve-site source is excluded by this statement.

The scalar dual degree lemma is unconditional under its actual
inverse hypotheses. Its ternary consequences use only reviewed
source identities and whole matching sums. They do not show that
far sets must be small in general, eliminate arbitrary short cycles,
establish coverage of the four-site rectangles, or provide the
missing existential weighted four-deletion witness.
