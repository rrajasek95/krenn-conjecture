# Pulling the six-site radical obstruction through a boundary cap

## 1. The universal six-site radical identity

Let `R_6` be the rational polynomial ring in the 135 entries of arbitrary
`3 by 3` matrices `Y_uv` on six named vertices.  For a coloring `c`, write

\[
 F_c(Y)=\sum_{M\in\operatorname{PM}(6)}
              \prod_{uv\in M}Y_{uv}(c_u,c_v),             \tag{1}
\]

let `I_mix` be the ideal generated by all `F_c` with `c` mixed, and put

\[
 P(Y)=F_{0^6}(Y)F_{1^6}(Y)F_{2^6}(Y).                     \tag{2}
\]

The arbitrary-complex six-site theorem in
`proofs/low-rank-graph-laurent-obstruction.md` says that a common zero of
the mixed coefficients cannot have all three constant coefficients
nonzero.  Indeed, three nonzero diagonal coefficients can be normalized to
one by an invertible diagonal map at one vertex.  Hilbert's Nullstellensatz
therefore gives

\[
 P\in\sqrt{I_{\rm mix}}.
\]

Equivalently, there are an integer `N>=1` and polynomials `Q_c in R_6`
such that

\[
 P(Y)^N=\sum_{c\ \mathrm{mixed}}Q_c(Y)F_c(Y).              \tag{3}
\]

The identity can be taken over `Q`.  Nullstellensatz first gives it after
extending scalars to the algebraic closure; membership of the rational
polynomial `P^N` in the rational ideal descends by faithful flatness (or by
solving the finite coefficient-matching linear system over `Q`).  No value
of `N` is asserted here.

Thus (3) is the algebraic content of the six-site theorem that is relevant
to a larger boundary gadget.  It is stronger than merely saying that one
particular normalized system is inconsistent: after localizing at `P`, the
mixed coefficient ideal is the unit ideal.

## 2. Exact pullback through an arbitrary cap

Let `B=U disjoint-union W`, where `|U|=6`, suppose hypothetically that

\[
 H_B(A)=\Delta_{B,3},                                     \tag{4}
\]

and choose any covector `K` on the `W` slots.  Use the boundary signature
and nilpotent logarithm notation of
`notes/uniform-six-vertex-reduction.md`:

\[
 s=C_\varnothing,\qquad
 \log(C/s)=L_2+L_4+L_6,\qquad Y=A|_U+L_2,                 \tag{5}
\]

where this notation is used on the open set `s!=0`.  Define the top
higher-boundary defect

\[
                         E=L_6+L_4Y.                       \tag{6}
\]

The exact boundary formula is

\[
 K\mathbin{\lrcorner}H_B(A)
       =s\{H_U(Y)+E\}
       =\sum_{i=0}^2\kappa_i e_i^{\otimes6},
 \qquad \kappa_i=K(e_i^{\otimes W}).                      \tag{7}
\]

Coefficient comparison in (7) gives

\[
 F_c(Y)=-E_c\quad(c\text{ mixed}),\qquad
 F_{i^6}(Y)=\kappa_i/s-E_{i^6}.                            \tag{8}
\]

Substituting (8) into the universal identity (3) proves the localized
boundary identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\{\prod_{i=0}^2(\kappa_i/s-E_{i^6})\right\}^{N}
   =-\sum_{c\ \mathrm{mixed}}Q_c(Y)E_c.}                  \tag{9}
\]

This is an exact identity in the cap parameters and source entries after
inverting `s`; multiplying by a sufficiently large power of `s` clears all
denominators and gives an ordinary polynomial identity.

## 3. High-boundary dichotomy

Equation (9) has an immediate pointwise consequence.

**Proposition (mixed defect or lost effective color).**  For every cap with
`s!=0`, either

1. some mixed component `E_c` is nonzero; or
2. `F_(i^6)(Y)=0` for at least one color `i`, equivalently
   `E_(i^6)=kappa_i/s`.

In particular, if `s*kappa_0*kappa_1*kappa_2!=0`, the full defect `E`
cannot vanish.

**Proof.**  If every mixed `E_c` vanishes, the right side of (9) is zero.
The product on the left must vanish, and (8) identifies its factors with
the three constant coefficients of the effective pair model.  If the full
defect vanished as well, those factors would be `kappa_i/s`, all nonzero,
a contradiction. `QED`

The proposition sharpens the usual statement that an arbitrary cap need
not be clean.  If its higher cumulants happen to have no mixed top
component, then they must carry **all** of at least one target constant
color, making that color disappear from the effective pair model.  On the
open locus where the three effective constant coefficients remain nonzero,
some mixed higher-boundary component is forced.

## 4. Scope of the result

This pullback is a necessary obstruction, not the missing all-even
compression.  It proves that every hypothetical larger realization avoids
six-site reduction in one of two explicit ways, but it does not show that a
cap avoiding both ways exists.  A uniform proof can now target the mixed
defect ideal in (9): either eliminate its components using overlapping caps,
or show that one cap retains all three effective constant colors while its
mixed defect vanishes.  Merely observing that `L_4` or `L_6` is nonzero is
insufficient, because those tensors may cancel in the combination (6).
