# Full arbitrary-matrix `P^2` filtration: global truncated interface

## Outcome

The old coefficient-aware degree-six residual is not the faithful next
linear system: it depends on one arbitrary modular right inverse in each
lower block.  The correct object is the single target-connected truncated
Macaulay map through the requested filtration degree, with every lower-degree
kernel retained.

A new dependency-free Rust engine now builds that object source-faithfully.
It exactly reproduces the frozen diagonal matrix at cutoff zero, and proves
that the complete global target components at cutoffs two and three have
explicit integer triangular full-row-rank blocks (hence contractions over
`Q` and every odd characteristic).  The first genuinely new critical layer is
therefore `K`-degree four.  Cutoff four did not produce a dead row: a bounded
target-rooted DFS accumulated 124,186 valid pivots before its 300-second cap,
while remaining inside the first target dependency closure.

The subsequently implemented sound Morse transfer through the full
543,640-row block also remained nonterminal.  Over `p=1,073,741,827` it
contracted the target to 23,681 degree-four rows, but its exact critical
component expanded to 1,641,542 rows and 979,983 columns after processing
only 48,021 rows.  It stopped internally at 270 seconds with 1,593,521 rows
still queued.  This is an unresolved component packet, not membership or a
separator.

This does **not** yet decide the degree-six correction.  It replaces the
noncanonical residual by a theorem-grade lower contracting block and gives
the exact Schur/Morse interface needed for the continuation.

## Faithful cutoff

Let `K` be generated by the 90 bichromatic endpoint-colour variables.  For a
mixed word `c`, a multiplier/source column has minimum filtration degree

    (# bichromatic variables in the multiplier)
      + min_(M perfect matching) # bichromatic edges of M coloured by c.

At cutoff `d` the engine retains:

1. every target-multidegree row orbit of `K`-degree at most `d` in the
   incidence component meeting `P^2`;
2. every literal mixed-generator multiplier orbit of minimum degree at most
   `d` incident to those rows; and
3. every one of its 15 matching outputs whose row degree is at most `d`.

Closing this bipartite incidence relation includes all lower-degree kernels.
No diagonal solution, modular right inverse, or associated-graded-only source
column is selected.

## Exact controls and sizes

At cutoff zero the Rust closure is

    874 rows x 5,530 columns, 11,682 nonzeros,

exactly the independently frozen diagonal matrix.  Its singleton-column peel
has 874 pivots and empty core.

At cutoff two the full component is

    76,987 rows x 637,696 columns, 1,704,576 nonzeros.

It consists of 874 degree-zero and 76,113 degree-two rows.  There are 224,469
initial singleton columns, and cascading removal produces 76,987 distinct
pivots and empty core.

At cutoff three the full component is

    543,640 rows x 4,424,353 columns, 13,266,454 nonzeros.

The row census is `874, 76,113, 466,653` in degrees `0,2,3`; column minima
are `5,530, 632,166, 3,786,657`.  Starting from 1,464,889 singleton columns,
the peel removes every one of the 543,640 rows.  Reversing the removal order
is an explicit triangular integer minor with nonzero diagonal.  Its diagonal
profile is `1^543304 2^335 4^1`; thus the inverse is certified over `Q` and
odd characteristics, but the block is not claimed unimodular over `Z`.

The complete pivot order is `cutoff3_full_peel.txt` (45,746,789 bytes,
543,640 pivots), SHA-256

    43d68092356eeb412e7215c0397ce7b3bdc46a64fd01b4c690bb698a22fbeab7.

The standalone verifier reconstructs each literal source column, checks its
stored diagonal, checks that every other truncated output occurs earlier,
and verifies coverage of all 663 target rows.  Replay takes about 15 seconds.

A smaller target-only DFS certificate has 9,528 pivots, with row degrees
`868,7421,1239` in degrees `0,2,3`.  Its digest is

    4985027e5f91bb462960f3e3dcd3c77edd181bc9e8ace1d938c7b506ff974222.

It is useful as a compact target solution, but it is not a right inverse on
all low rows and therefore cannot by itself transfer every lower kernel.

## Cutoff-four checkpoint

Materializing the full cutoff-four component is already the wrong shape.  An
incomplete traversal had reached 2,622,364 rows and 1,509,299 columns after
only 50,000 rows of a 1.30-million-row frontier.  The final component must
also contain every one of the 4,424,353 cutoff-three columns.

The target-rooted full-output DFS is much smaller.  Its bounded 300-second
run reached:

    170,000 recursive calls,
    124,186 completed, audited pivots,
    about 980 currently open dependency rows,
    no failed row and no terminal cycle.

The cap arrived while still resolving the first target row, so this is a
timeout, not a separator.  No cutoff-five or cutoff-six run is inferred from
it.

## Sound Morse transfer to `K4`

Write the cutoff-three map as `[B | C]`, where `B` is the frozen
543,640-column triangular block.  There are

    4,424,353 - 543,640 = 3,880,713

free lower columns.  Let `A4_pivot` and `A4_free` be the degree-four tails of
these two packets, and let `A4_min4` be the leading part of the genuinely
minimum-degree-four columns.  The complete lower-kernel transfer is

    D4 = [ A4_free - A4_pivot B^(-1) C  |  A4_min4 ],
    t4 = -A4_pivot B^(-1) b.

This is the smallest faithful `K4` system.  Using only the 9,528 target DFS
block in place of `B` would be unsound: a free low column can leave that
small row set, so its low part cannot generally be reduced there.

The full pivot ledger makes application of `B^(-1)` exact and linear in its
stored nonzeros.

That transfer has now been implemented without materializing the 4.42-million
column lower matrix.  A reverse sparse triangular solve contracts each free
column; its transpose forward solve constructs each exact row of `D4`.
The first 100 independently constructed row/column coefficients agreed.
For the target, 9,296 nonzero lower pivot coefficients produced 23,681
critical rows.  The bounded target component run over `p=1,073,741,827`
terminalized at its internal 270-second cap with:

    1,641,542 discovered degree-four rows,
      979,983 transferred/minimum-four columns,
       48,021 processed rows,
    1,593,521 queued rows.

The exact frozen snapshot is `morse4_p1073741827.txt`, SHA-256

    32f1122f252920ea4898b3419f9fd4c790669646ef8a314c3e32fb42596edc3b.

It records all 23,681 contracted target coefficients and the first 500
unprocessed canonical rows.  Since the critical incidence closure is not
complete, its partial graph cannot support either a dead-core or membership
claim.  No second prime is warranted before a finite terminal profile lands.

## Matrix-free Krylov assessment

Sparse products are feasible: the exact cutoff-three operator has 13.27
million nonzeros, and its triangular inverse is a streamed substitution.
Generic block Wiedemann is nevertheless a poor first solver.  Before Morse
reduction the rectangular dimension is already roughly five million, and
cutoff four has millions of additional rows.  Block Wiedemann reduces
sequential depth through blocking but not the total `dimension * nnz` field
work; at these sizes it is dominated by the avoidable contractible part.

If a modular Morse-core solve lands, characteristic-zero certification can be
recovered by freezing a nonsingular pivot minor, applying p-adic/Dixon
rational reconstruction, clearing denominators, and replaying the literal
integer equation.  A modular negative answer requires an independently
reconstructed integral left functional; agreement at two primes alone is not
a rational nonmembership proof.

## Logical scope

The cutoff-three full-row-rank block gives `P^2 in I+K^4` over `Q`, which is a **weaker**
containment than the already certified `P^2 in I+K^6`.  Its new content is
not a better exponent: it is the complete global lower-kernel interface and
the exact contraction needed to ask the degree-six question soundly.

The previously reported `p=1009` degree-six residual with 366,992 rows is one
choice-dependent remainder.  It remains a useful performance control, but
neither its cancellation nor a separator against it decides the global
truncated problem.
