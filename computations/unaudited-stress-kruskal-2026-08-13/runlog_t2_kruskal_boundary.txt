T2.0  for an m x r matrix:  full column rank  <=>  k-rank = r
========================================================================
  555732 matrices exhausted over F2, F3 with m in {3,4}, r=3:
  full column rank <=> k-rank = 3 holds in EVERY case.
  Reason: k-rank r requires every r-subset of the r columns to be
  independent, i.e. the single subset {all columns}.  A k-rank of 2
  therefore already means rank <= 2, i.e. NOT full column rank.

T2.2  Kruskal budget under the note's hypotheses
========================================================================
  Third factor of [[A,B,I_r]] is I_r, so k_3 = r = 3.
  Kruskal needs k_A + k_B + k_C >= 2r+2 = 8, i.e. k_A + k_B >= 5.
  The note assumes BOTH shore factors full rank; by T2.0 that is
  k_A = k_B = r = 3, so k_A + k_B = 6 >= 5: satisfied with
  slack 1.

  independent) and C in GL_r gives k_{C^T} = 3, sum 9 >= 8.

T2.7  structural closure: a_c in col(U) always; A full rank => U full rank
========================================================================
  12 enumerated fusion columns: every a_c lies in col(U).
  Consequence: rank(A) <= rank(U) and rank(B) <= rank(V).
  So 'both shore factors full column rank' already FORCES both
  palettes to be full column rank, which is the exact hypothesis
  under which the singleton-support argument closes.
  In the note's setting U = D_S, V = D_T are full rank BY
  CONSTRUCTION, so the degenerate-palette counterexamples of
  T2.4/T2.5 are structurally unreachable there.

T2.8  is the note's 'full rank' hypothesis needed? (redundancy test)
========================================================================
  2 squares with U = D_S(1), V = D_T(2) full rank and C in GL_3,
  with NO rank hypothesis on A, B: non-monomial count = 0.
  Moreover every such A and B came out full column rank anyway.
  => Under the note's own palettes the 'full rank' hypothesis is
     REDUNDANT (implied by C in GL_r), not wrong.  The note is
     sound but its hypothesis is not sharp.

T2 VERDICT
========================================================================
  The fusion square is exactly a CP-uniqueness question and Kruskal's
  bound 2r+2 = 8 is sound AND attained on it.  Under the note's
  hypotheses the budget is 9 (slack 1), so the Kruskal route is
  legitimate -- but the hypotheses are automatic, so it is also
  unnecessary: the singleton-support argument is shorter and covers
  strictly more (e.g. C singular, or A, B rank deficient).
