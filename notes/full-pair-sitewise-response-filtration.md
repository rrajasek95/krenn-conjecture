# The sitewise colour cover survives an arbitrary direct pair block

## 1. Statement

Let `p,q` be a deleted physical pair of a putative exact source of order
`N=2m+2`, and let `U` be the `2m` residual sites.  In the site-square-zero
algebra write

\[
 q=\sum_{u<v\in U}q_{uv},\qquad
 F=q^{[m-1]},\qquad h=q^{[m]}.
\]

Let `a=(a_ij)` be the arbitrary endpoint-ordered direct block on `pq`, and
let `p_i,s_j` be the two endpoint-star rows.  The complete nine pair
slices are

\[
        a_{ij}h+p_i s_jF=\delta_{ij}X_i,
        \qquad X_i=\bigotimes_{u\in U}e_i^{(u)}.          \tag{1}
\]

For every residual site define the internal incident space

\[
 W_u=\sum_{v\ne u}\operatorname{im}_u(q_{uv}),\qquad
 D_i=\{u:e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\}.                               \tag{2}
\]

Then, with **no rank or vanishing assumption on `a`**, equation (1)
forces

\[
             |D_i|\ge2m-2=N-4\quad(i=0,1,2),\qquad
             D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2=U.                         \tag{3}
\]

Thus the colour-cover and site-cover theorem from
[`sitewise-common-power-response-filtration.md`](sitewise-common-power-response-filtration.md)
is a theorem of the literal full pair packet, not only of the direct-free
response packet.

There is an immediate uniform rank consequence.  At least

\[
                              2m-6=N-8                         \tag{4}
\]

residual sites have `W_u=V_u`; this lower bound is interpreted as zero at
`m<=3`.  Indeed, if `t` sites are full and the rest have dimension at most
two, the total number of contained target axes is at most `4m+t`, whereas
the three covers require at least `6m-6`.

At `N=8` the bound permits every `dim W_u<=2`.  In that sharp boundary,
every `W_u` is a coordinate plane, each colour is omitted at exactly two
sites, and the three omission pairs partition the six sites.  The
pure-colour coefficient of `q^[3]` is then zero for every colour.
Consequently the corresponding diagonal target in (1) must come through
`p_i s_iq^[2]`; the pure-colour four-site coefficient of `q^[2]` is
nonzero and its complement is exactly the two-site omission pair.

This is a structural input for the remaining reciprocal/full-nine
classification.  It does not by itself couple two overlapping pair
charts, prove axis purification when some `W_u` has rank three, or resolve
the conjecture.

## 2. Scalarized full-pair matrix

Choose decomposable covectors `ell=(ell_u)`.  Let `C` be the symmetric
zero-diagonal cofactor matrix obtained by evaluating `q^[m-1]` off two
sites, and let `P,S` be the scalar endpoint-star matrices.  Evaluation of
(1) gives

\[
 M=a\,h(\ell)+P C(\ell)S^{\mathsf T}
   =\operatorname{diag}\left(
       \prod_u\ell_u(e_0),
       \prod_u\ell_u(e_1),
       \prod_u\ell_u(e_2)\right).                            \tag{5}
\]

Let `I_u` be the linear ideal generated by `W_u` and put
`J=sum_u I_u`.  Each entry of `PCS^T` has `J`-order at least `2m-2`.
Every monomial of `h=q^[m]` uses `m` internal edges and hence has one
incident factor at each of the `2m` residual sites.  Therefore

\[
 PCS^{\mathsf T}_{ij}\in J^{2m-2},\qquad
 h\in J^{2m}\cap\bigcap_{u\in U}I_u.                         \tag{6}
\]

The `i`th diagonal entry of (5) belongs to `J^(2m-2)`.  The exact order of
its target product is `|D_i|`, proving the three inequalities in (3).

## 3. Determinant and the site cover

Expand the determinant of (5) multilinearly.  A term choosing `k` direct
entries and `3-k` response entries has `J`-order at least

\[
        2mk+(2m-2)(3-k)=6m-6+2k.                             \tag{7}
\]

If `k>0`, it contains the common factor `h` and hence lies in every
`I_u`.  If `k=0`, it is a term of `det(PCS^T)`.  The exact Cauchy--Binet
incidence count from the direct-free theorem says that a nonzero such term
contains

\[
             3-\mathbf1_{u\in I}-\mathbf1_{u\in K}\ge1       \tag{8}
\]

internal factors at every site `u`; hence it too lies in every `I_u`.
It follows that

\[
        \det M\in J^{6m-6}\cap\bigcap_{u\in U}I_u.           \tag{9}
\]

But the determinant of the right side of (5) is

\[
                         \prod_{u\in U}\prod_{i=0}^2
                                  \ell_u(e_i).                \tag{10}
\]

Every `I_u` is prime.  Equations (9)--(10) force at least one target axis
into `W_u` for every site, proving the site cover.  Notice that the
argument uses the common `h` factor before expansion; it does not cancel a
hafnian or assume that the site-square-zero algebra is a domain.

## 4. The uniform full-site bound and the N=8 boundary

The three inequalities in (3) require at least `6m-6` target-axis
incidences.  The site cover gives at least one at every site.  If exactly
`t` sites are full and all remaining sites have dimension at most two,
there are at most

\[
                         3t+2(2m-t)=4m+t
\]

axis incidences.  Therefore `t>=2m-6`.  Since a subspace of a ternary
site containing all three target axes is the full site space, (4) follows.
The bound is incidence-sharp: take `2m-6` full sites and six coordinate
planes, two omitting each colour.

At `m=3`, if every `W_u` has dimension at most two, the site cover gives
at least one axis per site while the three four-cover inequalities give at
least twelve axis incidences.  There are at most twelve, so equality holds everywhere:
every site contains exactly two axes and every colour occurs at exactly
four sites.  Hence each colour has a two-site omission pair and the three
pairs partition `U`.

At either site of the colour-`i` omission pair, every incident internal
block has its local factor in a plane not containing `e_i`.  Thus no
monomial of `q^[3]` can have pure colour `i`.  The pure target `X_i` in
(1) must therefore come from the response term.  Its two star factors
occupy the two omitted sites, and the unique possible pure coefficient of
`q^[2]` occupies the other four.  Its coefficient is nonzero because the
target coefficient is one.
