# The uniform residue--Omega interface is a boundary-polar defect

## 1. Outcome

Let \(h\geq3\). On a physical cap line joining a clean unary endpoint

\[
                 (\sigma,F,X_0),\qquad \sigma\ne0,
\]

to its clean scalar-zero complementary endpoint

\[
                 (0,R,-X_1-X_2),
\]

the clean error has the exact factorization

\[
 {\cal E}(t,u)=tu\,\Omega(t,u),\qquad
 \Omega(t,u)=\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}t^{h-1-j}u^{j-1}E_j.       \tag{1}
\]

This note proves two uniform facts about the remaining degree-
\(d=h-2\) tensor polynomial.

First, after exposing one site and passing to the canonical odd-site
quotient, the missing diagonal residue is exactly the difference between
the physical first polar and the unary-endpoint value of the residual:

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma^{h-1}\operatorname {res}_{q_0}(r;t_c)
 =\pi_{q_0}\partial_{x,c}
       \bigl(RF^{[h-1]}-\Omega(1,0)\bigr).}             \tag{2}
\]

Here \(r=R|_{W\setminus\{x\}}\), and all notation is defined below. For
the displayed complementary target, (2) is

\[
 \pi_{q_0}\partial_{x,c}
       \bigl(RF^{[h-1]}-\Omega(1,0)\bigr)
 =-\sigma^{h-1}(\delta_{c1}\overline Y_1
                         +\delta_{c2}\overline Y_2).    \tag{3}
\]

Thus the residue is a canonical **boundary-polar defect** of the full
residual polynomial. It is not the value of the residual polynomial
alone: the physical first polar \(RF^{[h-1]}\) is the other term. The
difference in (2), unlike its two summands separately, descends through
the genuine vertex-gauge quotient.

Second, the exact uniform replacement for the \(h=3\) statement that the
two Omega columns are independent or exactly one is zero is the bounded
Bezout certificate

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 V(\Omega)\cap D(tu)=\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (tu)^{h-2}\in I_\Omega .
 \quad}                                                       \tag{4}
\]

The ideal \(I_\Omega\subset\mathbb C[t,u]\) is generated by all scalar
coordinates of \(\Omega\). Equivalently, there is

\[
 H(t,u)\in V^*\otimes\mathbb C[t,u]_{h-2},\qquad
                         \langle H,\Omega\rangle=(tu)^{h-2}.  \tag{5}
\]

This degree bound is uniform and retains all endpoint multiplicities. It
uses the whole degree-\((h-2)\) residual and makes no six-site
restriction.

For two overlapping source-provenant charts in this same diagonal
normalization, the power-free connection transports (2) exactly. It gives
the same class on both charts; for \(c=1,2\) this class is nonzero whenever
\(\overline Y_c\ne0\), and the connection does not make it vanish.
Simultaneous absence of a torus root therefore forces two certificates of
the form (5), together with one common boundary-polar defect. A formal
uniform guard in Section 7 shows that these statements alone allow an
arbitrary nonzero defect.

Section 8 consequently states a narrowly conditioned
**residue-compatible boundary exactness** target. It applies only when the
same literal source has both diagonal unary--complementary charts and a
compatible common-coloop corner which is visible to their exact
\(E_{00}-I\) scalar-zero responses. It does not supply that intersection
from the general common-coloop reduction: the latter naturally produces
an off-diagonal contraction
\(K_*=\tau E_{ab}-\alpha I\), and no comparison theorem between that
response and the diagonal Omega endpoint is proved here.

## 2. The exact residual polynomial and activity torus

Let \(W\) have \(2h\) sites and work in its site-square-zero algebra.
The two endpoint triples obey

\[
 F^{[h]}=\sigma^{h-1}X_0,\qquad
 R^{[h]}=0,\qquad
 Rq^{[h-1]}=-X_1-X_2.                                  \tag{6}
\]

On the ordered projective line use

\[
 (s(t,u),F(t,u),T(t,u))
  =(t\sigma,tF+uR,tX_0-uX_1-uX_2).                    \tag{7}
\]

The corresponding cap matrix is

\[
             tE_{00}+u(E_{00}-I)=\operatorname {diag}(t,-u,-u).
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]

Consequently the direct scalar and all three fixed diagonal coordinates
are nonzero exactly on

\[
                              D(tu)\subset\mathbf P^1.          \tag{9}
\]

There is no hidden additional activity divisor in this normalization.

Expanding the clean error gives (1), with

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 E_1&=R\bigl(F^{[h-1]}-\sigma^{h-1}q^{[h-1]}\bigr),\\
 E_j&=R^{[j]}F^{[h-j]},\qquad 2\leq j\leq h-1.
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{10}
\]

Indeed the \(j=0\) and \(j=h\) coefficients vanish by endpoint
cleanliness. Since the second scalar is zero, the only target correction
among the mixed coefficients occurs at \(j=1\), where (6) turns it into
the first line of (10). Every ordinary binomial coefficient and every
power of \(\sigma\) has therefore already been accounted for. In
particular,

\[
                         \Omega(1,0)=E_1.                      \tag{11}
\]

For \(h=3\), \(\Omega=tE_1+uE_2\), recovering the usual two-column
Omega packet. For \(h>3\), replacing (1) by its two endpoint columns
discards genuine mixed Hermite coefficients and is not valid.

## 3. Odd-site contraction and the boundary-polar identity

Expose one site \(x\in W\), put \(K=W\setminus\{x\}\), and write

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q&=q_0+\sum_c e_c^{(x)}t_c,\\
 F&=f+\sum_c e_c^{(x)}\ell_c,\\
 R&=r+\sum_c e_c^{(x)}n_c.
 \end{aligned}                                                \tag{12}
\]

Thus \(|K|=2h-1\). Set

\[
 A=q_0^{[h-1]},\qquad B=q_0^{[h-2]},\qquad
 C_{q_0}={{\cal R}_{2h-1}(K)\over {\cal R}_1(K)A},             \tag{13}
\]

and let \(\pi_{q_0}\) denote the quotient map. If
\(Z\in{\cal R}_2(K)\), recall the odd residue

\[
                 \operatorname {res}_{q_0}(Z;t_c)
                       =\pi_{q_0}(t_cZB).                     \tag{14}
\]

Let \(\partial_{x,c}\) take the coefficient of \(e_c^{(x)}\). From
(10)--(11), before making any quotient or cancellation,

\[
 RF^{[h-1]}-\Omega(1,0)
                    =\sigma^{h-1}Rq^{[h-1]}.                  \tag{15}
\]

This difference is intrinsic to the ordered endpoints:
\(RF^{[h-1]}\) is the \(u\)-normal derivative of
\((tF+uR)^{[h]}\) at \([1:0]\), while \(\Omega(1,0)\) is the
\(u\)-normal derivative of the full clean error there. Thus (15) is the
normal derivative of the fixed target term, not a chosen splitting of a
top tensor.

The coefficient of the right side at \(x,c\) is

\[
 \sigma^{h-1}\bigl(n_cA+rt_cB\bigr).                         \tag{16}
\]

The first summand belongs to \({\cal R}_1(K)A\). Applying
\(\pi_{q_0}\) to (15)--(16) proves (2), with no cancellation of \(A\)
or \(B\).

Taking the same coefficient in the last equation of (6) gives

\[
 n_cA+rt_cB
       =-\delta_{c1}Y_1-\delta_{c2}Y_2.                       \tag{17}
\]

Reduction modulo \({\cal R}_1A\) proves (3). More generally, if the
scalar-zero endpoint has target \(T_R=\sum_c\tau_cX_c\), then the right
side of (2) is

\[
                         \sigma^{h-1}\tau_c\overline Y_c.     \tag{18}
\]

This is the promised exact normalization. If raw canonical pair caps
are used instead, their quadratic is \(hR\) and their target row is
\(hT_R\); the factor \(h\) occurs on both sides and division by \(h\)
recovers (2). There is no missing \(h\) or \(h-1\) factor. The only
\(h-1\) multiplication identity used by the odd quotient is
\(q_0B=(h-1)A\).

## 4. Vertex-gauge invariance

For a vertex weight \(\beta\in\mathbb C^K\), let
\(Z_{q_0}^{\beta}\) be the genuine vertex-gauge quadratic. The
near-perfect matching identity is

\[
 Z_{q_0}^{\beta}t_cB
 =\left(\left(\sum_{y\in K}\beta_y\right)t_c
                 -\beta\mathbin\cdot t_c\right)A.             \tag{19}
\]

Hence

\[
                  \operatorname {res}_{q_0}
                     (Z_{q_0}^{\beta};t_c)=0.                 \tag{20}
\]

Equations (2) and (20) show that the **difference**

\[
 \pi_{q_0}\partial_{x,c}
       \bigl(RF^{[h-1]}-\Omega(1,0)\bigr)                     \tag{21}
\]

is invariant under changing the scalar-zero restriction by a vertex
gauge. No invariance is asserted for the two terms of (21) separately.
This is important: dropping the physical first polar would not define the
missing residue on the gauge quotient.

## 5. A bounded Bezout certificate for absence of a torus root

Let \(V\) be the full top-tensor space containing the coefficients of
\(\Omega\), put \(d=h-2\geq1\), and define the basis-independent ideal

\[
 I_\Omega=\bigl(\lambda(\Omega):\lambda\in V^*\bigr)
                         \subset S=\mathbb C[t,u].             \tag{22}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (uniform torus certificate)

For a nonzero \(\Omega\in V\otimes S_d\), the following are equivalent.

1. \(\Omega(t,u)\ne0\) for every \([t:u]\in D(tu)\).
2. \(I_\Omega:(tu)^\infty=S\).
3. \((tu)^d\in I_\Omega\).
4. There is \(H\in V^*\otimes S_d\) satisfying (5).

#### Proof

The equivalence of (1) and (2), the implication (3) to (2), and the
equivalence of (3) and (4) are immediate. It remains only to prove the
uniform exponent in (3).

Choose scalar coordinates \(f_i\in S_d\) for \(\Omega\). Under (1),
their greatest common divisor has no root in \(D(tu)\), so, up to a
nonzero scalar,

\[
                  \gcd(f_i)=g=t^au^b                          \tag{23}
\]

for some \(a,b\geq0\) with \(a+b\leq d\). Write

\[
                    f_i=g\widehat f_i,\qquad
                    e=d-a-b.                                  \tag{24}
\]

The degree-\(e\) forms \(\widehat f_i\) have gcd one. Over the infinite
field \(\mathbb C\), two linear combinations \(F,G\) of them may be
chosen coprime. If \(e>0\), the complete intersection \((F,G)\) contains
every binary form of degree at least \(2e-1\); if \(e=0\), it is the unit
ideal. Now

\[
 { (tu)^d\over g}=t^{d-a}u^{d-b},\qquad
 \deg\left({(tu)^d\over g}\right)=2d-a-b\geq2e-1.             \tag{25}
\]

Therefore \((tu)^d/g\in(F,G)\), with homogeneous multipliers of degree

\[
                       (2d-a-b)-e=d.                           \tag{26}
\]

Multiplying by \(g\) expresses \((tu)^d\) as degree-\(d\) polynomial
coefficients times linear combinations of the original \(f_i\). This
proves (3). \(\square\)

There is an equivalent endpoint-multiplicity description. If

\[
 \Omega=\sum_{k=0}^{d}t^{d-k}u^kE_{k+1},                      \tag{27}
\]

then the powers \(a,b\) in (23) are

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b&=\min\{k:E_{k+1}\ne0\},\\
 a&=d-\max\{k:E_{k+1}\ne0\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                \tag{28}
\]

After removing \(t^au^b\), the remaining coordinate forms have gcd one.
Thus absence of an active root permits arbitrary osculation at either
inactive endpoint; it says only that there is no further common factor.

The certificate also gives an exact full-coefficient syzygy. Write

\[
 H=\sum_{r=0}^{d}t^{d-r}u^rH_r,\qquad H_r\in V^*.             \tag{29}
\]

Then (5) is equivalent to the \(2d+1\) convolution identities

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{r+k=n}H_r(E_{k+1})=\delta_{n,d},\qquad 0\leq n\leq2d,
 }                                                            \tag{30}
\]

where \(0\leq r,k\leq d\). At \(d=1\), (30) is precisely the
two-column independence/one-zero alternative in dual form. For
\(d>1\), (30), not a rank test on \(E_1,E_{h-1}\), is the uniform
no-active packet.

## 6. Two source-provenant overlapping charts

Take two physical pair charts \(\nu=0,1\), each in the diagonal
normalization (7)--(8), which overlap in the same odd set \(K\). Their
exposed sites and star rows may differ, but their internal restriction is
literally the same \(q_0\). Denote the chart data by

\[
 (\sigma_\nu,F_\nu,R_\nu,\Omega_\nu,
       \partial_{\nu,c}),\qquad \sigma_\nu\ne0.                \tag{31}
\]

The power-free canonical-cap connection, multiplied only by
\(B=q_0^{[h-2]}\), gives the already audited transport law

\[
 \operatorname {res}_{q_0}(r_0;t_{0,c})
       =\operatorname {res}_{q_0}(r_1;t_{1,c}).                \tag{32}
\]

For the complementary scalar-zero row, both sides are simply

\[
        \zeta_c=-\delta_{c1}\overline Y_1
                        -\delta_{c2}\overline Y_2.             \tag{33}
\]

Combining (2) in the two charts gives the exact overlap syzygy

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \zeta_c
 &=\sigma_0^{1-h}\pi_{q_0}\partial_{0,c}
     \bigl(R_0F_0^{[h-1]}-\Omega_0(1,0)\bigr)\\
 &=\sigma_1^{1-h}\pi_{q_0}\partial_{1,c}
     \bigl(R_1F_1^{[h-1]}-\Omega_1(1,0)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{34}
\]

This is the uniform residue--Omega coupling. It retains the physical
first polars, all powers of \(\sigma_\nu\), the fixed diagonal targets,
and the common vertex-gauge quotient. The connection transports
\(\zeta_c\); it supplies no second equation which sets it to zero.

If neither chart has an active clean point, Theorem 5.1 supplies

\[
 H_\nu\in V_\nu^*\otimes S_d,\qquad
          \langle H_\nu,\Omega_\nu\rangle=(tu)^d
          \quad(\nu=0,1).                                    \tag{35}
\]

Equations (30) and (34) are therefore the uniform certificate--residue
interface to which the literal full-nine source overlap must be applied.
Notice that (30) constrains the \(E_j\)'s, whereas (34) compares \(E_1\)
to an additional source-provenant first polar. No formal operation on
(30) identifies those two tensors.

## 7. A sharp guard for the abstract interface

The failure just noted is algebraic, not an artifact of endpoint degree.
Let \(C\) be a one-dimensional vector space, choose
\(0\ne\zeta\in C\), let \(V\) contain independent vectors \(A,B\), and
choose a surjective linear map \(D:V\to C\). For each of two formal charts
put

\[
                        \Omega_\nu=t^dA+u^dB.                  \tag{36}
\]

There is no torus zero, and if \(\alpha(A)=1,\alpha(B)=0\), then

\[
             \left\langle u^d\alpha,\Omega_\nu\right\rangle
                            =(tu)^d.                           \tag{37}
\]

For arbitrary nonzero \(\sigma_\nu\), choose a formal first-polar value
\(\Pi_\nu\in V\) with

\[
                    D(\Pi_\nu-A)=\sigma_\nu^{h-1}\zeta.       \tag{38}
\]

Then both charts satisfy the exact degree-\(d\) torus certificates and
transport the same nonzero boundary defect in the form (34). The defect
is unconstrained.

This guard is deliberately only a guard for the **abstract interface**.
It does not assert that (36)--(38) arise from actual quadratics
\(F_\nu,R_\nu,q_\nu\), their consecutive divided powers, or the literal
full-nine source equations. Its conclusion is correspondingly exact:
the bounded Bezout identities, fixed boundary target, gauge-quotient
class, and flat transport do not by themselves force residue vanishing.
A successful proof must use how the first polar and all coefficients in
(10) arise from the same physical representatives on the two charts.

## 8. A conditioned diagonal common-coloop intersection target

The preceding identities remove both the special \(h=3\) rank language
and any implicit six-site restriction from this diagonal packet. They do
not route a general common-coloop residual into the packet. A sufficient
statement on the branch where the two structures are already known to
intersect can be made as one scalar boundary equality.

> **Conditioned diagonal residue--boundary exactness lemma.** Let one
> literal full-nine source have two overlapping source-provenant charts
> in the normalization (7)--(8), with common odd restriction \(q_0\),
> nonzero physical overlap curvature, and the required good endpoint
> stars. Assume in each chart that the exact response to
> \(K_{\rm diag}=E_{00}-I\) is the scalar-zero endpoint \(R_\nu\) used in
> its Omega polynomial. Assume additionally that the same source lies in
> a specified common-coloop residual configuration and that this exact
> diagonal response carries a surviving label
> \(c\in\{1,2\}\), so that
> \(K_{{\rm diag},cc}=-1\) and
> \[
> \operatorname {res}_{q_0}(r_\nu;t_{\nu,c})
>       =K_{{\rm diag},cc}\overline Y_c=-\overline Y_c\ne0
> \]
> in \(C_{q_0}\). If the two degree-
> \(d=h-2\) residuals admit the bounded certificates (35), then there are
> a chart
> \(\nu\in\{0,1\}\) and a functional \(\lambda\in C_{q_0}^*\), with
> \(\lambda(\overline Y_c)\ne0\), such that
> \[
> \lambda\pi_{q_0}\partial_{\nu,c}
>           \bigl(R_\nu F_\nu^{[h-1]}\bigr)
>  =\lambda\pi_{q_0}\partial_{\nu,c}
>           \bigl(\Omega_\nu(1,0)\bigr).                    \tag{39}
> \]

Equation (3) makes (39) impossible, so the lemma
would exclude simultaneous torus-root absence on the two charts. It is
strictly localized: it assumes the diagonal scalar-zero/Omega endpoint,
its compatibility with a specified common-coloop corner, and both finite
certificates, and asks only for equality of one scalar boundary
coefficient. It neither asserts a common root for an arbitrary tensor
polynomial nor assumes that a selected six-site restriction retains a
common divided power.

The compatibility hypotheses are essential. The general common-coloop
construction instead uses an off-diagonal
\(K_*=\tau E_{ab}-\alpha I\), whose three diagonal entries are
\(-\alpha\). The diagonal endpoint here has entries \((0,-1,-1)\) and
does not see a colour-zero corner. No result in this note converts the
off-diagonal response to this exact Omega endpoint or produces the two
overlapping diagonal charts. Thus the displayed lemma is an intersection
target, not a closure of the general common-coloop branch.

The guard in Section 7 proves that the literal full-nine representative
overlap is indispensable in this lemma. Conversely, the proved identity
(34) shows exactly what that overlap must add: it must turn one transported
boundary-polar defect into a boundary coboundary. Repeating the flat
connection or the Bianchi identity cannot do so, because those equations
already give (32)--(34).

No equality in (39) is claimed here. The proved advance is the uniform
identification (2), the bounded all-\(h\) no-root certificate (4)--(5),
and, on the conditioned diagonal intersection, the reduction of
simultaneous no-active behavior to the concrete compatibility of (30),
(34), and the literal full-nine overlap complex.
