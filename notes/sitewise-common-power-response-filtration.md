# A sitewise incidence filtration for the common-power response

## 1. Result

Let \(U\) be a six-set and work in

\[
 {\cal R}_U=\bigotimes_{u\in U}(\mathbb C\oplus V_u),
 \qquad
 q=\sum_{u<v}q_{uv},\quad q_{uv}\in V_u\otimes V_v.     \tag{1}
\]

Put \(F=q^{[2]}\), and suppose arbitrary multi-site rows satisfy

\[
 p_i s_jF=\delta_{ij}X_i,\qquad
 X_i=\bigotimes_{u\in U}e_i^{(u)},\qquad 0\leq i,j\leq2. \tag{2}
\]

The three target vectors are independent at every site. For each site,
let \(W_u\) be the smallest endpoint space for the incident blocks:

\[
 W_u=\sum_{v\ne u}\operatorname {im}_u(q_{uv})\subseteq V_u. \tag{3}
\]

Thus \(q_{uv}\in W_u\otimes W_v\). Define

\[
 D_i=\{u:e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\}.                              \tag{4}
\]

**Theorem 1.1 (four-cover and site cover).** Every system (1)--(2)
satisfies

\[
 |D_i|\geq4\quad(i=0,1,2),\qquad D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2=U. \tag{5}
\]

The proof is ideal-theoretic: it never selects a nonzero term from a
possibly cancelling sum. It allows arbitrary edge-block rank, parallel
blocks, multi-site rows, and complex cancellation.

The rank-at-most-two boundary is rigid.

**Corollary 1.2 (coordinate-plane boundary).** If

\[
                         \dim W_u\leq2\quad(u\in U),      \tag{6}
\]

then there are disjoint pairs \(B_0,B_1,B_2\) partitioning \(U\) such
that

\[
 W_u=\operatorname {span}\{e_d^{(u)}:d\ne i\}
                    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\in B_i. \tag{7}
\]

The single-colour (monochromatic) four-site part of \(F\) is exactly

\[
 \lambda_0E_0(B_0)+\lambda_1E_1(B_1)+\lambda_2E_2(B_2),
 \qquad \lambda_i\ne0,                                   \tag{8}
\]

where \(E_i(B_i)=\bigotimes_{u\notin B_i}e_i^{(u)}\).
Mixed four-site words may still occur in \(F\); (8) does not discard
them and is not yet the distinct-pure-lift theorem.

Sections 4--6 show the sharp boundary of this result. A one-site lift of
the audited scalar six-cycle realizes the full diagonal pencil
\(\operatorname {diag}(x_0,x_1,x_2)\), satisfies \(q^{[3]}=0\) and the
sitewise chain rule, but has \(W_u\) equal to only one target line. Thus
the improvement from one axis per site to the four-cover is genuinely
global across all six sites.

## 2. Scalar response and ideal orders

Choose decomposable covectors \(\ell=(\ell_u)_{u\in U}\), and put

\[
 P_{iu}=\ell_u(p_{i,u}),\qquad S_{ju}=\ell_u(s_{j,u}).    \tag{9}
\]

Let \(C=C_q(\ell)\) be the symmetric zero-diagonal cofactor matrix

\[
 C_{uv}=\left\langle\bigotimes_{w\ne u,v}\ell_w,
                   q^{[2]}\big|_{U\setminus\{u,v\}}\right\rangle . \tag{10}
\]

Keeping both endpoint orders in \(p_i s_j\) gives

\[
 M(\ell)=PC S^{\mathsf T}
  =\operatorname {diag}\left(
       \prod_u\ell_u(e_0),
       \prod_u\ell_u(e_1),
       \prod_u\ell_u(e_2)\right).                        \tag{11}
\]

Regard vectors in \(V_u\) as linear functions on \(V_u^*\). In the
polynomial ring on all covectors, let \(I_u\) be the linear ideal
generated by \(W_u\), and put

\[
                              J=\sum_u I_u.               \tag{12}
\]

Every monomial of an entry of \(C\) is a product of two edge-block
coefficients and therefore has four endpoint factors from the spaces
\(W_u\). The two row factors are unrestricted. Consequently

\[
                              M_{ij}\in J^4.              \tag{13}
\]

The exact \(J\)-adic order of the \(i\)-th target diagonal entry is

\[
 \operatorname {ord}_J\prod_u\ell_u(e_i)=|D_i|.          \tag{14}
\]

Indeed, \(\ell_u(e_i)\in J\) exactly when \(e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\), and
orders add because the associated graded ring of a polynomial ring along
a linear ideal is a domain. Equations (11)--(14) prove
\(|D_i|\geq4\). This is the sound per-colour incidence argument; it does
not infer that an individual matching term is nonzero.

For the determinant, Cauchy--Binet gives

\[
 \det M=\sum_{\substack{|I|=3\\|K|=3}}
       \det P_I\,\det C_{I,K}\,\det S_K.                 \tag{15}
\]

In every nonzero determinant monomial, the number of \(q\)-origin factors
at site \(u\) is

\[
              3-\mathbf1_{u\in I}-\mathbf1_{u\in K}\geq1. \tag{16}
\]

The sum of these six numbers is twelve. Hence

\[
 \det M\in J^{12},\qquad \det M\in I_u
                         \quad\hbox{for every }u.         \tag{17}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
                         \det M=\prod_{u,i}\ell_u(e_i).  \tag{18}
\]

Since \(I_u\) is prime, (17)--(18) force one of the three target factors
at site \(u\) into \(I_u\). This proves the site cover in (5). The global
\(J\)-order only recovers

\[
                         |D_0|+|D_1|+|D_2|\geq12,         \tag{19}
\]

which is already the sum of the three entrywise inequalities.

The adjugate gives no stronger count. Every two-by-two minor of \(M\)
belongs to \(J^8\), whereas

\[
 (\operatorname {adj}M)_{ii}
  =\prod_u\ell_u(e_j)\ell_u(e_k),\qquad\{i,j,k\}=\{0,1,2\}. \tag{20}
\]

Its order inequality is \(|D_j|+|D_k|\geq8\), the sum of two inequalities
already in (5).

## 3. The rank-at-most-two boundary

Under (6), each site contains at most two target axes. The site cover
gives at least one, while (19) gives

\[
 12\leq\sum_u\#\{i:e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\}\leq12.             \tag{21}
\]

Thus every site contains exactly two target axes and each colour occurs
at exactly four sites. Since \(\dim W_u\leq2\), each \(W_u\) is the
corresponding coordinate plane. Every site omits one colour and every
colour is omitted twice, proving (7).

Every occupied factor of \(F=q^{[2]}\) lies in its site's \(W_u\). A pure
lift \(E_i(P)\) can therefore occur only if every site outside \(P\)
contains \(e_i\), equivalently

\[
                              B_i\subseteq P.             \tag{22}
\]

Both are pairs, so \(P=B_i\). To obtain \(X_i\) in (2), the two row
factors must occupy the two sites of \(B_i\); hence its coefficient
\(\lambda_i\) is nonzero. This proves (8).

There is a useful response refinement. Write \(B_i=\{r_i,t_i\}\), let
\(f_0,f_1,f_2\) be the standard basis of the response-index space, and
define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_u^{(i)}
  &=\bigl(e_i^*(p_{0,u}),e_i^*(p_{1,u}),e_i^*(p_{2,u})\bigr)^{\mathsf T},\\
 b_u^{(i)}
  &=\bigl(e_i^*(s_{0,u}),e_i^*(s_{1,u}),e_i^*(s_{2,u})\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
                                                               \tag{23}
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking the coefficient of \(X_i\) in all nine equations gives

\[
 \lambda_i\left(a_{r_i}^{(i)}b_{t_i}^{(i)\mathsf T}
              +a_{t_i}^{(i)}b_{r_i}^{(i)\mathsf T}\right)
                         =f_i f_i^{\mathsf T}.            \tag{24}
\]

The second compound of the matrix in parentheses is

\[
 \left(a_{r_i}^{(i)}\wedge a_{t_i}^{(i)}\right)
 \left(b_{t_i}^{(i)}\wedge b_{r_i}^{(i)}\right)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{25}
\]

Since (24) has rank one, one wedge in (25) vanishes. The column or row
space of (24) then yields one of the exact orientations

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 P\hbox{-oriented}:&
  a_{r_i}^{(i)},a_{t_i}^{(i)}\in\mathbb C f_i,\\
 S\hbox{-oriented}:&
  b_{r_i}^{(i)},b_{t_i}^{(i)}\in\mathbb C f_i.
\end{array}                                               \tag{26}
\]

Both may hold. This includes cancellation between the two endpoint orders
and does not assert that either summand in (24) vanishes.

There is also an exact determinant frontier. In the sitewise multigraded
associated ring for the six plane ideals, the determinant target (18)
has order exactly two at every site. This statement concerns the
determinant, not an individual \(X_i\). Formula (16) shows that a term
with \(I\cap K\ne\varnothing\) also has a site of order three and cannot
contribute to that leading multidegree. Hence only

\[
                         I\cap K=\varnothing,\qquad I\cup K=U \tag{27}
\]

survives. The remaining plane-valued frontier is therefore controlled by
the complementary minors \(C_{I,U\setminus I}\), with a quotient
\(p\)-factor on \(I\) and a quotient \(s\)-factor on its complement.
No individual minor is asserted to be nonzero.

## 4. The local pencil on the incident annihilator

Fix \(u\), fix target-generic covectors at the other sites, and vary
\(x=\ell_u\). With \(u\) first, write

\[
 C(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&c^{\mathsf T}\\c&K(x)\end{pmatrix},
 \quad P=(p(x)\ \bar P),\quad S=(s(x)\ \bar S),          \tag{28}
\]

and put

\[
                         \alpha=\bar S c,\qquad
                         \beta=\bar P c.                 \tag{29}
\]

If \(x\in W_u^\perp\), every incident \(q\)-coefficient vanishes. Every
entry of \(K(x)\) uses one such coefficient, so \(K(x)=0\), and

\[
 M(x)=p(x)\alpha^{\mathsf T}+\beta s(x)^{\mathsf T}.     \tag{30}
\]

Thus \(\operatorname {rank}M(x)\leq2\), with exact adjugate

\[
 \operatorname {adj}M(x)
  =\bigl(\alpha\mathbin\times s(x)\bigr)
     \bigl(p(x)\mathbin\times\beta\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.    \tag{31}
\]

Suppose exactly one target axis \(e_a\) belongs to \(W_u\), and the
images of \(e_b,e_c\) are independent modulo \(W_u\). On \(W_u^\perp\),
the target pencil spans

\[
 \{\operatorname {diag}(0,r_bx_b,r_cx_c):x_b,x_c\in\mathbb C\}. \tag{32}
\]

A two-dimensional diagonal pencil can have the form (30) only if

\[
 \{\mathbb C\alpha,\mathbb C\beta\}
                         =\{\mathbb C f_b,\mathbb C f_c\}. \tag{33}
\]

For a generic member of (32), both rank-two factors in (30) have row and
column spaces equal to \(\mathbb C f_b\oplus\mathbb C f_c\); hence
\(\alpha,\beta\) lie in that plane. Write \(\alpha=(A,B)\) there. The
vector \((-B,A)\) lies in its kernel. Every diagonal matrix in (32) must
send that vector into the fixed line \(\mathbb C\beta\). Independent
variation of the two diagonal entries forces \(A=0\) or \(B=0\), and
\(\beta\) is the other coordinate line.

On the plane boundary (7), \(W_u^\perp\) kills the two contained axes and
the target in (30) has rank one along the omitted colour \(i\). Its
second compound is

\[
 \bigl(p(x)\wedge\beta\bigr)
 \bigl(\alpha\wedge s(x)\bigr)^{\mathsf T}=0.            \tag{34}
\]

On an irreducible target-generic chart, one factor vanishes identically,
giving one of the one-sided alignments

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 p(W_u^\perp)\subseteq\mathbb C f_i,\quad
                    \beta\in\mathbb C f_i,
 &\text{or}\\
 s(W_u^\perp)\subseteq\mathbb C f_i,\quad
                    \alpha\in\mathbb C f_i.
\end{array}                                               \tag{35}
\]

Thus the rank-two boundary carries both the omission-pair orientation
(26) and the local cofactor orientation (35).

## 5. The chain rule from \(q^{[3]}=0\)

The matching powers satisfy \(qF=3q^{[3]}\), but simply contracting
\(qF\) at all sites except \(u\) would count distinguished \(q\)-edges
that are not incident to \(u\). The clean derivation instead expands
\(q^{[3]}\) and groups each perfect matching by its unique edge incident
to \(u\).

For fixed covectors away from \(u\), put

\[
 z_{uv}=(\operatorname {id}_{V_u}\otimes\ell_v)(q_{uv})
                         \in W_u.                         \tag{36}
\]

Contracting the other four factors of each matching gives exactly
\(C_{uv}\). Therefore \(q^{[3]}=0\) implies the sitewise chain rule

\[
 \sum_{v\ne u}C_{uv}z_{uv}=0\quad\hbox{in }V_u.          \tag{37}
\]

Equivalently, if \(a_{uv}(x)=x(z_{uv})\), then

\[
                         \sum_{v\ne u}C_{uv}a_{uv}(x)=0. \tag{38}
\]

This vector relation is stronger than the scalar Euler relation, but the
next exact model shows that it does not raise the one-axis conclusion by
itself.

## 6. A one-site three-factor lift of the scalar six-cycle

Let \(V_0\) have basis \(e_0,e_1,e_2\), and let the other spaces be the
lines \(\mathbb Cz_1,\ldots,\mathbb Cz_5\). Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
q={}&2e_2z_1+z_1z_5-z_5z_2+z_2z_4+z_4z_3+2z_3e_2,\\
p_0={}&e_0,&p_1={}&(-z_1+z_3)/2,&p_2={}&z_4,\\
s_0={}&(-z_1+z_3)/2,&s_1={}&e_1,&s_2={}&-(z_1+z_3)/4.
                                                               \tag{39}
\end{aligned}
\]

The two perfect matchings of this cycle have weights \(-2\) and \(2\),
and both use \(e_2\) at site zero. Hence

\[
                              q^{[3]}=0.                  \tag{40}
\]

Normalize \(\ell_v(z_v)=1\) for \(v>0\), and write
\(x_i=\ell_0(e_i)\). The cofactor matrix is

\[
C(x_2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1&1&1&0&0\\
-1&0&0&0&-2x_2&2x_2\\
1&0&0&0&2x_2&2x_2\\
1&0&0&0&-2x_2&2x_2\\
0&-2x_2&2x_2&-2x_2&0&0\\
0&2x_2&2x_2&2x_2&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.                                           \tag{41}
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
 P C(x_2)S^{\mathsf T}=\operatorname {diag}(x_0,x_1,x_2). \tag{42}
\]

Thus the local determinant is the reduced product \(x_0x_1x_2\), and its
adjugate has the three expected pair products. Here

\[
                         W_0=\mathbb C e_2.               \tag{43}
\]

The chain vector is exact as well:

\[
 c=(-1,1,1,0,0),\qquad
 (z_{01},z_{02},z_{03},z_{04},z_{05})=(2e_2,0,2e_2,0,0), \tag{44}
\]

so \(\sum_vc_vz_{0v}=0\).

This is not a ternary six-site GHZ system: at sites \(1,\ldots,5\), the
three right-hand-side factors all lie on \(\mathbb Cz_v\). Its purpose
is to show that a one-site determinant, adjugate, full diagonal pencil,
and chain rule are simultaneously compatible with a one-dimensional
incident space.

audits the Cauchy--Binet incidence ledger, the compound and adjugate
