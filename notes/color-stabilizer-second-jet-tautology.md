# The color-stabilizer second jet is an Euler tautology

## Outcome

The diagonal local torus gives many exact constant-output curves through a
hypothetical source, but their mixed second derivatives supply no new
equations.  For two independent color-dependent vertex weights, the full
second-derivative identity is

\[
 S_{\alpha\beta}Q^{m-1}
 +(m-1)T_\alpha T_\beta Q^{m-2}=0.                       \tag{1}
\]

Here `T_alpha` is the first derivative of the source quadratic and
`S_(alpha beta)` is its mixed acceleration.  On every fixed coloring, the
left side of (1) is exactly the product of two Euler weights times the
original matching coefficient.  Thus (1) is coefficientwise in the linear
span of the defining equations `Q^m=m! Delta`.

After quotienting the ordinary color-blind vertex gauges, the genuinely
color-sensitive parameter space has dimension `2(n-1)`.  Nevertheless its
second fundamental form in the cokernel of the source Hessian is identically
zero: the explicit acceleration `S_(alpha beta)` kills it.  The same
tautology persists in the two-vertex contraction

\[
 (a_{cd}q+(m-1)p_cs_d)q^{m-2},                            \tag{2}
\]

so retaining the actual common power does not separate the direct-edge and
two-star contributions.

Consequently the pure color-stabilizer-orbit route cannot itself force a
structural contradiction for `n>=8` (or at any order).  A successful use of
second derivatives must insert a tangent not generated by the target
stabilizer, or an additional support, rank, norm, or integrability statement.

## 1. Euler derivations and the exact two-parameter curve

Let `B` have size `n=2m`, and work in the square-zero site algebra

\[
 \mathcal Z_B=\bigotimes_{v\in B}(\mathbb C\oplus V_v),
 \qquad V_vV_v=0,
\]

with color basis `x_(v,0),x_(v,1),x_(v,2)`.  Put

\[
 Q=\sum_{u<v}\sum_{i,j}A_{uv}(i,j)x_{u,i}x_{v,j}.         \tag{3}
\]

For a matrix of vertex-color weights `alpha=(alpha_(v,i))`, let
`D_alpha` be the diagonal derivation

\[
 D_\alpha x_{v,i}=\alpha_{v,i}x_{v,i}.                   \tag{4}
\]

It is a well-defined derivation of the square-zero quotient.  Define

\[
 T_\alpha=D_\alpha Q,
 \qquad S_{\alpha\beta}=D_\alpha D_\beta Q.              \tag{5}
\]

In edge coordinates these are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 T_\alpha(uv;i,j)
  &=(\alpha_{u,i}+\alpha_{v,j})A_{uv}(i,j),\\
 S_{\alpha\beta}(uv;i,j)
  &=(\alpha_{u,i}+\alpha_{v,j})
    (\beta_{u,i}+\beta_{v,j})A_{uv}(i,j).                \tag{6}
\end{aligned}

The Lie algebra of the diagonal target stabilizer is

\[
 \mathfrak t_\Delta=
 \left\{\alpha:\sum_v\alpha_{v,i}=0\quad(i=0,1,2)\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]

For `alpha,beta in mathfrak t_Delta`, the exact two-parameter source curve

\[
 Q(s,t)=\exp(sD_\alpha+tD_\beta)Q                         \tag{8}
\]

has edge entries

\[
 A_{uv}(i,j)
 \exp\!\left(s(\alpha_{u,i}+\alpha_{v,j})
             +t(\beta_{u,i}+\beta_{v,j})\right).         \tag{9}
\]

Every constant-color target monomial is fixed by (7).  Hence, if

\[
                         Q^m=m!\Delta_{B,3},              \tag{10}
\]

then `Q(s,t)^m=m! Delta_(B,3)` identically in `s,t`.

Differentiating first in `s` and then in `t` gives

\[
 mS_{\alpha\beta}Q^{m-1}
 +m(m-1)T_\alpha T_\beta Q^{m-2}=0,                      \tag{11}
\]

which is (1).  No arbitrary Hessian correction has been introduced: the
acceleration in (11) is the particular quadratic (6) on the original
source support.

## 2. Coefficientwise factorization

For a coloring `gamma:B->{0,1,2}`, write

\[
 x_\gamma=\prod_vx_{v,\gamma_v},
 \qquad
 A_\alpha(\gamma)=\sum_v\alpha_{v,\gamma_v}.             \tag{12}
\]

The monomial `x_gamma` is an eigenvector of `D_alpha` with eigenvalue
`A_alpha(gamma)`.  This immediately proves the following statement, which
does not assume (10).

**Lemma 2.1 (mixed Euler identity).**  For every quadratic `Q`, every
`alpha,beta`, and every coloring `gamma`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 [x_\gamma]T_\alpha Q^{m-1}
   &=\frac{A_\alpha(\gamma)}m[x_\gamma]Q^m,\\
 [x_\gamma]\bigl(
 S_{\alpha\beta}Q^{m-1}
 +(m-1)T_\alpha T_\beta Q^{m-2}\bigr)
   &=\frac{A_\alpha(\gamma)A_\beta(\gamma)}m
       [x_\gamma]Q^m.                                   \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]

**Proof.**  Apply `D_alpha` and then `D_beta` to `Q^m`.  The derivation
rule gives the left sides after multiplication by `m`; the eigenvalue rule
on `x_gamma` gives the right sides. `QED`

If (10) holds and `gamma` is mixed, its matching coefficient is zero.  If
`gamma` is constant with color `i`, then both Euler weights in (12) vanish
by (7).  Thus every instance of (11) is either a scalar multiple of a mixed
coefficient equation or zero on a constant coefficient.  In particular,
adjoining all first and mixed second orbit identities to the ideal of (10)
does not enlarge that ideal.

This also explains why differentiating different matching terms cannot
break a cancellation.  Every perfect matching compatible with one fixed
`gamma` uses each port `(v,gamma_v)` once, so all of its terms acquire the
same two Euler weights, independently of the matching.

## 3. Quotient by ordinary vertex gauges

The ordinary vertex-gauge subspace is

\[
 \mathfrak g=
 \left\{\alpha_{v,i}=a_v\text{ for all }i:
                   \sum_va_v=0\right\}\subset\mathfrak t_\Delta.       \tag{14}
\]

It has dimension `n-1`.  Every class in the quotient has a unique
representative

\[
 h_{v,i}=\alpha_{v,i}-{1\over3}\sum_{j=0}^2\alpha_{v,j}                \tag{15}
\]

satisfying both

\[
 \sum_i h_{v,i}=0\quad(v\in B),
 \qquad
 \sum_v h_{v,i}=0\quad(i=0,1,2).                         \tag{16}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \mathfrak t_\Delta/\mathfrak g
 \cong\{h:\text{the row and column sums in (16) vanish}\},
 \qquad \dim=2(n-1).                                     \tag{17}
\]

Let

\[
 \mathcal H_Q(Y)=YQ^{m-1}                                \tag{18}
\]

be the source-Hessian map into top degree.  At a solution (10), every
`T_alpha` with `alpha in mathfrak t_Delta` lies in `ker H_Q`.  The polarized
quadratic obstruction to lifting two such tangents is the cokernel class

\[
 \mathrm {II}_Q(\alpha,\beta)
 =\left[(m-1)T_\alpha T_\beta Q^{m-2}\right]
   \in\operatorname {coker}\mathcal H_Q.                 \tag{19}
\]

Equation (11) gives the explicit primitive

\[
 (m-1)T_\alpha T_\beta Q^{m-2}
                  =-\mathcal H_Q(S_{\alpha\beta}).        \tag{20}
\]

Hence `II_Q` is identically zero.  It consequently descends to (17), where
it is still identically zero.  Changing a representative by an ordinary
gauge changes (19) only by another zero class.  Thus removing the familiar
color-blind gauge kernel does not expose a residual color-sensitive
curvature obstruction.

## 4. The actual common-power pair contraction

The conclusion is unchanged if one retains the particular source and its
common internal power.  Fix distinct vertices `u,v`, let
`W=B\setminus{u,v}`, and write

\[
 Q=q+\sum_cx_{v,c}p_c+\sum_dx_{u,d}s_d
       +\sum_{c,d}a_{cd}x_{v,c}x_{u,d}.                  \tag{21}
\]

For `c,d` put

\[
 K_{cd}=a_{cd}q+(m-1)p_cs_d,
 \qquad R_{cd}=K_{cd}q^{m-2}.                            \tag{22}
\]

The exact two-vertex coefficient equation is

\[
 R_{cd}=(m-1)!\delta_{cd}
                 \prod_{w\in W}x_{w,c}.                 \tag{23}
\]

Let a dot with subscript `alpha` denote the derivative induced by (8) on
the displayed `q,p,s,a` data; for `p_c,s_d,a_cd` this includes the weight
at the deleted endpoint.  Put `r=m-2`.  Direct differentiation of the
particular expression (22) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (R_{cd})_{\alpha\beta}
  ={}&(K_{cd})_{\alpha\beta}q^r
   +r(K_{cd})_\alpha q_\beta q^{r-1}
   +r(K_{cd})_\beta q_\alpha q^{r-1}\\
  &+rK_{cd}q_{\alpha\beta}q^{r-1}
   +r(r-1)K_{cd}q_\alpha q_\beta q^{r-2},               \tag{24}
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (K_{cd})_{\alpha\beta}={}&
 a_{cd,\alpha\beta}q+a_{cd,\alpha}q_\beta
 +a_{cd,\beta}q_\alpha+a_{cd}q_{\alpha\beta}\\
 &+(m-1)(p_{c,\alpha\beta}s_d+p_{c,\alpha}s_{d,\beta}
       +p_{c,\beta}s_{d,\alpha}+p_cs_{d,\alpha\beta}).  \tag{25}
\end{aligned}
\]

For `n>=8`, all powers in (24) are nonnegative.  Equations (24)--(25) are
the requested exact mixed second-derivative identities with the common
power left unresolved.

Now fix a coloring `eta` of `W`, and extend it to `gamma` on `B` by
`gamma_v=c,gamma_u=d`.  Since

\[
 [x_\gamma]Q^m=m[x_\eta]R_{cd},                          \tag{26}
\]

Lemma 2.1 gives the sharper coefficient statement

\[
 [x_\eta](R_{cd})_{\alpha\beta}
 =A_\alpha(\gamma)A_\beta(\gamma)
                         [x_\eta]R_{cd}.                 \tag{27}
\]

Thus the long common-power expression (24) is exactly an Euler multiple of
(23) in every coefficient.  In particular it cannot distinguish the two
summands `a_cd q` and `(m-1)p_c s_d`: their cancellation in (23) is scaled
as a whole.

## 5. Consequence for the route

The target-stabilizer orbit is a family of genuine exact curves, so its
second fundamental form had to vanish.  Lemmas 2.1 and (27) make the
stronger algebraic fact explicit: the proposed identities do not merely
vanish at a solution; coefficient by coefficient they are scalar multiples
of the original defining equations.

There is therefore no order-specific contradiction to extract from these
orbit jets alone.  The `2(n-1)` color-sensitive quotient directions are
integrable symmetries, not candidate obstructed tangents.  A continuation
must leave the stabilizer orbit—for example by finding an extra
source-Hessian kernel direction—and then use (19) as a genuine
integrability test, or combine the source equations with independent
support/rank information that is absent from the Euler identities.
