# Source-ideal route at six vertices

## Result

The most direct source-parameter Nullstellensatz certificate does **not**
exist.  More precisely, work over

\[
 R=\mathbb Q[x_{uv}^{ab}:0\leq u<v<6,\ 0\leq a,b<3]
\]

and write

\[
 F_c=\sum_{M\in\operatorname{PM}(6)}
       \prod_{uv\in M}x_{uv}^{c(u)c(v)}.
\]

If `I` is the ideal generated by the 726 nonconstant (mixed) `F_c`, then

\[
 \boxed{F_{0^6}F_{1^6}F_{2^6}\notin I.}                     \tag{1}
\]

This is an exact characteristic-zero conclusion, not a floating-point rank
guess.  The reproducible audit is
`computations/test_degree9_source_ideal.py --koszul --prime 1009`.

Thus a proof of nonexistence cannot have the tempting degree-nine form

\[
 F_{0^6}F_{1^6}F_{2^6}=\sum_{c\text{ mixed}}Q_cF_c.          \tag{2}
\]

If the conjectured upper bound is true, the complex Nullstellensatz instead
implies that the product belongs to the *radical* of `I`; (1) says that one
must genuinely pass to a higher power (or use a localized/rational
argument).  Scheme-theoretically, the mixed-coefficient zero locus would
have nonreduced structure in the direction detected here.

## 1. Why degree nine and one multigraded piece suffice

Give `x_uv^{ab}` degree one at each of the two labels `(u,a)` and `(v,b)`.
Every `F_c` has degree one at `(v,c(v))` for each vertex `v`.  Put

\[
 P=F_{0^6}F_{1^6}F_{2^6}.
\]

Then `P` has degree one at all eighteen vertex/color labels and ordinary
degree nine.  Since the generators are homogeneous for this grading, any
ideal membership (2) could be replaced by its degree-nine, correct
multigraded part.  For fixed `c`, every monomial of `Q_c` must consequently
use exactly the two colors other than `c(v)` at every vertex.  It has degree
six.

Think of the twelve complementary vertex/color labels as stubs.  A monomial
of `Q_c` is exactly a perfect matching of these twelve stubs in which stubs
at the same underlying vertex may not be paired.  There are

\[
 \sum_{k=0}^6(-1)^k\binom6k(11-2k)!!=6040                 \tag{3}
\]

such monomials for each `c`.  Multiplication by `F_c` completes it in each
of the fifteen possible ways to a matching of all eighteen stubs.  This is
the entire relevant Macaulay map; no polynomial terms have been discarded.

## 2. Lossless symmetry reduction

Let

\[
 G=S_6\times S_3
\]

act by permuting vertices and globally permuting colors.  Both the ideal map
and `P` are `G`-invariant.  If (2) existed over `Q`, Reynolds averaging its
right side would give a `G`-invariant certificate.  It is therefore lossless
to restrict the Macaulay map to invariant orbit sums.

The mixed colorings have the six count types

\[
 5+1,\quad4+2,\quad4+1+1,\quad3+3,\quad3+2+1,\quad2+2+2.
\]

For representatives of these types, the numbers of stabilizer-orbits of the
6040 complementary matchings are respectively

\[
 70,\ 172,\ 188,\ 126,\ 556,\ 202,
\]

so the invariant domain has dimension `1314`.  The occurring full-monomial
orbits number `3102`.  Use normalized orbit averages as bases.  If a column
representative's fifteen completions hit a row orbit `m` times, its matrix
entry is simply `m`.  Hence the resulting integer matrix `A` has

\[
 \operatorname{shape}(A)=3102\times1314,
 \qquad \operatorname{nnz}(A)=16343.                         \tag{4}
\]

The expansion of `P` occupies eight row orbits, of sizes

\[
 15,270,360,1080,120,1080,90,360,                            \tag{5}
\]

which sum to `3375=15^3`, as required.  In the normalized-average basis,
these orbit sizes are the entries of the target vector `b`.

## 3. Exact characteristic-zero rank certificate

Sparse Gaussian elimination modulo the prime `1009` gives

\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_{1009}}A=1193,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_{1009}}[A\mid b]=1194.       \tag{6}
\]

The second number alone is not enough for a characteristic-zero conclusion,
because rank can fall after reduction modulo a prime.  An exact upper bound
over `Q` is supplied by integral Koszul syzygies.

For colorings `c,d` with `c(v)\ne d(v)` at every vertex, let `e(v)` be the
third color.  If both `c,d` are mixed and `R` is any vertex perfect matching,
then

\[
 (R_eF_d)F_c-(R_eF_c)F_d=0,                                 \tag{7}
\]

where `R_e` is the single monomial using the matching `R` with endpoint
colors prescribed by `e`.  Every multiplier in (7) has exactly the required
complementary multidegree.  Thus (7) is an integral kernel vector of the
Macaulay map.

Fixing `R=01|23|45`, there are 305 orbits of the pairs `(c,d)` under the
matching stabilizer and global color permutations.  Their Reynolds averages
give 260 nonzero integral invariant syzygies.  The script checks every one
directly over the integers (`A z=0`) and finds that 121 of their reductions
modulo `1009` are independent.  They are therefore also independent over
`Q`.  Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}A\le1314-121=1193.           \tag{8}
\]

The first rank in (6) gives the reverse inequality, so the rational rank is
exactly 1193.  The augmented modular rank in (6) then implies

\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}[A\mid b]\ge1194,
\]

and therefore `b` is not in the rational column span.  This proves (1).
Repeating the modular audit at `1013`, `10007`, and `65537` gives the same
ranks, although the single-prime argument plus the 121 integral syzygies is
already rigorous.

## 4. Interpretation and scope

The 121-dimensional invariant kernel is already spanned by the evident
monomial Koszul relations (7): modulo those relations the invariant
degree-nine source map is injective.  The class of `P` survives in the
quotient.  Thus the obstruction sought by this route is not a hidden linear
combination of the mixed equations at their first possible multidegree.

The stub-matching construction and Reynolds reduction work without change
for any even number of vertices and any palette size.  What does not yet
generalize is the small rank computation: both orbit counts grow.  At
`n=6,q=3`, the conclusion is complete for ordinary ideal membership but says
nothing by itself about radical membership.  It neither proves nor refutes
the existence of a finite complex solution; it sharply identifies the next
algebraic task as proving (or disproving)

\[
 P\in\sqrt I,
\]

equivalently finding some power `P^m` in `I`.

## 5. The square succeeds after diagonal specialization

There is a useful exact first step toward the radical question.  Set every
off-diagonal endpoint-color variable `x_uv^{ab}`, `a\ne b`, to zero, and let
`J` be the image of the mixed ideal in

\[
 R_{\rm diag}=\mathbb Q[x_{uv}^a].
\]

Then the complete degree-18 calculation gives

\[
 \boxed{P^2\in J.}                                           \tag{9}
\]

The audit is `computations/test_diagonal_power2.py`.  Here is why its finite
rank calculation is a rigorous rational membership proof.

A monomial of the target multidegree for `P^2` is, independently in each
color, a loopless multigraph on six labeled vertices having degree two at
every vertex.  There are 130 such graph monomials.  In the diagonal quotient
only mixed generators whose color-class sizes are `4+2+0` or `2+2+2` remain
nonzero.  For a generator `F_c`, a multiplier has vertex degree
`2-1_{c(v)=a}` in color `a`; enumerating all multigraphs with these degree
sequences gives the complete Macaulay domain.

After the same lossless `S_6 x S_3` Reynolds reduction, the two generator
types contribute respectively 3193 and 2337 column orbits.  The resulting
integer matrix has

\[
 874\text{ rows},\qquad5530\text{ columns},\qquad11682
 \text{ nonzero entries}.                                  \tag{10}
\]

The expansion of one squared hafnian has 120 monomials: doubled perfect
matchings have coefficient one and unions of distinct perfect matchings have
coefficient two.  The script checks that the represented target orbit mass
is

\[
 (15^2)^3=11{,}390{,}625,
\]

so no term of `P^2` is omitted.  Over `F_1009` the matrix has full row rank
874.  Therefore an 874-by-874 integer minor is nonzero modulo 1009, hence is
nonzero over `Q`; the rational matrix has full row rank and in particular
contains the target vector in its column span.  This proves (9).  The same
full rank occurs modulo 1013, 10007, and 65537 as a redundant audit.

This does **not** prove `P^2 in I` before diagonal specialization: (9) only
says that a degree-18 certificate exists modulo the ideal generated by all
off-diagonal variables.  Combined with (1), it localizes the next issue very
sharply.  Exponent one fails in the full arbitrary-matrix ring, while
exponent two already suffices once bichromatic edge entries are removed; any
new obstruction to a square certificate must be genuinely off-diagonal.

## 6. Full arbitrary-matrix square: exact filtered lifting through degree 5

Let `K` be the ideal generated by the 90 bichromatic variables
`x_uv^{ab}`, `a != b`.  Filtering the degree-18 Macaulay map by `K`-degree
turns the attempted lift of (9) into finite lower-triangular blocks.  The
scripts `computations/lift_power2_offdiag2.py`,
`computations/complete_power2_filtration.py`, and
`computations/dfs_power2_filtration.py` now prove the exact partial
lifting statement

\[
 \boxed{P^2\in I+K^6.}                                      \tag{11}
\]

The statement is over `Q`.  Its certification is as follows.

* The diagonal block has 874 rows and a nonzero 874-by-874 minor modulo
  1009, hence is surjective over `Q`.  Fix those 874 columns.  Taking all of
  their possible nonleading terms, independently of coefficient accidents,
  produces 8678 row orbits in `K`-degree two and 29 in degree three.
* In degree two, 8262 of the 8678 starting orbits are hit individually by a
  one-term leading `2+2+2` column.  After quotienting all such one-term rows,
  the components meeting the remaining 416 starts contain 2881 row orbits
  and 23919 column orbits.  Repeated singleton-column peeling removes all
  2881 rows.  Ordering the peeled rows and columns in reverse gives an
  explicit triangular integer minor, so this is characteristic-zero
  surjectivity without a modular inference.
* Reverse dependency minimization shows that the degree-two correction needs
  only 434 noncone triangular pivots and 8750 one-term cone pivots, for 9184
  columns in all.  Propagating this smaller correction leaves 396 starts in
  degree three.  The previously computed full degree-three component has
  20321 row orbits and 198783 column orbits and peels completely; its reverse
  dependency closure needs only 217 noncone pivots and 588 one-term pivots,
  for 805 correction columns.
* Degree four has 54693 starting row orbits.  Of these, 42969 are cone rows
  and 11724 survive the cone quotient.  Closing the entire component is too
  large, so the DFS script constructs the triangular minor goal-directly: it
  recursively assigns a leading column to each required row only after all
  other noncone rows in that column have been assigned.  Cycle rejection
  makes the resulting dependency order acyclic.  It uses 12889 noncone rows
  and 63139 one-term rows, hence 76028 correction columns, and encounters no
  failed start.  Reversing the dependency order gives a triangular integer
  minor with nonzero diagonal entries, so this remains an exact
  characteristic-zero argument.  The explicit ordered block is stored in
  `computations/certificates/p2_degree4_triangular.pkl.gz` (SHA-256
  `0c360238eaae086937dd2f11da535a2dabc9b861e2a5ade150d4652cc265882c`).
* Degree five has 76312 starts, of which 55921 are cones and 20391 survive.
  The same DFS construction succeeds on every start.  Its dependency closure
  has 24204 noncone rows and 94388 required one-term rows, for 118592
  correction columns in all.  Thus the degree-five block also has an explicit
  acyclic triangular certificate over the integers.  Its ordered block is
  `computations/certificates/p2_degree5_triangular.pkl.gz` (SHA-256
  `05f512d1d55fe8453d1a8bf8068a9b3d4bfe0f4d00ba390192785ed64e613c2c`).

The command `uv run python computations/verify_pivot_certificate.py` reloads
both compressed certificates, reconstructs every associated-graded column,
verifies distinct pivots and nonzero diagonal coefficients, and checks that
every off-diagonal entry precedes its pivot in the recorded order (or lies in
the one-term cone block).  It also reports the SHA-256 digest of each decoded
certificate payload.

After these corrections the first possible remainder is in `K^6`, proving
(11).  The checkpoint `/tmp/krenn_p2_filter_after5.pkl` records the structural
support bound and block counts.  Its remaining supports have 459843 orbits in
degree six, 1156307 in degree seven, and 698414 in degree eight.  These are
coefficient-independent upper supports, not assertions that every listed
coefficient is nonzero.  The DFS continuation resumes from the highest such
checkpoint; correcting degree six can create degrees seven through nine, so
degree eight is not a bound on the eventual tail.

### Off-diagonal degree constraints and the endgame

For any row, let `e_ab` be the number of bichromatic edges between colors
`a` and `b`, and let `d_a` be the number of monochromatic edges of color `a`.
The fixed target multidegree has twelve stubs of each color, hence

\[
 2d_0+e_{01}+e_{02}=2d_1+e_{01}+e_{12}
 =2d_2+e_{02}+e_{12}=12.                                  \tag{13}
\]

Consequently `e_01,e_02,e_12` have the same parity.  This rules out
off-diagonal degree one, but it does not split the reachable chain by parity:
all three counts may be even or all may be odd, and the diagonal certificate
already propagates to both degrees two and three.  Color balance also gives
only the sharp global bound `k<=18`; rows with all eighteen edges
bichromatic exist.  Thus neither parity nor color balance makes the current
degree-eight support terminal.

### Coefficient-aware degree-six probe

The support-only continuation contains a one-term degree-six cone whose
forced correction would emit a previously isolated unpivotable degree-nine
row.  This does **not** obstruct the actual square certificate.  The script
`computations/coefficient_power2_filtration.py` propagates one concrete
diagonal solution, with its coefficients, through the triangular blocks in
degrees two through five over `F_1009`.  It finds nonzero correction supports
of sizes `7941, 174, 63147, 90946`, respectively, and a degree-six residual
with `366992` nonzero row orbits.  The coefficient of the suspected cone row
is nevertheless exactly zero modulo `1009`.  Hence its unique cone correction
is not invoked and the alleged terminal degree-nine row is not produced.

This is an exploratory modular cancellation, not a rational membership
certificate.  It decisively invalidates only the proposed support-only
obstruction: any negative result at degree six must use a different row (and
its actual coefficient), while a positive continuation must retain
coefficients rather than the union of all reachable supports.

There is, however, a useful cap on the *leading blocks*.  A Macaulay column
has a residual monomial of degree fifteen.  If the generator coloring has
color-class sizes `n_0,n_1,n_2`, the minimum number of bichromatic edges in
one of its perfect matchings is zero when all `n_a` are even and one when
exactly two are odd.  Therefore a column of residual off-diagonal degree `l`
leads in degree `l` or `l+1`, and no associated-graded source column can lead
above degree sixteen.  A correction in degree `k` can still emit terms as
high as degree `k+3`, since the generator matching has three edges.  In
particular degree-six corrections can create degree nine, and degree-sixteen
corrections can create a terminal remainder in degrees seventeen or eighteen.
Those final two degrees have no later pivot block: they must cancel exactly
among the chosen lower-degree columns.  A coefficient-independent union of
possible supports will generally be too coarse to prove that cancellation,
so a full square certificate ultimately needs either coefficient tracking or
a structural homotopy that enforces a zero terminal tail.

### The dominant `4+2` pair-switch

There is a simple human-readable local move behind almost all of the DFS
pivots.  Let `S={i,j,k,l}` and let `{p,q}` be its complementary vertex pair.
Give the four vertices of `S` color `a` and `p,q` color `b`.  If a residual
monomial is `Q`, the leading part of this `4+2` column is exactly

\[
 Qx_{pq}^{bb}\left(
 x_{ij}^{aa}x_{kl}^{aa}+x_{ik}^{aa}x_{jl}^{aa}
 +x_{il}^{aa}x_{jk}^{aa}\right).                           \tag{14}
\]

Thus one may replace either color-`a` perfect matching of `S` by the other
two, while every bichromatic variable stays fixed.  This is the pair-switch
used by 12321 of the 12889 noncone degree-four pivots and 22048 of the 24204
degree-five pivots.

The local cone test is also transparent.  Let `M_0,M_1,M_2` be the three
perfect matchings of `S`, suppose the row contains `x_pq^{bb}` and the two
color-`a` edges of `M_0`, and let `d` be the third color.  If the residual
color-`d` diagonal support meets `M_i`, then the alternative term using
`M_i` contains a diagonal rainbow perfect matching: use that color-`d` edge,
the complementary color-`a` edge in `M_i`, and `pq` in color `b`.  Hence:

* if the color-`d` support meets both `M_1` and `M_2`, (14) kills the original
  row directly modulo the one-term cones;
* if it meets one, (14) has at most one surviving alternative and gives a
  one-dependency straightening step;
* only when it meets neither can both alternative terms survive this local
  cone test.

This explains the observed shape of the certificates: 11289 degree-four and
19695 degree-five noncone pivots have no noncone dependency, while another
1514 and 4287 respectively have exactly one.

It is important that this local move is not a global collapse theorem for all
rows.  There is already a cone-free row in degree nine with no incident
leading column at all.  Put, in each color, a diagonal triangle on vertices
`0,1,2`; on the nine nodes `(v,a)` with `v in {3,4,5}`, put a bichromatic
nine-cycle.  Every vertex/color node then has degree two, but the underlying
edge support is the disjoint union of two triangles and contains no perfect
matching.  Consequently no generator term divides the row, and no sequence
of pair-switches can start.  The exact audit is
`computations/verify_k9_isolated_row.py`.  This row cannot itself occur in a
Macaulay image for the same no-perfect-matching reason, so it does not refute
the target-specific lift; it does refute any proposed lemma claiming that
*every* row below degree sixteen straightens to a `2+2+2` cone.  A successful
theorem must use the provenance of rows reachable from the target/correction
chain.

Even that provenance is not enough for the present coefficient-independent
right inverse.  Define structural reachability recursively from the 874
diagonal pivot columns by adjoining every nonleading output of every selected
triangular correction column.  The checkpoint after degree five contains the
degree-six cone row

```
((0,0,3,30,57,72), (5491800,3188673,9566673)).
```

It has a unique one-term `2+2+2` leading column, namely

```
((0,0,1,1,2,2), (6,9,9,23,41,42),
 (3306744,13122,1595079)).
```

One of that column's nonleading terms is the degree-nine row

```
((0,0,3,3,11,56,58,77,87), (9566424,3188646,5314413)),
```

which is not a cone and has no incident leading column.  The script
`computations/verify_reachable_k9_obstruction.py` checks all four assertions
directly against `/tmp/krenn_p2_filter_after5.pkl`.  Hence the current
support-only lift cannot be completed by any theorem saying that its selected
corrections always straighten to cones: its degree-six cone right inverse
already seeds a dead degree-nine row.  This does not show that `P^2` is outside
`I`.  The degree-six row is only in an upper support; its coefficient in a
particular rational lift may vanish, and a different coefficient-aware right
inverse may arrange cancellation.  It shows that coefficient tracking or
terminal-tail constraints are mathematically necessary from degree six on.

### Why the unfiltered square matrix is impossible to materialize

A degree-18 target monomial is a loopless 2-regular multigraph on the 18
vertex/color nodes, with edges between nodes at different underlying
vertices.  Even the subset consisting of simple Hamilton cycles contains

\[
 18{,}818{,}838{,}501{,}120
\]

monomials.  Since `|S_6 x S_3|=4320`, these alone give at least
4,356,212,616 invariant row orbits.  At `K`-degree two there are exactly
312,975,000 raw rows, hence at least 72,448 orbits.  The filtered connected
component and one-term quotient are therefore essential, not optional
optimizations.

### Matching-complex interpretation

Let `Omega={(v,a)}` be the 18 vertex/color nodes.  A row basis element is a
degree-two multigraph on `Omega`.  For a coloring `c`, let
`T_c={(v,c(v))}` be its transversal.  A column is a residual multigraph of
degree `2-1_{T_c}`, and its image is the sum over the fifteen perfect
matchings of `T_c`.  Thus each filtered block is the relative incidence map

\[
 Q\longmapsto\sum_{M\in PM(T_c)}Q\cup M,                    \tag{12}
\]

where only matchings having the minimum possible number of bichromatic edges
are retained in the associated graded map.  A one-term `2+2+2` column is a
cone: it kills any row containing a diagonal rainbow perfect matching.  The
singleton peels in degrees two and three, and the acyclic dependency matchings
in degrees four and five, are explicit relative collapses of (12) after those
cones are removed.

This points to the required theorem-level continuation: prove that every
associated-graded block of (12), relative to the diagonal-rainbow cones, is
collapsible (or at least has full row rank) in the components reachable from
the diagonal target.  Such a contracting homotopy would finish `P^2 in I`
without enumerating degree four and would be the part most likely to extend
to larger even vertex sets.  The present computations prove that proposed
exactness in the first four nonzero filtered degrees; they do not yet prove it
in all degrees.  The current computation proves the lift through degree five.
