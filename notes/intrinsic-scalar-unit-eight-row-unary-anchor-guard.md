# The intrinsic scalar-unit collision needs the exceptional unary row

## 1. Outcome

Fix a characteristic-zero field \(\mathbb K\), \(h\geq3\), and the complete
physical
pair equations on \(2h\) residual sites

\[
 a_{ij}q^{[h]}+p_i s_jq^{[h-1]}=\delta_{ij}X_i,
 \qquad 0\leq i,j\leq2.                                  \tag{1}
\]

Suppose that the selected block is intrinsic:

\[
                         A=(a_{ij})=\alpha E_{aa},
                         \qquad \alpha\ne0.               \tag{2}
\]

This note gives one uniform lemma and one sharp physical guard.

**Intrinsic cap-quotient lemma.**  Expose any residual site \(x\), and
suppose that in the ordinary odd quotient only the selected target class
survives:

\[
             \overline Y_a\ne0,\qquad
             \overline Y_b=\overline Y_c=0,
             \qquad \{a,b,c\}=\{0,1,2\}.                 \tag{3}
\]

Then, for every \(h\geq3\):

1. in the formal nine-row coefficient space, the span of the eight
   non-\((a,a)\) cap rows is exactly the scalar-zero contraction space;
2. every row in that space has zero ordinary odd residue in all three
   labels;
3. the corresponding formal row-coefficient quotient is one-dimensional,
   generated by the unary cap \(E_{aa}\), whose normalized target--residue
   pair is
   \((X_a,\overline Y_a)\); and
4. every same-complement, same-power quadratic row lies on the selected
   target--residue graph

   \[
         (\lambda_a,\rho_a)=(\lambda_a,
                         \lambda_a\overline Y_a).          \tag{4}
   \]

Consequently a same-power companion which cancels the unary target also
cancels its residue.  The radial curvature carrier has zero ordinary odd
residue, even when its physical coefficient
\(\kappa\ne0\).  Thus no linear comparison which first passes through the
eight-row/scalar-zero cap quotient and the ordinary radial residue can
transport a nonzero unary class.  This is an impossibility theorem for
that specified class of operations, not a claim that no secondary
filtered operation can exist.

The guard in Section 4 shows that the lemma is sharp at the physical-row
level.  At \(h=4\) there is one literal ten-site aggregate packet with

* an intrinsic selected block \(E_{22}\);
* four good endpoint-star maps on two overlapping charts;
* a literal curvature minor \(1\);
* exactly the eight non-\((2,2)\) full-nine pair equations;
* on the displayed cap line, a clean scalar-zero complementary boundary
  and no active clean point;
* among the three target residues, ordinary odd residue supported only in
  label \(2\); and
* clean error exactly

  \[
                              {\cal E}(t,u)=-t^4X_2.       \tag{5}
  \]

Its sole failed pair row is the exceptional unary equation \(0=X_2\).
This is the selected-pair reinterpretation of the prior
[80-of-81 scalar-unit guard](uncontracted-four-cut-scalar-unit-eighty-row-injective-guard.md),
with one direct block specialized to \(E_{22}\).  It decisively rules out
forcing complementary residue survival from the other eight rows,
injectivity, curvature, and literal overlap alone.

The gain is therefore an exact boundary, not a proof of the conjecture.
Any positive intrinsic-collision argument must use the exceptional
\((a,a)\) row before ordinary residue, retain its order-\(h\) filtration
piece, and cancel its target by a genuinely cross-quotient or secondary
mechanism.  Isotropic packets and star injectivity cannot replace that
row.

## 2. The all-\(h\) cap quotient

Normalize the nine literal caps by

\[
 \widehat {\cal P}_{ij}
      ={1\over h}\bigl(a_{ij}q+h p_i s_j\bigr),
 \qquad
 \widehat {\cal P}_{ij}q^{[h-1]}=\delta_{ij}X_i.         \tag{6}
\]

For \(L=(L_{ij})\in\operatorname {Mat}_3\), put

\[
 \sigma(L)=\sum_{i,j}L_{ij}a_{ij},\qquad
 r(L)=\sum_{i,j}L_{ij}p_i s_j,\qquad
 T(L)=\sum_iL_{ii}X_i,                                   \tag{7}
\]

and

\[
 \widehat {\cal P}(L)={\sigma(L)\over h}q+r(L),
 \qquad \widehat {\cal P}(L)q^{[h-1]}=T(L).             \tag{8}
\]

Expose \(x\), write

\[
 q=q_0+\sum_de_d^{(x)}t_d,qquad
 X_d=e_d^{(x)}Y_d,qquad
 A_0=q_0^{[h-1]},\quad B_0=q_0^{[h-2]},                  \tag{9}
\]

and use

\[
 C_{q_0}={{\cal R}_{2h-1}(W\setminus\{x\})
             \over {\cal R}_1(W\setminus\{x\})A_0},
 \qquad \overline Y_d=[Y_d].                            \tag{10}
\]

If the restriction of a quadratic \(Q\) away from \(x\) is
\(\overline Q\), define

\[
                         \rho_d(\overline Q)
                             =[\overline Q,t_dB_0].       \tag{11}
\]

Taking the \((x,d)\)-coefficient of (8) gives a linear form times \(A_0\)
plus the term in (11).  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
   \rho_d\bigl(\widehat {\cal P}(L)|_{W\setminus\{x\}}\bigr)
                         =L_{dd}\overline Y_d.}          \tag{12}
\]

Only the nonzero scalar \(h\) was divided out.  Endpoint order, all three
physical labels, and every diagonal target are retained.

Under (2),

\[
                         \sigma(L)=\alpha L_{aa}.         \tag{13}
\]

Let

\[
 H_a=\operatorname {span}\{E_{ij}:(i,j)\ne(a,a)\}
       =\{L:L_{aa}=0\}.                                  \tag{14}
\]

Equations (13)--(14) prove the exact equality

\[
                         H_a=\ker\sigma.                  \tag{15}
\]

Thus every formal scalar-zero contraction is already a contraction of the
eight non-\((a,a)\) rows, and conversely.  This is a statement in the
coefficient space of physical rows; it does not assume that the nine
quadratics are linearly independent.  Under (3), formula (12) vanishes for
every \(L\in H_a\) and every label.  Its target lies in
\(\mathbb K X_b+\mathbb K X_c\).  On the formal coefficient quotient,

\[
 \operatorname {Mat}_3/H_a\cong\mathbb K[E_{aa}],
 \qquad
 [E_{aa}]\longmapsto
     \bigl(\alpha,X_a,\overline Y_a\bigr)                \tag{16}
\]

through the direct scalar, target, and normalized residue respectively.
All three displayed outputs are nonzero.  This proves the first three
claims of the lemma.

The point of (15) is stronger than the familiar statement that one
chosen scalar-zero cap is blind: **every** scalar-zero cap is blind, and
there is no adaptive scalar-zero direction inside this block which can
leave \(L_{aa}\ne0\).

## 3. Same-power and radial impossibility

The preceding conclusion does not use that a quadratic is a cap
contraction.  Let an arbitrary literal quadratic on the same even
complement satisfy

\[
                         \Theta q^{[h-1]}
                              =\sum_d\lambda_dX_d.        \tag{17}
\]

Write

\[
                         \Theta=\overline\Theta
                              +\sum_de_d^{(x)}\ell_d.     \tag{18}
\]

The \((x,d)\)-coefficient of (17) is

\[
                 \ell_dA_0+\overline\Theta,t_dB_0
                              =\lambda_dY_d.              \tag{19}
\]

Reduction modulo \({\cal R}_1A_0\) proves

\[
 \boxed{\rho_d(\overline\Theta)=\lambda_d\overline Y_d.}
                                                                  \tag{20}
\]

For \(d=a\), this is exactly the graph (4).  Hence a sum of
same-complement, same-power rows with zero selected target has zero
selected residue.  In particular,
the normalized unary cap has pair \((1,\overline Y_a)\), while every
companion with target \(-X_a\) has pair
\((-1,-\overline Y_a)\).  Their target-cancelled sum is \((0,0)\), not a
nonzero unary response class.

The selected overlap curvature does not change this ordinary quotient.
On the odd common complement its surviving internal carrier is radial,
say \(\kappa q_0\).  Divided-power multiplication gives

\[
 \rho_a(\kappa q_0)
   =\kappa[q_0t_aB_0]
   =\kappa(h-1)[t_aA_0]=0.                               \tag{21}
\]

Equation (21) remains true after coefficientwise multiplication by any
clean-line or transverse scalar polynomial.  It does not say that the
literal curvature row is useless before filtration.  It says precisely
that replacing it by its radial associated-graded carrier and then using
the ordinary odd quotient cannot transport the unary generator (16).

There is also a literal site-degree no-go for an ordinary cup.  The
four-cut curvature/direct-double comparison is top degree on the common
\((2h-2)\)-site complement.  Multiplication there by the unary cap
quadratic has degree \(2h\) and is zero in the site-square-zero algebra.
The desired odd class instead has degree \(2h-1\) on a different
\((2h-1)\)-site complement.  A successful operation must therefore include
a filtered restriction--insertion or another genuinely secondary
comparison; ordinary multiplication is not of the required type.

Finally, the order jump is intrinsic.  Let \(D\in\operatorname {Mat}_3\)
have

\[
 D_{aa}=0,qquad d_b=D_{bb}\ne0,qquad d_c=D_{cc}\ne0,
 \qquad K_0=E_{aa},\quad K_1=-\alpha D.                  \tag{22}
\]

For \(K(t,u)=tK_0+uK_1\), equations (2) and (22) give

\[
 s(t,u)=\alpha t,qquad
 T(t,u)=tX_a-\alpha u(d_bX_b+d_cX_c).                    \tag{23}
\]

Therefore the target correction in the clean error is

\[
 \boxed{
 s^{h-1}T
   =\alpha^{h-1}t^hX_a
     -\alpha^h t^{h-1}u(d_bX_b+d_cX_c).}                 \tag{24}
\]

The selected target is zero in every transverse principal part of order
less than \(h\), and its order-\(h\) symbol is the nonzero class
\(\alpha^{h-1}X_a\).  The complementary target begins in order \(h-1\).
Equations (15), (20), and (24) give the exact necessary condition:

> An intrinsic collision proof using the surviving class
> \(\overline Y_a\) must retain the exceptional \((a,a)\) cap through the
> order-\(h\) Rees symbol and null-homotope its target outside the
> same-complement same-power graph.  Any comparison truncated at order
> \(h-1\), factoring through \(H_a\), or applying ordinary radial residue
> first is identically blind to that class.

This is a guard/necessary condition.  No order-\(h\) secondary
null-homotopy is constructed here.

## 4. A physical \(h=4\) eight-row guard

Use ten sites

\[
                       \{i,j,p,q,0,1,2,3,4,5\}
\]

and three fixed physical colours.  Every displayed cell has weight one
and equal colours at its endpoints.  Put

\[
                         A_{ij}=E_{22},\qquad A_{pq}=E_{22},
                                                                  \tag{25}
\]

make all four cross blocks between \(\{i,j\}\) and \(\{p,q\}\) zero, and
on \(D=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) set

\[
 z=(23)_0+(04)_1,                                        \tag{26}
\]

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 d&0&1&2\\ \hline
 t_d=A_{iD}(d,-)&e_0^{(0)}&e_1^{(1)}&e_2^{(3)}\\
 v_d=A_{jD}(d,-)&e_0^{(1)}&e_1^{(2)}&e_2^{(3)},
\end{array}                                               \tag{27}
\]

and

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 d&0&1&2\\ \hline
 x_d=A_{pD}(d,-)&e_0^{(4)}&e_1^{(5)}&0\\
 y_d=A_{qD}(d,-)&e_0^{(5)}&e_1^{(3)}&0.
\end{array}                                               \tag{28}
\]

These are exactly the data of the 80-of-81 guard with its arbitrary
block specialized to \(E_{22}\), but now viewed from the \(ij\)-pair.
The complete aggregate quadratic has the exact binary top tensor

\[
                         Q^{[5]}=X_0+X_1.                 \tag{29}
\]

Let \(W=\{p,q,0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) be the complement of \((i,j)\).  Its internal
quadratic is

\[
 q=(pq)_2+e_0^{(p)}x_0+e_1^{(p)}x_1
       +e_0^{(q)}y_0+e_1^{(q)}y_1+z.                    \tag{30}
\]

Sorting (29) by the colours at \((i,j)\) gives the nine pair rows

\[
 (E_{22})_{cd}q^{[4]}+t_cv_dq^{[3]}
   =\begin{cases}
       X_c^W,&c=d\in\{0,1\},\\
       0,&c\ne d\text{ or }c=d=2.
     \end{cases}                                         \tag{31}
\]

Thus all eight non-\((2,2)\) equations are exact and the sole physical
residual is

\[
                         0-X_2^W=-X_2^W.                  \tag{32}
\]

Both triples in (27) are injective.  The overlapping pair \(ip\) is good
as well: its \(i\)-star contains

\[
             (t_0,t_1,t_2+e_2^{(j)}),
\]

and its \(p\)-star contains

\[
             (x_0,x_1,e_2^{(q)}),
\]

which are again injective triples.  At the all-colour-two rectangle,

\[
 \kappa=A_{ij}(2,2)A_{pq}(2,2)
          -A_{ip}(2,2)A_{jq}(2,2)=1.                    \tag{33}
\]

All target-free, power-free connection, normal, and four-cut compatibility
identities are literal because these are blocks of one aggregate quadratic.

The odd residue is exactly the hard one-colour packet.  Expose site \(1\)
of \(D\).  No edge of \(q\) meets that site, so its restriction is still
\(q_0=q\).  Put \(A_0=q_0^{[3]}\).  Direct multiplication gives

\[
                    e_0^{(0)}A_0=Y_0,qquad
                    e_1^{(2)}A_0=Y_1.                    \tag{34}
\]

Hence \(\overline Y_0=\overline Y_1=0\).  Exact row reduction gives

\[
 \operatorname {rank}({\cal R}_1A_0)=15,
 \qquad
 \operatorname {rank}({\cal R}_1A_0+\mathbb K Y_d)
                         =(15,15,16)_{d=0,1,2},             \tag{35}
\]

and adjoining all three targets still has rank \(16\).  Thus the span of
the three target residues has a one-dimensional image in the quotient,
generated by \(\overline Y_2\).  Concretely, \(q\) has only one colour-two
edge, \((pq)_2\).  No product of three \(q\)-edges,
followed by one linear factor, can equal the seven-factor pure word
\(Y_2\).  Therefore

\[
                         \overline Y_2\ne0.              \tag{36}
\]

Finally put

\[
 K_0=E_{22},\qquad K_1=E_{22}-I,qquad
 R=-(t_0v_0+t_1v_1).                                    \tag{37}
\]

The two edges in \(R\) meet at site \(1\), and (31) gives

\[
 q^{[4]}=0,qquad R^{[2]}=0,qquad
 Rq^{[3]}=-(X_0+X_1).                                   \tag{38}
\]

For \(K(t,u)=tK_0+uK_1\), the direct scalar and target are

\[
                         s=t,qquad
                         T=tX_2-u(X_0+X_1).              \tag{39}
\]

Using (38) in the divided-power expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (tq+uR)^{[4]}&=-t^3u(X_0+X_1),\\
 s^3T&=t^4X_2-t^3u(X_0+X_1),
\end{aligned}
\]

and hence (5).  Here the direct scalar and the three target coefficients
are respectively \(t\) and \((-u,-u,t)\), so the exact activity polynomial is

\[
                         \operatorname {Act}(t,u)=t^2u^2. \tag{40}
\]

The clean-error ideal is \((t^4)\), hence its only projective clean root is
\([t:u]=[0:1]\), the scalar-zero complementary boundary.  It is inactive;
the other inactive point \([1:0]\) is dirty, and every point with
\(tu\ne0\) is active and dirty.  Thus this cap line has no active clean
point.  Also

\[
                         \sigma(K)=K_{22},               \tag{41}
\]

so every scalar-zero cap has \(K_{22}=0\), exactly as in the intrinsic
all-\(h\) lemma.

The guard has an exact binary **top tensor**, not an exact ternary target.
Its quadratic uses a third, matching-dormant colour, so it is not a
two-palette quadratic in the sense required by binary-source reductions.
Its failure is deliberately the one ternary equation whose use is at
issue.  It does not disprove a theorem using all nine rows; it proves that
the other eight rows, good stars, curvature, clean complementary boundary,
and common power do not already force complementary residue survival or an
active clean point.

## 5. Relation to the prior scalar-unit guards

The two earlier guards now have a common interpretation.

* The
  [full-isotropic-packet guard](uncontracted-four-cut-scalar-unit-full-isotropic-packet-guard.md)
  permits dense injective padding but restricts contractions by
  \(\alpha_2\beta_2=0\).  It therefore suppresses the same exceptional
  \((2,2;2,2)\) coefficient.  Its density cannot certify that coefficient.
* The
  [80-of-81 guard](uncontracted-four-cut-scalar-unit-eighty-row-injective-guard.md)
  restores every nonisotropic row except that coefficient.  Viewed from
  the \(E_{22}\)-pair, it is precisely Section 4: the selected endpoint
  stars are good, a direct \(E_{22}\) block completes the overlapping good
  chart and gives curvature \(1\), and the missing four-cut coefficient is
  the missing unary pair row.

Thus neither all isotropic contractions nor endpoint injectivity can be
used in place of the exceptional row.  A positive argument may also need
the dense rank-three graph or a source-faithful common-power overlap to
*use* that row, but it must retain the row itself.  The all-\(h\) graph
obstruction in Sections 2--3 then says what such an argument must do next:
it must keep the order-\(h\) unary class before ordinary residue and leave
the same-complement target--residue graph.
