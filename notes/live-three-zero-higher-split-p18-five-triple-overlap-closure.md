# Higher splits: the \(p=18\) five-triple overlap closure

## 1. Result

On the no-extra-singular live-three-zero stratum, put

\[
                  p=h+k=18,\qquad 13\leq h\leq17.                  \tag{1}
\]

Every \(a=5\) family on the exact five-dimensional boundary is
impossible.  Explicitly, there is no profile

\[
 \boxed{\qquad 3^5\,2^b\,1^{\,h+5-2b},
                    \qquad b=0,1,2,3.\qquad}                      \tag{2}
\]

These are exactly the four \(a=5\) members of the fifty-family
[\(p=18\) census](live-three-zero-higher-split-q5-boundary-census.md).
Indeed, the census equation \(3a+2b+u=20\) gives \(u=5-2b\), and its
applicability alternatives give precisely \(0\leq b\leq3\).

The proof again compares two formal selections of one collision profile.
Unlike the six-triple closure, no partial-fraction normal form is needed:
the relevant complementary Schubert problems already have degree one,
and a single common simple-root equation is incompatible with exchanging
one selected value.

## 2. Exact classification of all formal selections

Let \(t\in\{0,1\}\) record whether a selected role-two class is an exact
triple.  The complete list of \(d\leq2\) formal selections for (2), with
the selected labels removed, is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
b&u=5-2b&(d,t)&\text{complement}\\ \hline
0&5 &(0,0)&3^5 1^3\\
 &&(1,1)&3^4 1^6\\ \hline
1&3 &(0,0)&3^5 2\,1\\
 &&(1,0)&3^5 1^3\\
 &&(1,1)&3^4 2\,1^4\\
 &&(2,1)&3^4 1^6\\ \hline
2&1 &(1,0)&3^5 2\,1\\
 &&(1,1)&3^4 2^2 1^2\\
 &&(2,0)&3^5 1^3\\
 &&(2,1)&3^4 2\,1^4\\ \hline
3&-1&(2,0)&3^5 2\,1\\
 &&(2,1)&3^4 2^2 1^2
\end{array}                                                       \tag{3}
\]

Thus the first three families have a particularly convenient selection
with complement \(3^5 1^3\), while the last has one with complement
\(3^5 2\,1\).  These two complement types suffice to close all four
families.

For every selection in (3), the selected-row kernel is exactly
five-dimensional and its row-relation space is exactly
three-dimensional.  This is Section 2 of
[the six-triple overlap closure](live-three-zero-higher-split-p18-six-triple-overlap-closure.md):
the \(q=6\) Wronskian gap is ten, and the four-dimensional incidence
branch is impossible.  Since every complementary part is at most three,
the dual Wronskian bound is saturated and its relation three-space
\(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathbb C[z]_{\leq c-4}\) is gcd-free.

We use the exact differential identity

\[
 {d\over dz}{(z+\mu)^{k+1}N\over A}
 ={(z+\mu)^kg\over A^2}\,Q^2H\,S,
 \qquad S\in\mathcal S,                                  \tag{4}
\]

where \(A\) is the complementary polynomial,
\(g=\prod_{A(a)=0}(z-a)^{\operatorname {ord}_a(A)-1}\), and \(Q,H\)
are the products of the selected role-two and singleton plus-pole
factors.

## 3. Three complementary singletons close \(b=0,1,2\)

Let \(b\leq2\).  Select all \(b\) exact double classes at role two and
select

\[
                              h+2-2b                              \tag{5}
\]

singleton classes.  This leaves five complementary triples and three
complementary singletons.  Write

\[
 X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_5\},\quad R=\prod_{x\in X}(z-x),\quad
 B=\{a_1,a_2,a_3\},\quad J=\prod_{a\in B}(z-a).                   \tag{6}
\]

Thus \(A=R^3J\), \(g=R^2\), and \(c=8\).  The saturated relation space is
the unique solution of three codimension-two simple-root conditions in
\(\mathbb C[z]_{\leq4}\):

\[
 \mathcal S_B=\operatorname {span}\bigl\{
 (z-a_1)^2(z-a_2)^2,
 (z-a_1)^2(z-a_3)^2,
 (z-a_2)^2(z-a_3)^2\bigr\}.                              \tag{7}
\]

Its Wronskian is

\[
                 C\prod_{a\in B}(z-a)^2.                         \tag{8}
\]

Fix \(a\in B\), and put \(J_a=J/(z-a)\).  At this simple complementary
root, (4) has local unit

\[
                  U_a={ (z+\mu)^kQ^2H\over R^4J_a^2}.              \tag{9}
\]

The unique line of (7) which is nonzero at \(a\) is generated by
\(J_a^2\).  The residue equation \((U_aS)'(a)=0\) therefore says

\[
 {U_a'(a)\over U_a(a)}=-{(J_a^2)'(a)\over J_a^2(a)}.              \tag{10}
\]

The two \(J_a\) terms cancel after taking the logarithmic derivative of
(9), leaving the simple equation

\[
 \Phi_{Q,H}(a):={k\over a+\mu}
       +2\sum_{u\in Q}{1\over a+u}
       +\sum_{y\in H}{1\over a+y}
       -4\sum_{x\in X}{1\over a-x}=0.                            \tag{11}
\]

Choose distinct \(a,c\in B\) and any selected singleton \(r\in H\).
Swap \(a\) and \(r\): select \(a\), leave \(r\) complementary, and keep
all other roles unchanged.  This is another formal selection of the same
profile, with

\[
                   B'=(B\setminus\{a\})\cup\{r\},\qquad
                   H'=(H\setminus\{r\})\cup\{a\}.                 \tag{12}
\]

The common complementary singleton \(c\) satisfies both (11) and its
primed version.  Subtracting them gives

\[
                       {1\over c+a}={1\over c+r},                   \tag{13}
\]

and all denominators are structurally nonzero.  Thus \(a=r\), contrary
to the distinctness of the selected and complementary value classes.
This closes \(b=0,1,2\).

## 4. One complementary double closes \(b=3\)

For the last family, select two of its three exact double classes and
\(h-2\) singleton classes.  Denote the selected double values by \(u,w\),
the complementary double value by \(v\), and the one complementary
singleton by \(a\).  The five complementary triples again have product
\(R^3\).  Thus

\[
                   A=R^3(z-v)^2(z-a),\qquad
                   g=R^2(z-v).                                    \tag{14}
\]

Here \(c=7\), and the relation space is the unique hyperplane in
\(\mathbb C[z]_{\leq3}\) with Wronskian

\[
                              C(z-a)^2(z-v).                        \tag{15}
\]

For \(P=p_0+p_1z+p_2z^2+p_3z^3\), it is explicitly

\[
 \mathcal S_{a,v}=\ker L_{a,v},\qquad
 L_{a,v}(P)=p_0+{2a+v\over3}p_1
             +{a^2+2av\over3}p_2+a^2v\,p_3.                     \tag{16}
\]

At \(a\), the exact simple-root row \(D_a+\beta E_a\) annihilating this
hyperplane has

\[
                              \beta={3\over v-a}.                   \tag{17}
\]

On the other hand, (4) gives the local unit

\[
                         U_a={ (z+\mu)^kQ^2H
                                  \over R^4(z-v)^3},                \tag{18}
\]

where \(Q=(z+u)(z+w)\).  Since \(3/(v-a)=-3/(a-v)\), comparison of the
logarithmic derivative of (18) with (17) cancels the complementary-double
term and leaves

\[
 \Psi_{Q,H}(a):={k\over a+\mu}
       +2\sum_{q\in\{u,w\}}{1\over a+q}
       +\sum_{y\in H}{1\over a+y}
       -4\sum_{x\in X}{1\over a-x}=0.                            \tag{19}
\]

Now exchange the selected double \(u\) with the complementary double
\(v\).  The singleton \(a\), the triple set, and \(H\) are unchanged, so
the second selection gives the same equation with
\(\{u,w\}\) replaced by \(\{v,w\}\).  Subtraction yields

\[
                         {2\over a+u}={2\over a+v},                 \tag{20}
\]

and hence \(u=v\), again contradicting structural distinctness.  This
closes \(b=3\) and completes the proof of (2).
