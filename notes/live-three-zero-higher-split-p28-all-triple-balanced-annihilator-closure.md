# Higher splits: balanced-annihilator closure of the \(p=28\) all-triple core

## 1. Result and scope

Continue from the independently audited
[residual-quartic frontier](live-three-zero-higher-split-p28-all-triple-q5-residual-quartic-frontier.md)
and its
[balanced-splitting corollary](live-three-zero-higher-split-p28-all-triple-residual-quartic-balanced-splitting.md).
The hypothetical restored baseline is

\[
                                  3^{10}.                    \tag{1}
\]

It occurs for the two residual tuples

\[
                    (e,a,b,u)=(0,10,0,0),(0,10,1,-2).       \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem 1.1 (balanced-annihilator closure).**  The exact common-lift
setup for (1) is impossible.  Consequently both collision profiles in
(2) are closed at \(p=28\).

The proof has two terminal branches.  If the annihilator line curve has
generic tangent rank two, its derivative four-wedge has a square scalar,
contradicting the ten distinct Hermite factors.  If it has generic
tangent rank one, the only nonzero branch is the tangent-line curve of a
rational normal cubic.  That branch puts

\[
       A(z)\mathbb C[z^2]_{\leq3}
\]

inside the common polynomial six-space and forces an unlisted
Wronskian zero at \(z=0\).

## 2. The balanced annihilator and the derived plane \(L_t\)

Let \(V=\mathbb C^6\).  The residual four-plane

\[
 W_t=\langle E(t),O(t),E'(t),O'(t)\rangle\subset V
\]

has a primitive degree-four Pluecker vector \(Q(t)\), and its
annihilator bundle has a polynomial minimal basis

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda(t)&=\lambda_0+t\lambda_1+t^2\lambda_2,\\
 \mu(t)&=\mu_0+t\mu_1+t^2\mu_2,
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{3}
\]

with splitting \({\cal O}(-2)\oplus{\cal O}(-2)\).  The original
four-wedge is

\[
 E\wedge O\wedge E'\wedge O'
       =C(t)Q(t),\qquad
 C(t)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{10}(t-a_\nu^2),                         \tag{4}
\]

where the ten squares are distinct.  Thus \(C\) is squarefree.

The four annihilation identities for each row include

\[
                  \lambda E=\lambda E'=0,\qquad
                  \lambda O=\lambda O'=0.
\]

Differentiating the first member of each pair and using the second gives

\[
                  \lambda'E=\lambda'O=0.
\]

The same holds for \(\mu\).  Hence

\[
 E(t),O(t)\in L_t:=
   \ker\langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle.              \tag{5}
\]

Because \(E,O\) are generically independent, the generic rank

\[
 r:=\dim\langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle
\]

is at most four.  It is at least three: rank two would make the
two-plane \(\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle\) constant in characteristic zero,
contrary to its Pluecker degree four.  We treat \(r=4\) and \(r=3\)
separately.

Let

\[
 U=\langle\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,
          \mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2\rangle\subset V^*,
 \qquad m=\dim U.                                            \tag{6}
\]

This is the fixed linear span of all annihilator fibers.

## 3. Generic tangent rank two

Assume \(r=4\), so \(\dim L_t=2\).  If \(m<6\), the fixed space
\(U^\perp\ne0\) lies in every \(L_t\).  For \(m=4\), the plane \(L_t\)
is constant.  For \(m=5\), every \(L_t\) contains one fixed vector.
Locally write it as \(\langle c,u(t)\rangle\).  Since \(E,O\) span
\(L_t\), their derivatives modulo \(L_t\) are both multiples of the
single class of \(u'\).  In either case

\[
                         E\wedge O\wedge E'\wedge O'=0,
\]

contrary to (4).  Therefore \(m=6\).

The six coefficient covectors in (6) are now a basis of \(V^*\).  A
constant change of basis puts (3) in the form

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda&=(1,t,t^2,0,0,0),\\
 \mu&=(0,0,0,1,t,t^2).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{7}
\]

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 u&=(t^2,-2t,1,0,0,0),\\
 v&=(0,0,0,t^2,-2t,1).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{8}
\]

Then \(L_t=\langle u,v\rangle\).  The polynomial syzygy module of
\(\lambda,\lambda'\) is generated by the primitive row
\((t^2,-2t,1)\), and similarly in the second block.  Thus there are
polynomials \(p,q,r,s\) with

\[
                         E=pu+qv,\qquad O=ru+sv.             \tag{9}
\]

Their determinant

\[
                              D=ps-qr
\]

is nonzero because \(E,O\) are generically independent.  Modulo
\(\langle u,v\rangle\), the two derivative columns have coefficient
matrix

\[
                         \begin{pmatrix}p&q\\r&s\end{pmatrix}
\]

on \(u',v'\).  Therefore

\[
 E\wedge O\wedge E'\wedge O'
                    =D(t)^2\,u\wedge v\wedge u'\wedge v'.   \tag{10}
\]

The four-vector on the right is primitive and represents
\(\ker\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle\); under Hodge duality it is a nonzero
constant multiple of \(\lambda\wedge\mu\).  Since \(Q\) is also
primitive and represents the same four-plane, the two differ by a
nonzero constant.  Equations (4) and (10) imply

\[
                              C(t)=cD(t)^2,\qquad c\ne0,      \tag{11}
\]

which is impossible because \(C\) is a product of ten distinct linear
factors.  This excludes \(r=4\).

## 4. Classification of generic tangent rank one

Assume \(r=3\).  The line curve

\[
                  {\cal A}_t=\langle\lambda(t),\mu(t)\rangle
                         \subset\mathbb P(U)
\]

has rank-one second fundamental map.  Its saturated kernel gives the
standard developable dichotomy: either all lines contain a fixed vertex,
or they are tangent lines to a nonconstant edge curve \(\gamma\).

The cone branch is incompatible with balanced splitting.  A fixed
nonzero covector in every fiber would be a nowhere-zero section

\[
              {\cal O}\longrightarrow
              {\cal O}(-2)\oplus{\cal O}(-2),
\]

but this bundle has no global sections.  Hence the edge is nonconstant.
It spans \(\mathbb P(U)=\mathbb P^{m-1}\), because its tangent lines
span exactly the same fixed coefficient space as the annihilator
fibers.

If \(m=3\), then

\[
             \langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle=U
\]

generically, so \(L_t=U^\perp\) is one fixed three-space.  All four
vectors \(E,O,E',O'\) lie in it, contradicting (4).  Thus \(m\geq4\).

Let \(e\) be the degree of the nondegenerate edge in
\(\mathbb P^{m-1}\), and put

\[
 R_1=\sum_x(a_1(x)-1)
\]

for its total first ramification.  Its tangent-line curve is precisely
\({\cal A}\), of Pluecker degree four, so

\[
                           4=2e-2-R_1.                       \tag{12}
\]

The total ramification of a nondegenerate \(g^{m-1}_e\) is

\[
                           m(e-m+1).
\]

One unit of \(R_1\) shifts at least the last \(m-1\) vanishing orders.
Consequently

\[
                   (m-1)R_1\leq m(e-m+1).                  \tag{13}
\]

For \(m=5\), substituting \(R_1=2e-6\) in (13) gives
\(3e\leq4\), impossible for \(e\geq4\).  For \(m=6\), it gives
\(4e\leq0\).  For \(m=4\), it gives \(e\leq3\), while
nondegeneracy gives \(e\geq3\).  Therefore the sole branch is

\[
                        m=4,\qquad e=3,\qquad R_1=0.         \tag{14}
\]

The edge is the rational normal cubic.  Because its coordinate series
is the complete \(H^0({\cal O}(3))\), a target change of basis, with the
fixed parameter \(t\) unchanged, gives

\[
                       \gamma=(1,t,t^2,t^3).                 \tag{15}
\]

One global quadratic frame for its tangent lines is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda&=3\gamma-t\gamma'=(3,2t,t^2,0),\\
 \mu&=\gamma'=(0,1,2t,3t^2).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{16}
\]

Its homogenizations are fiberwise independent also at infinity, so
(16) is the balanced \((2,2)\) frame, not a merely affine frame.

## 5. The twisted-cubic branch forces ramification at zero

Complete (16) by two constant covector coordinates, and let
\(c_1,c_2\in V\) span their common annihilator.  Put

\[
                         u=(-t^3,3t^2,-3t,1,0,0).            \tag{17}
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_t&=\ker\langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle
       =\langle u,c_1,c_2\rangle,\\
 W_t&=\ker\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle
       =\langle u,u',c_1,c_2\rangle.                         \tag{18}
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E&=p(t)u+a(t)c_1+b(t)c_2,\\
 O&=q(t)u+c(t)c_1+d(t)c_2.                                  \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]

Polynomiality follows from the last nonzero coordinate of \(u\).
The degree bounds inherited from
\(F(z)=E(z^2)+zO(z^2)\in V[z]_{\leq10}\) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
 &p&q&a&b&c&d\\ \hline
 \deg&\leq2&\leq1&\leq5&\leq5&\leq4&\leq4.
\end{array}                                                  \tag{20}
\]

For completeness, put

\[
                  M_1=pc-qa,\qquad M_2=pd-qb.                \tag{21}
\]

Relative to the primitive frame
\(u\wedge c_1\wedge c_2\wedge u'\), the scalar in the derivative
four-wedge is

\[
 \det(E,O,E',O')
       =M_2M_1'-M_1M_2'.                                    \tag{22}
\]

Both \(M_1,M_2\) have degree at most six, so (22) has degree at most
ten after cancellation of its leading degree-eleven term.  Thus the
ten roots in (4) are exactly compatible with the critical polynomial of
a degree-at-most-six rational map; root count alone does not exclude
this branch.

The square-cover branch point does.  Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A(z)&=p(z^2)+zq(z^2),\\
 f(z)&=a(z^2)+zc(z^2),\\
 g(z)&=b(z^2)+zd(z^2).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{23}
\]

The six coordinate polynomials of the common kernel have the exact
normal form

\[
 \boxed{
 {\cal K}=A(z)\mathbb C[z^2]_{\leq3}
              +\langle f(z),g(z)\rangle.}                   \tag{24}
\]

The polynomial \(A\) is nonzero, since otherwise the four coordinate
polynomials from (17) vanish and the derivative four-wedge is zero.
Let \(\rho=\operatorname {ord}_{z=0}A\).  The four-space in (24) has
local vanishing sequence

\[
                   \rho,\ \rho+2,\ \rho+4,\ \rho+6.         \tag{25}
\]

These four orders must occur among the six orders of \({\cal K}\);
only two additional orders can be inserted.  If \(\rho=0\), the
smallest possible full sequence is

\[
                            (0,1,2,3,4,6),
\]

of Wronskian weight one.  If \(\rho=1\), the minimum is
\((0,1,2,3,5,7)\), of weight three.  For \(\rho\geq2\), the
weight is larger still.  Hence

\[
                   \operatorname {ord}_{z=0}
                     \operatorname {Wr}({\cal K})\geq1.      \tag{26}
\]

But the saturated \(3^{10}\) six-space has echelon degrees
\((5,6,7,8,9,10)\).  Its ten exact nonzero order-three rows already
consume the entire Wronskian degree

\[
                     10(6-3)=30=6(11-6).                   \tag{27}
\]

It has no Wronskian zero at the unlisted point \(z=0\).
Equations (26)--(27) are the final contradiction.  The rank-one branch
is impossible, completing the proof of Theorem 1.1.
