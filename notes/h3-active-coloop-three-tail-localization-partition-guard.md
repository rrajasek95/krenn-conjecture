# A coloop partition of unity does not produce a sparse private tail

## The tempting cover is exact

For a normalized pure-colour coloop write

\[
             \alpha C=1,
 \qquad      C=t_1+t_2+t_3,                           \tag{1}
\]

where the three residual matching monomials are, after relabelling,

\[
 t_1=q_{23}q_{45},\qquad
 t_2=q_{24}q_{35},\qquad
 t_3=q_{25}q_{34}.                                   \tag{2}
\]

Since `C` is a unit, the three principal opens cover the coloop chart:

\[
                 D(t_1)\cup D(t_2)\cup D(t_3)=D(C).  \tag{3}
\]

Moreover

\[
                  \lambda_i=\alpha t_i,
 \qquad           \lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1.  \tag{4}
\]

Thus there is a genuine algebraic partition of unity.  The obstruction is
not failure of (3) or (4).

## Principal-open is not sparse

The completed special processor of `93cf9ae` starts on the axis stratum

\[
 t_it_j=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad C\ne0,                 \tag{5}
\]

where exactly one residual cofactor monomial is nonzero.  By contrast,
localizing at `t_i` says only `t_i != 0`; it does not impose the other two
equations in (5).

All seven nonempty support patterns occur under (1) over the rationals.  In
particular the checker realizes the symmetric point

\[
                \alpha=1,qquad
                t_1=t_2=t_3={1\over3}.               \tag{6}
\]

It is literal at the cofactor/support level: set `q01=1`, take the first edge
of each matching in (2) to be `1/3`, and its second edge to be `1`.  There are
exactly three nonzero pure target matchings and every one contains the coloop
`01`; their sum is one.  The point lies in all three opens (3) and in none of
the sparse strata (5).

At (6), the weights in (4) are `(1/3,1/3,1/3)`.  They are neither
idempotent nor orthogonal.  This is a cover, not a product decomposition
into three tail components.

## The exact rank-two obstruction

Let

\[
 B=\mathbb Q[\alpha,C,C^{-1}]/(\alpha C-1),
 \qquad
 R=B[t_1,t_2,t_3]/(t_1+t_2+t_3-C).                   \tag{7}
\]

Eliminating `t3` gives `R=B[t1,t2]`.  Hence

\[
 \Omega_{R/B}=R,dt_1\oplus R,dt_2,
 \qquad dt_3=-dt_1-dt_2.                             \tag{8}
\]

The tail-redistribution obstruction has rank two.  Localizing any `t_i`
does not change this rank.

At (6), the complete cofactor row is

```text
a = (1,1,1).
```

It has rank one on the three tail columns.  The two redistribution
directions

```text
(1,-1,0),   (1,0,-1)
```

annihilate `a` and span the relative tangent/cotangent quotient.  A selected
coordinate row `e_i` raises the rank; it is not generated by aggregate rows
or by their scalar multiples.

This persists on the open cover.  Normalize the local selectors by `t_i`:

\[
                 s_i={e_i\over t_i}.                 \tag{9}
\]

At the triple overlap the differences `s2-s1` and `s3-s1` have rank two
modulo `a`.  They are the first Čech obstruction to gluing the three local
choices.  Partition recombination does not fix it:

\[
       \sum_i\lambda_i s_i
       =\sum_i \alpha t_i {e_i\over t_i}
       =\alpha(e_1+e_2+e_3),                         \tag{10}
\]

which is only the aggregate row again.

## Source-validity of a coordinate projector

Let `E_i=t_i partial_(t_i)` be the logarithmic coordinate projector.  For

\[
                         F=\alpha(t_1+t_2+t_3)-1,
\]

its first proper face is

\[
                         E_iF=\alpha t_i=\lambda_i.  \tag{11}

This is nonzero on `D(t_i)`.  Dividing by `t_i` changes (11) to the unit
`alpha`; it does not make the operation source-valid.  Indeed

\[
                         \sum_iE_iF=1                \tag{12}

on the source fibre.  A combination `sum r_i partial_(t_i)` is tangent to
(1) only when

\[
                         \sum_i r_i t_i=0.            \tag{13}

The global solutions are generated by the ordinary Koszul differences

```text
(t2,-t1,0), (t3,0,-t1), (0,t3,-t2).
```

They move tails relative to one another; no coordinate selector satisfies
(13).  Thus selecting a bright tail and dividing by it has exactly the same
pointed-source defect as the earlier occurrence selector.

## Closed-shore data do not kill the module

At (6), small rational moves in either direction

```text
(1,-1,0), (1,0,-1)
```

preserve the cofactor sum and keep all three tail monomials nonzero.  Any
closed-shore/Hall predicate depending only on occupied support is therefore
constant along the full rank-two redistribution plane.  It cannot make one
tail private.

This is a sharp scope statement.  The displayed point is not asserted to
solve every GHZ equation.  It proves that the normalized coloop equation,
localization, and support-level closed-shore data alone do not imply sparse
entry.  Additional complete source rows may still break the two directions.

## Smallest positive theorem

The missing input can now be stated precisely as a pointed cofactor-tail
comparison.  Either:

1. construct two independent, source-valid tail-asymmetric rows killing
   (8), equivalently graph coordinates with compatible overlap homotopies;
   or
2. construct one such row and prove that its residual branch deletes one of
   the other occupied tails, then iterate to the support-one stratum.

Only after that reduction does the completed special processor of
`93cf9ae` apply.  The partition-of-unity argument by itself returns the
aggregate and cannot replace this theorem.
