# The corrected binomial problem is an exact signed group-algebra test

## Outcome

The missing hypothesis in the bare odd-dependence statement can be handled
without guessing phases.  For a fixed decorated support on which every
nonempty mixed fibre is a binomial, there is an exact necessary-and-sufficient
integer test for the existence of nonzero complex weights which

1. cancel every mixed binomial, and
2. leave all three **complete** constant-fibre sums nonzero.

It is not enough to test consistency of the mixed signs.  After that test,
one must reduce the product of the three full constant polynomials in a
twisted group algebra.  The product is nonzero there if and only if a common
complex weighting with all three nonzero constants exists.

On the 48-cell support of
[`binomial-incidence-odd-dependence-countermodel.md`](binomial-incidence-odd-dependence-countermodel.md),
the mixed sign system is consistent, but each constant polynomial is already
zero in the quotient.  Moreover, this is forced by a three-mixed-fibre
Laurent certificate for each color.  Thus the failure of the constants is
universal for that support, not special to its displayed `+/-1` weighting.

The independent finite audit is
`computations/verify_parallel_binomial_constant_toric_boundary.py`.  The
search implementation of the general quotient test is
`computations/search_parallel_binomial_nonzero_constants_cegar.py`.

## 1. The signed lattice

Let `E` be the set of supported aggregate cells and give them nonzero
variables (x_e).  Orient the two matching monomials in every nonempty
mixed fibre (f) as (M_f,N_f), and put

\[
 d_f=\mathbf1_{M_f}-\mathbf1_{N_f}\in\mathbb Z^E.          \tag{1}
\]

The mixed coefficient equation is

\[
                             x^{d_f}=-1.                   \tag{2}
\]

Let (L\subseteq\mathbb Z^E) be the lattice generated by the rows (d_f).
Equations (2) are consistent over ((\mathbb C^*)^E) precisely when the
assignment (d_f\mapsto-1) defines a character of (L).  Equivalently,
every integer relation

\[
                         \sum_f z_fd_f=0                  \tag{3}
\]

has even coefficient sum (sum_fz_f).  Necessity follows by multiplying
(2).  For sufficiency, extend the resulting character from (L) to
(mathbb Z^E); this is possible because (mathbb C^*) is divisible.

There is a convenient single-Hermite-form encoding.  Adjoin a formal sign
coordinate and form the lattice

\[
 \widetilde L=
 \left\langle (d_f,1),\ (0,2)\right\rangle
 \subseteq\mathbb Z^{E+1}.                               \tag{4}
\]

The mixed equations are inconsistent exactly when
((0,1)\in\widetilde L).  A row Hermite normal form of (4) tests this
without computing complex roots or a transformation matrix.

## 2. The full constant-product criterion

For color (a), let

\[
 C_a(x)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal F_{a^n}}x^{\mathbf1_M}         \tag{5}
\]

be the **complete** constant-fibre Laurent polynomial.  Assume (2) is
consistent, and let

\[
 R=\mathbb C[x_e^{\pm1}:e\in E]/
       \langle x^{d_f}+1:f\text{ mixed and nonempty}\rangle . \tag{6}
\]

**Lemma 2.1 (constant-product criterion).**  There is a point
(x\in(\mathbb C^*)^E) satisfying all mixed equations and

\[
                        C_0(x)C_1(x)C_2(x)\ne0            \tag{7}
\]

if and only if the class of (C_0C_1C_2) is nonzero in (R).

**Proof.**  The forward implication is evaluation.  For the converse,
choose one solution (x_0) of (2) and translate (x=x_0y).  Then (6) is a
twisted presentation of the group algebra
(mathbb C[\mathbb Z^E/L]).  A finitely generated abelian group is a
product of a free abelian group and a finite abelian group.  Over
(mathbb C), its group algebra is therefore a finite product of Laurent
polynomial rings.  It is reduced, and its closed points are the complex
characters trivial on (L).  A nonzero element is nonzero at some such
character.  Translating that character by (x_0) gives (7).  (square)

This also explains why testing the three factors separately is insufficient
when (L) is not saturated: the ring can be a product of components and
have zero divisors.  The product in (7), not three unrelated nonidentity
tests, is exact.

Computationally, reduce an exponent ((u,0)) modulo the HNF of (4).  The
last remainder coordinate is `0` or `1` and contributes sign `+1` or `-1`;
the other coordinates give its Laurent class.  Adding the signed
multiplicities of all triples of constant matchings computes the class of
(C_0C_1C_2) exactly.

## 3. A local three-fibre constant-killing certificate

The smallest reusable obstruction exposed by this quotient is a Laurent
rectangle.  Suppose a constant fibre consists of two terms with difference
(d_{00}), and three exact mixed binomials have differences
(d_{10},d_{01},d_{11}) satisfying

\[
                         d_{00}=d_{10}+d_{01}-d_{11}.      \tag{8}
\]

Their cancellations force

\[
 x^{d_{00}}=(-1)(-1)(-1)^{-1}=-1.                       \tag{9}
\]

Hence the two complete constant terms cancel.  More generally, any odd
integer combination of mixed differences equal to a constant-term
difference has the same consequence.

The 48-cell support has exactly two terms in every constant fibre, and each
color has such a three-row certificate.  In the deterministic matching
orientation of the verifier they are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
a&\text{mixed colourings}&\text{coefficients}\ \hline
0&000002,\ 001000,\ 001002&(1,1,-1)\\
1&010100,\ 011100,\ 110111&(-1,1,1)\\
2&002002,\ 022002,\ 202222&(-1,1,1).
\end{array}                                               \tag{10}
\]

The checker reconstructs all matching terms from the 48 cells and verifies
(8) coordinate by coordinate for all three rows of (10).  Consequently,
**every** nonzero complex weighting on this support which cancels all mixed
binomials has

\[
                             C_0=C_1=C_2=0.                \tag{11}
\]

This strengthens the earlier displayed-sign audit and identifies an
additional honest perfect-matching incidence property: the three corners of
a recoloring rectangle translate the constant alternating-cycle ratio.

## 4. Exact search boundary

The HNF/quotient CEGAR treats two distinct failure modes soundly:

* an odd dependency among mixed rows forbids retaining all binomials in that
  dependency;
* a zero reduced constant product forbids retaining the certificate rows
  together with the exact supported-term sets of the constant colors used.

For the canonical six-vertex Hamilton triple, the search repeatedly reduces
phase-consistent survivors to one-color certificates using only three to
five mixed rows.  The first 48-cell survivor has the three-row certificates
above.  Separately,
`computations/search_parallel_binomial_signed_sat.py --unique-constants`
exhausts all twelve orbits of triples of selected constant matchings at
six vertices for arbitrary parallel decorated supports and `+/-1` cell
weights: all are UNSAT.  This is a finite signed subchart result, not a
replacement for the arbitrary-complex six-site theorem.

At eight vertices, the one-cell-per-underlying-pair search already supplies
the exact three-row odd dependency in
`computations/verify_monomial_n8_counterexample.py`; all 38 mixed fibres are
binomial, but their cancellations are inconsistent.  Allowing parallel
decorated cells is the remaining finite-search frontier.  Lemma 2.1 gives
the correct acceptance test: phase consistency alone is not a survivor;
the full reduced constant product must be nonzero.

The note does not prove that every arbitrary-order parallel support has an
odd mixed dependency or a zero constant product.  It turns that proposed
uniform theorem into a precise lattice statement and isolates the smallest
constant-killing incidence pattern that a proof can seek.

## 5. The literal recolouring-rectangle lemma

Here is the exact local statement, with the incidence hypothesis made
explicit.  For an underlying perfect matching `M` and a colouring `c`, write
`e(M,c)` for the exponent vector of its decorated matching term.  Fix two
pure supported matchings `M,N` of colour `a`, two vertices `v,w`, and two
replacement colours `b,d`.  Assume that `v,w` are not paired in either
`M` or `N`.  Let `c_ij` be the colouring obtained from `a^n` by recolouring
`v` to `b` when `i=1` and `w` to `d` when `j=1`.

**Lemma 5.1 (literal rectangle).**  If `M` and `N` are supported at all four
colourings `c_00,c_10,c_01,c_11`, then, with the same orientation at every
corner,

\[
 d_{00}=d_{10}+d_{01}-d_{11},\qquad
 d_{ij}=e(M,c_{ij})-e(N,c_{ij}).                         \tag{12}
\]

If the three non-pure fibres are binomials consisting of `M,N` and cancel,
then the two pure terms cancel as well.

**Proof.**  Because `v,w` lie on distinct edges of each matching, changing
the decoration at `v` and changing it at `w` affect disjoint aggregate
cells.  Hence the second discrete difference of each of `e(M,c_ij)` and
`e(N,c_ij)` is zero.  Subtracting gives (12).  The three mixed equations are
`x^d_10=x^d_01=x^d_11=-1`, and therefore

\[
 x^{d_{00}}=(-1)(-1)(-1)^{-1}=-1.                       \tag{13}
\]

Thus the two pure monomials have opposite values.  `QED`

The clause generator in
`computations/search_parallel_binomial_nonrectangle_boundary.py` enumerates
exactly this configuration; it also checks the nonadjacency condition rather
than silently assuming the two recolourings are independent.

## 6. Literal rectangles are not forced

The literal lemma is valid, but its incidence hypothesis is not a
consequence of the `0/2` fibre condition, even when the mixed phases are
globally consistent.  The independent checker
`computations/verify_parallel_binomial_nonrectangle_boundary.py` records a
six-site support with the following exact statistics:

\[
 |S|=40,\qquad
 \#\{\text{empty fibres}\}=603,\qquad
 \#\{\text{binomial fibres}\}=126.                      \tag{14}
\]

The 126 binomials comprise all three pure fibres and 123 mixed fibres.
Every decoration of the underlying edge `14` is present.  Give those nine
cells weight `-1` and every other supported cell weight `+1`.  Each
nonempty fibre then has one matching containing `14` and one not containing
it, so every binomial cancels.  This supplies a global sign character and
proves mixed-phase consistency without a lattice computation.

Exhaustive enumeration finds **zero** literal rectangles from Lemma 5.1.
Thus an extra pure matching and its alternating cycles do not force the same
two underlying matchings to survive at the three recoloured corners.

There is nevertheless a slightly more flexible three-row obstruction.  In
the deterministic matching orientation used by the checker, the following
identities hold coordinate by coordinate:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
a&\text{mixed colourings}&\text{coefficients}\\ \hline
0&000010,\ 001010,\ 001000&(1,-1,1)\\
1&000010,\ 111010,\ 001110&(1,1,-1)\\
2&000010,\ 022022,\ 001110&(1,1,-1).
\end{array}                                               \tag{15}
\]

For colour zero the three colourings are the corners of a two-vertex
recolouring rectangle, but the matching pair changes: the pure fibre and
`000010` use one pair, while `001000` and `001010` use another.  This
"translated rectangle" still reconstructs the pure exponent difference,
and the odd coefficient sum forces the pure ratio to be `-1`.  The other
two rows of (15) do the same for colours one and two.  Hence every complex
weighting cancelling the mixed binomials on this support has
`C_0=C_1=C_2=0`, despite the absence of a literal rectangle.

This countermodel rules out the most direct alternating-cycle proof.  A
uniform argument must allow the matching pair to change along the
recolouring chain, or use a longer odd Laurent relation.

## 7. A uniform support theorem: an extra pure matching is forced

One graph-theoretic consequence *does* hold at every even order, using the
established characteristic-two obstruction.

**Theorem 7.1 (pure-fibre parity).**  Let `n>=6` be even and let `S` be a
support of ternary decorated aggregate cells.  Suppose every mixed
colouring fibre contains an even number of supported perfect matchings (in
particular, it may contain zero or two).  If all three pure fibres are
nonempty, then at least one pure fibre has even cardinality.  Consequently,
at least one of the three selected pure matchings has an additional pure
matching of the same colour.

**Proof.**  Work over the algebraic closure of `F_2`, put weight one on
every cell in `S`, and put weight zero on every other decorated cell.  The
coefficient at a colouring `c` is then the cardinality of its supported
matching fibre modulo two.  Every mixed coefficient vanishes by hypothesis.
If all three pure cardinalities were odd, the resulting matching tensor
would be exactly

\[
                         \Delta_{n,3}
             =e_0^{\otimes n}+e_1^{\otimes n}+e_2^{\otimes n}. \tag{16}
\]

The exact six-site characteristic-two ideal certificate in
`notes/delta-matroid-transversal.md`, together with the Pfaffian Schur
reduction in `notes/char2-schur-reduction.md`, proves that no such transverse
matching realization exists for any even `n>=6`.  Therefore one pure fibre
has even size.  Since it is nonempty, its size is at least two.  `QED`

The order bound is sharp: the three one-factors of `K_4`, assigned three
different pure colours, have pure-fibre sizes `(1,1,1)` and no mixed terms.
At order eight the monomial support in
`computations/verify_monomial_n8_counterexample.py` has pure sizes
`(24,1,4)`, illustrating the forced even fibre, although its mixed
cancellation equations are phase-inconsistent.

Theorem 7.1 is a genuine uniform incidence theorem, but it stops exactly at
the current gap: parity forces an alternating cycle between two pure
matchings; it does not force that cycle's same matching pair to persist
under vertex recolouring.  Section 6 supplies the exact six-site
counterexample to that stronger local claim.

## 8. An arbitrary parity mate can have the wrong sign

There is a second obstruction to choosing an arbitrary extra pure matching
and following its alternating cycles.  The checker
`computations/verify_parallel_binomial_even_chain_boundary.py` gives a
55-cell six-site support with 388 mixed binomial fibres and pure-fibre sizes

\[
                              (4,2,2).                    \tag{17}
\]

A displayed seven-cell negative set supplies globally consistent `+/-1`
phases and cancels every mixed binomial.  The four matchings in the
colour-zero pure fibre have deterministic indices `0,2,4,7` and signs

\[
                              (+,-,-,+).                  \tag{18}
\]

In particular, the selected matching `M_0` and the extra matching `M_7`
reinforce rather than cancel.  This is not an accident of the displayed
signing.  Two mixed fibres give the exact exponent identity

\[
 d_{0,7}^{\rm pure}
    =d_{0,2}^{110000}+d_{2,7}^{000001}.                  \tag{19}
\]

Both mixed ratios on the right must equal `-1`, so every complex solution
of the mixed cancellation equations satisfies

\[
                       x^{d_{0,7}^{\rm pure}}=+1.         \tag{20}
\]

The other two pure terms make the *complete* four-term sum zero.  Thus this
is not a survivor of the constant-product test, but it disproves the local
inference that any parity-supplied extra matching must cancel the initially
selected matching.  A uniform proof has to use the complete even pure
fibre (or choose a mate by an additional criterion), rather than one
uncontrolled alternating-cycle mate.

## 9. The strongest uniform consequence of characteristic two

The parity theorem can be sharpened from the total number of pure terms to
the quotient classes relevant to the toric problem.  It also exposes the
one missing assertion needed for a complex proof.

Let `L` be the lattice generated by all mixed differences and put

\[
                         \Gamma=\mathbb Z^E/L.            \tag{21}
\]

Write `T_2` for the 2-primary torsion subgroup of `Gamma`.  Reduce signs
modulo two, so the mixed relations are `x^d=1`, and let

\[
 \overline P=\overline C_0\overline C_1\overline C_2
       \in\overline{\mathbb F}_2[\Gamma].                \tag{22}
\]

**Proposition 9.1 (classwise parity).**  For every even `n>=6`, under the
`0/2` mixed-fibre hypothesis, `overline P` is nilpotent.  Equivalently, its
image in the reduced group algebra

\[
              \overline{\mathbb F}_2[\Gamma/T_2]          \tag{23}
\]

is zero.  In particular, if `Gamma` has no 2-primary torsion, then
`overline P=0` already in `overline F_2[Gamma]`.

**Proof.**  A closed point of the group algebra is a character
`chi:Gamma -> overline F_2^*`.  Assign the supported aggregate cells the
corresponding nonzero character values and all unsupported cells zero.
For every nonempty mixed fibre, its two monomials have exponent difference
in `L`, so their values are equal and their characteristic-two sum is zero.
Empty mixed fibres vanish as well.  If all three pure values were nonzero,
this would be a transverse matching realization with three nonzero constant
coefficients; rescaling one vertex normalizes them to `Delta_(n,3)`,
contradicting the uniform characteristic-two no-go cited in Theorem 7.1.
Thus `overline P` vanishes at every closed point and is nilpotent by the
Nullstellensatz.

For a finitely generated abelian group, the nilradical of its group algebra
in characteristic two is generated precisely by `g-1` for elements `g` of
2-power order.  Quotienting by these elements gives (23), which is reduced.
This proves the equivalent statement.  `QED`

Coefficientwise, Proposition 9.1 says that in every exponent class modulo
`L` and 2-primary torsion, the number of triples of pure matching terms is
even.  Theorem 7.1 is the much coarser consequence obtained by applying the
augmentation and looking only at the total number of triples.

Now return to characteristic zero and assume the mixed signs are
consistent.  Within one exact `Gamma`-class, the mixed character divides
the pure triples into a `+` side and a `-` side.  The coefficient of that
class in the twisted product is exactly

\[
                         |V_+|-|V_-|.                    \tag{24}
\]

Therefore the desired complex binomial theorem is equivalent to the
following **phase-balance statement**:

> Every pure-triple quotient class has equally many terms on the two sides
> of the mixed sign character.

When `T_2=0`, characteristic two proves only that
`|V_+|+|V_-|` is even.  With 2-primary torsion it proves the still weaker
aggregate parity over a whole `T_2`-coset.  The 55-cell
example in Section 8 has a four-term class with signs `(+,+,-,-)`: it is
balanced, but the same-sign pair `M_0,M_7` shows that balance cannot be
obtained by pairing an arbitrarily chosen extra matching with the selected
one.  A translated-cycle proof must instead construct a sign-reversing
involution on the *whole* quotient class, or prove the two bipartition sizes
equal by another incidence invariant.

Conversely, any genuine binomial counterexample must exhibit a quotient
class with a nonzero **even** signed imbalance (at least two surplus terms
of one phase).  This is a substantially sharper computational acceptance
test than merely asking for an even pure fibre, and it is the precise
remaining dichotomy after the characteristic-two theorem.
