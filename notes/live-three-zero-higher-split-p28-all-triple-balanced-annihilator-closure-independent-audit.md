# Independent audit: balanced-annihilator closure of the \(p=28\) all-triple core

## 1. Verdict and exact scope

I independently reconstructed the
[balanced-annihilator closure](live-three-zero-higher-split-p28-all-triple-balanced-annihilator-closure.md)
without importing its checker.  The argument is sound.

Conditional on the already audited residual-quartic frontier and balanced
splitting, the common-lift setup for the baseline

\[
                                  3^{10}
\]

is impossible.  Thus the two residual tuples

\[
                    (e,a,b,u)=(0,10,0,0),(0,10,1,-2)
\]

are closed at \(p=28\).

The two generic-rank branches are exhaustive.  In rank four, the derivative
four-wedge acquires a genuinely primitive square scalar, which cannot equal
the squarefree ten-factor polynomial.  In rank three, the global
developable classification leaves only the tangent lines of the rational
normal cubic; its square pullback forces positive Wronskian weight at the
unlisted point \(z=0\).

## 2. Audited starting data and the derived plane

Let \(V=\mathbb C^6\), \(t=z^2\), and

\[
 F(z)=E(t)+zO(t).
\]

The audited antecedents give

\[
 E\wedge O\wedge E'\wedge O'=C(t)Q(t),\qquad
 C(t)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{10}(t-a_\nu^2),                       \tag{A1}
\]

where the ten \(a_\nu^2\) are distinct and nonzero.  The four-vector
\(Q\) is primitive of projective degree four, and its annihilator bundle
has balanced splitting

\[
 {\cal A}\simeq{\cal O}_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)
                    \oplus{\cal O}_{\mathbb P^1}(-2).       \tag{A2}
\]

Choose a global homogeneous minimal frame and write it on the affine
\(t\)-chart as

\[
\lambda=\lambda_0+t\lambda_1+t^2\lambda_2,\qquad
\mu=\mu_0+t\mu_1+t^2\mu_2.                                 \tag{A3}
\]

Each row annihilates \(E,O,E',O'\).  Differentiating
\(\lambda E=\lambda O=0\), and then using
\(\lambda E'=\lambda O'=0\), gives

\[
                         \lambda'E=\lambda'O=0,
\]

and similarly for \(\mu\).  Hence

\[
 E,O\in L_t:=\ker\langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle.  \tag{A4}
\]

Put

\[
 r=\dim\langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle
\]

at a generic point.  The nonzero four-wedge in (A1) makes \(E,O\)
generically independent, so \(r\leq4\).  If \(r=2\), both derivatives
of the annihilator two-plane are internal to that plane.  Its Pluecker
point is then constant in characteristic zero, contrary to the primitive
degree-four map.  Therefore

\[
                              r\in\{3,4\}.                  \tag{A5}
\]

This argument is generic, which is sufficient: every later wedge identity
is polynomial and therefore extends across the exceptional fibers.

## 3. Generic rank four gives a primitive square

Let

\[
 U=\langle\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,
          \mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2\rangle,\qquad m=\dim U.
\]

Assume \(r=4\).  Then \(\dim L_t=2\).  If \(m=4\), the plane \(L_t\)
equals the fixed plane \(U^\perp\), so its derivative four-wedge is zero.
If \(m=5\), every \(L_t\) contains the fixed line \(U^\perp\).  Locally
write \(L_t=\langle c,u(t)\rangle\).  Since \(E,O\) span \(L_t\), their
two derivative classes modulo \(L_t\) are both multiples of the one class
of \(u'\).  Again the four-wedge has rank at most three.  Thus \(m=6\).

The six coefficient covectors are a basis of \(V^*\).  A constant target
change, which does not touch \(t\), gives

\[
\lambda=(1,t,t^2,0,0,0),\qquad
\mu=(0,0,0,1,t,t^2).                                      \tag{A6}
\]

The two primitive polynomial syzygies are

\[
 u=(t^2,-2t,1,0,0,0),\qquad
 v=(0,0,0,t^2,-2t,1).                                     \tag{A7}
\]

The unit last coordinates show directly that the polynomial kernels of
\((\lambda,\lambda')\) and \((\mu,\mu')\) are generated by \(u\) and
\(v\), respectively.  Consequently

\[
 E=pu+qv,\qquad O=ru+sv
\]

for polynomials \(p,q,r,s\).  With \(D=ps-qr\), reduction of the derivative
columns modulo \(\langle u,v\rangle\) gives the exact identity

\[
 E\wedge O\wedge E'\wedge O'
          =D(t)^2\,u\wedge v\wedge u'\wedge v'.             \tag{A8}
\]

There is no hidden scalar factor in (A8).  The maximal minors of
\((u,v,u',v')\) have polynomial gcd one up to a nonzero numerical
constant.  Moreover,

\[
 u\wedge v\wedge u'\wedge v'
       =c_0\,\star(\lambda\wedge\mu),\qquad c_0\ne0,         \tag{A9}
\]

coordinate by coordinate.  Both sides are primitive and represent
\(\ker\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle\).  The primitive \(Q\) represents the
same four-plane, so the rational proportionality between it and (A9) is
a nonzero constant: a nonconstant numerator or denominator would divide
all coordinates of one of the two primitive vectors.

Equations (A1) and (A8) therefore imply

\[
                              C(t)=cD(t)^2,\qquad c\ne0.     \tag{A10}
\]

Every irreducible factor on the right has even multiplicity, whereas the
ten distinct factors of \(C\) have multiplicity one.  This excludes
\(r=4\).

## 4. The global developable classification

Assume \(r=3\), and consider the curve of annihilator lines

\[
                 {\cal A}_t=\mathbb P\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle
                              \subset\mathbb P(U).
\]

The derivative map

\[
 {\cal A}_t\longrightarrow U/{\cal A}_t
\]

has generic rank one.  Its saturated kernel is a line in
\({\cal A}_t\).  Choose a local generator \(\gamma(t)\).  The kernel
condition says \(\gamma'(t)\in{\cal A}_t\).  If
\(\gamma'\) is projectively proportional to \(\gamma\), its projective
point is a fixed vertex and all the lines form a cone.  Otherwise

\[
                       {\cal A}_t
                         =\langle\gamma(t),\gamma'(t)\rangle,           \tag{A11}
\]

so the lines are the tangent lines of the edge \(\gamma\).  Saturation
extends this dichotomy over all of \(\mathbb P^1\), including infinity;
there is no omitted affine branch.

The cone branch would supply a fixed nonzero vector in every fiber of
\({\cal A}\), hence a nowhere-zero section

\[
 {\cal O}\longrightarrow{\cal O}(-2)\oplus{\cal O}(-2).
\]

But \(H^0({\cal O}(-2))=0\), so this branch is impossible.

If \(m=3\), the four covectors in (A4) generically span all of \(U\).
Then \(L_t=U^\perp\) is a fixed three-space containing \(E,O\); it also
contains their derivatives, contradicting (A1).  Thus \(4\leq m\leq6\).
The nonconstant edge spans \(\mathbb P(U)\), because its tangent lines
span the same fixed coefficient space as the annihilator fibers.

Let \(e\) be the degree of this nondegenerate edge in
\(\mathbb P^{m-1}\), and let

\[
 R_1=\sum_{x\in\mathbb P^1}(a_1(x)-1)
\]

be its total first ramification, including the point at infinity.  The
raw tangent wedge has degree \(2e-2\); at \(x\) its common vanishing order
is \(a_1(x)-1\).  Because the saturated tangent-line curve is the
degree-four curve (A2),

\[
                              4=2e-2-R_1.                  \tag{A12}
\]

The total ramification of the basepoint-free \(g^{m-1}_e\) is
\(m(e-m+1)\).  If \(a_1-1=s\), strict increase forces each of the last
\(m-1\) vanishing orders up by at least \(s\).  Hence

\[
                    (m-1)R_1\leq m(e-m+1).                 \tag{A13}
\]

Substituting \(R_1=2e-6\) leaves the exact margins

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
m& m(e-m+1)-(m-1)R_1&\text{consequence}\\ \hline
4&6-2e&e\leq3\\
5&4-3e&\text{impossible for }e\geq4\\
6&-4e&\text{impossible for }e\geq5.
\end{array}
\]

Nondegeneracy in \(\mathbb P^3\) also gives \(e\geq3\).  The sole branch is

\[
                       m=4,\qquad e=3,\qquad R_1=0.         \tag{A14}
\]

## 5. The fixed source parameter and the infinity fiber

A nondegenerate degree-three \(g^3_3\) is the complete
\(H^0({\cal O}_{\mathbb P^1}(3))\).  Therefore, in the existing source
coordinates \([s:u]\), a target basis change alone puts the edge in the
form

\[
                 \gamma_h=(s^3,s^2u,su^2,u^3).             \tag{A15}
\]

No source Möbius transformation is needed.  On \(s=1\), with \(t=u/s\),
this is \(\gamma=(1,t,t^2,t^3)\).

A global quadratic frame for its tangent lines is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda_h&=(3s^2,2su,u^2,0),\\
 \mu_h&=(0,s^2,2su,3u^2).
\end{aligned}                                              \tag{A16}
\]

On the affine chart it becomes

\[
 \lambda=3\gamma-t\gamma'=(3,2t,t^2,0),\qquad
 \mu=\gamma'=(0,1,2t,3t^2).                               \tag{A17}
\]

At infinity \([s:u]=[0:1]\), the two rows in (A16) are

\[
 (0,0,1,0),\qquad(0,0,0,3),
\]

so they remain independent.  Their maximal minors have no common
homogeneous factor.  Thus (A16) is a genuine global \((2,2)\) frame and
not an affine frame with a lost infinity fiber.

Extend these four covector coordinates by two zero coordinates, and let
\(c_1,c_2\in V\) be the two constant dual vectors.  Put

\[
                         u_0=(-t^3,3t^2,-3t,1,0,0).         \tag{A18}
\]

Direct multiplication and exact ranks give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \ker\langle\lambda,\mu,\lambda',\mu'\rangle
     &=\langle u_0,c_1,c_2\rangle,\\
 \ker\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle
     &=\langle u_0,u_0',c_1,c_2\rangle.                    \tag{A19}
\end{aligned}
\]

## 6. Polynomial coefficients and the derivative scalar

Equation (A4) and the first line of (A19) imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E&=p(t)u_0+a(t)c_1+b(t)c_2,\\
 O&=q(t)u_0+c(t)c_1+d(t)c_2.                              \tag{A20}
\end{aligned}
\]

These coefficients are polynomials, not rational functions: the fourth
coordinate of \(u_0\) is the constant one, while \(c_1,c_2\) occupy the
last two coordinates.

The even coordinates of \(F\) have \(t\)-degree at most five and the odd
coordinates have \(t\)-degree at most four.  The first coordinate of
\(u_0\) has degree three, with no \(c_1,c_2\) contribution, so

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
 &p&q&a&b&c&d\\ \hline
\deg&\leq2&\leq1&\leq5&\leq5&\leq4&\leq4.
\end{array}                                                \tag{A21}
\]

Let

\[
                         M_1=pc-qa,\qquad M_2=pd-qb.
\]

In the ordered primitive frame
\((u_0,c_1,c_2,u_0')\), the four columns
\((E,O,E',O')\) have determinant

\[
                    M_2M_1'-M_1M_2'.                      \tag{A22}
\]

Both \(M_i\) have degree at most six.  The apparent degree-eleven
coefficient in their Wronskian cancels because it is the difference of
two products with the same leading factor.  Hence (A22) has degree at
most ten.  This agrees exactly with the ten simple factors in \(C\);
root counting alone does not close the branch.

## 7. The square-cover branch point closes the cubic branch

Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A(z)&=p(z^2)+zq(z^2),\\
 f(z)&=a(z^2)+zc(z^2),\\
 g(z)&=b(z^2)+zd(z^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

The six coordinate polynomials of \(F\) span exactly

\[
              {\cal K}=A(z)\mathbb C[z^2]_{\leq3}
                         +\langle f(z),g(z)\rangle.         \tag{A23}
\]

Indeed, the first four coordinates are nonzero scalar multiples of
\(Az^6,Az^4,Az^2,A\).  The polynomial \(A\) cannot vanish identically:
its even and odd parts would give \(p=q=0\), leaving \(E,O\) in the fixed
two-space \(\langle c_1,c_2\rangle\) and making the derivative four-wedge
zero.  Since the six coordinate polynomials are a basis of the
six-space \({\cal K}\), the four displayed multiples and the two remaining
coordinates have the required total dimension.

Let \(\rho=\operatorname{ord}_0 A\).  From (A21), \(\deg A\leq4\), so
\(0\leq\rho\leq4\).  The four-dimensional subspace in (A23) has
vanishing orders

\[
                         \rho,\rho+2,\rho+4,\rho+6.         \tag{A24}
\]

These orders really occur in the ambient vanishing sequence.  For the
\(z\)-adic filtration, each associated-graded piece of a scalar polynomial
space has dimension at most one.  A nonzero graded piece of the four-space
therefore injects into a nonzero graded piece of \({\cal K}\); the two
remaining basis directions can only insert two additional orders.

The exact minimum sequences and Wronskian weights are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\rho&\text{smallest possible six-sequence}&
 \sum_{i=0}^5(a_i-i)\\ \hline
0&(0,1,2,3,4,6)&1\\
1&(0,1,2,3,5,7)&3\\
2&(0,1,2,4,6,8)&6\\
3&(0,1,3,5,7,9)&10\\
4&(0,1,4,6,8,10)&14.
\end{array}                                                \tag{A25}
\]

Thus

\[
                       \operatorname{ord}_{z=0}
                            \operatorname{Wr}({\cal K})\geq1.          \tag{A26}
\]

Finally, the audited saturated six-space has echelon degrees
\((5,6,7,8,9,10)\).  Its affine Wronskian degree is

\[
 (5+6+7+8+9+10)-(0+1+2+3+4+5)=30.                         \tag{A27}
\]

At infinity the vanishing sequence is \((0,1,2,3,4,5)\), so there is no
hidden infinity weight.  The ten listed nonzero points each have exact
weight three and already account for all \(30\) units.  Since every
\(a_\nu\ne0\), the point \(z=0\) is unlisted.  Equation (A26) is therefore
impossible.

This excludes \(r=3\), while Section 3 excludes \(r=4\).  The conditional
closure follows.
