# The quadratic locks factor through mixed conormals

## Exact outcome

At the rational point (p) of the five-parameter (n=8) mixed torus, put

\[
 Q_0=q_0-q_{00000010},\qquad
 Q_1=q_1-q_{11000111}.
\]

The earlier second-jet checker proved that these two ambient quadratic
forms vanish after restriction to the 56-dimensional mixed tangent space.
The vanishing is now explained by explicit polynomial identities:

\[
 \boxed{Q_0=\sum_{k=1}^{5}N_{0k}M_{0k},\qquad
        Q_1=\sum_{k=1}^{9}N_{1k}M_{1k},}
\]

where every (N_{ik}) is an exact linear combination of differentials of
mixed hafnian coefficients.  Thus the two locks belong to the degree-two
part of the ideal generated by the mixed Jacobian linear forms.  This is
strictly stronger and more structural than evaluating the forms on a
basis of the tangent kernel.

Here (x_{ij}^{ab}) is abbreviated to `ijab`.

## The five-product identity for (Q_0)

The 24-term quadratic is

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_0={}&(-2400+2500+3400+3500)(6700-6710)\\
 &+(2601-2600)(4700-5700)\\
 &+(3600-3601)(4700+5700)\\
 &+(4600-4601)(3700-2700)\\
 &+(2700+3700)(5600-5601).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

The five first factors are mixed conormals.  Writing (D_w=dH_w|_p), an
explicit certificate is

\[
\begin{aligned}
-2400+2500+3400+3500 &= -D_{00000001},\\
2601-2600 &= \tfrac12(D_{00000100}-D_{00000110}
                       +D_{22002002}-D_{22002012}),\\
3600-3601 &= \tfrac12(-D_{00000100}+D_{00000110}
                       +D_{22002002}-D_{22002012}),\\
4600-4601 &= -D_{00220100}+D_{00220110},\\
2700+3700 &=D_{00000220}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

So (1) is already a sparse, literal factorization through eight distinct
mixed gradient rows.

## The nine-product identity for (Q_1)

The 96 terms split into three disjoint 32-term bipartite rectangles.  Their
exact bilinear ranks are (4,1,4).  The middle rectangle is the single
product

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(1511-1611-5711-6711)\\
 &\quad\cdot(0210-0211+0310-0311+2400-2411+3400-3411).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The first rectangle is the sum of

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
2501+3501&0111-0711+1410-4701\\
2511+3511&-0111+0711-1411+4711\\
2601+3601&-0111-0711-1410-4701\\
2611+3611&0111+0711+1411+4711
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

and the third rectangle is the sum of

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
1210+1310&0511-0611+4501-4601\\
1211+1311&-0511+0611-4511+4611\\
2701+3701&-0511-0611-4501-4601\\
2711+3711&0511+0611+4511+4611.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]

Each table row denotes the product of its two entries.  In (3)--(5), every
left entry is in the mixed conormal.  The checker records and replays an
explicit representation by mixed gradients for each of the nine entries.
The middle normal is especially revealing:

\[
 1511-1611-5711-6711=-\tfrac12D_{11000111}.
\tag{6}
\]

The other representations use between one and nine mixed rows, with only
coefficients in 
\(\{\pm1,\pm\tfrac12\}\).  They are kept as exact data in the checker
rather than expanded here.

## Port-graded transport

The identities are not confined to the specialization (a=b=c=d=e=1).
Let χ(c) be the five-component port character of coordinate (c), and
let ρ(w) be the character of (H_w).  A rational linear form

\[
 N=\sum_c n_c z_c
\]

transports along the Laurent orbit as

\[
 N(t)=\sum_c n_c,t^{-\chi(c)}z_c.
\]

If (N=\sum_w a_wD_w) at (p), then exactly

\[
 N(t)=\sum_w a_w,t^{-\rho(w)}dH_w|_{p(t)},
\tag{7}
\]

so it remains a mixed conormal.  Transport the partner of a product with
character ρ of the relevant pure coefficient.  Every resulting quadratic
monomial has character

\[
 ρ-\chi(c)-\chi(d),
\]

which is precisely its Hessian character.  The selected mixed equations
must be scaled by

\[
 t^{\rho(0^8)-\rho(00000010)}=e,
 \qquad
 t^{\rho(1^8)-\rho(11000111)}=a^{-1}.
\]

Thus (1) and (3)--(5) give port-graded conormal factorizations all along
the generic five-parameter orbit.

## Structural interpretation

The second-order lock is a rectangle identity among complementary
two-edge pieces of the hafnian expansion.  It is not an accidental rank
zero discovered only after a 56-dimensional kernel computation: the
quadratic defect is visibly a sum of products having a mixed conormal as
one factor.  This is the correct shape for an all-orders argument.  The
next question is whether the cubic defect belongs to the ideal generated
by the mixed first and second Hasse equations, or whether a genuine third
fundamental form survives.
