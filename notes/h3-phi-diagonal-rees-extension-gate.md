# The finite Phi packet carries Rees labels, but not the diagonal target homotopy

## Result

Let

\[
 F:k^{18}\longrightarrow U_{15}
\]

be the direction-forgetting map for the complete determinant-dark lower
face.  Its kernel is generated by the three differences of the two cut
copies of the shared physical labels.  A physical comparison

\[
 \Phi:U_{15}\longrightarrow L_3,
 \qquad J_3\Phi=A J_{\rm col}                              \tag{1}
\]

therefore has exactly three matching-label coherence conditions.

There is a positive formal extension of the **base-changed label packet**.
For the truncated Rees jet algebra

\[
 R_r=k[\ell]/(\ell^r),
\]

one has

\[
 \ker(F\otimes1_{R_r})=(\ker F)\otimes R_r.              \tag{2}
\]

Thus a **Hasse/Rees-linear** comparison

\[
                  \widehat\Phi_r=\Phi\otimes1_{R_r}       \tag{3}
\]

carries the same fifteen physical labels at every formal jet order.  It
introduces no new chart-coherence type.  The three seed coherences propagate
through all divided derivatives.  At length `r`, the direction presentation has
`18r` labels, the physical quotient has `15r`, and the chart kernel has
rank `3r`.

This is the maximal positive consequence of the finite packet alone.  A
physical source-labelled identification of the actual diagonal
principal-parts module with this base change is part of the
Hasse/Rees-naturality hypothesis; equation (2) does not construct that
identification.
Agreement with `Phi` only at order zero is weaker than (3): the higher jet
maps can be changed independently and violate any of the remaining
`3(r-1)` coherence equations.  Hasse/Rees-linearity is additional physical
naturality, not a consequence of the original three equalities.

## The smallest literal Rees module

For a routed generic diagonal branch, let `M` be the filtered literal
source module, `N_lit` its actual boundary submodule, and

\[
                         \epsilon:M\longrightarrow V
\]

the evaluated coefficient map.  Let `U_scr` be the one actual clean-error
family, not every polynomial in `M tensor k[ell,w]`.  Define

\[
 D_{\ell,r}=
 \{P\in U_{scr}:\epsilon(P)\in
                   \ell^r(V\otimes k[\ell,w])\}.
\]

The exact obstruction module for the family is

\[
 \mathcal R_{\ell,r}=
 {D_{\ell,r}\over
  (N_{lit}\otimes k[\ell,w]+\ell^rM\otimes k[\ell,w])
       \cap U_{scr}}.                                      \tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, the obstruction of `P` is its truncated principal part

\[
 \operatorname {obs}_{\ell,r}(P)
   =[P\bmod\ell^r]
   \in {\ker\epsilon\over N_{lit}}
       \otimes(k[\ell,w]/(\ell^r)).                       \tag{5}
\]

Literal third-boundary division is valid precisely when (5) vanishes.  This
is a criterion for the actual family; proving the whole ambient module
relatively saturated is unnecessary.

The smallest guard has

```text
M=<b,z,r>,  N_lit=<b>,
epsilon(b)=epsilon(z)=0,  epsilon(r)=1.
```

At multiplicity two, the representatives

\[
 P_{good}=w^2b+\ell wb+\ell^2r,
 \qquad
 P_{bad}=w^2z+\ell wb+\ell^2r                         \tag{6}
\]

have the same evaluation `ell^2`.  They may be coupled to exactly the same
`Phi` and anchor data on a disjoint summand.  But `P_good` has zero class in
(5), while `P_bad` has first principal part `[z]w^2 != 0`.  Hence `Phi` plus
its ordinary physical-anchor law does not imply diagonal Rees membership.

## The target-grade obstruction is primitive

The literal one-face cell required by the current fifteen-label comparison
has

```text
protected target = 0,
ordinary residue = 0.
```

The two generic diagonal cap rows do not.  With selected label `a`,
`alpha != 0`, `beta != 0`, and `h=3`, their target vectors are

\[
 hT(J_1)=h(\beta,-\alpha,-\alpha),
 \qquad
 hT(J_2)=h(-\beta,-2\alpha,-2\alpha).                    \tag{7}
\]

They have rank two.  Therefore no combination of the target-zero `M_v`
comparison cells can equal both jets—or even one chosen nonzero jet—before
an additional target-bearing cell is adjoined.  The ordinary anchor row is
an independent domain readout and cannot repair this codomain target grade.

A same-power target companion is also insufficient.  In the exposed cap
row, target and ordinary odd residue have the same coefficient.  Cancelling
the target with another quadratic against the same divided power cancels
the residue with it.  The new cell must therefore be an
**adjacent-power target nullhomotopy**: it kills the target in the
`lambda A` grade while retaining the lower `p t_c B` response grade.

This gives the sharp answer to whether (1) carries the diagonal jets:

* as a matching-label and overlap-coherence skeleton, yes, provided the
  comparison is Hasse/Rees-linear;
* as the current physical target-zero mapping-cone comparison, no; one
  target-bearing adjacent-power direction is missing.

## Minimal generic comparison theorem

The asymmetric two-boundary saturation chooses one third-boundary factor
and one of the two rows `J1,J2`.  It is smaller than the symmetric
three-boundary certificate.  On this routed stratum the following data are
sufficient.

1. A source-labelled identification of the physical diagonal jet packet
   with the base change above, and a Hasse/Rees-linear extension `Phi_hat_r`
   as in (3), with `r<=2` at `h=3` because the residual degree is at most
   two.
2. One target-bearing adjacent-power cone cell for the chosen `J` row,
   with total target zero only after its homotopy and with the normalized
   lower odd residue retained.
3. Vanishing of the actual class (5).  Equivalently, it is enough that the
   extended comparison sends that class to zero and is injective on the
   line which it spans.
4. After tail extraction, the inactive Omega correction with middle
   coefficient the negative of the surviving residue, together with
   restricted homology injectivity on that physical residue line.

Under 1--3, the literal Taylor--Rees lemma produces the source-valid
two-boundary quotient.  Under 4, the existing torus--Koszul calculation
turns every noncentral coefficient into an explicit primitive and the
remaining physical class contradicts restricted injectivity.  This is a
positive conditional comparison theorem, not merely a list of coefficient
identities.

The symmetric generic route needs two target-bearing directions because
the vectors in (7) are independent.  The asymmetric route is therefore the
smallest one.

## Trace collision and exhaustive routing

At `beta=0`, the two rows in (7) become proportional and are blind to the
selected colour.  Their target rank drops from two to one, but the selected
target first appears at principal-part order `h` while the complementary
target appears at order `h-1`.  At `h=3`, the label skeleton can still be
prolonged to length three by (2).  Physical closure additionally requires
either

* an order-three unary target/anchor cell carrying the selected colour; or
* a separate physical theorem forcing a nonzero complementary residue.

This is not the ordinary anchor law used after the determinant-dark marked
lift.  That law pairs a completed kernel with pure/target incidence.  The
collision datum is an exceptional order-`h` target-bearing principal part.

Finally, an arbitrary all-inactive root has not yet been routed into this
diagonal packet.  Off-diagonal, diagonal, and trace-only activity boundaries
are physically distinct.  A complete coverage theorem must first construct
a source-labelled normal-face map into one of those ledgers; simultaneous
vanishing of the internal deleted-face coefficients is not known to imply
the cap-line all-inactive condition.  Thus the exhaustive branch needs, in
order:

```text
physical inactive-root boundary routing
  -> diagonal: Rees-linear Phi extension + adjacent-power target cell
  -> off-diagonal/trace: their own target-cancelled secondary comparison
  -> middle correction + restricted homology faithfulness.
```

## Exact frontier

The finite comparison does remove one possible source of complexity: no
new support census or matching-label inventory is required for diagonal
jets.  The remaining data beyond `Phi` plus its ordinary anchor law are
exactly:

```text
normal-face routing;
source-labelled diagonal-jet identification and Hasse/Rees naturality;
one chosen target-bearing adjacent-power cell (two for a symmetric route);
the low-jet class in ker(epsilon)/N_lit is zero;
inactive middle-coefficient normalization and restricted homology injection;
at beta=0, the exceptional order-h unary jet or complementary survival.
```

The first finite obstruction is the target-grade mismatch (7), followed by
the one-dimensional class (5).  Neither is seen by the three matching-label
coherences or the ordinary anchor readout.
