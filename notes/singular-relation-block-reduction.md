# Singular corank-two relation blocks reduce to a `2-2-2-2` edge

## 1. Outcome

Retain the four-dimensional relation space

\[
 \mathscr D\subset Z_0=\{M\in\operatorname {Mat}_3(\mathbb C):
 M_{00}=M_{11}=M_{22}=0\}
\]

from
[`corank-two-local-block-classification.md`](corank-two-local-block-classification.md).
Thus `mathscr D` meets none of the six coordinate row and column
two-planes.  On an internal edge write

\[
 \mathcal L(M)=P M T^{\mathsf T}+S M^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \mathcal L(\mathscr D)\subseteq\mathbb C H,       \tag{1}
\]

where `H` is invertible.  Call the edge **live** when the image in (1)
is nonzero.

Two elementary consequences of row-column avoidance substantially reduce
the singular branch.

1. Every nonzero one-vector evaluation of `mathscr D` has rank at least
   two.
2. No two-by-two compression of `mathscr D` can have one-dimensional
   image generated by an invertible map.

It follows that a live edge has exactly two possibilities:

* `P,Q,S,T` are all invertible, in which case Theorem 3.1 of the cited
  note gives the common invertible diagonal chart already excluded there;
* all four matrices have rank exactly two.

Thus ranks zero and one, an isolated singular endpoint, and a mixed
singular/invertible pair are not residual cases.  In particular the only
new live singular object is a `2-2-2-2` edge.

There is a second independent reduction when the full physical image of
`mathcal L` has dimension three.  Its determinant is a nonzero plane
cubic containing the six projective lines supplied by the three row and
three column planes.  Hence those six lines take at most three distinct
values.  Their incidence has exactly seven types up to colour
permutation, relabelling the determinant lines, and transposition: two
types with two distinct lines and five types with three.  The resulting
singular-pencil chase, sharpened by the fixed-line filter below, excludes
all seven types.

The dimension-two physical-image case is also made explicit below.  It
is not silently discarded: its nonzero generator graph is a disconnected
spanning subgraph of the directed six-cycle, hence either a perfect
matching or two paths obtained by deleting two nonadjacent edges.
The fixed-line classification of those three graph orbits excludes them
as well.  Consequently **no live singular relation edge exists**: every
live edge has all four outer matrices invertible.

## 2. The dual no-empty-row property

For fixed colours put

\[
 R_c=\operatorname {span}\{E_{cd}:d\ne c\},\qquad
 C_d=\operatorname {span}\{E_{cd}:c\ne d\}.             \tag{2}
\]

The row-column basis property is

\[
 \mathscr D\cap R_c=\mathscr D\cap C_d=0.              \tag{3}
\]

Let `mathscr D^perp subset Z_0^*` be the two-dimensional annihilator.
Because the dimensions in (3) add to six, restriction gives
isomorphisms

\[
 \mathscr D^\perp\longrightarrow R_c^*,\qquad
 \mathscr D^\perp\longrightarrow C_d^*.                \tag{4}
\]

Consequently every nonzero member of `mathscr D^perp` has a nonzero
entry in every off-diagonal row and every off-diagonal column.  We use
this repeatedly in place of coordinates for the six quotient classes.

## 3. Evaluation and compression lemmas

Let `pi_0` denote deletion of the three diagonal coefficients.

**Lemma 3.1 (two-dimensional evaluation).**  For every nonzero
`w in C^3`, both maps

\[
 \mathscr D\longrightarrow\mathbb C^3,\qquad M\longmapsto Mw,
 \qquad
 \mathscr D\longrightarrow\mathbb C^3,\qquad M\longmapsto M^{\mathsf T}w
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

have rank at least two.

**Proof.**  Suppose the first map has rank at most one.  Its annihilator
in `(C^3)^*` has dimension at least two; call it `A`.  For every
`a in A`,

\[
             \pi_0(aw^{\mathsf T})\in\mathscr D^\perp. \tag{6}
\]

If `w` has at least two nonzero coordinates, the map
`a mapsto pi_0(aw^T)` is injective.  Choose a nonzero `a in A` whose
`c`-th coordinate vanishes.  The member (6) then has an empty row, in
contradiction with (4).

If `w` is supported at the single coordinate `delta`, every member (6)
is supported in column `delta`.  By (4) it must vanish.  But the kernel
of `a mapsto pi_0(ae_delta^T)` is only `C e_delta^*`, whereas `A` has
dimension at least two.  This is again impossible.  Transposition proves
the second assertion. `QED`

For two-planes `A subset (C^3)^*` and `W subset C^3`, write

\[
 \rho_{A,W}(M)(a,w)=aMw
 \quad\text{in }\operatorname {Bil}(A,W).               \tag{7}
\]

**Lemma 3.2 (no invertible one-line `2 by 2` compression).**  The image
`rho_(A,W)(mathscr D)` cannot be a line generated by a nondegenerate
bilinear form on `A times W`.

**Proof.**  Suppose it is the line `C h`, with `h` nondegenerate.  On
duals, let

\[
 X=A\otimes W\subset\operatorname {Mat}_3,\qquad
 K=h^\perp\subset X.                                    \tag{8}
\]

Thus `dim X=4`, `dim K=3`, and

\[
                         \pi_0(K)\subseteq\mathscr D^\perp.           \tag{9}
\]

In particular `rank(pi_0|K)<=2`, so `K` contains a nonzero diagonal
matrix.  Put `Delta=X intersection Diag`.  A diagonal matrix belongs to
`A tensor W` exactly when its column support lies in `A` and its row
support lies in `W`.  Hence either

\[
 \Delta=\mathbb C E_{cc},                               \tag{10}
\]

or, for distinct `c,d`,

\[
 A=W=\operatorname {span}(e_c,e_d),\qquad
 \Delta=\operatorname {span}(E_{cc},E_{dd}).            \tag{11}
\]

In case (11), `pi_0(K)` is nonzero and is supported on the two directed
entries `cd,dc`.  It has an empty third row, contradicting (4).

In case (10), the rank bound forces `E_cc in K`, so `h(E_cc)=0`.
Write

\[
 A=\operatorname {span}(e_c,a),\qquad
 W=\operatorname {span}(e_c,w),\qquad a_c=w_c=0.        \tag{12}
\]

The three independent off-diagonal images of `X` are

\[
 r=\pi_0(e_cw^{\mathsf T}),\quad
 s=\pi_0(ae_c^{\mathsf T}),\quad
 z=\pi_0(aw^{\mathsf T}).                              \tag{13}
\]

Relative to the bases in (12), nondegeneracy of `h` and `h(E_cc)=0`
say that the coefficients of `h` on `e_c tensor w` and
`a tensor e_c` are nonzero.  Therefore `pi_0(K)` is the plane

\[
 \{xr+ys+tz:\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma t=0\},
 \qquad\alpha\beta\ne0.                                \tag{14}
\]

Setting `x=0` in (14) gives a nonzero member of `pi_0(K)` with empty row
`c`.  (The two remaining vectors in (13) are independent; otherwise
`Delta` would have dimension two.)  This contradicts (4) and proves the
lemma. `QED`

The evaluation lemma has a useful exact converse.  Give
`mathscr D^perp` homogeneous coordinates `z_cd` by its six off-diagonal
entries, and retain the cubic

\[
             F=z_{01}z_{12}z_{20}-z_{02}z_{21}z_{10}.    \tag{14a}
\]

**Corollary 3.3 (rank-drop evaluations are a binary cubic).**  The three
coordinate vectors always give rank exactly two:

\[
                \operatorname {rank}(M\mapsto Me_c)=2.  \tag{14b}
\]

Every other rank-two evaluation is obtained from a unique full-support
factorization

\[
              z_{cd}=a_cw_d\quad(c\ne d)                \tag{14c}
\]

of a point `[z] in P(mathscr D^perp)` on `F=0`; conversely every such
factorization gives a rank-two evaluation at `w`.  Hence there are at
most three noncoordinate rank-drop directions unless
`F|P(mathscr D^perp)` vanishes identically.

**Proof.**  The image `mathscr D e_c` lies in the coordinate plane
`e_c^perp`, and Lemma 3.1 makes its dimension exactly two.  For a general
`w`, rank two is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero annihilator
`a` with

\[
                         \pi_0(aw^{\mathsf T})\in\mathscr D^\perp.    \tag{14d}
\]

If the projection in (14d) is zero, `a,w` are supported on the same
coordinate, which is the preceding case.  Otherwise (4) makes both
factors full-support.  Their factorization implies `F=0`.  Conversely,
on the dense coordinate torus the single six-cycle equation `F=0` is
exactly the projected rank-one condition (14c), and (14d) gives rank at
most two; Lemma 3.1 makes it exactly two.  Uniqueness follows by comparing
the six nonzero coordinate ratios. `QED`

## 4. Exact rank reduction on a live edge

Since `mathscr D` has codimension two and its image in (1) is a line,

\[
                            \operatorname {rank}\mathcal L\le3.       \tag{15}
\]

**Proposition 4.1 (`2-2-2-2` reduction).**  On a live edge, either all
four matrices `P,S,Q,T` are invertible or all four have rank two.

**Proof.**  Suppose first that `P` is singular and put
`K_P=ker P^T`, of dimension `k=3-rank P`.  For `alpha in K_P`, transpose
the left contraction of (1):

\[
 QMS^{\mathsf T}\alpha
        =\lambda(M)H^{\mathsf T}\alpha\qquad(M\in\mathscr D),        \tag{16}
\]

where `lambda` is nonzero because the edge is live.  At an `M_0` with
`lambda(M_0)!=0`, the right side is injective as a map of `alpha`.
Thus `S^T|K_P` is injective and `rank Q>=k`.

If `k>=2`, set `W=S^T K_P`.  Equation (16) makes the compression of
`mathscr D` from `W` to a suitable two-dimensional quotient of the
output a line generated by an invertible two-by-two map: restrict to a
two-plane in `W` if `k=3`, and choose the quotient so that the rank-`k`
right side remains rank two.  This contradicts Lemma 3.2.  Hence
`rank P=2`.

Now `K_P` is a line.  For its nonzero `alpha`, put
`w=S^T alpha`, which is nonzero by (16).  If `Q` were invertible, (16)
would make `M mapsto Mw` have one-dimensional image, contrary to Lemma
3.1.  Thus `Q` is singular.  Applying the same argument after transposing
the edge exchanges `P` and `Q`; hence `rank Q=2` as well.  We have proved

\[
       P\text{ singular}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q\text{ singular},
       \qquad \operatorname {rank}P=\operatorname {rank}Q=2.         \tag{17}
\]

Transpose `mathscr D` and interchange the two summands of (1).  The same
argument gives the analogous dichotomy for `S,T`.  If `P,Q` have rank
two while `S,T` are invertible, apply Theorem 3.1 of the preceding note
with `S,T` as the normalized primary pair and `mathscr D^T` as relation
space.  Its live alternative forces `P,Q` invertible, a contradiction.
The reverse mixed case is identical.  This proves the proposition.
`QED`

Notice also that (16) proves a useful transversality statement intrinsic
to a live singular edge:

\[
 \ker P^{\mathsf T}\cap\ker S^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad
 \ker Q^{\mathsf T}\cap\ker T^{\mathsf T}=0.           \tag{18}
\]

A common left kernel would annihilate the invertible live line.

## 5. The rank-three determinant net

Assume now `rank mathcal L=3` and put `U=im mathcal L`.  Then
`dim ker mathcal L=3`.  Since the live restriction of `mathcal L` to
`mathscr D` has a three-dimensional kernel,

\[
                 \ker\mathcal L\subset\mathscr D,
 \qquad \mathcal L^{-1}(\mathbb C H)=\mathscr D.        \tag{19}
\]

It follows from (3) that `mathcal L` is injective on every `R_c,C_d`.
Write

\[
 \mathcal R_c=\mathcal L(R_c),\qquad
 \mathcal C_d=\mathcal L(C_d).                         \tag{20}
\]

These are two-planes in `U`, hence projective lines in `P(U)=P^2`.
Every matrix on each line has rank at most two, since

\[
 \mathcal L(e_cz^{\mathsf T})
   =p_c(Tz)^{\mathsf T}+(Sz)q_c^{\mathsf T}             \tag{21}
\]

and the column formula is analogous.  The point `[H]` lies on none of
the six lines.

**Proposition 5.1 (determinant-line cap).**  The six lines in (20) take
at most three distinct values.

**Proof.**  The restriction of the determinant to `U` is a nonzero
homogeneous plane cubic because it is nonzero at `H`.  It vanishes
identically on every line in (20).  The equation of each distinct line
therefore divides this cubic.  A nonzero cubic has at most three distinct
linear factors. `QED`

For `c!=d`, put

\[
 v_{cd}=\mathcal L(E_{cd}).                             \tag{22}
\]

It is nonzero by injectivity, and its projective point is the intersection
of `P(mathcal R_c)` and `P(mathcal C_d)`.  In each fixed row and each
fixed column, the two points in (22) are distinct.

There is a short complete incidence list.  Denote distinct determinant
lines by `A,B,C`.  Up to a simultaneous permutation of the three colours,
a permutation of `A,B,C`, and exchange of rows with columns, the tuples

\[
 ((\mathcal R_0,\mathcal R_1,\mathcal R_2),
   (\mathcal C_0,\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_2))            \tag{23}
\]

are exactly

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 & (\mathcal R_0,\mathcal R_1,\mathcal R_2)
 & (\mathcal C_0,\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_2)\\ \hline
2a&(A,A,B)&(A,B,A)\\
2b&(A,A,B)&(B,B,A)\\ \hline
3a&(A,A,B)&(A,C,A)\\
3b&(A,A,B)&(B,C,A)\\
3c&(A,B,C)&(A,B,C)\\
3d&(A,B,C)&(A,C,B)\\
3e&(A,B,C)&(B,C,A).
\end{array}                                             \tag{24}
\]

Indeed, if the two column lines opposite row `c` were equal to a line
different from `mathcal R_c`, the two points in that row would be the
same intersection point, contradicting injectivity.  The transposed
condition holds for columns.  Enumerating partitions of two triples into
at most three labels subject to precisely these two rules gives (24).
This enumeration is checked exactly by the companion script.

Thus a rank-three `2-2-2-2` edge is reduced to seven arrangements of
singular Kronecker pencils.  In particular, any proof that four of the
six lines must be distinct closes the singular live branch immediately.

The following observation makes three of the seven cases immediate.

**Lemma 5.2 (kernel compression quadratic).**  On a live rank-three
`2-2-2-2` edge, let `K=ker mathcal L` and let `r_P,r_T` generate the
right kernels of `P,T`.  Then

\[
              r_T^{\mathsf T}\operatorname {adj}(M)r_P=0
              \qquad(M\in K).                          \tag{24a}
\]

**Proof.**  If this scalar were nonzero for some `M`, then
`PMT^T` would have rank two.  But `M in K` gives

\[
                   PMT^{\mathsf T}=-SM^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T}.      \tag{24b}
\]

The column spaces of the two rank-two sides would be `im P` and `im S`,
so they would be equal.  Every physical value of `mathcal L` would then
have column space in this common two-plane, contradicting the
invertibility of `H`. `QED`

Type `3c` can now be excluded directly.

**Proposition 5.2 (type `3c` is impossible).**  There is no live
`2-2-2-2` edge of incidence type `3c`.

**Proof.**  The projective incidences give three reverse-pair
proportionalities.  Since `ker mathcal L` has dimension three, after
choosing the upper-triangular entries as coordinates it has the exact
form

\[
 K=\ker\mathcal L=
 \left\{
 M(x,y,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&x&y\\ Ax&0&z\\ By&Cz&0
 \end{pmatrix}:x,y,z\in\mathbb C
 \right\},                                             \tag{24c}
\]

where `A,B,C` are nonzero.  Direct expansion gives

\[
                         \det M(x,y,z)=(AC+B)xyz.        \tag{24d}
\]

The compression quadratic cannot vanish identically on `K`.  Indeed,

\[
 \operatorname {adj}M=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -Cz^2&Cyz&xz\\
 Byz&-By^2&Axy\\
 ACxz&Bxy&-Ax^2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                        \tag{24e}
\]

If `u^T adj(M)v` vanished identically, the square coefficients would
give `u_i v_i=0` for all `i`.  Thus the nonempty supports of `u` and `v`
would be disjoint.  If either support had at least two elements, the two
mixed coefficients involving the remaining coordinate would kill the
other vector.  If both supports were singletons, the mixed coefficient
joining those two distinct coordinates would be nonzero.  Both are
contradictions.  This contradicts Lemma 5.2. `QED`

**Proposition 5.3 (types `3d` and `3e` are impossible).**  There is no
live `2-2-2-2` edge of either incidence type.

**Proof.**  In type `3d`, its two fixed vertex proportionalities and the
one remaining relation put the kernel, after nonzero rescalings, in the
form

\[
 M(x,y,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&By+Cz&Ax+Dz\\ x&0&Ez\\ y&z&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad ABE\ne0.                         \tag{24f}
\]

For `u=(u_0,u_1,u_2)^T` and `v=(v_0,v_1,v_2)^T`, the `x^2,y^2,xy`
coefficients of `u^T adj(M)v=0` give

\[
 u_1v_2=0,\qquad u_2v_1=0,\qquad
 Au_1v_1+Bu_2v_2=0.                                   \tag{24g}
\]

If `u_1` or `u_2` is nonzero, these equations and respectively the
`yz` or `xz` coefficient kill all of `v`.  Thus `u_1=u_2=0`.  The
`xz,yz,z^2` coefficients then successively kill `v_1,v_2,v_0`.
There are no two nonzero vectors `u,v` for which the quadratic vanishes
on this kernel, contrary to Lemma 5.2.

In type `3e`, the three vertex generators form a basis and each of the
other three lies on its indicated component line.  Row-column
injectivity makes all six displayed coefficients nonzero, and the kernel
has the form

\[
 M(x,y,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-ax-fz&x\\ y&0&-bx-dy\\-cy-ez&z&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad abcdef\ne0.                      \tag{24h}
\]

The square coefficients of `u^T adj(M)v=0` are

\[
                         u_0v_2=u_1v_0=u_2v_1=0.        \tag{24i}
\]

There are only twelve nonempty support pairs obeying (24i).  If both
supports are singletons, one of the mixed coefficients is a single
nonzero monomial.  If one support has two elements, (24i) makes the
other a singleton, and again one mixed coefficient is a single nonzero
monomial.  Neither support can have three elements.  Thus the quadratic
never vanishes identically for nonzero `u,v`, again contradicting Lemma
5.2.  The exact support enumeration is included in the verifier. `QED`

**Proposition 5.4 (types `2a` and `3a` force a common right kernel).**
A live `2-2-2-2` edge of either incidence type has a common right
colour-kernel line at an endpoint.

**Proof.**  In both types a direct choice of the three physical output
coordinates puts the kernel in the form

\[
 M(x,y,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+Cy+Ez&Bx+Dy+Fz\\ x&0&y\\z&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.                                        \tag{24j}
\]

The avoidance of the six coordinate row and column planes gives

\[
                  C(AD-BC)(AF-BE)\ne0.                 \tag{24k}
\]

Comparing the six coefficients of `u^T adj(M)v` and using (24k) shows
that its nonzero projective solutions are exactly the union

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 v=e_0,&u\in\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_2),\\
 u=e_0,&v\in\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_1).
\end{array}                                             \tag{24l}
\]

For completeness, the `y^2` coefficient first gives `u_0v_2=0`.  If
`v_2!=0`, the `x^2,xy` coefficients and `AD-BC!=0` kill `u`.  Thus
`v_2=0`.  If also `v_1=0`, the `yz` coefficient gives `u_1=0`, which is
the first line of (24l).  If `v_1!=0`, the `xz,z^2` coefficients and
`AF-BE!=0` give `u_1=u_2=0`, which is the second line.

Apply Lemma 5.2 first with `(u,v)=(r_T,r_P)`.  Apply its transposed
counterpart to the other product in (24b), which gives (24l) with
`(u,v)=(r_S,r_Q)`.  If the first alternatives differ, either
`r_P=r_S=e_0` or `r_Q=r_T=e_0`, which is the desired common kernel.
If both use the first line, then `r_P=r_Q=e_0`; hence `p_0=q_0=0` and
both generators in row zero vanish, contradicting row injectivity.  If
both use the second line, then `r_T=r_S=e_0`; hence `t_0=s_0=0` and both
generators in column zero vanish, contradicting column injectivity.
This exhausts the alternatives. `QED`

We use one convenient description of the quadratic in Lemma 5.2 for the
last two types.  For nonzero `u,v`, choose full-rank `2 by 3` matrices
`B_u,A_v` whose row spaces are `u^perp,v^perp`, respectively, and put

\[
             \kappa_{u,v}(M)=A_vMB_u^{\mathsf T}.       \tag{24m}
\]

By Cauchy--Binet, its determinant is a nonzero scalar multiple of
`u^T adj(M)v`.  If `ker P=Cv` and `ker T=Cu`, there are injective
`3 by 2` matrices `U,Y` with

\[
               P=UA_v,\qquad T=YB_u,
 \qquad PMT^{\mathsf T}=U\kappa_{u,v}(M)Y^{\mathsf T}. \tag{24n}
\]

For the second summand, with `(u,v)=(r_S,r_Q)`, the corresponding
factorization is `SM^TQ^T=X\kappa_{u,v}(M)^TV^T`.  Thus a two-dimensional
compression consisting of matrices with one fixed zero row gives a
two-dimensional physical **column ruling** in the first summand and a
physical **row ruling** in the transposed second summand.  A fixed zero
column exchanges the two roles.

**Proposition 5.5 (type `3b` is impossible).**  There is no live
`2-2-2-2` edge of incidence type `3b`.

**Proof.**  Choosing coordinates along its three component lines gives

\[
 M(x,y,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+Cy+Ez&Bx+Dy+Fz\\ x&0&y\\Gz&z&0
 \end{pmatrix},                                      \tag{24o}
\]

where row-column injectivity and the incidence at the three vertices give

\[
 BCFG(AD-BC)\ne0,\qquad BE=AF.                         \tag{24p}
\]

The second identity says that the `A`-line part of the `z`-relation is
at its intersection with the `B`-line.  Comparing coefficients in
`u^T adj(M)v=0` gives exactly

\[
 u=(G,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 v=(r,s,0)^{\mathsf T}\quad((r,s)\ne(0,0)).            \tag{24q}
\]

Here is the short coefficient chase.  The `y^2` coefficient and then the
`x^2,xy` coefficients, using `C(AD-BC)\ne0`, show that `v_2=0`.  Put
`h=u_0-Gu_1`.  If `v_1=0`, the `xz,yz` coefficients give `u_2=h=0`.
If `v_1\ne0`, the `z^2` coefficient, together with `BE=AF`, gives
`AGu_2+Bh=0`; the `xz` coefficient gives `u_2v_0=0`, and if `v_0=0`
the `yz` coefficient is a nonzero multiple of `(BC-AD)u_2`.  Hence again
`u_2=h=0`, proving (24q).

Use the row bases

\[
 A_v=\begin{pmatrix}-s&r&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B_u=\begin{pmatrix}1&-G&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then (24m) has only its first row nonzero, and the two entries of that
row, as linear forms in `(x,y,z)`, have coefficient matrix

\[
 R_{r,s}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 AGs+r&CGs&EGs\\-Bs&-Ds+r&-Fs
 \end{pmatrix}.                                      \tag{24r}
\]

This matrix always has rank two.  If `rs\ne0`, its `(x,z)` minor is
`-Fsr`, after using `BE=AF`.  If `s=0`, the `x,y` columns contain the
diagonal entries `r,r`.  If `r=0`, their minor is
`-Gs^2(AD-BC)`.  Thus both kernel-compressions furnished by Lemma 5.2
have two-dimensional image.  By (24n), the first physical image on `K`
is a column ruling, while the equal second image is a row ruling.  Their
intersection has dimension at most one, contradicting (24b). `QED`

**Proposition 5.6 (type `2b` is impossible).**  There is no live
`2-2-2-2` edge of incidence type `2b`.

**Proof.**  The two component lines meet at the common point represented
by `v_01` and `v_10`.  Their two remaining points are distinct on each
line.  The kernel consequently has the normal form

\[
 M(x,y,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+Cy+Ez&Dy\\x&0&y\\Fz&z&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad ACDEF\ne0.                      \tag{24s}
\]

The square coefficients and the `yz` coefficient in
`u^T adj(M)v=0` show that its nonzero solutions are precisely

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 u=k=(F,1,0)^{\mathsf T},&v\in W=\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_1),\\
 u\in W,&v=\ell=(D,1,0)^{\mathsf T}.
 \end{array}                                          \tag{24t}
\]

Indeed, first `v_2=0` and `u_2v_1=0`; the remaining coefficient is

\[
                    (u_0-Fu_1)(Dv_1-v_0)=0,            \tag{24u}
\]

with the cases `v_1=0` and `u_2\ne0` disposed of by the `xz,z^2`
coefficients.

The compression ranks distinguish the crossing point of the two rulings.
For `u=k`, `v=(r,s,0)`, the only nonzero row of (24m) is

\[
 ((AFs+r)x+CFs y+EFs z,\ (r-Ds)y),                    \tag{24v}
\]

so its image has dimension one exactly when `v=ell`, and dimension two
otherwise.  Dually, for `u=(r,s,0)`, `v=ell`, the only nonzero column is

\[
 ((Ar+Ds)x+Cr y+Er z,\ (r-Fs)z)^{\mathsf T},           \tag{24w}
\]

and its image has dimension one exactly when `u=k`.

Apply this to `(u,v)=(r_T,r_P)` and `(r_S,r_Q)`.  Their physical images
on `K` are equal by (24b), so their dimensions agree.  If both dimensions
were two, equal choices of the ruling in (24t) would make their common
physical image simultaneously a row and a column ruling, hence at most
one-dimensional.  Unequal choices would make equality force respectively
`im T=im Q` or `im P=im S`, contradicting (18).  Hence both dimensions
are one, and (24v)--(24w) force

\[
        \ker P=\ker Q=\mathbb C\ell,\qquad
        \ker S=\ker T=\mathbb C k.                    \tag{24x}
\]

Choose rank-two quotient maps `A,B` with kernels
`\mathbb C\ell,\mathbb Ck`.  There are
injective `3 by 2` matrices `U,V,X,Y` such that

\[
 P=UA,\quad Q=VA,\quad S=XB,\quad T=YB,
\]

and, on putting `Z=AMB^T`,

\[
 \mathcal L(M)=\Phi(Z),\qquad
 \Phi(Z)=UZY^{\mathsf T}+XZ^{\mathsf T}V^{\mathsf T}. \tag{24y}
\]

The nonzero compression line (24v) of `K` is generated by a rank-one
matrix `h`, and (24b) says `Phi(h)=0`.  Choose quotient bases with
`h=E_00`.  The equality `Phi(E_00)=0` permits the normalization

\[
 U=(u\ a),\quad X=(u\ b),\qquad
 Y=(y\ c),\quad V=(-y\ d).                            \tag{24z}
\]

The two transversality statements (18) say that `(a,u,b)` and `(c,y,d)`
are bases of the two physical three-spaces.  In these bases, for
`Z=(z_ij)`, direct multiplication gives

\[
 \Phi(Z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 z_{11}&z_{10}&0\\ z_{01}&0&z_{10}\\0&-z_{01}&z_{11}
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det\Phi(Z)=0.                  \tag{24aa}
\]

Thus the entire image of `Phi`, and therefore of `mathcal L`, is
singular.  This contradicts the presence of the invertible live value
`H`. `QED`

There is a useful strengthening of Proposition 5.4.  For `M in K`, the
common matrix in (24b) has column space in
`im P intersection im S` and row space in `im T intersection im Q`.
Transversality (18) makes both intersections lines, so each kernel
compression has nonzero one-dimensional image.  (If it were zero, apply
Lemma 3.2 to the one-dimensional compression image on `mathscr D`; the
two summands of `H` would each have rank at most one.)  On either ruling
in (24l), the compression image has dimension one only at its intersection
`(u,v)=(e_0,e_0)`.  The exact minors are recorded in
[`rank-three-singular-fixed-line-obstruction.md`](rank-three-singular-fixed-line-obstruction.md).
Thus both kernel pairs equal `(e_0,e_0)`, so

\[
                 \ker P=\ker S=\ker Q=\ker T=\mathbb C e_0.         \tag{24ab}
\]

Every generator in coordinate row zero and coordinate column zero then
vanishes, contradicting the injectivity used in (19)--(22).  Hence types
`2a,3a` are impossible as well.  Together with Propositions 5.2, 5.3,
5.5, and 5.6, this excludes every rank-three all-rank-two live edge.

## 6. The dimension-two physical image

For clarity, suppose instead that `rank mathcal L=2`.  Put
`K=ker mathcal L`; then `dim K=4` and
`dim(K intersection mathscr D)=3`.  On every coordinate row or column
plane the restriction of `mathcal L` has rank exactly one:

* rank zero would put a two-plane in `K`, which must meet the
  three-plane `K intersection mathscr D` inside `K`, violating (3);
* rank two would make its image the full physical pencil, which contains
  `H`, although every matrix in a row or column image has rank at most
  two.

Let `r_c` and `c_d` be the resulting projective image points.  A generator
`v_cd` is nonzero only when `r_c=c_d`.  Every row and every column has a
nonzero generator, while the nonzero-generator graph cannot be connected
(otherwise all six points agree and `rank mathcal L=1`).  The off-diagonal
incidence graph is the six-cycle `K_(3,3)` minus its diagonal matching.
Its disconnected spanning subgraphs with minimum degree one are exactly:

1. either directed perfect matching (three nonzero generators); or
2. two paths obtained by deleting two nonadjacent cycle edges (four
   nonzero generators).

The three realizations are excluded exactly in
[`rank-two-singular-fixed-line-obstruction.md`](rank-two-singular-fixed-line-obstruction.md).
On `K`, both summands must have the same nonzero fixed rank-one physical
line.  Classifying the resulting one-line colour compressions in the
three graph normal forms gives either a coordinate kernel which kills an
active generator, or crossed common kernels whose universal physical
image has identically zero determinant.  Thus a live all-rank-two edge
cannot have physical image dimension two.

## 7. Four toric contraction points

There is one more constraint on every live `2-2-2-2` edge.  Choose
generators

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \alpha_P\in\ker P^{\mathsf T},&
  \alpha_S\in\ker S^{\mathsf T},\\
  \beta_Q\in\ker Q^{\mathsf T},&
  \beta_T\in\ker T^{\mathsf T}.
 \end{array}                                             \tag{25}
\]

All four kernel lines are distinct at their respective endpoints by
(18).  Put

\[
 w_P=S^{\mathsf T}\alpha_P,quad
 w_S=P^{\mathsf T}\alpha_S,quad
 w_Q=T^{\mathsf T}\beta_Q,quad
 w_T=Q^{\mathsf T}\beta_T.                              \tag{26}
\]

They are nonzero.  The four contractions of (1) are

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 QM w_P=\lambda(M)H^{\mathsf T}\alpha_P,&
 PM w_Q=\lambda(M)H\beta_Q,\\
 TM^{\mathsf T}w_S=\lambda(M)H^{\mathsf T}\alpha_S,&
 SM^{\mathsf T}w_T=\lambda(M)H\beta_T.
\end{array}                                             \tag{27}
\]

In the first equation, the nonzero right side lies in the two-plane
`im Q`.  Pull back its one-dimensional annihilator in that plane through
`Q`; this gives a nonzero colour covector `a_P` such that

\[
                         \gamma_P=\pi_0(a_Pw_P^{\mathsf T})
                                      \in\mathscr D^\perp.             \tag{28}
\]

The other three equations give, in the same way,

\[
 \gamma_Q=\pi_0(a_Qw_Q^{\mathsf T}),\qquad
 \gamma_S=\pi_0(w_Sa_S^{\mathsf T}),\qquad
 \gamma_T=\pi_0(w_Ta_T^{\mathsf T})\in\mathscr D^\perp.             \tag{29}
\]

Here the names of the auxiliary covectors record only which contraction
produced them.  There are two possibilities for each outer product.
If its off-diagonal projection is nonzero, (4) implies that both factors
have all three coordinates nonzero: an empty coordinate in the left
factor would give an empty row, and one in the right factor would give an
empty column.  If its off-diagonal projection is zero, the two factors
are supported on the same single coordinate line.  Call the first case
**toric** and the second **coordinate-trivial**.

Consider the six-cycle cubic on `P(Z_0^*)=P^5`,

\[
 F(z)=z_{01}z_{12}z_{20}-z_{02}z_{21}z_{10}.            \tag{30}
\]

Every nonzero projected rank-one point `pi_0(xy^T)` lies on `F=0`.  Thus
each toric direction in (28)--(29) lies in

\[
             \mathbb P(\mathscr D^\perp)\cap\{F=0\}.   \tag{31}
\]

**Proposition 7.1 (toric collision/common-kernel dichotomy).**  On a
live `2-2-2-2` edge for which all four contractions are toric, either the
two endpoint star pairs have a common right colour-kernel line, or at
least two of the four named directions

\[
                  [\gamma_P],[\gamma_Q],[\gamma_S],[\gamma_T]         \tag{32}
\]

coincide.

**Proof.**  Suppose the four points are distinct.  The restriction of
the cubic `F` to the projective line `P(mathscr D^perp)` then has four
zeros, so it vanishes identically.  No coordinate `z_cd` vanishes
identically on this line, since the restriction in (4) to its row or
column is an isomorphism.  Unique factorization of binary cubics in (30)
therefore matches the three linear coordinate forms on the left with
the three on the right.

A match cannot join `z_cd` to the other coordinate in row `c`, nor to
the other coordinate in column `d`, because either proportionality would
make the corresponding restriction in (4) singular.  The matching is
therefore forced:

\[
                     z_{01}\parallel z_{10},\qquad
                     z_{12}\parallel z_{21},\qquad
                     z_{20}\parallel z_{02}.            \tag{33}
\]

Dualizing gives the same proportionalities for the six quotient classes
`u_cd`.  Equality of the two products in (30) gives the product-one
condition on the three proportionality constants.  Hence the same
diagonal colour rescaling as in Theorem 4.1 of
[`hessian-corank-two-osculating-dichotomy.md`](hessian-corank-two-osculating-dichotomy.md)
makes `u_cd=u_dc` for all `c!=d`.

The three corresponding alternating matrices then belong to the
rescaled relation space, so their physical alternating pencil has image
in `C H`.  If it is nonzero, Lemma 5.1 of that note gives one common
right kernel at both endpoints.  If it is zero and neither endpoint has
a common right kernel, Lemma 5.2 makes both endpoint pairs proportional
and invertible, contradicting the `2-2-2-2` ranks.  Thus the assumed four
distinct points force the common-kernel alternative. `QED`

The fixed-line classifications in Sections 5--6 supersede this conditional
toric reduction for live singular edges: no physical-rank-two or
physical-rank-three `2-2-2-2` edge survives.  Since physical rank one is
also impossible on a live edge, Proposition 4.1 now says that every live
relation edge has all four outer matrices invertible.  Equivalently, any
edge carrying a singular endpoint star in the global corank-two branch
must be a dead relation edge, `mathcal L(mathscr D)=0`.

The resulting global dead-edge propagation is now proved in
[`live-component-zero-cut-propagation.md`](live-component-zero-cut-propagation.md).
Once one live edge exists, all invertible-star sites form a single complete
live component; every rank-three edge leaving it lands at a literal
two-star-zero site.  After one common diagonal normalization, every dead
singular edge is exactly an `H`-orthogonal pair
`P_iHP_j^T=0`, so its two endpoint ranks sum to at most three.
