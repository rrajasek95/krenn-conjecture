# Independent audit of the first direct-free reset syzygy gate

This is an independent audit of commit `e1968b2`.  The finite calculation and
the two-row algebraic conclusion pass.  The result remains a bounded

## Fine degree and the first possible multiplier

Let

\[
 0=00000000,\qquad m=01211222,
\]

and give (w_{ab}^{ij}) fine degree
(e_{a,i}+e_{b,j}\in\mathbb N^{8\times3}).  The independent checker uses a
24-coordinate vector for this degree.  It finds that the two word degrees
agree only in the slot ((0,0)).  Their componentwise LCM therefore has 15
slots, and its deficit from either eight-slot word degree is seven.

An edge-degree (d) multiplier contributes exactly (2d) slots.  Any common
homogeneous degree must dominate that LCM, so (2d\ge7).  This excludes
(d=0,1,2,3), and parity forces (d\ge4).  The common degree

\[
                     \mu(0)+\mu(m)
\]

has coefficient deficits of eight slots on both rows.  The two universal
hafnians (H_0,H_m), each independently reconstructed with 105 distinct
four-edge terms, realize those deficits.  Hence edge degree four both is the
lower bound and is attained.

## Coprimality, uniqueness, and sign

Introduce (u) with fine degree (mu(0)) and matching degree four.  The two
homogeneous equations are

\[
                 f_0=H_0-u,\qquad f_m=H_m.
\]

The checker verifies every monomial has the stated fine degree and that the
variable sets of (H_0-u) and (H_m) are disjoint.  The latter follows
geometrically because a shared edge variable would require two agreement
sites, whereas the words agree only at site zero.

This is also a coprimality certificate over the universal polynomial ring.
Indeed, a divisor of (f_0), which has degree zero in all (H_m)-variables,
must itself have degree zero in those variables.  If it also divides (f_m),
the same argument in the (H_0,u)-variables makes it a scalar.  Thus
(gcd(f_0,f_m)=1).

For two coprime elements in a polynomial UFD, a relation
(af_0+bf_m=0) has (f_m\mid a) and (f_0\mid b).  The two-row syzygy module
is consequently generated by

\[
 K_m=H_mr_0-(H_0-u)r_m
    =H_mr_0-H_0r_m+ur_m.
\]

Any nonconstant multiple raises the matching degree, so at edge degree four
this cell is unique up to a scalar on the support ({r_0,r_m}).  The sparse
polynomial replay verifies its boundary is literally zero.

The sign also survives the audit.  In the coefficient of (r_m), the only
term of physical edge degree zero after (u=1) is (+1); all 105 quartic
terms from (H_0) have coefficient (-1).  Therefore the lowest symbol is
(+r_m), and multiplying by (1/4=-\kappa_{\rm df}) produces
(+\frac14r_m).  Resetting its mixed odd tag would give the requested
associated symbol (+\frac14Y_0=-\kappa_{\rm df}Y_0).

This uniqueness statement is only for two-row support.  It says nothing
about syzygies involving the other EqSystem rows.

## Scope and non-flat specialization

The degree-four Koszul cell resolves the two EqSystem rows only.  It does not
annihilate the denominator defects, provide their five degree-two
homotopies, construct a corrected extraction functional, or prove zero
indeterminacy.  The calculation also does not rule out any of those objects.

The guard evaluation is non-flat.  For example, the nonzero universal
variable (w_{23}^{21}), which occurs in the defects, specializes to zero.
Multiplication by it is injective in the universal polynomial domain, whereas
after tensoring with the guard field it becomes the zero map.  Equivalently,
the elementary module (R/(w_{23}^{21})) already exhibits a nonzero
(operatorname{Tor}_1) under this base change.  This is a witness for the
general caveat, not a claim that the desired source complex has the relevant
Tor class.  A specialization-created chain is neither excluded nor
constructed by this audit.
