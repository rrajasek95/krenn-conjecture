# Independent audit of the uniform full-nine target-incidence invariant

## Verdict

**PASS.** A clean-room derivation found no cancellation assumption, hidden
coordinate-span hypothesis, endpoint-order error, nonzero-block assumption, or
gap in the total-source double count in
[`uniform-full-nine-target-incidence-invariant.md`](uniform-full-nine-target-incidence-invariant.md).
For every boundary of size \(2m\) in the stated range, the nine cap equations
indeed imply

\[
 |D_i|\ge 2m-2,\qquad D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2=U,\qquad
 n_3\ge n_1+2m-6.
\]

The overlap conclusion is also valid: for \(N\ge14\), some physical pair has
an injective **aggregate** direct-sum star at each endpoint.  This does not
imply that every row of every individual endpoint block is nonzero.

The independent executable audit is
[`audit_uniform_full_nine_target_incidence_invariant_independent.py`](../computations/audit_uniform_full_nine_target_incidence_invariant_independent.py).
It imports neither the primary proof nor its checker.

## 1. Endpoint-ordered scalarization

Write \(n=2m\).  No symmetry internal to a physical block is needed.  For an
oriented block \(q_{uv}\in V_u\otimes V_v\), its scalar contraction is

\[
 b_{uv}(\ell)=\langle\ell_u\otimes\ell_v,q_{uv}\rangle.
\]

Reversing the endpoint order replaces the matrix of \(q_{uv}\) by its
transpose and leaves this scalar unchanged.  The deletion cofactor

\[
 C_{uv}=\sum_{M\text{ matching }U\setminus\{u,v\}}
             \prod_{xy\in M}b_{xy},\qquad C_{uu}=0,
\]

is consequently symmetric in the deleted labels even when every physical
block matrix is nonsymmetric.  Expanding \(p_i s_jq^{[m-1]}\) first by the
ordered sites occupied by \(p_i,s_j\), and then by the remaining matching,
gives exactly

\[
 [p_i s_jq^{[m-1]}]_{\ell}
 =\sum_{u\ne v}P_{iu}C_{uv}S_{jv}=(PCS^{\mathsf T})_{ij}.
\]

Both orders \(u,v\) occur.  The independent checker reconstructs this identity
from scratch for boundaries of sizes four and six, in nine exact rational
specializations containing nonsymmetric blocks and physical zero blocks.  It
also points every full matching by each of its \(m\) edges and obtains
\(q q^{[m-1]}=m q^{[m]}\), confirming the normalization of the cap equation.

## 2. Cancellation-safe ideal orders

For each site \(u\), let \(I_u\) be generated by evaluations on the complete
endpoint support

\[
 W_u=\sum_{v\ne u}\operatorname{im}_u(q_{uv}),
 \qquad J=\sum_u I_u.
\]

Every block lies in \(W_u\otimes W_v\).  Hence its scalar contraction belongs
to \(I_uI_v\), a statement which is independent of a coordinate expansion of
the tensor.  It follows more precisely that

\[
 C_{uv}\in\prod_{w\notin\{u,v\}}I_w\subset J^{n-2},
 \qquad Q=q^{[m]}\in\prod_w I_w\subset J^n.
\]

Multiplication by the arbitrary star entries cannot lower ideal order, so the
diagonal cap equations give \(x_i\in J^{n-2}\).

Choose independent linear coordinates adapted to every \(W_u\).  Then \(J\)
is generated by a subset of the coordinate variables and its associated
graded ring is a polynomial domain.  The factor
\(\ell_u(e_i^{(u)})\) has \(J\)-order one exactly when
\(e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\), and order zero otherwise.  Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_J(x_i)=|D_i|.
\]

This proves \(|D_i|\ge n-2\) without selecting a nonzero matching term.  The
independent checker makes non-coordinate changes of basis at six sites,
including a zero span, three oblique proper spans, and an oblique full span.
Scaling the adapted ideal variables by a filtration parameter gives exact
orders \((2,2,2)\), equal to the three independently computed incidence
counts.  Thus the argument is not silently restricted to coordinate spans.

There is also a shorter independent quotient check.  If three sites lay
outside \(D_i\), quotient the covector polynomial ring by the sum of their
three local ideals.  Every cofactor \(C_{uv}\) covers all but two sites, so
each response summand vanishes in this quotient; \(Q\) vanishes as well.  But
the three target factors survive by the definition of \(D_i\), and the
quotient is a polynomial domain, so \(x_i\) survives.  The diagonal equation
would read \(0=x_i\), a contradiction.  This recovers the same two-site
defect bound without using the numerical value of the global \(J\)-order.

## 3. Cauchy--Binet and the site cover

The cleanest local-ideal formulation avoids any termwise tensor
decomposition.  Fix \(u\).  Whenever \(u\notin\{r,s\}\), every matching in
\(C_{rs}\) covers \(u\), and hence

\[
                         C_{rs}\in I_u.                 \tag{A1}
\]

In a potentially nonzero determinant term of a \(3\times3\) minor
\(C_{R,K}\), the three entries are \(C_{r_a,k_{\sigma(a)}}\).  If \(u\) is in
neither index set, all three satisfy (A1); if it is in exactly one set, two do;
and if it is in both sets, either the term contains \(C_{uu}=0\), or the two
occurrences of \(u\) lie in different entries and the third entry satisfies
(A1).  Thus every nonzero determinant product belongs to \(I_u\).  Applying
Cauchy--Binet to \(M=PCS^{\mathsf T}\) gives

\[
                              \det M\in I_u
\]

for every site, with no choice of a nonvanishing minor.

Since every perfect matching in \(Q\) covers \(u\), also \(Q\in I_u\).  In

\[
 M=\operatorname{diag}(x_0,x_1,x_2)-Qa,
\]

all terms of the determinant except \(x_0x_1x_2\) contain \(Q\).  Therefore
\(x_0x_1x_2\in I_u\).  The quotient by the linear ideal \(I_u\) is a polynomial
domain, while factors belonging to other sites remain nonzero.  At least one
of the three factors at \(u\) must lie in \(I_u\), which is precisely

\[
                         D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2=U.
\]

The checker independently enumerates all potentially nonzero cofactor-minor
terms for \(n=4,6,8\).  The ledgers contain respectively \(44,1420,12712\)
terms, and the least number of cofactor factors covering a fixed site is one
in every size.  It separately sets every block incident to one site to zero
and verifies that \(Q\) and every \(3\times3\) cofactor-minor term vanish.
Thus a zero incident span causes a contradiction with the target equation; it
is not excluded by an implicit division.

## 4. Incidence arithmetic and the small boundaries

Let \(n_r\) count sites in exactly \(r\) of the sets \(D_i\).  The site cover
gives \(n_0=0\), and the three ideal bounds give

\[
 n_1+2n_2+3n_3=\sum_i|D_i|\ge3(n-2),
 \qquad n_1+n_2+n_3=n.
\]

Subtracting twice the second identity yields

\[
                         n_3\ge n_1+n-6
                              =n_1+2m-6.
\]

An exhaustive set-level audit represents each \(D_i\) by a complement of
size at most two, requires the three complements to have empty intersection,
and checks the inequality for \(n=4,6,8,10\).  The numbers of surviving triples
and the minimum possible \(n_3\) are

| \(n\) | survivors | minimum \(n_3\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 1081 | 0 |
| 6 | 9367 | 0 |
| 8 | 46585 | 2 |
| 10 | 165661 | 4 |

The primary theorem assumes \(m\ge3\).  The independent audit finds that the
same proof actually works at \(m=2\): a four-site cofactor matrix still has
\(3\times3\) minors, the local coverage argument is unchanged, and the
conclusion is \(|D_i|\ge2\), site cover, and
\(n_3\ge n_1-2\).  This extension adds no useful forced full-frame site, but it
shows that \(m=3\) is not hiding a marginal failure.  At \(m=3\), the exact
conclusion is the advertised four-cover, site cover, and
\(n_3\ge n_1\).

## 5. Total-source double count

For a source on \(N=n+2\) sites, the incidence inequality in every pair cap
implies that at most six internal sites are not target-full.  Fix \(r\), and
put

\[
 S_u^{(r)}=\sum_{v\notin\{r,u\}}\operatorname{im}_u(A_{uv}).
\]

If \(S_u^{(r)}\ne V_u\), then after deleting \(r,t\), for any
\(t\notin\{r,u\}\), the internal incident span at \(u\) is a subspace of
\(S_u^{(r)}\) and is still deficient.  Thus a fixed deficient ordered pair
\((r,u)\) is counted for exactly \(N-2\) choices of \(t\).  On the other hand,
each of the \(N-1\) caps \(r,t\) contributes at most six such \(u\).  If \(b_r\)
is the number of deficient \(u\), then

\[
                         b_r(N-2)\le6(N-1).
\]

For \(N\ge10\), the integer bound is \(b_r\le6\).  Hence there are at most
\(6N\) deficient ordered pairs.  The aggregate star at \(u\) in the cap for
the physical pair \(r,u\) is the direct-sum map

\[
 V_u^*\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\notin\{r,u\}}V_v.
\]

Its kernel is the annihilator of \(S_u^{(r)}\), so it is injective exactly
when \(S_u^{(r)}=V_u\).  If every physical pair had a deficient endpoint, at
least one of its two orientations would be among the deficient ordered pairs,
forcing

\[
                         \binom N2\le6N.
\]

This fails first at \(N=14\).  Therefore every \(N\ge14\) source has some
physical pair whose aggregate stars are injective at both ends.  The
independent checker audits the exact \((t,u)\) ledger, the integer bound, and
the threshold through \(N=50\).

The related overlapping-pair statement in the primary note is also sound.  A
target-full \(u\) in the cap deleting \(r,t\) gives an injective map using only
the blocks to \(U\setminus\{u\}\).  In the cap deleting \(r,u\), the endpoint
star at \(u\) contains those direct-sum components plus the component at \(t\);
adding a direct-sum component cannot create a kernel.  The same argument with
\(r,t\) exchanged handles the cap deleting \(t,u\).

## 6. Aggregate is strictly weaker than blockwise

Represent an endpoint block by a \(3\times3\) matrix, with rows indexed by the
selected endpoint.  Take three blocks to three different neighbours:

\[
 A_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0),\qquad
 A_1=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0),\qquad
 A_2=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1).
\]

The stacked contraction map

\[
 \alpha\longmapsto(A_0^{\mathsf T}\alpha,
                    A_1^{\mathsf T}\alpha,
                    A_2^{\mathsf T}\alpha)
\]

has rank three, equivalently the sum of the endpoint images is all of
\(V_u\).  Nevertheless, each individual block has rank one and two identically
zero endpoint rows.  The construction may be installed at both endpoints of
a chosen pair.  The checker verifies both aggregate ranks and all six missing
row statements exactly.  Consequently the primary note is right to stop short
of the blockwise all-row-nonzero hypothesis used by the source-Hessian route.
