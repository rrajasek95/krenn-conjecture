## Aggregate-Tor attachment-or-dual theorem

The apparent five-surjectivity requirement can be weakened exactly. Let

\[
 W=S^5,\qquad \epsilon(y)=\sum_v y_v,
\]

and let \(D\subset W\) be the clean C5 collision-edge image. Since the
oriented C5 incidence lattice is saturated,

\[
                         D=\ker\epsilon.               \tag{8}
\]

For any physical reduced-companion transgression
\(\tau:K\to W\), put

\[
                         I=(\epsilon\tau(K))\subset S. \tag{9}
\]

Then there is a canonical isomorphism

\[
                    \boxed{\operatorname{coker}[D,\tau]\simeq S/I.} \tag{10}
\]

Consequently:

1. if \(I=S\), one combination \(y=\tau(k)\) has unit aggregate and,
   after normalization, \(y-e_v\in D\). It constructs every face from one
   transgression plus the clean collision edges;
2. if \(I=0\), \(\epsilon\) descends as an \(S\)-valued primitive separator;
3. if \(0\ne I\ne S\), the residual is genuinely \(S/I\). Merely knowing
   that one aggregate is nonzero does not permit normalization over the
   source ring. Over a field, or after localizing at that aggregate, it does.

The integer model \(I=(2)\) gives cokernel \(\mathbb Z/2\) and is a sharp
counterguard to the naive “nonzero implies unit” version.
