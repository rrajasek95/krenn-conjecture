# A dark Cartan potential is a generator or a terminal-safe presentation cancellation

## Result

Let

\[
                       \widehat J:L\longrightarrow E
\]

be the complete physically augmented correction map.  Separate its domain
into occupied scalar columns `C=(C_1,...,C_m)` and one source-provenant
Cartan column `G`.  On the dark branch, suppose the complete lift satisfies

\[
                         \widehat JG=\widehat JC\,y.       \tag{1}
\]

Then

\[
                         k=(-y,1)\in\ker\widehat J          \tag{2}
\]

has unit coefficient on `G`.  For any physically typed terminal readout
`q:L->k`, there are exactly two useful outcomes.

1. If `q(k)!=0`, the normalized class `k/q(k)` is the relative generator.
2. If `q(k)=0`, quotienting `L` by `<k>` removes the Cartan generator while
   preserving `im(Jhat)`, `coker(Jhat)`, and the image of `q` on the
   remaining correction kernel.

The second outcome is a chain/presentation cancellation.  It does **not**
require the scalar columns in (1) to lie in one endpoint row, and it does
not claim deletion of an original graph coefficient.  It therefore survives
the exact type-split counterguard that blocked the earlier support-deletion
inference.

## Proof

Equation (2) is immediate from (1).  Because the coefficient of `G` is one,
the quotient `L/<k>` is represented by the original scalar generators: in
the quotient,

\[
                              G=Cy.                     \tag{3}
\]

Hence the image of `Jhat` is unchanged.  Its cokernel is therefore unchanged
as well, while the kernel loses exactly the direct summand generated by
`k`.  When `q(k)=0`, `q` descends to the quotient, and

\[
 q(\ker\widehat J)=q\bigl(\ker(\widehat J:L/\langle k\rangle\to E)\bigr).
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

Thus every later Fredholm or generator/annihilator test sees exactly the
same terminal ambiguity.  When `q(k)!=0`, the normalization of (2) preserves
all augmented zero rows and makes the terminal one, which is precisely the
already proved relative-generator branch.

Notice that `q(k)=0` does not assert that `q` kills the entire correction
kernel.  Another kernel class may still be terminal-visible and give the
generator.  The quotient in (4) preserves exactly that possibility; only
after `q` kills the whole quotient kernel does the physical Fredholm theorem
produce its left separator.

This is ordinary unit cancellation in a two-term complex.  Its importance
here is typing: it is legal even when `G` is a relative Cartan/Spencer
generator rather than an occupied scalar cell.

## Transverse consequence

Let `pi_u,pi_v` be the two one-dimensional deficient endpoint quotients.  If
the Cartan image in (1) is visible in both,

\[
                 \pi_u(\widehat JG)\ne0,\qquad
                 \pi_v(\widehat JG)\ne0,                \tag{5}
\]

then its scalar expansion contains either

* one `C_i` visible in both quotients; or
* one scalar column visible at `u` and another scalar column visible at `v`.

Indeed, if every scalar column were invisible at `u` (or at `v`), their
linear combination in (1) would be invisible there.  If no column is visible
at both, the two nonempty visibility sets are disjoint and supply the two
split exits.

This replaces the false implication

```text
dark potential => same-row scalar support deletion
```

by the sounder trichotomy

```text
terminal-detected kernel          -> relative generator
terminal-killed unit kernel       -> presentation cancellation
double-visible Cartan image       -> one double or two split scalar exits.
```

Landing the scalar exits at a distinct-head four-good pair remains a
physical theorem.  The transverse Cartan audit has already shown that a
one-root column is only split-visible and that the first single
double-visible head is the two-root `(c,c)` corner.  The result here says
that, after dark cancellation, this visibility cannot remain confined to a
purely formal Cartan generator: it is represented by occupied scalar
columns, possibly split across the two endpoint rows.

## Effect on the frontier

The type-split dark guard no longer forces a same-row deletion theorem.
For Theorems B/C, (2)--(4) completely dispose of a dark unit direction once
the terminal is physically typed.  For Theorem A, the remaining work is
narrower: prove that the one double-visible scalar column, or the two
split-visible scalar columns, land in the existing active-fan/Hall machinery
with a strict decrease.  This is a rank/attachment theorem, not a further
Cartan source-provenance theorem.

## Scope

The theorem assumes the complete equality (1), not merely its critical
component projection.  A nonzero complete residual `R=G-Cy` is still the
typed-exit branch and requires fine-label saturation.  The terminal `q` must
be the actual physical anchor/ridge readout; a chart-only functional does not
qualify.  Presentation cancellation preserves the augmented proof complex,
but by itself does not remove an edge or source from the original graph.
