# Rank-three singular relation blocks have no live all-rank-two edge

## 1. Outcome and the dimension audit

Retain the notation of
[`singular-relation-block-reduction.md`](singular-relation-block-reduction.md).
Thus

\[
 Z_0=\{M\in\operatorname {Mat}_3(\mathbb C):M_{00}=M_{11}=M_{22}=0\},
 \qquad \dim Z_0=6,
\]

and `mathscr D subset Z_0` is a four-plane meeting none of the six
coordinate row or column planes.  On a live edge,

\[
 \mathcal L(M)=PMT^{\mathsf T}+SM^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \mathcal L(\mathscr D)=\mathbb C H,             \tag{1}
\]

where `H` is invertible and `P,S,Q,T` all have rank two.  Assume in this
note that

\[
                  \operatorname {rank}(\mathcal L:Z_0\to\operatorname {Mat}_3)=3.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

There is a potentially confusing but important dimension point.  The
restriction in (1) has rank **one**, not rank three.  Hence

\[
 \dim\ker(\mathcal L|_{\mathscr D})=3,
 \qquad \dim\ker(\mathcal L|_{Z_0})=3.
\]

The first kernel is contained in the second, so they are equal.  Put

\[
                    K=\ker(\mathcal L|_{Z_0})\subset\mathscr D,
                    \qquad \dim K=3.                   \tag{3}
\]

The result proved below is:

**Theorem 1.1.**  Under (1)--(3), a live edge cannot have all four outer
matrices of rank two.

Together with Proposition 4.1 of the preceding note, this eliminates the
entire rank-three physical-image singular branch.  The proof uses the seven
determinant-line incidences already enumerated there, but strengthens the
usual singular-compression test: on `K`, each compression has image in one
fixed rank-one line.

## 2. The fixed-line strengthening

Write the four right kernels as

\[
 p=\ker P,\qquad s=\ker S,\qquad q=\ker Q,\qquad t=\ker T. \tag{4}
\]

The live transversality statement (18) of the preceding note says that

\[
 \operatorname {im}P\ne\operatorname {im}S,
 \qquad \operatorname {im}T\ne\operatorname {im}Q.     \tag{5}
\]

For `M in K`, equation (1) becomes

\[
                       PMT^{\mathsf T}=-SM^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T}. \tag{6}
\]

The common matrix in (6) has column space in
`im P intersection im S` and row space in
`im T intersection im Q`.  Both intersections are lines by (5).  Therefore

\[
          \{PMT^{\mathsf T}:M\in K\}\subseteq\mathbb C ab^{\mathsf T} \tag{7}
\]

for fixed nonzero physical vectors `a,b`.  The same is true of the second
summand.  Since the rank-two outer maps identify the corresponding
two-dimensional quotients, (7) says

\[
 \dim\rho_{p,t}(K)\le1,
 \qquad \dim\rho^{\mathsf T}_{s,q}(K)\le1,              \tag{8}
\]

and every nonzero member of either image has rank one.  Here

\[
 \rho_{p,t}(M):t^\perp\longrightarrow\mathbb C^3/\mathbb Cp,
 \qquad [w\mapsto Mw],                                 \tag{9}
\]

and the second expression is the analogous compression of `M^T`.

Neither image in (8) is zero.  Indeed, if the first vanished on `K`, then
on `mathscr D=K+CM_0` it would be a line.  Lemma 3.2 of the preceding note
says that this line cannot be generated by an invertible two-by-two form;
hence `PM_0T^T` has rank at most one.  Equation (6) gives the same vanishing
for the second summand on `K`, so the identical argument makes
`SM_0^TQ^T` rank at most one.  Their sum could not be the invertible
multiple of `H` required by (1).

## 3. Bad Segre pairs

For a three-plane `K subset Z_0`, define

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=\{([u],[v])\in\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2:
             u^{\mathsf T}\operatorname {adj}(M)v=0\quad(M\in K)\}. \tag{10}
\]

Cauchy--Binet for a rank-two compression gives

\[
 \det\rho_{p,t}(M)=c\,t^{\mathsf T}\operatorname {adj}(M)p,
 \qquad c\ne0.                                        \tag{11}
\]

Consequently

\[
                         ([t],[p]),([s],[q])\in\mathcal B(K).        \tag{12}
\]

Membership in (10) only says that every compressed matrix is singular.
Condition (8) is sharper: the *linear span* of the compressed matrices must
have dimension one.  This extra rank filter is what removes the two
incidence types which retain bad-pair rulings.

## 4. Normal forms for the seven incidences

Choose coordinates in the three-dimensional physical image so that two
determinant lines are coordinate lines, and, when three occur, so that they
form the coordinate triangle.  They cannot be three concurrent lines:
one of the rows or columns in every incidence in (24) would then have its
two generator points equal, contradicting injectivity on coordinate
planes.  Nonzero row and column rescalings in `Z_0` preserve (10), up to
the corresponding invertible changes of `u,v`.

Direct row reduction of the six generators gives the following kernel
normal forms.  In every display `x,y,z` are free.

### 4.1 The three empty types

Type `3c` is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&ax&by\\cx&0&dz\\ey&fz&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad abcdef\ne0.                                    \tag{13}
\]

Type `3d` is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&Cy+Dz&Ax+Bz\\x&0&Rz\\y&z&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad ACR\ne0,                                      \tag{14}
\]

where `B,D` may vanish.  Type `3e` is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&Cy+Dz&y\\x&0&Ax+By\\Ex+Fz&z&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad ABCDEF\ne0.                                   \tag{15}
\]

For (13), the coefficients of `x^2,y^2,z^2` in
`u^T adj(M)v` give

\[
                       u_2v_2=u_1v_1=u_0v_0=0.          \tag{16}
\]

Thus the nonempty supports of `u,v` are disjoint.  For every pair of
indices in the two supports, exactly one term in the corresponding mixed
coefficient survives, and its scalar is nonzero.  This is impossible.

For (14), the decisive coefficients are

\[
 Au_1v_2,\qquad Cu_2v_1,\qquad-Au_1v_1-Cu_2v_2.        \tag{17}
\]

Splitting according as `v_1,v_2` are both nonzero, exactly one is nonzero,
or both vanish, the `xz`, `yz`, and `z^2` coefficients successively force
all of `u` to vanish.  For (15), the square coefficients give

\[
                       u_1v_0=u_0v_2=u_2v_1=0.          \tag{18}
\]

The seven possible nonempty supports of `u` are immediate.  If it has one
element, two mixed coefficients kill `v`; if it has two elements, the
remaining singleton support of `v` leaves respectively the nonzero terms
`-E u_1v_1`, `-B u_0v_0`, or `-D u_2v_2`; and full support makes `v=0`.
Therefore

\[
                  \mathcal B(K)=\varnothing
                  \quad\text{in types }3c,3d,3e.       \tag{19}
\]

### 4.2 Types `2a` and `3a`

Both types reduce to

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+Cy+Ez&Bx+Dy+Fz\\x&0&y\\z&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad C(AD-BC)(AF-BE)\ne0.                           \tag{20}
\]

Some of `B,E,F` may vanish individually; the displayed products are the
exact generator-independence conditions.  Expanding
`u^T adj(M)v` gives, in the order `x^2,xy,xz,y^2,yz,z^2`,

\[
\begin{array}{l}
 v_2(-Au_2+Bu_1),\\
 v_2(Au_0-Cu_2+Du_1),\\
 Au_2v_1-Bu_1v_1-Eu_2v_2+Fu_1v_2,\\
 Cu_0v_2,\\
 Cu_2v_1-Du_1v_1+Eu_0v_2+u_1v_0,\\
 v_1(Eu_2-Fu_1).
\end{array}                                             \tag{21}
\]

The fourth coefficient first forces `u_0v_2=0`.  If `v_2` is nonzero,
the first two and `AD-BC\ne0` kill `u_1,u_2`, and then kill `u_0`.
Putting `v_2=0` in the remaining equations and using
`AF-BE\ne0` gives exactly

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=
 \{([u],[e_0]):u\in\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_2)\}
 \cup
 \{([e_0],[v]):v\in\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_1)\}.   \tag{22}
\]

On the second ruling write `v=(r,s,0)`.  The coefficient matrix of the
compression of `K` has an `x,z` minor

\[
                         s^2(AF-BE).                    \tag{23}
\]

On the first ruling write `u=(r,0,s)`; its `x,z` minor is `s^2` after
harmless basis choices.  Thus the compression image has dimension one
only at the common point

\[
                              ([u],[v])=([e_0],[e_0]).   \tag{24}
\]

Both pairs in (12) must therefore be (24).  In the notation (4),

\[
                    p=s=e_0,\qquad q=t=e_0.            \tag{24a}
\]

Thus all four outer matrices kill the same genuine coordinate vector
`e_0`.  For every `d\ne0` and `c\ne0`, this makes

\[
 \mathcal L(E_{0d})=Pe_0(Te_d)^{\mathsf T}
                    +Se_d(Qe_0)^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(E_{c0})=Pe_c(Te_0)^{\mathsf T}
                    +Se_0(Qe_c)^{\mathsf T}=0.          \tag{24b}
\]

But in the rank-three case `\mathcal L` is injective on every coordinate
row and column plane.  Equation (24b) is impossible.  Hence types `2a`
and `3a` are empty as well.

### 4.3 Type `3b`

Here

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+Cy+Ez&Bx+Dy+Fz\\x&0&y\\Gz&z&0
 \end{pmatrix},                                       \tag{25}
\]

with

\[
 BCFG(AD-BC)\ne0,\qquad BE=AF.                         \tag{26}
\]

The same six-coefficient elimination gives

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=
 \{([(G,1,0)],[(r,s,0)]):(r,s)\ne(0,0)\}.             \tag{27}
\]

For completeness, choose bases

\[
 (r,s,0)^\perp=\langle(-s,r,0),e_2\rangle,
 \qquad(G,1,0)^\perp=\langle(1,-G,0),e_2\rangle.
\]

The two nonzero rows of the compression coefficient matrix are

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 AGs+r&CGs&EGs\\
 -Bs&-Ds+r&-Fs
 \end{pmatrix}.                                       \tag{28}
\]

If `rs\ne0`, its `x,z` minor is `-F r s`, using `BE=AF`.  If `s=0`,
the `x,y` minor is `r^2`; if `r=0`, it is
`G s^2(BC-AD)`.  Hence every bad pair in (27) has a
two-dimensional compression image.  This contradicts (8).

### 4.4 Type `2b`

The last normal form is

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+Cy+Ez&Dy\\x&0&y\\Fz&z&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad ACDEF\ne0.                                    \tag{29}
\]

Put

\[
                  k=(F,1,0),\qquad \ell=(D,1,0),
                  \qquad W=\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_1).          \tag{30}
\]

The coefficient equations give exactly

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=
       \{([k],[v]):v\in W\}\cup\{([u],[\ell]):u\in W\}.           \tag{31}
\]

For the first ruling, `v=(r,s,0)`, the two nonzero compression rows are

\[
       (AFs+r,CFs,EFs),\qquad(0,-Ds+r,0).              \tag{32}
\]

They span a line only when `r=Ds`, namely `v=ell`; the minor
`EFs(Ds-r)` and the case `s=0` prove this without division.  Dually, on
the second ruling the two relevant rows are

\[
       (Ar+Ds,Cr,Er),\qquad(0,0,Fs-r),                 \tag{33}
\]

and they span a line only when `r=Fs`, namely `u=k`.  Thus (8) forces

\[
                   ([t],[p])=([s],[q])=([k],[\ell]).   \tag{34}
\]

This is crosswise kernel sharing:

\[
                         p=q=\ell,\qquad s=t=k.         \tag{35}
\]

It remains to exclude (35), because it does not itself violate (5).

## 5. Crosswise sharing has only singular physical values

Choose quotient maps `A,B:\mathbb C^3\to\mathbb C^2` with kernels
`\ell,k`.  Equation
(35) gives injective maps `U,V,X,Y:C^2 to C^3` such that

\[
 P=UA,\qquad Q=VA,\qquad S=XB,\qquad T=YB.             \tag{36}
\]

For `Z=AMB^T`, the physical map factors as

\[
                  \Phi(Z)=UZY^{\mathsf T}+XZ^{\mathsf T}V^{\mathsf T}. \tag{37}
\]

At the unique point (34), the compression line is nonzero and rank one.
Let its generator be `h=ab^T`.  Since it comes from `K`,
`Phi(h)=0`.  Both rank-one summands are nonzero, so their column and row
factors are proportional.  Choose quotient bases with `a=b=e_0` and
rescale the four injections to write

\[
 U=[u,a_1],\quad X=[u,b_1],\quad
 Y=[y,c_1],\quad V=[-y,d_1].                           \tag{38}
\]

Transversality (5) says `(a_1,u,b_1)` and `(c_1,y,d_1)` are bases of the
two physical three-spaces.  Relative to these bases, the value of (37) on

\[
              Z=\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\\\beta&\gamma\end{pmatrix}
\]

is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \gamma&\beta&0\\
  \alpha&0&\beta\\
  0&-\alpha&\gamma
 \end{pmatrix}.                                       \tag{39}
\]

Its determinant is identically zero: the two terms are
`alpha beta gamma` and `-alpha beta gamma`.  Therefore every member of
`im Phi`, hence every member of `im\mathcal L`, is singular.  This
contradicts the invertible `H` in (1) and eliminates type `2b`.  Types
`3c,3d,3e,3b,2a,3a,2b` are now all impossible, proving Theorem 1.1.

## 6. Exact audit

[`verify_rank_three_singular_fixed_line_obstruction.py`](../computations/verify_rank_three_singular_fixed_line_obstruction.py)
checks over the rational function field that:

1. the adjugate coefficients in (13)--(15), (20), (25), and (29) are
   exactly the displayed ones;
2. the bad-pair families (22), (27), and (31) annihilate every quadratic
   coefficient;
3. the minors (23), (28), (32), and (33) give precisely the stated
   fixed-line rank filters; and
4. the determinant in (39) vanishes identically.
