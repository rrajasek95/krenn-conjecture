# A squarefree Groebner shortcut for normalized chart 26

Research target only.  This note proves a finite criterion which would make
the normalized chart-26 calculation decisive.  It does not claim that the
criterion passes.

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
 A=\mathbb Q[y_f:f\notin S],\qquad
 I=(\bar H_c:c\text{ mixed}),
\]

be the exact normalized chart-26 ring and mixed ideal.  Homogenize every
\(\bar H_c\) to degree four with a variable \(t\), and call the resulting
homogeneous ideal \(I^h\subset A[t]\).  Order monomials by graded lexicographic
order, with all \(y_f\) preceding \(t\).

The exact normalized enumeration gives the following special fact:

* all 6,558 generators have degree four;
* their leading monomials contain no \(t\);
* those leading monomials are distinct squarefree products of four
  off-support variables.

Write these generators as \(g_c\), and their leading monomials as \(m_c\).

## 2. Only degree-at-most-seven critical pairs are nonautomatic

For two generators, the Buchberger pair has homogeneous degree

\[
                    \deg\operatorname {lcm}(m_c,m_d).
\]

If \(m_c\) and \(m_d\) are disjoint, their gcd is one and Buchberger's
product criterion makes the pair reduce to zero.  Otherwise the two
squarefree degree-four monomials share at least one variable, and hence

\[
            \deg\operatorname {lcm}(m_c,m_d)\leq7.       \tag{1}
\]

Consequently the original 6,558 generators form a Groebner basis if and
only if every overlapping-leading-monomial pair reduces to zero.  This is a
finite homogeneous calculation entirely in degrees five, six, and seven.
Pairs with disjoint leading terms require no matrix calculation.

This criterion is stronger than closure of one chosen degree-seven target
component.  Buchberger reduction must be checked for every orbit of
overlapping leading terms.  Conversely, no degree-eight pair is missing:
every degree-eight pair has coprime leading terms and is covered by the
product criterion.

## 3. Passing the audit makes the ideal radical

Assume every pair in Section 2 reduces to zero.  Then

\[
                \operatorname {in}(I^h)=(m_c:c\text{ mixed}). \tag{2}
\]

The monomial ideal on the right is generated by squarefree monomials, so it
is radical.  The standard initial-ideal lemma now implies that \(I^h\) is
radical: if \(f^r\in I^h\), then
\(\operatorname {in}(f)^r\in\operatorname {in}(I^h)\); radicality of the
initial ideal gives \(\operatorname {in}(f)\in\operatorname {in}(I^h)\),
and subtracting a suitable Groebner-basis multiple lowers the leading term.
Well-ordering and induction give \(f\in I^h\).

To pass to the affine chart, localize at \(t\).  Localization preserves
radicality.  Under the usual isomorphism

\[
 \mathbb Q[y,t,t^{-1}]
   \simeq \mathbb Q[y/t][t,t^{-1}],
\]

the degree-zero contraction of \(I^h[t^{-1}]\) is precisely the
dehomogenized ideal \(I\).  A contraction of a radical ideal is radical, so
\(I\) is radical as well.  This localization argument is important: an
arbitrary quotient of a radical ideal need not remain radical.

## 4. Decisive target dichotomy

Put \(F=\bar H_0\bar H_1\bar H_2\).  If the audit passes, then

\[
                       F\in\sqrt I\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\in I.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

The right side is decided by one ordinary normal-form computation, or by
the homogeneous degree-twelve test for \(F^h\):

* zero remainder gives a finite exponent-one chart certificate and excludes
  every three-pure mixed zero on chart 26;
* nonzero remainder proves \(F\notin\sqrt I\).  Over \(\mathbb C\), the
  Nullstellensatz then gives a mixed common zero with all three pure
  coefficients nonzero, hence proves existence of an eight-site
  counterexample after diagonal output normalization.  The task's stronger
  counterexample deliverable would still require extracting an exact closed
  point and replaying all coefficients.

Thus a successful squarefree Groebner audit eliminates both open-ended
features of the current calculation: the unknown \(t\)-saturation exponent
and the unknown radical exponent.

## 5. Failure is still useful

If an overlapping pair has nonzero remainder, freeze the first such pair,
its exact remainder, and its new leading monomial.  That polynomial is the
first required higher basis cell.  Its \(t\)-exponent records exactly how an
inhomogeneous high-degree cancellation returns to a lower normalized
degree.  Subsequent saturation should then be organized by adjoining these
actual Buchberger cells, rather than by an arbitrary total-degree cap.

Failure of the original basis is not yet failure of the radical shortcut.
One may continue Buchberger completion while tracking only the *minimal*
new leading monomials.  If the completed initial ideal still has squarefree
minimal generators (a new leading monomial may contain \(t\), but at most
to the first power), the argument of Section 3 still proves radicality.
The first indispensable leading monomial with a repeated variable is the
precise combinatorial location where nonreduced structure, and hence a
genuine radical exponent, can enter.
