# Square-zero invariance on the anchored old-source boundary plane

## Outcome

The positive-degree quotient obstruction is invariant on the full
fourteen-parameter old-source plane generated by the admissible cells on
edges 23 and 67.  For every one of the 62 quotient witnesses:

1. the base matrix remains support-rank deficient by exactly one for every
   value of the fourteen parameters; and
2. the selected augmented square is the anchored square times a unipotent
   matrix in a square-zero incidence algebra.

Consequently every selected augmented determinant is identically equal to
its nonzero anchored value on this plane.  This is an exact structural
identity for arbitrary complex weights, not a coefficient grid and not only
a collection of one-cell charts.

The result is still not an arbitrary-old-source theorem.  It proves
invariance on the complete boundary-deformation plane visible at the
anchored source.  It does not prove that the old-source exactness ideal is
generated, even locally, by these boundary directions.

## The fourteen-dimensional plane

The directions are

```text
23;01 23;02 23;10 23;11 23;12 23;20 23;22
67;01 67;02 67;10 67;11 67;20 67;21 67;22
```

The earlier one-cell theorem proves that each direction preserves the three
old complete cuts 2, 3, and 4.  All 91 unit two-direction sums also preserve
those cuts, as does the sum of all fourteen unit directions.  Eighty-nine
direction pairs raise the base-plus-target character rank from 15 to 17 and
therefore have independently torus-normalizable nonzero weights.  Two pairs
have rank 16 and retain a weight ratio; the matrix identity below is valid
for that ratio as well.

Most importantly, the quotient identity itself is proved for every point of
the fourteen-dimensional affine plane, whether or not that point satisfies
the old exactness equations.  It therefore holds automatically on the
intersection with the exactness locus.

## Why only linear and bilinear terms occur

All seven directions in the first group occupy the same physical edge 23,
and all seven in the second group occupy edge 67.  A perfect matching can
use at most one decorated cell on each physical edge.  Hence every selected
matrix has exact bidegree at most $(1,1)$:

\[
 M(x,y)=M_0+
   \sum_i x_iD_i+
   \sum_j y_jE_j+
   \sum_{i,j}x_i y_jF_{ij}.                            \tag{1}
\]

The four literal corners for each pair recover $D_i,E_j,F_{ij}$ exactly.
No untested higher interaction can occur.

Normalize by the nonzero anchored square:

\[
 K_i=M_0^{-1}D_i,\qquad
 L_j=M_0^{-1}E_j,\qquad
 K_{ij}=M_0^{-1}F_{ij}.                                \tag{2}
\]

The checker retains the sparse support of every matrix in (2), including
entries which vanish at the anchor and appear only in $F_{ij}$.

## The square-zero incidence algebra

For each witness, put all arrows $p\to q$ for which one of the normalized
matrices in (2) has a nonzero $(p,q)$ entry.  The full-plane census is

| arrow support | witnesses |
|---|---:|
| no arrows | 7 |
| one arrow | 48 |
| two parallel, noncomposable arrows | 7 |

Thus every arrow graph has longest directed path at most one.  After a
simultaneous permutation of the selected columns, all normalized matrices
lie in one strictly triangular algebra $R_w$ satisfying

\[
                              R_w^2=0.                  \tag{3}
\]

Equation (1) becomes

\[
 M(x,y)=M_0(I+N(x,y)),\qquad N(x,y)\in R_w.             \tag{4}
\]

Since $N^2=0$, the second factor is unipotent and

\[
                      \det M(x,y)=\det M_0\ne0.         \tag{5}
\]

This proves augmented full rank for arbitrary parameter values.

Independently, the union support of every base matrix in (1) has maximum
matching rank exactly one below its row count.  Therefore no specialization
of the fourteen weights can fill the base rank.  Together with (5), this
preserves the quotient rank jump on the entire plane.

## Genuine bilinear interactions

For 88 of the 91 direction pairs, every bilinear normalized term is the
ordinary product of the two one-cell shears.  Three pairs create a new
bilinear arrow:

| direction pair | affected witnesses |
|---|---:|
| $23;01,\ 67;11$ | 4 |
| $23;11,\ 67;11$ | 12 |
| $23;11,\ 67;21$ | 10 |

These 26 interactions show why simply multiplying the one-cell identities
is insufficient.  Nevertheless, every new arrow lies in the same
square-zero incidence algebra.

The first interaction occurs on support

```text
((0,8,1,0), (0,8,1,2), (2,9,0,0), (5,9,1,0), (5,9,1,2))
```

in `aug1`.  Both one-cell shears vanish, while the bilinear term adds the
selected old-hole column `(3,1)` to the residual column with coefficient
one.  This is a literal elementary column operation.

## Proof-level consequence and sharp gap

The available theorem is now:

> On the anchored cut-2 quotient, every admissible boundary deformation on
> edges 23 and 67 acts on each positive-degree Fitting frame through a
> square-zero incidence algebra.  Hence the base/augmented rank obstruction
> is invariant on the full fourteen-parameter plane.

This identifies a plausible general mechanism: exact boundary deformations
should act unipotently on a source-provenance filtration.  To obtain an
arbitrary-old-source theorem, one must still prove that arbitrary admissible
old-source deformation reduces to such a nilpotent boundary action modulo
the old cylinder exactness ideal, or exhibit the first deformation whose
normalized arrows form a cycle.  Occupied-support moduli and directions on
other physical edges are not covered here.
