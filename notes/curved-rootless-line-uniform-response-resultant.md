# A rootless physical cap line has a uniform response-power resultant

## 1. Outcome

Let an exact source be capped at a physical pair, leaving \(2h\) sites,
where \(h\geq3\).  For a pair covector \(K\), write

\[
 s=s(K),\qquad r=r(K),\qquad F=sq+r,
\]

where \(q\) is the internal quadratic.  The complete pair row and the
denominator-cleared clean error are

\[
 s q^{[h]}+r q^{[h-1]}=T(K),\qquad
 {\cal E}(K)=F^{[h]}-s^{h-1}T(K).                    \tag{1}
\]

Eliminating the target in (1) gives the uniform identity

\[
 \boxed{
 {\cal E}(K)=\sum_{j=2}^{h}s^{h-j}q^{[h-j]}r^{[j]}.} \tag{2}
\]

Thus the exact clean error has no constant or linear response term.  At a
scalar-zero cap it is exactly the top matching power \(r^{[h]}\).

Now restrict to a physical projective line \(K(u,v)\).  Its clean error is
a vector-valued binary form of degree \(h\).  It has no projective zero if
and only if the multiplication map generated by its scalar coordinates,

\[
 L_{\cal E}\otimes\operatorname {Sym}^{h-1}\mathbb C^2
       \longrightarrow\operatorname {Sym}^{2h-1}\mathbb C^2, \tag{3}
\]

has rank \(2h\).  Equivalently, two scalar combinations of the physical
clean rows have nonzero degree-\(h\) resultant.  Hence the all-order
no-clean-root branch always has a finite \(2h\)-row Sylvester certificate;
it is not an arbitrary collection of tensor coordinates.

For the canonical curvature line \(K(u,v)=uE_{ab}+vI\), let

\[
 \alpha=A_{pq}(a,b)\ne0,\qquad \tau=\operatorname {tr}A_{pq}. \tag{4}
\]

Its scalar-zero point is \(K_*=\tau E_{ab}-\alpha I\).  If the line is
rootless, then

\[
 r_*q^{[h-1]}=T(K_*),\qquad r_*^{[h]}\ne0.             \tag{5}
\]

When \(a\ne b\), this specializes to the full ternary packet

\[
 \boxed{r_*q^{[h-1]}=-\alpha\Delta_{2h,3},\qquad
        r_*^{[h]}\ne0,\qquad\det K_*=(-\alpha)^3\ne0.} \tag{6}
\]

When \(a=b\), the target is ternary and \(K_*\) is invertible unless the
single trace equation \(\tau=\alpha\) holds; on that hyperplane (5) is a
binary packet.  Equations (2)--(6) do not prove that a clean root exists.
They identify two uniform proof targets: exclude the nonnilpotent
direct-free common-power packet, or force every physical Sylvester
certificate to lose rank.

The first-boundary refinement, including the literal four-cut rows and
good-star selector alternatives, is recorded in
[the cubic Macaulay packet theorem](curved-no-root-macaulay-and-scalar-zero-packet.md).

## 2. Elimination of the target row

All products are in the site-square-zero commutative tensor algebra on the
residual sites.  Divided-power polarization gives

\[
 (sq+r)^{[h]}
   =\sum_{j=0}^{h}s^{h-j}q^{[h-j]}r^{[j]}.             \tag{7}
\]

Substitute the first equation of (1) into the second:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\cal E}(K)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{h}s^{h-j}q^{[h-j]}r^{[j]}
     -s^h q^{[h]}-s^{h-1}r q^{[h-1]}.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{8}
\]

The last two terms cancel exactly the \(j=0\) and \(j=1\) summands.  This
proves (2), without division by \(s\) and without choosing a representative
modulo a Hessian annihilator.

In particular,

\[
 s(K_*)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 {\cal E}(K_*)=r(K_*)^{[h]}.                            \tag{9}
\]

For \(h=3\), (2) becomes

\[
 {\cal E}=sqr^{[2]}+r^{[3]}
          ={r^2(r+3sq)\over6},                         \tag{10}
\]

which is the factorization used by the first-boundary theorem.

## 3. The uniform Sylvester criterion

The following elementary lemma applies to the physical error without any
assumption on the dimension of its tensor target.

**Lemma 3.1.**  Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex vector space and

\[
 E(u,v)\in V\otimes\operatorname {Sym}^{h}\mathbb C^2,
 \qquad h\geq1.                                        \tag{11}
\]

Let \(L_E\subseteq\operatorname {Sym}^{h}\mathbb C^2\) be the span of its
scalar coordinate forms, with the gcd of the identically zero family
defined to be \(0\).  The following are equivalent.

1. \(E(u,v)\ne0\) for every \([u:v]\in\mathbb P^1\).
2. The nonzero scalar coordinate forms have gcd one.
3. Multiplication is surjective in

   \[
   \mu_E:L_E\otimes\operatorname {Sym}^{h-1}\mathbb C^2
       \longrightarrow\operatorname {Sym}^{2h-1}\mathbb C^2.
                                                               \tag{12}
   \]
4. Two forms \(f,g\in L_E\) have nonzero classical resultant.

**Proof.**  A positive-degree homogeneous binary gcd has a projective
linear factor over \(\mathbb C\), proving the equivalence of 1 and 2.  If
all coordinate forms share a linear factor \(\ell\), the image of (12) is
contained in the codimension-one space
\(\ell\operatorname {Sym}^{2h-2}\mathbb C^2\), so (12) is not
surjective.

Conversely assume gcd one and choose a nonzero \(f\in L_E\).  At each of
the finitely many roots of \(f\), some member of \(L_E\) is nonzero.  A
linear combination avoiding the corresponding finite union of hyperplanes
gives \(g\in L_E\) with \(\gcd(f,g)=1\).  If

\[
       fA=gB,\qquad \deg A=\deg B=h-1,                 \tag{13}
\]

coprimality forces \(f\mid B\); since \(\deg B<h\), one has \(A=B=0\).
Therefore the two \(h\)-dimensional subspaces

\[
 f\operatorname {Sym}^{h-1}\mathbb C^2,qquad
 g\operatorname {Sym}^{h-1}\mathbb C^2               \tag{14}
\]

intersect trivially and span the \(2h\)-dimensional target of (12).  The
matrix of their direct-sum map is the Sylvester matrix of \(f,g\), whose
determinant is their resultant.  This proves 2--4.  \(\square\)

For one scalar coordinate

\[
 e(u,v)=\sum_{j=0}^{h}c_j u^{h-j}v^j,                 \tag{15}
\]

its contribution to (12) is the usual \(2h\)-by-\(h\) Toeplitz block.
Consequently a rootless physical error has a nonzero \(2h\)-by-\(2h\)
minor using \(2h\) shifted scalar-coordinate columns.  This is a
certificate of rootlessness, not yet a contradiction; the missing positive
lemma must force those minors to vanish from common-edge provenance or
good-star incidence.

## 4. The scalar-zero response packet

On the canonical line, direct contraction gives

\[
 s(K(u,v))=\alpha u+\tau v.                            \tag{16}
\]

Hence \(K_*=\tau E_{ab}-\alpha I\) is its unique scalar-zero point.  The
physical row in (1) and (9) give

\[
 r_*q^{[h-1]}=T(K_*),\qquad
 {\cal E}(K_*)=r_*^{[h]}.                              \tag{17}
\]

If the line is rootless, the second tensor in (17) is nonzero.  For
\(a\ne b\), the diagonal of \(K_*\) is \(-\alpha I\), proving (6).  For
\(a=b\), its diagonal is

\[
 K_{*,aa}=\tau-\alpha,\qquad
 K_{*,cc}=-\alpha\quad(c\ne a),                       \tag{18}
\]

which gives the asserted ternary/binary split and determinant criterion.

The packet (6) retains arbitrary complex cancellation, endpoint order,
and parallel decorated sources through aggregation.  Its response has the
additional physical factorization

\[
 r_*=\sum_{i,j}(K_*)_{ij}p_i s_j,                     \tag{19}
\]

and, off diagonal, \(K_*\) is invertible.  A promising independent
formulation of the remaining theorem is therefore:

> An invertibly paired response of two injective physical endpoint stars
> cannot satisfy (6) together with the nine original pair rows.

This is a tangent/common-power problem for two quadratics \(q,r_*\), and a
rank-\(2h\) resultant problem for the whole line.  Either formulation uses
the unconditional curvature witness and avoids reopening the eliminated
flat graph cases.
