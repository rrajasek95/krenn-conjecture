## 1. A strict rate gap for a complete cancellation family

Partition six sites into triples $A,B$. Let the ground entries be
$a$ on every edge within $A$, $b$ on every edge within $B$, and
$c$ on every cross edge. All other colored source entries are unrestricted.
Suppose the output is exactly $\lambda W_6\ne0$.
With total squared colored source norm $S$, use
$R=6|\lambda|^2/S^3$.

**Theorem.** Every such source satisfies

$$
\boxed{
R<\frac{16}{1053}
=\frac{80}{81}\,\frac1{65}.
}
\tag{1}
$$

The theorem also allows relabeling and arbitrary unit-modulus site phases.
Thus this entire family, including its complete-support ground
cancellations, cannot improve the known rate $1/65$.
The bound in (1) is not asserted to be the attained optimum of the family.

Write $C_{ij}=\operatorname{haf}D[V\setminus\{i,j\}]$ for the
ground cofactors. The scalar response relaxation has an exact
optimum in this family:

$$
\boxed{
F(D):=\left(\sum_e|D_e|^2\right)^2
\sum_i\frac1{\sum_{j\ne i}|C_{ij}|^2}
\ \ge\ \frac{117}{2}
=\frac{81}{80}\,\frac{520}{9}.
}
\tag{2}
$$

Throughout this note $F(D)=s^2\beta(D)$, with
$s=\sum_e|D_e|^2$ and $\beta(D)=\sum_i r_i^{-2}$.
Here and below all response rows are required nonzero.
Equality in the cancellation branch occurs when

$$
ab=-\frac23c^2,\qquad |a|=|b|=\sqrt{\frac23}|c|.
\tag{3}
$$

After scaling and site phases, take the real representative

$$
D_*:\qquad a=k,\quad b=-k,\quad c=1,\qquad k=\frac{\sqrt6}{3}.
\tag{4}
$$

**Local-minimum theorem.** On the full complex manifold
$\operatorname{haf}D=0$, the family generated by $D_*$ under
scaling and site phases is a strict local minimum of $F$ transverse
to those symmetries. Its value is $117/2$.
There are no support or symmetry restrictions on the perturbation.
After fixing ground norm, the excess controls squared distance to
the phase orbit in a sufficiently small neighborhood.

Consequently (1) also holds for arbitrary colored exact-W sources
whose ground part is sufficiently close, after normalization, to this
family. No explicit neighborhood radius is provided.

This is a local minimum of the scalar relaxation, not a demonstrated
local optimizer among exact colored W sources.

## 2. Exact ground responses and the sharp scalar family bound

Counting matchings gives

$$
\operatorname{haf}D=9abc+6c^3=3c(3ab+2c^2).
\tag{5}
$$

The ground amplitude must vanish. If $c=0$, the two triples are
disconnected. Nonzero response at every site requires $ab\ne0$.
Then the response bound gives $R\le1/81$, already strictly below (1):
indeed $s=3(|a|^2+|b|^2)$ and
$\beta=2/(|a|^2|b|^2)$, so $F\ge72$.

If $c\ne0$, rescale to $c=1$. Equation (5) gives $ab=-2/3$.
The cofactors are

$$
C_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
3b,&i,j\in A,\\
3a,&i,j\in B,\\
ab+2=4/3,&i\in A,\ j\in B.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
$$

Put $u=|a|^2$, $v=|b|^2$, and $z=u+v$.
Then $uv=4/9$ and $z\ge4/3$.
The ground strength and row norms are

$$
s=3(z+3),\qquad
r_A^2=18v+\frac{16}{3},\qquad
r_B^2=18u+\frac{16}{3}.
$$

Each row value occurs three times. Direct simplification yields

$$
F(z)=\frac{81}{8}
\frac{(z+3)^2(27z+16)}{54z+97}.
\tag{7}
$$

Its logarithmic derivative is

$$
\frac{2}{z+3}+\frac{27}{27z+16}-\frac{54}{54z+97}
=\frac{2}{z+3}+
\frac{1755}{(27z+16)(54z+97)}>0.
$$

Thus the minimum is at $z=4/3$, exactly $F=117/2$.
Equality forces $u=v=2/3$, proving (2)--(3).
These sources are equivalent to (4) by scaling and site phases.

The [unrestricted response inequality](w-state-unrestricted-response-bound-2026-09-27.md)
is

$$
R\le\frac{8}{9F(D)}.
\tag{8}
$$

It initially gives the non-strict version of (1).
Section 5 excludes equality even at (4), proving the strict assertion.

At $D_*$ the exact data are

$$
s=13,\quad T=\sum_e|C_e|^2=52,\quad
r_i^2=\frac{52}{3}\ \text{for all }i,\quad
\beta=\frac9{26},\quad F=\frac{117}{2}.
\tag{9}
$$

The cofactor efficiency from the universal bound is
$\eta=T/((9/20)s^2)=80/117$, while the row-imbalance loss is zero.
The sufficient threshold for beating $1/65$ at zero imbalance is
$\eta>9/13=81/117$. This family falls short by exactly $1/117$.

## 3. The full constrained quadratic form

Let $D(t)$ be any smooth path in $\operatorname{haf}D=0$ with
$D(0)=D_*$ and first derivative $d$.
Decompose its entries under the two triples:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{ij}&=\alpha+u_i+u_j &&(i,j\in A),\\
d_{ij}&=\beta+v_i+v_j &&(i,j\in B),\\
d_{ij}&=\gamma+p_i+q_j+Z_{ij} &&(i\in A,\ j\in B).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

All four site vectors $u,v,p,q$ have zero sum.
The $3\times3$ cross matrix $Z$ has zero row and column sums.
The letters $\alpha,\beta$ in this decomposition are perturbation
parameters, unrelated to the reciprocal response sum in section 2.
This decomposition is unique: use within-triple averages and row
sums, then cross-block row and column averages.

The tangent condition is

$$
-3\sqrt6\,\alpha+3\sqrt6\,\beta+12\gamma=0,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{\sqrt6}{4}(\alpha-\beta).
\tag{11}
$$

Set $a_+=(\alpha+\beta)/2$.
Then

$$
F(D(t))=\frac{117}{2}
+t^2\bigl(Q_{\mathbb R}+Q_{\mathbb I}\bigr)+O(t^3),
\tag{12}
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathbb R}={}&\frac{4023}{26}(\operatorname{Re}a_+)^2\\
&+\frac1{52}\left(
423\|\operatorname{Re}u\|^2+
450\sqrt6\,\langle\operatorname{Re}u,\operatorname{Re}p\rangle+
1791\|\operatorname{Re}p\|^2\right)\\
&+\frac1{52}\left(
423\|\operatorname{Re}v\|^2-
450\sqrt6\,\langle\operatorname{Re}v,\operatorname{Re}q\rangle+
1791\|\operatorname{Re}q\|^2\right)
+\frac{15}{2}\|\operatorname{Re}Z\|_F^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

and

$$
Q_{\mathbb I}=
\frac92\left\|\operatorname{Im}p-\frac{\sqrt6}{2}\operatorname{Im}u\right\|^2
+\frac92\left\|\operatorname{Im}q+\frac{\sqrt6}{2}\operatorname{Im}v\right\|^2
+\frac{33}{4}\|\operatorname{Im}Z\|_F^2.
\tag{14}
$$

The real two-by-two coefficient blocks have positive leading
diagonal $423/52$ and determinant $34911/208>0$.
Thus (13) is positive except in the real scaling direction.
The zeros of (14), together with the two imaginary constant
directions, are exactly the six site-phase directions.
The total real tangent dimension is 28, and the nullspace has
dimension seven: one scale direction and six phases.
All other 21 directions have positive curvature.

### Derivation

Here are finite matrix formulas that also permit an independent replay.
Index vectors by the fifteen unordered edges. At $D_*$ let $D,C$
denote its real edge and cofactor vectors, and let $J(x)$ be the
symmetric matrix with

$$
J(x)_{ef}=
\begin{cases}
x_g,&e,f\text{ disjoint and }g=V\setminus(e\cup f),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

This is the Hessian of the six-site hafnian at $x$.
Let $L_{ie}=C_e$ when $i\in e$, and zero otherwise, and put
$M=LJ(D)$. The exact identities

$$
D^{\mathsf T}C=0,\qquad J(D)D=2C,\qquad
J(D)C=8D+C
\tag{15}
$$

give
$\nabla_{\mathbb R}F(D_*)=-\frac94C$.
In particular $F$ is stationary on the zero-hafnian manifold.

For a real tangent vector $x$ with $C^{\mathsf T}x=0$, the real
and imaginary constrained quadratic forms are respectively
$x^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathbb R}x$ and $x^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathbb I}x$, where

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathbb R}={}&9I-\frac{54}{13}DD^{\mathsf T}
+\frac{27}{208}M^{\mathsf T}M
-\frac98J(D)^2-\frac98J(C)+\frac98J(D),\\
A_{\mathbb I}={}&9I-\frac98J(D)^2
+\frac98J(C)-\frac98J(D).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

To obtain these formulas, expand the ground norm, each cofactor,
and each reciprocal row norm through second order.
For a real first derivative $x$, a second-order correction $w$
enforcing zero hafnian satisfies
$C^{\mathsf T}w=-\frac12x^{\mathsf T}J(D)x$.
Its contribution through the gradient of $F$ is
$+\frac98x^{\mathsf T}J(D)x$.
For an imaginary first derivative $ix$ the signs reverse.
These are the last terms in (16).
The other terms are the direct norm and reciprocal expansions.

Substitution of (10)--(11) into (16) gives (13)--(14).
Terms between different zero-sum components vanish; the replay
also checks every quadratic coefficient on a 14-dimensional real
tangent basis for each of the two forms.
There are no real-imaginary mixed terms, because the source
representative and polynomial coefficients are real and the
constrained objective is invariant under complex conjugation.

## 4. From positive curvature to the local exclusion

The ground constraint is smooth at $D_*$, since $C\ne0$, and all
row norms are positive. Fix the ground norm and choose a local
slice transverse to the six independent site phases.
The implicit function theorem supplies this slice.
Equations (13)--(14) are positive definite on its tangent space.
Taylor's theorem therefore gives $F\ge117/2$, with equality
locally only on the scale-and-phase family.
At fixed ground norm, the excess bounds squared distance to that
family by a positive constant.

This proves the local-minimum theorem. Inserting the result in (8)
gives the stated local rate bound for every colored completion,
not just completions close to a particular colored design.

## 5. Why equality in the rate bound is impossible

Equality in (8) requires every single-excitation row to attain its
Cauchy--Schwarz minimum, and all source strength in other colored
entries to vanish. At the representative (4), the mixed entries
would therefore have to be

$$
X_{ij}=\lambda\,\frac{C_{ij}}{r_i^2}
=\frac{3\lambda}{52}C_{ij}
$$

for an excitation at endpoint $i$ and a ground color at $j$.
All two-excitation edge entries would be zero.
The cofactors are real here, and all rows have the same norm.

Evaluate the output with local test vector $a+zb$ at every site.
The scalar edge matrix becomes $D_*+(3\lambda/26)zC$.
Direct substitution into (5) gives the exact identity

$$
\operatorname{haf}(D_*+sC)
=52s+26s^2-\frac{520}{9}s^3.
\tag{17}
$$

Its linear output is correctly $6\lambda z$.
But the coefficient of $z^2$, the sum of all two-excitation output
coefficients, is

$$
\frac9{26}\lambda^2\ne0.
$$

Thus the minimum-norm single-excitation completion is not an exact
W source. The conclusion is unchanged by scale or site phases.
Equality in either the global family bound or the local bound
is therefore impossible, proving (1).

Exact W completions do exist at $D_*$. Choose site $0$ as the only
site incident to excitation-carrying edges. On edge $0j$, set the
entry exciting $j$ to $\lambda/C_{0j}$ and the entry exciting $0$
to $\lambda C_{0j}/r_0^2$. All other excitation entries are zero.
Every matching uses exactly one edge incident to $0$, so there
cannot be two excitations. The single-excitation coefficients are
all $\lambda$, and the ground output cancels. Their source cost
strictly exceeds the scalar relaxation.

## 6. What this says about the remaining proof

The known one-root ground family has scalar cost $520/9$.
The new balanced family has cost $117/2$, only $1.25\%$ higher,
and is another strict local minimum in the full complex ground space.
Consequently a proof that every scalar local minimum is the one-root
family would be false. Local descent can settle near the balanced
family without reaching the lower one-root value.

Both local minima are compatible with the proposed global optimum:
the new one is strictly worse and cannot attain its own scalar
rate bound as an exact W source. The result excludes another
complete-support cancellation family and its neighborhood.
It does not classify all scalar critical points or prove the
global harmonic-response inequality.
