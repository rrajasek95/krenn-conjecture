# Finite reachable--observable criterion for the P5 bend recurrence

## 1. The correct transfer object

Let `R` be the localized generic-`L` coefficient ring, let `V` be a finite
free `R`-module, and let

\[
 A:V\to V,\qquad B:R\to V,\qquad C:V\to R                 \tag{1}
\]

be a linear input--state--output system.  Its Markov coefficients are

\[
                         m_k=CA^kB.                         \tag{2}
\]

The raw Schur-coordinate state can have many propagation poles which never
reach the selected compatibility row.  Therefore one should not try to
conjugate an arbitrarily selected state block to the desired three-state
companion matrix.  The intrinsic state space is the reachable--observable
quotient

\[
 {\cal R}/({\cal R}\cap{\cal N}),\qquad
 {\cal R}=\sum_{k\ge0}\operatorname {im}A^kB,qquad
 {\cal N}=\bigcap_{k\ge0}\ker CA^k.                       \tag{3}
\]

Poles on \({\cal R}\cap{\cal N}\) cancel from the transfer function.  This is exactly the
mechanism permitted by the P5 counterguard: the visible raw cascades have
characteristic factors involving `z10,z37,z40`, not the observed
`z0,z30,z52` recurrence.

## 2. Finite recurrence certificate

Put

\[
 p(X)=X^3+e_1X^2+e_2X+e_3,
 \quad e_i=e_i(z_0,z_{30},z_{52}).                         \tag{4}
\]

The following is an exact all-order certificate.

> **Lemma.**  Suppose there is an `A`-invariant submodule \(K\) with
> \(K\subseteq\ker C\) and
> \[
>                              p(A)B\in K.                  \tag{5}
> \]
> Then
> \[
>       m_{k+3}+e_1m_{k+2}+e_2m_{k+1}+e_3m_k=0             \tag{6}
> \]
> for every `k>=0`.

Indeed, applying \(CA^k\) to (5) gives (6), since
\(A^kK\subseteq K\subseteq\ker C\).  This uses one finite
invariant-submodule identity, not an induction
over bend order.

Equivalently, it is enough to construct a quotient map

\[
 P:V\to R^3,qquad PA=TP,qquad C=LP,qquad PB=b,           \tag{7}
\]

for some row \(L:R^3\to R\) and column \(b:R\to R^3\), where

\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}-e_1&-e_2&-e_3\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]

Then `ker P` is the required invisible invariant module and
Cayley--Hamilton for `T` proves (6).  The load-bearing difference from a
naive block intertwiner is that `P` is allowed to use every Schur state and
to kill pole-cancelling states.

There is also a purely Krylov version.  Compute

\[
 w=p(A)B.                                                   \tag{9}
\]

If the submodule generated by `w,Aw,A^2w,...` stabilizes at a finite list
`w,...,A^dw`, is `A`-closed, and every listed vector lies in `ker C`, then
that submodule is a checked `K`.  Sparse exact column reduction can therefore
certify the recurrence without forming a symbolic determinant or a global
Groebner basis.

## 3. P5 consequence and scope

The checked equations

```text
W4 = r4+e1*r3+e2*t+e3*s,
W5 = r5+e1*r4+e2*r3+e3*t
```

are the first two observed coefficients of (6).  The finite next task is to
extract `(A,B,C)` from the linearized 207-row Schur solve and verify (5),
(7), or (9).  A positive certificate proves the three-step bend recurrence
at every later order and yields the rational series

\[
 R(T)={N_{\le3}(T)\over
              (1+z_0T)(1+z_{30}T)(1+z_{52}T)}.             \tag{10}
\]

Because the source map has bounded degree, the same rational substitution
can then be checked directly in the finite Rees equations after clearing a
bounded denominator.  If it parametrizes the whole unit-Jacobian mixed
branch, evaluating `H0,H1` on (10) converts their all-order membership into
two finite numerator identities.

This note is a criterion, not a verified P5 transfer theorem.  In
particular, two matching coefficients do not establish the invariant
submodule (5), and failure of a hand-selected raw state block says nothing
about the reachable--observable quotient (3).
