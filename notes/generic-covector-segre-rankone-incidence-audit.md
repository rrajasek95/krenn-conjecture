# Generic-covector Segre audit: valid, but already superseded

## Outcome

The proposed generic-covector argument is valid over \(\mathbb C\), after
two repairs:

1. quotient **simultaneously** at every active route, not at one site; and
2. use the identity at zero or cofactor-inactive routes, so that a zero block
   cannot be reported as a coordinate-line witness.

In fact the argument proves the colorwise conclusion: for every site \(v\)
and every target color \(a\), there is a distinct active neighbor \(u_a\)
such that, in endpoint order,

\[
 A_{v u_a}=c_{v,a}\otimes e_a^{(u_a)},\qquad
 c_{v,a}\ne0,\qquad
 H_{B\setminus\{v,u_a\}}(A)\ne0.                 \tag{1}
\]

Thus every vertex has at least three distinct active incident rank-one
aggregate blocks.  This is not a new theorem.  It is strictly contained in
the already committed [one-slice covering theorem](slice-cover.md), which
proves (1) without genericity, without invoking a Segre-line
classification, and over every field.

## 1. Exact simultaneous quotient

Let \(B\) have even size \(N\ge4\), fix \(v\in B\), and contract the
matching tensor at \(v\) by \(\alpha\in V_v^*\).  With

\[
 C_u=H_{B\setminus\{v,u\}}(A),\qquad
 p_u(\alpha)=(\alpha\otimes\operatorname{id})A_{vu}\in V_u,
\]

the partner expansion, with tensor slots restored to physical order, is

\[
 \sum_{u\ne v}p_u(\alpha)^{(u)}\otimes C_u
   =\sum_{a=0}^2\alpha(e_a)e_a^{\otimes(B\setminus\{v\})}.   \tag{2}
\]

Call \(u\) an active route when \(C_u\ne0\) and the map
\(p_u:V_v^*\to V_u\) is nonzero.  Choose \(\alpha\) in the coordinate
torus and outside the finitely many kernels of the active maps.  For an
active route let

\[
 q_u:V_u\longrightarrow V_u/\langle p_u(\alpha)\rangle;
\]

at every other site use the identity.  Applying \(\bigotimes_{u\ne v}q_u\)
to (2) kills the whole left side: the summand indexed by an active \(u\)
dies in its own factor, while every omitted route was already zero.  Hence

\[
       \sum_{a=0}^2\alpha(e_a)T_a=0,
 \qquad
       T_a=\bigotimes_{u\ne v}q_u(e_a).                    \tag{3}
\]

Every quotient factor has dimension two and every identity factor has
dimension three.  At every site the three vectors \(q_u(e_0),q_u(e_1),
q_u(e_2)\) span that factor.  In particular they never all lie on one
line.  This also records the zero-route guard: quotienting by the zero
vector is neither needed nor allowed to create a witness.

## 2. The three-term Segre step

All three tensors in (3) vanish.

First suppose all are nonzero.  Three linearly dependent decomposable
tensors either are all proportional, or give three distinct points on a
projective line in the Segre variety.  In the latter case the line is
contained in the Segre variety (its ideal is generated by quadrics), so all
factors are shared except possibly one.  Since \(N-1\ge2\), there is a
shared site.  At that site the three coordinate images lie on one line,
contrary to their spanning property.  The all-proportional case gives the
same contradiction at every site.

If exactly one \(T_a\) vanishes, the other two are proportional.  At a site
where \(q_u(e_a)=0\), the other two coordinate images span the two-dimensional
quotient and therefore are not proportional, again a contradiction.  If
exactly two vanish, (3) leaves one nonzero term.  Consequently

\[
                              T_0=T_1=T_2=0.               \tag{4}
\]

For each fixed \(a\), (4) gives an active \(u\) with
\(q_u(e_a)=0\), equivalently

\[
                         p_u(\alpha)\in\mathbb C^*e_a.     \tag{5}
\]

The committed slice-cover proof establishes the same three-term conclusion
by explicit evaluations; see `slice-cover.md`, lines 46--145.  Its finite
checker exhausts the step over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\).

## 3. Density and endpoint order

Fix \(a\).  As generic \(\alpha\) varies, (5) covers the irreducible open
torus by finitely many locally closed sets

\[
 S_{u,a}=\{\alpha:C_u\ne0,\ p_u(\alpha)\in\mathbb C^*e_a\}. \tag{6}
\]

If no active map had image in \(\mathbb Ce_a\), every \(S_{u,a}\) would be
contained in a proper projective linear subspace, and their finite union
could not cover an open subset of \(\mathbb P^2\).  Thus one active nonzero
map has image \(\mathbb Ce_a\), proving (1).  The witnesses for distinct
colors use distinct neighbors, since one nonzero linear map cannot have
image in two different coordinate lines.

Here \(A_{vu}\in V_v\otimes V_u\), and contraction is in its first factor.
Thus image \(\mathbb Ce_a^{(u)}\) means literally
\(A_{vu}=c\otimes e_a^{(u)}\).  If the stored endpoint order is reversed,
the displayed matrix is transposed.  The conclusion concerns the aggregate
block after summing parallel decorated edges; it does not select an
individual parallel source edge.

## 4. Why functional cycles do not yet give clean descent

Choosing one witness at each vertex gives a functional digraph and hence a
directed cycle.  The stronger colorwise theorem already gives an underlying
active rank-one graph of minimum degree at least three.  Neither statement
selects a clean pair:

* the uncontrolled factor \(c_{v,a}\) at the tail need not be a coordinate
  vector, and the same block need not be selected in the reverse direction;
* activity \(C_u\ne0\) does not say that either pair-deleted endpoint star is
  injective;
* the essential-subspace bound permits three essential neighbors.  In its
  equality case the local support is exactly three rank-one coordinate
  lines, so all three available rank-one arcs can be bad at that endpoint;
  and
* even a tensor-active coordinate anchor does not force a clean cap: the
  exact covector obstruction in `pair-covector-selection-obstruction.md`
  retains a nonzero higher cumulant for every nondegenerate cap covector.

Abstractly, a cubic graph with the three incident mode-supports labelled by
the three coordinate lines realizes all local rank-one and essential-star
conclusions while every rank-one edge is pair-bad.  This is a counterguard
to a deduction from the two local lemmas alone, not an exact Krenn source.
The full matching equations exclude additional cubic configurations, but no
committed theorem turns the remaining rank-one cycle into a doubly injective
pair with vanishing higher cumulants.

The sharp global frontier is therefore unchanged: one must couple the
rank-one incidence graph to the independently forced cancellation cycle in
the degree-at-least-four core, or find a new nonlinear shared-edge argument.
