### 1.1 Signed-character machinery → Zaslavsky signed-graph theory

**What we have.**  Gauge-invariant exponent lattices, odd-holonomy
`1 = -1` certificates, alternating-cycle characters, and the D1/chart-26
CEGAR dichotomy pattern (odd signed holonomy / one-class residual /
visible-cell atom), built ad hoc across
[`oo-binomial-scc-holonomy-cokernel.md`](oo-binomial-scc-holonomy-cokernel.md)
and the orbit-4 character-graph notes.

**Named home.**  The invariant lattice of a weighting on a connected
non-bipartite graph under the vertex gauge `w_uv -> b_u b_v w_uv` is the
kernel of the **unsigned** incidence map — Zaslavsky's even-cycle (frame)
matroid lattice — of rank `|E| - |V|`, generated by even cycles **plus
odd-cycle handcuffs**; it is *not* the cycle space (rank `|E| - |V| + 1`)
**[verified: elementary; ranks recomputed in §2]**.  The handcuffs are the
specifically non-bipartite generators, and they are exactly where the
`1 = -1` mechanism lives.  The character formalism is
Eisenbud–Sturmfels partial characters (Duke Math. J. 84 (1996))
**[verified]** with mesoprimary decomposition (Kahle–Miller,
arXiv:1107.4699) **[provisional]**; the published model family exhibiting
this program's exact phenomena — odd walks ↔ minimal primes, char-2
collapse, no squarefree Gröbner basis — is **parity binomial edge
ideals** (Kahle–Sarmiento–Windisch, arXiv:1503.00584) **[provisional]**.

**What it buys.**  (i) The CEGAR dichotomy becomes a conjecture *inside*
an established theory rather than an empirical pattern.  (ii) The global
even/odd split of the 404 mixed anchor factors (396 even-complement
factors vs the unique odd `(5,3)` chart 26; see the
[2026-08-09 roadmap](2026-08-09-resolution-roadmap.md) §1) is organized
by this lattice: chart 26 is the stratum where the odd part is forced.
(iii) Audit hygiene: any certificate that enumerated its invariant
lattice by ordinary cycle space would carry a phantom dimension.  A
first-pass audit found the repo already correct — the checker for
`oo-binomial-scc-holonomy-cokernel.md` verifies the signless-incidence
rank dichotomy (`n-1` iff bipartite) over all 27,475 connected graphs on
at most six vertices — so this is a naming upgrade, not a repair.

### 1.2 The signed Morse contraction → algebraic discrete Morse theory via homological perturbation

**What we have.**  The
[augmented HPL lemma](augmented-hpl-terminal-bockstein-lemma.md)
(`a_H = a(1+h\delta)^{-1}i`, `D_2 = -p\delta h\delta i`), the
Schur–Bockstein survival criterion
([`n8-filtered-macaulay-bockstein-schur-criterion.md`](n8-filtered-macaulay-bockstein-schur-criterion.md)),
and [path-forest straightening](hafnian-path-forest-straightening.md)
terminating at alternating Hamilton paths whose endpoints are the
candidate clean pair.

**Named home.**  The HPL lemma *is* the Brown–Gugenheim homological
perturbation lemma, and Sköldberg proved that algebraic discrete Morse
theory is a consequence of it, with **explicit** contracting-homotopy
formulas (arXiv:1311.5803; also arXiv:2404.10165) **[provisional]**.  The
Schur–Bockstein criterion is the `d_2` of a two-step filtration.

**What it buys.**  The explicit-homotopy form is the only known route by
which a Morse contraction yields an actual cofactor certificate (push a
cycle to the Morse complex, solve, pull back an explicit boundary) —
i.e. it converts the roadmap's "signed source-labelled Morse
contraction" from an architecture into a computation scheme.  What
remains genuinely new is the **source-constrained** version: homotopies
restricted to source-provenant maps, with the zero-indeterminacy clauses
as vanishing of one relative Ext^1.  Verification confirmed this does
not exist in the literature ("signed acyclic matchings" is not a
concept; ADMT has never been used for ideal membership), so building it
would be original — but on the named scaffold above, together with §1.1
supplying the sign local system.

### 1.3 Hafnian coefficients → genus-2 Kasteleyn (16-Pfaffian expansion)

**What we have.**  Every coefficient handled as a 105-term hafnian; the
Laplace star-minor transport identity, Bianchi squares, and the chart-26
straightening frontier
([`n8-chart26-weighted-degree6-census.md`](n8-chart26-weighted-degree6-census.md))
fought in raw coordinates.

**Named home.**  General (non-bipartite) Kasteleyn theory on surfaces:
the matching generating function of a genus-`g` graph is a signed
combination of `4^g` Pfaffians (Galluccio–Loebl, Electron. J. Combin. 6
(1999) #R6 **[verified]**; coefficients via discrete spin structures,
Cimasoni–Reshetikhin, arXiv:math-ph/0608070 **[verified]**).
`K_8` has genus 2 (Ringel–Youngs), so each `haf(A^\chi)` is a signed sum
of **16 Pfaffians** of 8×8 antisymmetric matrices, with Arf-invariant
signs.  This buys structure, not speed.

**What it buys — three touchpoints with open components.**
(i) Two-site deletion (clean-pair descent) is Pfaffian row/column
deletion, and the Laplace transports become Grassmann–Plücker
relations, which carry a known quadratic squarefree straightening law —
aimed at the nonsquarefree chart-26 cells.  (ii) Component III's
"source-provenant residual Macaulay annihilator" becomes a concrete
question about Buchsbaum–Eisenbud syzygies of the 16 constituent
Pfaffian ideals (Pfaffian ideals are the *nice* case: Gröbner, radical,
CM — Herzog–Trung 1992 **[provisional]**).  (iii) Component II's
Hessian-kernel interface acquires Pfaffian Jacobi identities
(derivative = Pfaffian × adjugate structure).  The Arf signs are a
`±1` datum that may interact with §1.1 rather than being noise.
None of this is done; these are the reformulated coordinates in which
the open components can be attacked with pre-existing syzygy theory.

### 1.4 P5 transfer recurrence → realization theory / Hilbert–Serre

**What we have.**  The conjectured order-3 recurrence with transfer
polynomial `(1+z0*T)(1+z30*T)(1+z52*T)`, two coefficients checked
(`W4`, `W5`), the ruled-out raw `2+1` Schur cascade, and the roadmap
target "extract the transfer/continuant matrix and apply
Cayley–Hamilton" ([roadmap](2026-08-09-resolution-roadmap.md) §P5).

**Named home and the two promotion routes.**  Over a field, scalar-
sequence realizability is exactly finite Hankel rank
(Kalman–Falb–Arbib 1969 Ch. 10, base field arbitrary **[verified]**;
scalar case avoids all module pathologies over rings, Sontag 1976 §3.D
**[verified]**).  Consequences:

* **Route A (Cayley–Hamilton module).**  Exhibit *any* finitely
  generated module with 3 generators, an endomorphism `F`, and
  `W_k = H F^{k-1} G`: the order-3 recurrence then holds at all orders
  for free, and the checked prefix merely identifies coefficients.  The
  module need not use the raw state coordinates already ruled out — only
  the Hankel column module needs three generators.
* **Route B (Hilbert–Serre).**  If `W_k` is (a coefficient of) a
  multigraded Hilbert function of a f.g. graded module over an algebra
  with three generators in the degrees of `z0,z30,z52`, rationality with
  a three-linear-factor denominator is automatic, and only the numerator
  needs finite checking.  The elementary-symmetric shape of the observed
  transfer polynomial is exactly this signature.

**Negative fact recorded [verified].**  Berlekamp–Massey cannot supply
the promotion: it *consumes* a linear-complexity bound (needs a prefix of
length `2L`); a two-coefficient prefix against a conjectured order-3
recurrence sits inside an explicitly parametrized family of continuations
(Kalman partial-realization theorem, case (c)).  Prefix extension alone,
at any finite length, is not a proof.

**Outcome of the feasibility run (2026-08-11), AUDITED same day by an
independent adversarial re-derivation** (own truncated-power-series
solver sharing no code with the prototypes, consuming only the committed
`F2.audit`/`SCHUR.audit` chain with frozen-ledger re-verification; a
symbolic cross-check ran the committed `G.source_graph` call itself and
matched all 117 order-6/7/8 values; refutation reproduced at a third
generic-`L` point, seed 811, all localized units nonzero; targeted
mutation controls all propagate).  Audit verdicts: (A) pipeline PASS,
(B) `W6` PASS, (C) refutation PASS, (D) numbers PASS / inference
corrected below, (E) PASS with wording fixes incorporated below.
Prototypes and the audit suite:
`computations/unaudited-p5-arc-probe-2026-08-11/` (untracked; the
`audit/` subdirectory is the independent implementation).

* `W6` holds (new), **`W7` fails**: the rational-Rees substitution
  kills `Q_7,Q_8,Q_9` but leaves `Q_10 ≠ 0` on exactly the two selected
  rows, with ratio exactly `u/v`, at the committed point and two
  independent random generic-`L` points.  All localized units are
  nonzero there, so this **refutes the transfer identity**; the
  Weierstrass structure survives, the transfer polynomial does not.
  The elementary-symmetric pattern was a three-term coincidence — the

* Route A (Cayley–Hamilton on the `W_k` sequence) is dead, but **via

### 1.5 Arc-contact results → integral closure via jet-support closure

**What we have.**  The local congruences
`H_0 \in I_mix + m_p^7`, `H_1 \in I_mix + m_p^6` and the quintic-contact
statements along mixed-fibre arcs
([frontier](consolidated-proof-frontier.md) §4).

**Named home.**  de Fernex–Ein–Ishii (arXiv:1704.07494, Prop. 4.12(b))
**[provisional]**: over a regular ambient ring, the jet support closure
equals the integral closure — `f \in \bar a` iff
`ord_\gamma(f) \ge ord_\gamma(a)` for **every** arc `\gamma` — and
`\bar a \subseteq \sqrt a`.  Companion: the plain arc closure is trivial
(Mallory, arXiv:1811.12577) **[provisional]**, so all content is in
contact *orders*.

**What it buys.**  It makes the missing step exact: the proved contact
bounds are at arcs through the distinguished torus point; the bridge to
chartwise radical membership needs the quantifier widened to all arcs
(equivalently, comparison of contact orders against the Rees valuations
of the localized mixed ideal).  **Boundary drawn by verification:** this
integral-closure bridge is the live one.  Skoda/Briançon–Skoda
*division into the ideal itself* is dead here — it requires membership
in the closure of `I^{min(n,r)}` (a high power; exponent `min(n,p-1)`,
Skoda 1972 Théorème 1 **[verified]**), far stronger than what descent
needs, and the Łojasiewicz input is itself Gröbner-hard.

### 1.6 Graded truncations → multigraded NulLA + Buss dual

**What we have.**  The filtered Macaulay truncation ladder
(`I_mix + K^4`, `J^6`, the degree-six half-integral dual) — i.e. the
NulLA method (De Loera–Lee–Malkin–Margulies, arXiv:0801.3788)
**[provisional]** executed without its name.

**What the reformulation adds.**  (i) Every matching sum is
multihomogeneous of multidegree `(1,...,1)` in the `Z^8` vertex grading,
so the certificate linear system block-diagonalizes exactly by
multidegree, with only blocks reachable from the target multidegree
relevant; no published NulLA work uses this **[provisional]**.
(ii) Buss's design/LP duality (DIMACS 1996) **[provisional]**: over a
field, no degree-`d` certificate exists iff a dual functional on the
degree-`d` Macaulay block annihilates the generator span and pairs to 1
with the target — a finite object converting the empirical "certificates
need degree ≥ 6" into a theorem about this instance.  Same matrix, both
uses.  (iii) Perspective from proof complexity **[verified at the level
of statements]**: all published degree lower bounds for matching
principles are Boolean-axiom-dependent and vacuous at `n = 8`; the
qualitative reading (degree linear in `n`, i.e. single digits here;
coefficient size, not degree, as the plausible wall — Potechin–Zhang)
is consistent with everything observed and imposes no barrier.

## 4. Routes checked and found dead (do not reopen without a new input)

Each verdict was verified against the primary text during the survey.

* **Matchgate/graphical-calculus expressibility no-go.**  The GHZ/W
  Frobenius dichotomy is `C^2`-only and imported from the tripartite
  SLOCC classification (finite only there); the planar W-calculus
  completeness theorem has planarity *as* the fragment, and adding the
  swap makes it universal; `d = 2` Krenn graphs *do* realize GHZ, so no
  fragment-expressibility statement can be the conjecture.  The proven
  neighbouring theorem — Equality over domain `q >= 3` is not a
  matchgate signature under holographic transformations
  (Fu–Yang–Yin, Inf. & Comp. 264 (2019)) — lives on the other side of
  both the planarity axis and the colour-vs-occupation encoding axis.
  The only open question worth keeping: which of those two axes carries
  the `d = 3` difficulty.
* **Hyperfield (phase) relaxations.**  Sound as a one-directional
  relaxation (Gross–Gunn), but the phase hyperfield is the literature's
  designated pathology: a trinomial already has a continuum of roots,
  and by Wendel's bound a generic 10-term equation is phase-satisfiable
  with probability ≈ 0.98.  Bites only on binomial strata, where the
  signed-character machinery of §1.1 already operates.
* **Brane tilings / dimer (Jacobi) algebras.**  Their ideals are path
  relations from cyclic derivatives of a superpotential; perfect
  matchings enter only as gradings; hypotheses are bipartite-on-torus.
  No object matches ours.
* **Deep dimer machinery** (spectral curves, amoebas, cluster
  structure): requires bipartite + torus; `K_8` is neither.  Only the
  §1.3 genus-`g` Pfaffian theorem transfers.
* **Lattice-gauge duality / character expansion.**  It is literally the
  multigraded decomposition already in use; the physics duality needs a
  gauge-group integration this problem does not have.
* **Tensor-network varieties / algebraic-statistics implicitization.**
  Dead by this repo's own Border theorem
  ([`tensor-route.md`](tensor-route.md) §6): the target lies in the
  closure of the matching-tensor image, so every equation or dimension
  bound in that literature vanishes on it.  (Exact-image membership is
  decidable-but-infeasible in general — Chevalley plus quantifier
  elimination — not open; still useless at this scale.)
* **Skoda / Briançon–Skoda division** into `I_mix`: needs
  integral-closure membership of a `min(n,r)` power — strictly stronger
  than the statement sought; the Łojasiewicz certificate is itself
  Gröbner-based.  (The integral-closure *bridge* of §1.5 is unaffected.)
* **Creative telescoping / holonomic systems.**  No summation or family
  parameter exists at fixed `K_8`; the determinant/Pfaffian telescoping
  ansatz is powered by alternation, which hafnians lack; no holonomic
  treatment of permanents/hafnians exists.  The honest name for our
  certified recurrences is commutative syzygy certification, which the
  computations already perform.
* **Intrinsic Nullstellensatz degree bounds** (Garg–Saxena-type): closed
  by the rank-245 computation of §2.
* **Cyclotomic / vanishing-sums-of-roots-of-unity obstructions**
  (Lam–Leung): applicable only if solutions were gauged to cyclotomic
  entries; no mechanism produces that, and Laurent/Manin–Mumford runs
  the wrong direction.  (Note for a restricted subfamily: a full-support
  solution in 2-power roots of unity is impossible outright — 105 is
  odd — but zeros void the argument.)
* **Noetherianity-up-to-symmetry** at fixed `N` (`Sym(\infty)` methods):
  nothing to stabilize on fixed 252 variables.  A Brauer-category
  formulation over the tower of all even `N` was not pursued past
  feasibility (non-effective even if true).

## Appendix: reproduction script for §2

```python
"""Jacobian-rank and lattice-rank checks (survey 2026-08-10).
seed 20260810, prime 2^31-1."""
import itertools, numpy as np

P = 2_147_483_647
rng = np.random.default_rng(20260810)
N, D = 8, 3
verts = list(range(N))
pairs = [(u, v) for u in verts for v in verts if u < v]
var_idx = {}
for (u, v) in pairs:
    for i in range(D):
        for j in range(D):
            var_idx[(u, v, i, j)] = len(var_idx)
NV = len(var_idx)  # 252

def matchings(vs):
    if not vs:
        yield []
        return
    u = vs[0]
    for k in range(1, len(vs)):
        rest = vs[1:k] + vs[k+1:]
        for m in matchings(rest):
            yield [(u, vs[k])] + m

PMs = list(matchings(verts))  # 105
point = rng.integers(1, P, size=NV, dtype=np.int64)
colourings = list(itertools.product(range(D), repeat=N))
mixed = [c for c in colourings if len(set(c)) > 1]  # 6558

def jac_rows(chis):
    J = np.zeros((len(chis), NV), dtype=np.int64)
    for r, chi in enumerate(chis):
        ev = {(u, v): int(point[var_idx[(u, v, chi[u], chi[v])]])
              for (u, v) in pairs}
        for M in PMs:
            vals = [ev[e] for e in M]
            for t in range(4):
                cof = 1
                for s in range(4):
                    if s != t:
                        cof = (cof * vals[s]) % P
                u, v = M[t]
                k = var_idx[(u, v, chi[u], chi[v])]
                J[r, k] = (J[r, k] + cof) % P
    return J

def rank_mod_p(A, p):
    A = A % p
    rows, cols = A.shape
    r = 0
    for c in range(cols):
        piv = next((i for i in range(r, rows) if A[i, c] % p), None)
        if piv is None:
            continue
        A[[r, piv]] = A[[piv, r]]
        A[r] = (A[r] * pow(int(A[r, c]), p - 2, p)) % p
        col = A[:, c].copy(); col[r] = 0
        A = (A - np.outer(col, A[r])) % p
        r += 1
        if r == rows:
            break
    return r

J = jac_rows(mixed)
print("mixed Jacobian rank:", rank_mod_p(J.copy(), P))          # 245
Jp = jac_rows([(0,)*N, (1,)*N, (2,)*N])
print("with pure rows:", rank_mod_p(np.vstack([J, Jp]), P))     # 245

nodes = {(v, i): k for k, (v, i) in enumerate(
    [(v, i) for v in verts for i in range(D)])}
Inc = np.zeros((NV, len(nodes)), dtype=np.int64)
for (u, v, i, j), e in var_idx.items():
    Inc[e, nodes[(u, i)]] += 1
    Inc[e, nodes[(v, j)]] += 1
print("gauge rank:", rank_mod_p(Inc.copy(), P),
      "invariant lattice:", NV - rank_mod_p(Inc.copy(), P))     # 24, 228
```
