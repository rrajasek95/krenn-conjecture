# Rank-two singular relation blocks have no live all-rank-two edge

## 1. Outcome

Continue with the live singular edge

\[
 \mathcal L(M)=PMT^{\mathsf T}+SM^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \mathcal L(\mathscr D)=\mathbb C H,             \tag{1}
\]

where `mathscr D subset Z_0` is the four-dimensional row/column-avoiding
relation space, `H` is invertible, and `P,S,Q,T` all have rank two.  This
note settles the residual case

\[
                    \operatorname {rank}(\mathcal L:Z_0\to
                    \operatorname {Mat}_3)=2.          \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem 1.1.**  Equations (1)--(2) have no live all-rank-two edge.

Together with
[`rank-three-singular-fixed-line-obstruction.md`](rank-three-singular-fixed-line-obstruction.md),
this closes both physical-image ranks left by the `2-2-2-2` reduction.
Rank one was already impossible: every coordinate row and column image is
singular, whereas the live image line contains the invertible `H`.

## 2. Kernel pairs and the fixed-line condition

Put

\[
 K=\ker(\mathcal L|_{Z_0}),\qquad \dim K=4.             \tag{3}
\]

As proved in Section 6 of
[`singular-relation-block-reduction.md`](singular-relation-block-reduction.md),
the restriction of `mathcal L` to each coordinate row or column plane has
rank exactly one.  Let

\[
 p=\ker P,\quad s=\ker S,\quad q=\ker Q,\quad t=\ker T. \tag{4}
\]

The live transversality relations make `im P,im S` distinct planes and
`im T,im Q` distinct planes.  For every `M in K`,

\[
                  PMT^{\mathsf T}=-SM^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T}.      \tag{5}
\]

Thus each side of (5) has column space in the fixed line
`im P intersection im S` and row space in the fixed line
`im T intersection im Q`.  For kernel lines `v,u`, let
`rho_(v,u)(M)` denote the induced map obtained by quotienting the left
colour space by `Cv` and the right colour space by `Cu`.  Both two-by-two
compression images have dimension at most one:

\[
                     \dim\rho_{p,t}(K)\le1,
                     \qquad\dim\rho^{\mathsf T}_{s,q}(K)\le1.       \tag{6}
\]

Neither compression image is zero.  Suppose, for example, that the first
vanished.  Since `dim(K intersection mathscr D)=3`, its image on
`mathscr D` would have dimension at most one.  Lemma 3.2 of the cited
reduction says that this line cannot be generated by an invertible
two-by-two form, so `PMT^T` would have rank at most one on `mathscr D`.
Equation (5) makes the second summand vanish on `K` as well, and the same
argument gives rank at most one for `SM^TQ^T` on `mathscr D`.  At an
`M_0` with `mathcal L(M_0)=H`, their sum would have rank at most two,
contrary to the invertibility of `H`.  Therefore both dimensions in (6)
are exactly one.

Define the bad-pair locus

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=\{([u],[v]):u^{\mathsf T}\operatorname {adj}(M)v=0
                         \text{ for every }M\in K\}.                \tag{7}
\]

Cauchy--Binet gives the conventions without ambiguity:

\[
 \det\rho_{p,t}(M)=c_1t^{\mathsf T}\operatorname {adj}(M)p,
 \qquad
 \det\rho^{\mathsf T}_{s,q}(M)
       =c_2s^{\mathsf T}\operatorname {adj}(M)q,       \tag{8}
\]

with `c_1c_2\ne0`.  The transpose in the second formula uses
`adj(M^T)=adj(M)^T`.  Hence

\[
                         ([t],[p]),([s],[q])\in\mathcal B(K).        \tag{9}
\]

We will first compute (7), then impose the stronger fixed-line rank bound
(6).

## 3. The three graph orbits without enumeration

Let `r_c` and `c_d` be the projective row and column image points in the
physical pencil `P(im mathcal L)=P^1`.  A nonzero generator
`v_cd=mathcal L(E_cd)` forces `r_c=c_d`.  (The converse need not hold;
this scalar degeneration is retained below.)  Therefore the
nonzero-generator graph is a spanning subgraph of

\[
             K_{3,3}\setminus\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2)\}=C_6             \tag{10}
\]

with minimum degree one.  It is disconnected: if it were connected, all
six projective labels, and hence all nonzero generator values, would lie
on one physical line, forcing `im\mathcal L` to be a line.

A disconnected spanning subgraph of a six-cycle with minimum degree one
is obtained by deleting at least two cycle edges.  Deleted edges cannot be
adjacent, since their common vertex would become isolated.  At most three
edges can be deleted, since a spanning graph of minimum degree one has at
least three edges.  Consequently there are exactly three cases up to the
dihedral symmetry of the cycle:

1. three alternating deleted edges, leaving a perfect matching;
2. two opposite deleted edges, leaving two disjoint two-edge stars; or
3. two nonopposite, nonadjacent deleted edges, leaving a three-edge path
   and an isolated edge.

This proves the orbit list intrinsically; no finite enumeration is used.

## 4. The perfect-matching degeneration is also impossible

Take the active matching to be `01,12,20`.  Its three nonzero vectors span
the physical pencil and have one relation.  Thus, after rescaling
parameters,

\[
 K=\left\{
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&ar&x\\y&0&br\\cr&z&0
 \end{pmatrix}:r,x,y,z\in\mathbb C\right\},
 \qquad\text{at least two of }a,b,c\text{ are nonzero}.             \tag{11}
\]

All three coefficients are nonzero exactly when the three active
generator directions are pairwise distinct.  Exactly one is zero when
two directions coincide; two cannot vanish because every active generator
is nonzero.

We classify the one-dimensional compressions directly, including that
optional zero.  For an ordered pair `([u],[v])`, let
`bar e_i^v` and `bar e_i^u` denote the images of `e_i` in the two quotient
spaces.  The three free coordinate generators in (11) compress to

\[
 \bar e_0^v(\bar e_2^u)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \bar e_1^v(\bar e_0^u)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \bar e_2^v(\bar e_1^u)^{\mathsf T}.                 \tag{12}
\]

If none vanished and they spanned a line, all three left quotient vectors
would be proportional, contradicting the fact that the quotient is
two-dimensional.  If exactly one vanished, proportionality of the other
two would put `u,v` in coordinate planes incompatible with that
vanishing.  Hence at least two tensors in (12) vanish.  This leaves either
`u=v=e_i` or a cyclic cross pair such as `u=e_2,v=e_1`.  At that cross
pair, the `r`-generator contains
`a bar e_0^v(bar e_1^u)^T+c bar e_2^v(bar e_0^u)^T`; at least one term is
present and neither is proportional to the surviving tensor in (12).
The other cross pairs are cyclically identical and are impossible.

For the three diagonal pairs, direct quotienting gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
([u],[v])&\rho_{v,u}(K)&\dim\rho_{v,u}(K)=1\text{ iff}\\ \hline
([e_0],[e_0])&\left\{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&br\\z&0\end{psmallmatrix}\right\}
 &b=0\\
([e_1],[e_1])&\left\{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&x\\cr&0\end{psmallmatrix}\right\}
 &c=0\\
([e_2],[e_2])&\left\{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&ar\\y&0\end{psmallmatrix}\right\}
 &a=0.
\end{array}                                             \tag{13}
\]

Thus pairwise-distinct active directions contradict (6)--(9).  In the
only residual case, both ordered kernel pairs in (9) equal the same
`([e_i],[e_i])`; hence

\[
                              p=s=q=t=e_i.              \tag{13a}
\]

But then every generator in coordinate row `i` and coordinate column `i`
vanishes.  One of each is active in the perfect matching, a contradiction.

## 5. The two-star orbit forces a singular physical pencil

Use active edges `01,02,10,20`.  Each pair in a connected component is
projectively proportional, and the two component values are independent.
The kernel has the normal form

\[
 K=\left\{
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&ax&bx\\cy&0&z\\dy&w&0
 \end{pmatrix}:x,y,z,w\in\mathbb C\right\},
 \qquad abcd\ne0.                                     \tag{14}
\]

Put

\[
 k=(0,a,b),\qquad \ell=(0,c,d),\qquad
 W=\operatorname {span}(e_1,e_2).                     \tag{15}
\]

The coefficients involving `xz,xw,yz,yw,zw` first force `u_0=v_0=0`.
The remaining `xy` coefficient factors as

\[
              -(bu_1-au_2)(dv_1-cv_2).                \tag{16}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=
       \{([k],[v]):v\in W\}\cup\{([u],[\ell]):u\in W\}.           \tag{17}
\]

For `u=k`, write `v=(0,r,h)`.  In suitable quotient bases the only
nonzero compression coefficient rows are

\[
                    (0,ch-dr,0,0),\qquad(0,0,-ah,-br),              \tag{18}
\]

where the columns are `x,y,z,w` (renaming `r,h` if necessary merely
interchanges the displayed products).  They span a line exactly when
`v=ell`.  Dually, on the second ruling the coefficient rows span a line
exactly when `u=k`.  Thus (6) sharpens (9) to

\[
                         ([t],[p])=([s],[q])=([k],[\ell]).           \tag{19}
\]

In particular

\[
                         p=q=\ell,\qquad s=t=k.         \tag{20}
\]

Choose quotient maps `A,B:\mathbb C^3\to\mathbb C^2` with kernels
`\ell,k`.  There are injections `U,V,X,Y:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^3`
such that

\[
 P=UA,\quad Q=VA,\quad S=XB,\quad T=YB,
\]

and, for `Z=AMB^T`,

\[
                  \mathcal L(M)=\Phi(Z)=UZY^{\mathsf T}
                                           +XZ^{\mathsf T}V^{\mathsf T}. \tag{21}
\]

The unique compression line in (19) is nonzero and rank one; let its
generator be `E_00` after quotient basis changes.  The equality
`Phi(E_00)=0` lets us normalize the four injection columns exactly as in
Section 5 of the rank-three note.  Relative to physical bases, every value
of `Phi` is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \gamma&\beta&0\\
  \alpha&0&\beta\\
  0&-\alpha&\gamma
 \end{pmatrix},                                       \tag{22}
\]

whose determinant is identically zero.  Hence the physical pencil cannot
contain the invertible `H`, a contradiction.

## 6. The path-plus-edge orbit forces a zero active row

Use active edges `01,02,10,21`, with `01,02,21` in the three-edge
component and `10` isolated.  The kernel condition kills the isolated
coordinate and imposes one full-support relation on the three-edge
component.  Thus

\[
 K=\left\{
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&Ax+By&x\\0&0&z\\w&y&0
 \end{pmatrix}:x,y,z,w\in\mathbb C\right\},
 \qquad AB\ne0.                                       \tag{23}
\]

Direct expansion gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
xy&u_0v_1\\
xz&A u_0v_2\\
xw&-v_1(-A u_2+u_1)\\
yz&-u_0(-Bv_2+v_0)\\
yw&B u_2v_1\\
zw&u_1v_0.
\end{array}                                             \tag{24}
\]

The first, second, third, and fifth equations force `v_1=0` and `u_0=0`.
The last equation then gives precisely

\[
 \mathcal B(K)=
 \{([e_2],[v]):v\in\operatorname {span}(e_0,e_2)\}
 \cup
 \{([u],[e_2]):u\in\operatorname {span}(e_1,e_2)\}.   \tag{25}
\]

On the first ruling write `v=(r,0,h)`.  The compression coefficient rows
have disjoint supports

\[
                       (0,0,0,-r),\qquad(Ah,Bh-r,0,0),              \tag{26}
\]

so their span has dimension one exactly when `r=0`, namely `v=e_2`.
The transpose calculation on the second ruling gives the same conclusion
`u=e_2`.  Conditions (6) and (9) therefore force

\[
                           p=s=q=t=e_2.                \tag{27}
\]

These are actual colour-coordinate kernels: only coordinate-preserving
rescalings and permutations were used in obtaining (23).  Hence

\[
 \mathcal L(E_{2d})=Pe_2(Te_d)^{\mathsf T}
                   +Se_d(Qe_2)^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(E_{c2})=Pe_c(Te_2)^{\mathsf T}
                   +Se_2(Qe_c)^{\mathsf T}=0.          \tag{28}
\]

This contradicts the active edge `21` (and also `02`) in the graph used
to derive (23).  The third orbit is impossible, completing the proof.

## 7. Exact audit

[`verify_rank_two_singular_fixed_line_obstruction.py`](../computations/verify_rank_two_singular_fixed_line_obstruction.py)
checks exactly that:

1. all three normal-form compression formulas are correct;
2. every optional-zero case in the perfect-matching relation has exactly
   the one-line compression stated in (13);
3. the two-star and path-plus-edge forms have the unique ordered
   fixed-line pairs stated in (19) and (27); and
4. the crosswise physical matrix (22) has identically zero determinant.

The exhaustive finite-field portion is an exact audit of the symbolic
case split, not a substitute for the characteristic-zero arguments above.
