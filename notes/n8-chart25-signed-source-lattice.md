# The complete chart-25 signed source lattice has no (4D) boundary

This is an exact theorem on the frozen chart-25 partial-character support.
It closes the even-cycle/odd-handcuff and ordinary label-diagonal alternatives
inside that support.  It does not construct the new relative cell and does
not prove `SP-CLEAN-BRIDGE` or Krenn's conjecture.

## The saturated physical source lattice

Expand the frozen functional to its 20 actual monomial rows.  There are 16
rows of weight (-1/4), four rows of weight (+1/4), and 56 individually
labelled physical source columns.  Every column meets exactly one negative
row and one positive row.  The resulting multigraph is four disjoint
four-leaf stars.  Its 16 leaf--centre edge types have physical-label
multiplicities three or four, eight of each.

For each component (c), write its centre as (D_c) and its leaves as
(A_{c,1},\ldots,A_{c,4}).  Define the primitive integral partial character

\[
 \pi_c(x)=x(D_c)-\sum_{i=1}^4x(A_{c,i}).                 \tag{1}
\]

Let (B:\mathbb Z^{16}\to\mathbb Z^{20}) contain one column
(A_{c,i}+D_c) for every simple edge type, let (Q) read the 16 leaf
coordinates, and let (T:\mathbb Z^4\to\mathbb Z^{20}) include the four
centres.  The checker verifies the integral identities

\[
 PB=0,\qquad PT=I_4,\qquad I_{20}-BQ=TP.                \tag{2}
\]

Consequently

\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{16}
   \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{B}\mathbb Z^{20}
   \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{P}\mathbb Z^4
   \longrightarrow0                                    \tag{3}
\]

is split exact.  In particular, the physical source image is saturated:
its cokernel is torsion-free of rank four, with basis the four component
partial characters.  This is stronger than a rational rank calculation.

## Signed cycles do not change the boundary image

Restoring all 56 physical labels only repeats the 16 simple columns.  The
integer column kernel is the direct sum of the zero-sum lattices in the 16
parallel classes.  It has rank (56-16=40) and is generated by differences
of two parallel labelled columns.

In signed-graph language these are doubled even cycles.  The graph is
bipartite, with negative leaves on one side and positive centres on the
other, so it has no odd cycle and therefore no odd handcuff.  Every
even-cycle combination is a source syzygy with boundary zero.  It can change
a labelled representative but cannot move its class in (3).

The canonical missing row (D) is one positive centre.  Hence

\[
                         P(4D)=4e_c\ne0.                 \tag{4}
\]

No integral or rational combination of the complete 56 columns has boundary
(4D).  Adding any even-cycle syzygy leaves this unchanged.  There is no
odd-handcuff alternative to try.

The already committed ordinary label-diagonal Koszul closure also cannot
help.  After physical diagonal specialization every positive-exterior
boundary contains a vanished label difference, so its physical degree-zero
image remains (ker P).  The checker reruns the complete Koszul/tensor
audit and its frozen digest.  Even if every old cycle and Koszul chain is
granted (operatorname {tgt}=operatorname {ores}=0), its boundary still
has zero (P)-character, while (4) does not.

## The genuinely new edge type

The signed lattice identifies the missing object precisely.  It cannot be
another edge or cycle among the old 20 vertices.  It must be a half-edge at
(D), or equivalently an edge from (D) to a new relative target vertex
(	au).  In chain notation the minimal form is a mixed
source--diagonal transgression (r) with

\[
       d r=4D-\tau,\qquad
       \widehat a(\tau)=\widehat a(4D)=1,\qquad
       \operatorname {tgt}(r)=\operatorname {ores}(r)=0. \tag{5}
\]

The formal target mapping cylinder contains exactly (5), but that is not a
physical construction: it inserts the new edge by definition.  A successful
source-constrained HPL proof must derive this relative edge from a genuine
mixed source--diagonal lower face.  Ordinary label Koszul cells, Reynolds
transfer, even cycles, and odd handcuffs are now all excluded on this fibre.

The later single-edge cap theorem remains downstream.  Equation (5) is the
first missing source-provenant edge; only after it exists does it make sense
to ask whether the terminal cap preserves scalar zero, a diagonal target,
and support on one physical edge.
