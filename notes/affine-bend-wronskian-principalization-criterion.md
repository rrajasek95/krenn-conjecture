# Affine-bend Wronskians reduce principalization to bend-free membership

## 1. Algebraic criterion

Let `A` be a commutative ring and work in `A[r]`, localized so that `a` is
a unit.  Suppose

\[
                 f=ar+b,\qquad g_i=c_i r+d_i                 \tag{1}
\]

are affine in `r`, with coefficients in `A`.  Define the affine Wronskian

\[
                 \Omega_i=a g_i-c_i f=a d_i-c_i b.          \tag{2}
\]

It is independent of `r`.  Then

\[
 \boxed{\quad g_i\in(f)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                  \Omega_i=0.\quad}                         \tag{3}
\]

Indeed one may divide (2) by the unit `a`.  If `Omega_i=0`,
then `g_i=(c_i/a)f`.  Conversely, reducing `g_i` modulo the monic-linear
equation `f/a=r+b/a` gives the unique remainder

\[
                        d_i-c_i b/a=\Omega_i/a,             \tag{4}
\]

so membership in `(f)` forces `Omega_i=0`.  This is just Weierstrass
division of degree one; it needs no completion and no Groebner basis using
`f`.

More generally, let `J` be an ideal in a larger source ring and assume its
localized quotient is identified with `A[r]`, with the displayed images of
`f,g_i` affine and their coefficients in `A`.  Then (3) becomes

\[
 g_i\in(J,f)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Omega_i\in J.   \tag{5}
\]

The hypothesis that `r` remains a free coordinate modulo `J` is load-bearing.
For an arbitrary ideal containing relations in `r`, polynomial-division
remainders need not be unique.  Without that hypothesis, `Omega_i in J` is
still a sufficient certificate for `g_i in (J,f)`, but the converse can fail.

For the ideal `I=J+(f,g_1,...,g_s)`, equations (2)--(5) give

\[
       I=(J,f)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_s\in J.                       \tag{6}
\]

If another row `h` is affine in `r`, the same test with
`Omega_h=a*h-(partial_r h)*f` decides `h in (J,f)`.  In particular, when
`h` is independent of `r`, its test is the bend-free row `a*h`.

## 2. Saturated form

Suppose the selected strict row has coefficient

\[
                         a=\tau^k u,                         \tag{7}
\]

where `u` is a unit on the chosen chart.  Apply the criterion after passing
to the `tau`-saturated localization and replacing `f` by its strict quotient
`tau^{-k}f`.  Equivalently, form the polynomial cross-products before
localization, remove their common certified power of `tau`, and test the
result against the saturated graph/centre ideal.  This is important: a
finite truncation modulo `tau^m` is not a substitute for the saturated
membership in (3).

There are two honest ways to combine the construction with a Schur graph.

1. Prove that the localized graph quotient is a polynomial or power-series
   ring over the remaining base with `r` as a free coordinate, and form the
   affine Wronskians after that identification.
2. Keep the graph variables independent and form the raw cross-products (2)
   in the finite source polynomial ring.  Membership of those cross-products
   in the graph ideal is a sufficient principalization certificate, even if
   the graph substitution would make the rows nonlinear in `r`.

For necessity in a nonlinear graph quotient one must instead lift
`partial_r` through the implicit graph.  If the graph is `F(x,r)=0` with
`F_x` invertible, its canonical lift is

\[
 \delta=\partial_r-(F_x^{-1}F_r)\cdot\partial_x.             \tag{8}
\]

One then uses `(delta f)g-(delta g)f` and identifies the constant ring of
`delta`.  In characteristic zero, if the graph quotient is a formal
one-variable disc over the base and `delta f` is a unit, vanishing of this
Wronskian is equivalent to `g/f` being a base constant.  Those hypotheses
are load-bearing.  A failed raw-cross membership is therefore not by itself
an obstruction.  Substituting the formal graph first can turn affine source
rows into large power series and obscure the finite cancellation, but simply
ignoring that effect would overclaim equivalence.

## 3. P5 application

In the finite generic-`L` P5 Rees chart, the exact source audit proves that
all raw mixed strict rows are affine in the newest site-7 bend.  The 207 Schur
rows and the triangular centre form `J`.  The selected row `M30` has a
localized-unit bend coefficient after removing its certified strict power of
`tau`.  This gives the finite sufficient list

\[
 \Omega_i=(\partial_r M30)M_i-(\partial_r M_i)M30,
               \qquad i\ne30.                              \tag{9}
\]

Every raw `Omega_i` is independent of `r`.  Showing all 27 rows (9) lie in
the tau-saturated Schur/centre ideal proves that the complete mixed ideal on
this chart is generated by `M30`.  Applying the same cross-product to
`H0,H1` supplies the analogous sufficient pure-membership certificates
without constructing an explicit all-order bend recurrence.  If some raw
test fails, the next exact object is the graph-lifted total-derivative
Wronskian; failure alone is not a P5 survivor.

The already checked oriented relation between `M30` and `M33` is the first
filtered coefficient of (9).  The exact `W4/W5` three-step bend recurrence is
further evidence that the full Wronskian vanishes, but neither finite prefix
alone proves the saturated membership required here.

This note is a reduction criterion, not a P5 verdict.  Its value is that a
positive raw test replaces large normal forms modulo `(J,M30)` by smaller,
bend-free membership tests modulo `J`; the graph-lifted version supplies the
exact converse when the raw sufficient test is too strong.
