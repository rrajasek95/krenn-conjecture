# Every certificate for the terminal class is pinned before it is solved

Research evidence only.  Krenn's conjecture remains open, `SP-CLEAN-BRIDGE`
is untouched, and no certified dependency changes.

## 1. Outcome

The \(h=3\) landing says: if all nine GHZ rows hold then \(\chi=0\).  Its
algebraic form is an ideal-membership question — does \(\chi\) lie in the
ideal \(I\) generated by the \(6561\) polynomials
\(\operatorname{Row}(i,j,w)-\Delta(i,j,w)\)?  This note settles the structure
of any possible certificate, and closes the two cheapest classes.

**The multigrading.** Grade a monomial by its *site-colour degree* — for each
site \(x\) and colour \(c\), the number of half-edges it puts at \((x,c)\) —
together with the two endpoint-label degrees.  Then

\[
 \operatorname{Row}(i,j,w)\ \text{is multihomogeneous of degree}\
 D(i,j,w)=\bigl(\sigma(w),e_i,e_j\bigr),
\]

because every row monomial covers each site exactly once, in the colour the
word prescribes, and carries one left-label and one right-label object.  This
holds with cross-colour internal edges live.  The terminal class sits at
\(D^\*=(\sigma(2^6),3e_0,3e_1)\).

**Top-level theorem.**  Exhaustively over all \(6561\) generator degrees,
**exactly one row degree** satisfies \(D\leq D^\*\), namely \((0,1,2^6)\), and the forced
multiplier degree admits exactly one monomial, \(d_{01}^2\).  Hence every
representation reads, at \(\chi\)'s own multidegree,

\[
 \boxed{\;\chi=c\,d_{01}^2\operatorname{Row}(0,1,2^6)-N,\qquad
 N=m_{00,0^6}^{D^\*}+m_{11,1^6}^{D^\*}+m_{22,2^6}^{D^\*}.\;}
\]

One generator with a *scalar* multiplier is all the row sector can contribute;
the rest of \(\chi\) must be handed over verbatim by the pure-word anchors'
constants.  (\(c=-1\) is the audited cap split
\(\operatorname{haf}(\alpha q+R)=\alpha^2\operatorname{Row}(0,1,2^6)+\chi\).)
This proves the anchor-consumption constraint by grading rather than by
guard packet, and names the unique companion generator.

**Two closures.**

* **Degree.**  \(N\) always carries the twenty \(p^3s^3\) monomials of
  \(-Q_3\), each of total degree six.  So **no certificate has all multipliers
  of total degree \(\leq5\)**: some anchor multiplier's \(D^\*\)-component is
  nonzero and every monomial there has total degree exactly six, so that
  multiplier has degree **at least** six.  It may carry higher-degree monomials
  at other multidegrees.
* **Depth.**  Call a certificate *depth-one* when its three anchor multipliers
  are multidegree-homogeneous **of degree \(D^\*\)**, that is
  \(m_{cc,c^6}=N_c\).  The pinning matters: an anchor multiplier homogeneous of
  the higher degree \(D^\*+D(c,c,c^6)\) would contribute a free term and break
  the argument.  With \(V\) the \(215\)-dimensional space at \(D^\*\), the
  spaces \(W_c\) of admissible anchor multipliers satisfy

  \[
   \dim W_0=\dim W_1=\dim W_2=1
  \]

  (columns \(20985/20985/2520\), ranks \(20890/20890/2517\)) — **one, not
  zero**.  Each \(W_c\) is spanned by the trivial Koszul syzygy
  \(N_c=d_{01}^2\operatorname{Row}(0,1,2^6)\), for which
  \(N_c\operatorname{Row}(c,c,c^6)=[d_{01}^2\operatorname{Row}(c,c,c^6)]\operatorname{Row}(0,1,2^6)\)
  and the right-hand side is an available generator with its multiplier inside
  the enumerated space.  That identity needs no linear algebra and is checked
  directly.

  **There is still no depth-one certificate**, but the reason is a *shape*
  argument, not \(W_c=0\).  Since \(\dim W_c=1\), any depth-one certificate
  has \(N=\Lambda d_{01}^2\operatorname{Row}(0,1,2^6)\) for a scalar
  \(\Lambda\), hence
  \(\chi=(c-\Lambda)d_{01}^2\operatorname{Row}(0,1,2^6)\).  Every monomial
  on the right carries d-degree at least two, while \(\chi\) has twenty
  **d-free** monomials of shape \((3,3,0,0)\).  So \(\chi=0\) — false.  The
  anchor cascade has length at least two.

## 2. The discriminating control returns null

Both closures were run against the constant \(1\) at the same bound.  No
generator lies below degree \(0\), and the only generator below
\(D(c,c,c^6)\) is that anchor itself, so depth-one forces
\(k_c\operatorname{Row}(c,c,c^6)=0\) and hence \(1=0\).  **The constant \(1\)
has no depth-one certificate either.**

So at this bound the method separates nothing.  It is *not* an emptiness proof
in disguise — but it does not certify \(\chi\) either.  This is recorded as a
null on the discrimination test, not as a win.

## 3. Why only bounded statements are available

The nine-row system on this chart is the \(n=8\), \(d=3\) case: six residual
sites plus two endpoints, targets \(1\) exactly at the three monochromatic
words.  Therefore \(1\in I\) is, by the Nullstellensatz, emptiness of the
nine-row variety over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) — which is the **open** case.
Two consequences that bound what this route can ever produce:

1. \(\chi\in I\) is *implied by* the conjecture at \((8,3)\), so membership is
   presumably true for a reason that proves nothing.  Only a **low-degree,
   structured, few-generator** certificate would be an advance.
2. A full non-membership proof would *refute* the conjecture at \((8,3)\).  So
   every available negative is necessarily a bounded-certificate statement —
   which is exactly what the two closures in section 1 are.

## 4. Prior art, and what is new

The site-colour multigrading itself is **not new**: it is used in section 1 of
[`ideal-membership-route.md`](ideal-membership-route.md), on the bare
six-vertex source ideal.  Two committed items on this very chart are closer still and are also
precedent: block B1 of
[`verify_fourhole_allword_row_identity_grade_ladder.py`](../computations/verify_fourhole_allword_row_identity_grade_ladder.py),
recording that every row monomial has \(p\)-degree equal to \(s\)-degree and
at most one — the unlabelled endpoint grading — and the \(\tau\)-weight grading
of [the weight note](terminal-class-weight-invisibility-and-fourhole-grade-ladder.md).
Weight is in any case a linear functional of the multidegree, so the weight
statement is implied by the grading statement.  New here are the endpoint-label
refinement
\(\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb Z^3\) — the decorated chart has two endpoints whose
colours are the row's label pair, absent in the six-vertex setting — the
application to \(\chi\) rather than to a product of pure-word rows, and the
top-level theorem and depth-one decision that follow.

That precedent sharpens the read rather than diminishing it.  On its own
object, the tempting certificate at the first natural bound **provably does not
exist** either.  Two independent negatives at the first bound of this route, on
different targets, is a pattern rather than an accident of target choice.
Given section 2, however, it is evidence that these first bounds are too small
to certify **anything** here — the constant \(1\) fails them too — not evidence
that no short certificate exists.

## 5. The frontier, quantified

Depth-two is the next bound.  Available generators two anchor steps out number
\(128\), \(384\), \(2916\) and \(4\) for \((0,0)\), \((0,2)\), \((0,1)\) and
\((2,2)\); one step out gives \(128\), \(128\) and \(4\).  Even the smallest branch carries \(913{,}040\) multiplier unknowns.
Exact rational linear algebra there is out of reach by this method, and no
degree bound is available from the grading — a weight-six piece of a
mixed-sign grading is infinite-dimensional.  There are also linear degree lower
bounds for Nullstellensatz certificates on colouring systems in the literature,
and this is a colouring system, so low degree must not be assumed; that report
is relayed and is not verified here.  The \(913{,}040\) figure is likewise a
census computed alongside rather than asserted by the checker.  If the line
continues, support minimization on the depth-two blocks is better aimed than a
larger uniform degree bound.

## 6. What this does not say

1. It does not decide membership, and cannot: see section 3.
2. It says nothing about \(h\neq3\), and nothing about the uniform conjecture.
   Even a certificate would be the \(h=3\) local normal form only, entering the
   spine solely through a named supersession after independent audit.
3. The depth-one closure is a statement about *depth*, not about existence: it
   says the three anchor multipliers cannot all be multidegree-homogeneous of
   degree \(D^\*\).

## 7. Audit

The dependency-free checker
[`verify_terminal_class_ideal_membership_multigrading.py`](../computations/verify_terminal_class_ideal_membership_multigrading.py)
verifies the multigrading on all \(6561\) rows with cross-colour edges live;
\(\chi\)'s degree, weight and fine shapes; the top-level theorem by exhaustion
over all generator degrees; the forced multiplier \(d_{01}^2\); the cap split
which is the \(c=-1\) member; the degree bound via the twenty \(p^3s^3\) monomials; the
three rank computations giving \(\dim W_c=1\), the Koszul identity that
exhibits the spanning element directly, and the d-degree shape argument that
closes depth one; the null result for the constant \(1\); the cascade census; and both
standard probes — the rank-two clean packet stays clean and the seven-row guard
misses exactly two generators with \(\chi=-2\).

Standard library only, exact `Fraction` arithmetic, about twenty seconds of
CPU, passing
normal, `-O` and `-I -S`, deterministic across hash seeds.

The multigrading and the top-level theorem were independently re-derived
against a separately written model, including the uniqueness of the generator
below \(D^\*\) and the forced multiplier.
