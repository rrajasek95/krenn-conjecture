# Two singleton rows cannot share a singular column

## 1. Result

Consider the two-`K_4` chart with standard ternary equality tensors on both
shores.  Let `B_ij` be its `3 by 3` cross blocks.

**Theorem 1.1.**  Two block rows cannot have all their singular blocks
confined to the same block column.  Equivalently, there do not exist distinct
rows `r,s` and a column `h` such that

\[
 \det B_{rj}\ne0,\qquad \det B_{sj}\ne0
                         \qquad(j\ne h),                \tag{1}
\]

with no hypothesis on `B_rh` and `B_sh`.

The transposed statement holds for two block columns.  In particular, this
closes the exact-six row-degree case `(2,2,1,1)` in which the two singleton
rows have their singular blocks in the same column.  The singular blocks may
have arbitrary ranks, including rank zero.

The new input is a coincident-defect Hessian lemma.  Unlike separated-defect
eight-cell erasure, the effective quadratic need not vanish: a Koszul class
on the three regular sites can survive.  What does vanish is every block
incident to the common exceptional site, and that is precisely what the
two-`K_4` endpoint obstruction needs.

The exact checker is
[`verify_two_k4_coincident_defect_incident_obstruction.py`](../computations/verify_two_k4_coincident_defect_incident_obstruction.py).

## 2. Coincident-defect incident erasure

Let

\[
 \mathcal R=\bigotimes_{i=0}^3(\mathbb F\oplus V_i),
 \qquad V_i^2=0,
 \qquad \dim V_i=3,                                    \tag{2}
\]

over a field of characteristic different from two.  Let `U,W` be
three-spaces, let

\[
 P_i:U\longrightarrow V_i,
 \qquad S_i:W\longrightarrow V_i,                      \tag{3}
\]

and put

\[
 p_x=\sum_iP_ix,
 \qquad s_y=\sum_iS_iy.                                \tag{4}
\]

Fix a site `h`.  Suppose `P_i` and `S_i` are isomorphisms for every
`i != h`; the two maps at `h` are arbitrary.  Let `K subset U` and
`L subset W` be two-planes.

**Lemma 2.1 (coincident-defect incident erasure).**  If `q in R_2` obeys

\[
 q p_x s_y=0
       \qquad\text{whenever }x\in K\text{ or }y\in L,  \tag{5}
\]

then

\[
                         q_{hi}=0\qquad(i\ne h).        \tag{6}
\]

Thus eight erased input cells determine the three blocks incident to the
common exceptional site.  They need not determine the other three blocks.
For example, if `P_h=S_h=0`, every quadratic supported on the complementary
three-site triangle is invisible to (5).

## 3. Two elementary kernels

We use two standard square-free multiplication facts.

First, suppose that `S_i` is invertible at the three sites different from
`h`.  The annihilator of the star `s(W)` in degree three has dimension

\[
 \dim\{T\in\mathcal R_3:T s_y=0\ (y\in W)\}
 =\begin{cases}
   27,&S_h=0,\\
   1,&S_h\ne0.
  \end{cases}                                           \tag{7}
\]

When `S_h=0`, its elements are exactly the arbitrary cubics supported on
the three sites other than `h`.  When `S_h != 0`, the annihilator is
generated by a generalized determinant `Omega_S`.  Its component missing
`h` is the full determinant tensor on the three regular sites, so it has
mode rank three at every one of those sites.  This statement depends only
on whether `S_h` is zero, not on its positive rank.  It follows directly by
reducing `S_h` to `diag(I_d,0)` and comparing the coefficients of
`T s_y`.

Second, if `a=sum_i a_i` has a nonzero component at every site and `qa=0`,
then

\[
 q_{ij}=z_{ij}a_i\otimes a_j,
 \qquad z_{ij}+z_{ik}+z_{jk}=0                         \tag{8}
\]

on every three-site subset.  This is the kernel of multiplication by one
full-support linear element.

We will also need the following very small consequence of the same
coefficient comparison.

**Lemma 3.1 (three-leg cancellation).**  Let `a_i,a_j,a_k` be nonzero and
let `Q_t in H tensor V_t` for `t in {i,j,k}`.  If

\[
                   Q_u a_v+Q_v a_u=0
                   \qquad(\{u,v\}\subset\{i,j,k\}),    \tag{9}
\]

then `Q_i=Q_j=Q_k=0`.

Indeed, the equation for `(i,j)` lies in the intersection

\[
 (H\otimes V_i\otimes\langle a_j\rangle)
 \cap(H\otimes\langle a_i\rangle\otimes V_j),         \tag{10}
\]

and hence gives `Q_i=z_ij tensor a_i` and
`Q_j=-z_ij tensor a_j` for some `z_ij in H`.  Comparing the three pairs
gives `z_ij=z_ik=z_jk` from the two occurrences of `Q_i,Q_k`, while
the two occurrences of `Q_j` give

\[
                         z_{jk}=-z_{ij}.                \tag{11}
\]

so `2z_ij=0`.  Characteristic different from two proves the claim.

## 4. Proof of incident erasure

We first prove an oriented version of the lemma.  Assume `S_h != 0` and
use only the six-cell slab `K times W` from (5).  For every `x in K`, put

\[
                              T_x=q p_x.                \tag{12}
\]

By (5), `T_x` belongs to the one-dimensional annihilator in (7).  The
linear map `x mapsto T_x` from the two-plane `K` to that line has a nonzero
kernel.  Choose nonzero `x in K` with

\[
                              q p_x=0.                  \tag{13}
\]

Write `a_i=P_i x`.  At the three regular sites `a_i != 0`.

If `a_h=0`, the component of (13) on every triple `{h,i,j}` is

\[
                         q_{hi}a_j+q_{hj}a_i=0.         \tag{14}
\]

Lemma 3.1 immediately gives (6).

Suppose instead that `a_h != 0`.  The full-support kernel (8) writes every
block as `q_ij=z_ij a_i tensor a_j`.  Choose `x' in K` independent of `x`.
The cubic `q p_x'` is a scalar multiple of `Omega_S`.  Its component missing
`h`, however, lies in

\[
 \bigotimes_{i\ne h}\operatorname {span}(P_ix,P_ix'), \tag{15}
\]

whose mode ranks are at most two.  The corresponding component of a
nonzero `Omega_S` has all mode ranks three.  Therefore

\[
                              q p_{x'}=0.               \tag{16}
\]

In the component of (16) on the three regular sites, its three summands
have the `x'` direction at three different sites.  Since `P_i x` and
`P_i x'` are independent at every regular site, those summands are
linearly independent.  Thus all three `z_ij` on the regular triangle
vanish.  The three relations in (8) on triples `{h,i,j}` now say

\[
 z_{hi}+z_{hj}=0\qquad(i,j\ne h).                      \tag{17}
\]

Their odd sign cycle, and characteristic different from two, kill all
three incident scalars.  This again proves (6).

It remains to orient arbitrary exceptional maps.  If `S_h != 0`, the
preceding argument applies.  If `S_h=0` but `P_h != 0`, exchange the two
stars and use the other six-cell slab `U times L` from (5).  Finally, if
`P_h=S_h=0`, fix any nonzero `x in K`.  Equation (7) says that every
component of `q p_x` on a triple containing `h` vanishes.  Those components
are exactly (14), so Lemma 3.1 proves (6).  This completes the proof of
Lemma 2.1.

## 5. The two-`K_4` application

Assume (1).  Let `a,b` be the two left rows complementary to `r,s` and put

\[
                         c=\kappa(ab).                 \tag{18}
\]

For each left row and colour define its right-site star

\[
 p_{i,x}=\sum_{j=0}^3\operatorname {row}_x(B_{ij})^{(j)}.             \tag{19}
\]

Let `q_R` be the weighted standard right-`K_4` quadratic.  If the internal
left edge `ab` has nonzero weight `lambda_ab`, set

\[
                 q_{\rm eff}=\lambda_{ab}q_R+p_{a,c}p_{b,c}.         \tag{20}
\]

Fix the colours at `a,b` to `c`, and give `r,s` colours `x,y`.  Grouping
the two-cross and four-cross matchings gives the exact sector identity

\[
 q_{\rm eff}p_{r,x}p_{s,y}
 =\text{the complete two-/four-cross coefficient}.     \tag{21}
\]

For `(x,y)!=(c,c)`, the left word is nonconstant and `ab` is its unique
compatible internal edge.  The target and zero-cross coefficients vanish,
so the actual tensor equations give

\[
 q_{\rm eff}p_{r,x}p_{s,y}=0
                         \qquad((x,y)\ne(c,c)).         \tag{22}
\]

The two row stars are invertible at every right site other than `h`, by
(1), and both components at `h` are arbitrary.  Lemma 2.1 applies to the
eight cells (22) and yields

\[
                         (q_{\rm eff})_{hj}=0
                                  \qquad(j\ne h).       \tag{23}
\]

This is impossible.  At endpoint `h`, the three incident blocks of
`lambda_ab q_R` have the three distinct nonzero coordinate lines

\[
                         \mathbb F e_{\kappa(hj)}
                                  \qquad(j\ne h),       \tag{24}
\]

which span the three-space `V_h`.  Every incident block of the product
`p_(a,c)p_(b,c)` has its endpoint-`h` image in the fixed space

\[
 \operatorname {span}\bigl(
      \operatorname {row}_c(B_{ah})^{\mathsf T},
      \operatorname {row}_c(B_{bh})^{\mathsf T}\bigr),               \tag{25}
\]

of dimension at most two.  Equation (23) would place all three lines in
(24) inside (25), a contradiction.  This proves Theorem 1.1.

## 6. Exact-six consequence and audit

If six singular positions have row-degree partition `(2,2,1,1)` and the
two singleton rows meet the same column `h`, those two rows satisfy (1).
Theorem 1.1 excludes the support.  For example, a support meeting every
row and column is

\[
 \{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,3),(2,2),(3,2)\}.             \tag{26}
\]

Combining Theorem 1.1 with separated-defect erasure gives a sharper global
statement: no two block rows can each have degree at most one in the
singular-position graph, and the same holds for columns.  For a six-edge
support, a shore with four positive degrees would have partition
`(2,2,1,1)` or `(3,1,1,1)`, both forbidden.  Hence each shore has one
degree-zero vertex, while its other three degrees are at least two.  Their
sum is six, so both degree partitions are forced to be

\[
                              (2,2,2,0).                \tag{27}
\]

After deleting the isolated row and column, the support is a two-regular
bipartite graph on `3+3` vertices and therefore a six-cycle.  There are
exactly `96` labelled supports in this final orbit.

The checker verifies:

1. the almost-star annihilator dimensions and generalized determinant;
2. the three-leg map has Smith invariants `1` (24 times) and `2` (three
   times), so it is injective over every field of characteristic not two;
3. exact coincident-defect Hessian matrices have no kernel vector with an
   incident block, including positive-rank and zero-zero exceptional cases;
4. all `1458` coefficients of the actual sector identity (21), using both
   nonzero and zero-zero common defects, on the
   exact-six support (26); and
5. the endpoint-plane contradiction and the reduction of all `8008`
   exact-six supports to the `96` labelled six-cycles.
