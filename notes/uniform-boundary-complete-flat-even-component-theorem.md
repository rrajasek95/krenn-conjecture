# A face-complete flat component has an occurrence-centred charge

## Result

Mandatory primitive-`C4` boundary completion has a uniform finite
classification once every live boundary occurrence has a unique binomial
mate of the same Laurent character.

For each retained boundary direction `j`, let `tau_j(x)` be the mate of an
occurrence `x`.  Cancellation is symmetric, no live occurrence can mate
with itself, and uniqueness makes

\[
                    \tau_j^2=1,\qquad \tau_j(x)\ne x.  \tag{1}
\]

Thus each direction is a fixed-point-free involution, and its rows are

\[
                            e_x+e_{\tau_j(x)}.          \tag{2}
\]

Let `G` be one connected component of the union of these matchings.  Exactly
one of the following occurs.

1. `G` is nonbipartite.  It contains an odd sign circuit, so (2) has full
   column rank and gives the usual localized odd-holonomy unit.
2. `G` is bipartite.  Its row cokernel is generated by the alternating shore
   charge.  Every `tau_j` maps either shore bijectively onto the other, so
   the shores have equal cardinality.  The charge therefore has total
   augmentation zero: it is an **occurrence-centred selector**.

This closes the abstract flat-even branch under the stated face-completeness
hypothesis.  A finite component cannot retain a noncentred Fredholm charge.
That phenomenon requires a missing face, a nonunique/multiterm mate, or an
unequal Laurent character.

## Proof

After absorbing compatible Laurent monomial potentials into the occurrence
columns, all flat binomials have the signless form (2).  The standard
signless-incidence theorem gives rank `|G|` on a nonbipartite component and
rank `|G|-1` on a bipartite component.  In the latter case its unique
annihilator is

\[
 z_x=\begin{cases}+1,&x\in G_+,\\-1,&x\in G_-.
                  \end{cases}                         \tag{3}
\]

Because the edge `x--tau_j(x)` crosses the bipartition, `tau_j(G_+)` is
contained in `G_-`.  The involution is a bijection and preserves the
connected component, so

\[
               \tau_j:G_+\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}G_-.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

Consequently `|G_+|=|G_-|`, and

\[
                         \sum_{x\in G}z_x=0.            \tag{5}

This equal-shore conclusion is the new information supplied by total face
completion.  An arbitrary bipartite binomial graph need not have equal
shores.

## Why the hypotheses are sharp

### Missing totality

The path

\[
                       X_0+X_1=0,\qquad X_1+X_2=0       \tag{6}

\]

is flat and connected.  Its charge is `(1,-1,1)`, with augmentation `1`.
It is not occurrence-centred.  Equation (6) cannot be the union of total
mate involutions on its three vertices: its two endpoints are missing a
mate in one direction.  This is the smallest exact guard showing why merely
following available cancellation arrows is insufficient.

### Unequal character

A signless square has centred charge `(1,-1,1,-1)` and rank three.  Replacing
one row `X_0+X_1` by `X_0+2X_1` raises its rank to four.  The unweighted
face incidence is unchanged, but the Laurent character is no longer flat.
This is already a unit, not a counterexample to the theorem.

### Multiterm fibres

If a boundary word has two or more cancellation mates, no canonical
involution is defined.  Such a row must be retained as a higher-valence
source relation.  Choosing one mate discards data and can manufacture a
false flat component.  The theorem intentionally routes this case to the
full signed row module rather than claiming a graph reduction.

## Exact finite audit

The checker exhausts every family of one, two, or three fixed-point-free
involutions on `2`, `4`, and `6` occurrences, allowing repeated directions.
There are `835` families.  Their connected components are

```text
flat size 2 : 304
flat size 4 : 144
flat size 6 : 380
odd  size 4 :  16
odd  size 6 : 240
```

Every flat component has equal shores, rank `|G|-1`, and centred charge.
Every odd component has full rank and an explicitly reconstructed odd
cycle.  This census checks the finite move algebra; the proof above is
uniform in the component size and the number of boundary directions.

## Interface with the active-cap route

Occurrence-centred is the correct algebraic landing, but it is not by itself
a physical source switch.  There is one exact sufficient placement already
available.  If the component varies in a common physical same-star
four-port space

```text
p1, p2, s1, s2,
```

and each port is supported at at most one residual site, then

\[
 p_1^{[2]}=p_2^{[2]}=s_1^{[2]}=s_2^{[2]}=0.           \tag{7}

\]

After retaining the normalized one-bad response rows, the explicit cap

\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}  \tag{8}

\]

is active and clean by the pinned uniform square-zero theorem.  Hence the
new graph theorem produces the exact right kind of selector for that
landing: it remains only to prove common-star placement.

The complete local alternative is now:

```text
mandatory boundary occurrence
  -> no mate                         : singleton unit
  -> incompatible Laurent character : holonomy/Fitting unit
  -> total unique mates, odd graph   : odd-holonomy unit
  -> total unique mates, flat graph  : occurrence-centred selector
  -> centred selector in four ports  : active clean cap
  -> otherwise                       : physical placement remains open.
```

This is strictly stronger than the previous primitive boundary theorem: a
finite flat-even return is no longer an unstructured escape.

## Remaining source theorem

For the actual decorated-hafnian rows one must still prove:

> Every primitive-`C4` boundary component either has a missing mate, a
> nonflat character, or a multiterm/full-row exit, or else its total mate
> involutions are source-provenant and its centred charge lies in a common
> physical same-star four-port switch space.

The graph half is now finished.  The unresolved half is source placement:
tracking physical endpoint stars and matching/repeated grades through all
mandatory mates.  The global paired-Weyl telescope does not supply this,
because its occurrence profile is matching-constant.
