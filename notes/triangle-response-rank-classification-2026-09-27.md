## 1. Statement

Let a triangle have $q$ colors at each site and three nonzero complex edge
blocks. Use independent variable groups $z_1,z_2,z_3$. Write its edge
polynomials as

$$
 F_1(z_2,z_3),\quad F_2(z_1,z_3),\quad F_3(z_1,z_2),
$$

where each is bilinear in the indicated groups. The triangle response is

$$
 U(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_1F_1+x_2F_2+x_3F_3,                 \tag{1}
$$

and $x_i$ ranges over the $q$-dimensional space $L_i$ of linear forms
at site $i$. This is the usual map from the unmatched site and its color
to a triple output tensor.

**Theorem.** The kernel of $U$ has dimension zero, one, or two. These cases
are exactly as follows, allowing a permutation of the sites.

1. **Regular:** no relation of the forms below, and rank $3q$.
2. **One-dimensional kernel:** there are nonzero $x_1,x_2,z_3$ such that
   $F_1=x_2z_3$, $F_2=-x_1z_3$, but
   $F_3\notin x_1L_2+x_2L_1$. The kernel is generated by
   $(x_1,x_2,0)$, and rank is $3q-1$.
3. **Two-dimensional kernel:** for two independent tuples of site forms
   $x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)$, $y=(y_1,y_2,y_3)$,

$$
 (F_1,F_2,F_3)
  =(x_2y_3-x_3y_2,\ x_3y_1-x_1y_3,\ x_1y_2-x_2y_1).    \tag{2}
$$

   The kernel is spanned by $x,y$, and rank is $3q-2$.
   Every displayed $F_i$ is still required to be nonzero. A common
   nonzero scalar can be absorbed into one of the tuples.

In the second case, two rank-one edge blocks share the same endpoint
factor at their common site. In the third, all edge blocks have rank at
most two. Some components of $x$ or $y$ may be zero or proportional;
the statement includes scalar triangles and other degenerations.

For ternary sources, the only possible ranks are therefore **9, 8, and 7**.
No assumption of positive entries or diagonal blocks is used.

## 2. Why there cannot be three independent kernel directions

Work in the polynomial ring over all three disjoint variable groups.
The three nonzero polynomials $F_i$ have greatest common divisor one:
a common factor must be independent of group 1 since $F_1$ is, independent
of group 2 since $F_2$ is, and independent of group 3 since $F_3$ is.

Any nonzero kernel tuple has at least two nonzero components, since a tuple
with only component $i$ would give the nonzero polynomial $x_iF_i$.
If two kernel tuples $x,y$ have cross product zero, they are proportional
over the field of rational functions. For two nonzero components of $x$,
the common ratio belongs to the rational function field of either of two
disjoint variable groups. Their intersection is the constant field.
Thus $x,y$ are already proportional over $\mathbb C$.

Now take two independent kernel tuples. Over the rational function field,
their cross product is nonzero and parallel to $F=(F_1,F_2,F_3)$: both
tuples are orthogonal to $F$. Write $x\times y=hF$. In lowest terms,
the denominator of $h$ divides all three $F_i$, so it is constant.
Then $h$ is a polynomial. Every cross-product component has exactly the
same multidegree as the corresponding $F_i$, forcing $h$ to be a
nonzero constant. Rescale $y$ to obtain (2).

For any further kernel tuple $z$, the same reasoning gives
$x\times z=cF$, including $c=0$. Hence $z-cy$ has zero cross product
with $x$, so it is a constant multiple of $x$. This proves
$\dim\ker U\le2$ and proves the asserted description whenever the
kernel has dimension two.

This is an elementary argument using unique factorization and cross
products. It does not appeal to a classification inferred from the finite
examples in the replay.

## 3. A relation using all three sites forces the two-dimensional case

Suppose $x_1F_1+x_2F_2+x_3F_3=0$, with each $x_i\ne0$.
Pass to the quotient by the linear form $x_1$; it is still a polynomial
ring. In that quotient,

$$
                         x_2F_2=-x_3F_3.
$$

The forms $x_2,x_3$ involve different variable groups and are coprime.
Therefore $F_2=x_3y_1$ and $F_3=-x_2y_1$ modulo $x_1$, for a linear
form $y_1$ at site 1. Lift $y_1$ to the original ring. Multidegrees give
site forms $y_2,y_3$ such that

$$
 F_2=x_3y_1-x_1y_3,\qquad F_3=x_1y_2-x_2y_1.
$$

Substitution in the original relation gives
$F_1=x_2y_3-x_3y_2$. Thus (2) holds. Its nonzero entries ensure that $x,y$
are independent, so the kernel has dimension two.

It follows that a one-dimensional kernel must have a generator supported
on exactly two sites. Write it as $(x_1,x_2,0)$. The relation and disjoint
variable groups force $F_1=x_2z_3,F_2=-x_1z_3$, with $z_3\ne0$.
There is a second independent relation exactly when the remaining block
has the form $F_3=x_1y_2-x_2y_1$: set $y_3=z_3$ in (2). This proves
the complete classification. ∎

## 4. Consequences for balanced six-site GHZ limits

The [balancing theorem](site-balancing-rate-reduction-2026-09-26.md) shows
that a balanced six-site support without a perfect matching is precisely
two triangles with all six edge-block squared norms equal to $S/6>0$.
Let their response maps be $U,V$. At zero crossing entries,

$$
                   DH_6(A_0)[B]=UBV^{\mathsf T}.                  \tag{3}
$$

The 54 internal perturbation entries have zero derivative. The crossing
map in (3) has rank $\operatorname{rank}U\operatorname{rank}V$, by the
elementary rank formula for a tensor product of linear maps. The possible
full Jacobian ranks are therefore

| Triangle ranks | Six-site derivative rank | Invisible crossing dimensions |
|---|---:|---:|
| 9, 9 | 81 | 0 |
| 9, 8 | 72 | 9 |
| 9, 7 | 63 | 18 |
| 8, 8 | 64 | 17 |
| 8, 7 | 56 | 25 |
| 7, 7 | 49 | 32 |

Every case is attained with balanced edge-block norms; rescaling all edges
of one triangle by a common nonzero factor does not change its rank.
The replay computes the full six-site Jacobian directly for all six cases.

In particular **all balanced two-triangle limits have derivative rank at
least 49**. There is also a uniform lower bound on the 49th-largest singular
value over the normalized balanced two-triangle set. This follows from
compactness, continuity of singular values, and the everywhere rank bound.
We do not supply a numerical value for that bound, and it is not a bound
on the smallest nonzero singular value when the rank is larger than 49.

For a singular triangle, the old four-site classification says its response
image cannot contain all three pure triple words. Otherwise adjoining their
preimages as a fourth site would force a regular monochromatic triangle.
Thus if either triangle is singular,

$$
                     \Delta\notin\operatorname{im}DH_6(A_0).
$$

Writing $\eta=\|P_{\operatorname{im}(DH_6(A_0))^\perp}\Delta\|>0$,
a source at distance $\delta$ from $A_0$ consequently obeys

$$
 |\lambda|\eta\le\varepsilon+45\sqrt{S(A_0)}\,\delta^2+15\delta^3. \tag{4}
$$

Indeed, project the cubic matching expansion onto the orthogonal complement
of its linear image. There are at most 45 terms with two perturbation edges
and one base edge, and 15 terms with three perturbation edges. Each tensor
product is bounded by the product of its block Frobenius norms.
For a near-GHZ sequence, $\varepsilon=o(|\lambda|)$, so
$|\lambda|=O(\delta^2)$. This onset restriction still does not compare
error with the cube of the signal, as the rate law requires.

## 5. A balanced complete-support limit with rank 25

The rank-49 statement depends on the two-triangle support. A different
explicit family shows why supports containing perfect matchings need their
own analysis.

Use one color $a$, with $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$. On sites (0,1,2,3), put

$$
 D_{01}=D_{23}=1,\quad D_{02}=D_{13}=\omega,\quad
 D_{03}=D_{12}=\omega^2.
$$

Join each of those sites to each of (4,5) with weight $r>0$, and put
$D_{45}=-2r^2$. The six-site hafnian vanishes: the four-site core hafnian
is zero, as is the sum of its six edges. The nonzero complementary four-site
hafnians are exactly

$$
 C_{ij}=2r^2(1-D_{kl}),\quad
 \{k,l\}=\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{i,j\},
$$

on the four edges (02,03,12,13). All cofactors involving site 4 or 5 vanish.
For a ternary source with only ground-color base entries, this yields

$$
 \operatorname{rank}DH_6
   =1+2\,(\text{four active cofactor vertices})
       +4\,(\text{four active cofactor edges})=25.
$$

The contributions correspond to zero, one, and two non-ground colors in
the output and have disjoint supports. Choose

$$
 t=r^2=\frac{\sqrt{13}-1}{4}.
$$

The first four site strengths are $3+2t$; the last two are $4t+4t^2$.
They are equal because $4t^2+2t-3=0$. This is therefore a balanced
zero-output source with all fifteen edge blocks nonzero and rank 25.

The exact replay checks the rational-direction representative $r=1$.
It has the same cofactor support and rank. The finite site-balancing theorem
supplies a balanced representative with that rank: if $G$ multiplies source
entries by product-one site factors, $H(GA)=H(A)$, and differentiation gives
$DH(GA)G=DH(A)$. Thus invertible site scaling preserves Jacobian rank.
The displayed $t$ independently gives explicit balanced weights.

This ground core cannot be used for exact uniform W output, regardless of
additional colored entries: the two zero cofactor rows make the required
single-excitation coefficients at sites 4 and 5 identically zero. The
[unrestricted W response bound](w-state-unrestricted-response-bound-2026-09-27.md)
formalizes this diagnostic.
