# A saturated special fibre criterion for one-generator completion

## 1. The algebra lemma

Let `(A,m)` be a Noetherian local ring, let `tau` lie in `m`, and let `I`
be a finitely generated ideal.  Assume

```text
(I : tau) = I.
```

Let `e` belong to `I`.  If the images of `I` and `(e)` in `A/(tau)` agree,

```text
(I+(tau))/(tau) = (e,tau)/(tau),
```

then

```text
I = (e).
```

Indeed, for every `f` in `I` the special-fibre equality gives `a,b` in `A`
with

```text
f-a*e = tau*b.
```

The left side belongs to `I`.  Saturation therefore gives `b in I`, and
hence

```text
I = (e) + tau*I.
```

The finite `A`-module `I/(e)` consequently satisfies

```text
I/(e) = tau*(I/(e)).
```

Since `tau` lies in the maximal ideal, Nakayama's lemma gives `I/(e)=0`.
No regularity, reducedness, irreducibility, or explicit coefficient lift is
needed.

The same proof works in a complete local ring and after localization.  For
an ideal initially presented by generators `J`, take its stable saturation

```text
I = (J : tau^infinity).
```

The load-bearing hypothesis is the equality of the **stable** special fibre,
not equality after one finite truncation.  New initial equations can appear
when a later `tau`-colon is taken.

## 2. Membership version

Let `h` be another element of `A`, and put

```text
K = ((I,h) : tau^infinity).
```

If `K` is proper and its stable special fibre is again generated by the
image of `e`, then the lemma applied to `K` gives

```text
K=(e).
```

In particular `h in (e)=I`.  Thus ideal membership can be certified by a
stable-special-fibre calculation without constructing a conormal primitive
or a full division vector.

## 3. P5 generic-L application target

Work in the localized completed ring left after the exact 207-row Schur
block and the generic-`L` centre `L,F1,F2,G`.  Let `J` be the 28 mixed
strict transforms, let

```text
I = (J : tau^infinity),
```

and let `e` be the saturated `M30` germ.  Its current Weierstrass initial is
a localized unit times the monic polynomial `G`.

The complete P5 mixed-principality and pure-membership statements therefore
follow from the three finite equalities

```text
special_fibre(I)                         = (G),
special_fibre((I,H0):tau^infinity)       = (G),
special_fibre((I,H1):tau^infinity)       = (G),
```

together with properness of the three saturated ideals.  The first equality
gives `I=(e)`.  The other two give `H0,H1 in I`.

This is weaker computationally than printing identities

```text
M_i = a_i*M30 + tau*sum_j B_ij*M_j
```

at every filtered order.  Such identities prove the same conclusion, but
the lemma shows that only the stable initial ideal is essential.

The committed shifted S-pair is the first nontrivial positive test of this
criterion.  On the localized centre,

```text
v*M30-u*M33 in tau*(M30,M33)  (mod tau^2),
```

with the opposite sign leaving a nonzero remainder.  It proves that this
first pair does not introduce a new special-fibre generator at the next
dual order.  It does not prove that later colons stabilize, so the three
displayed stable-fibre equalities remain the exact open calculation.

## 4. Computational form

For a finite polynomial presentation, one may compute a saturation by
adjoining an auxiliary variable `T` and eliminating from

```text
J + (1-T*tau).
```

After elimination, add `tau` and compare the resulting special-fibre ideal
with `(G)`.  Equivalent colon iteration or a filtered standard basis is also
valid.  Any implementation must remain in the localized Schur/centre chart:
testing before localization mixes unrelated P5 components, while replacing
stable saturation by a fixed jet prefix is not sufficient.
