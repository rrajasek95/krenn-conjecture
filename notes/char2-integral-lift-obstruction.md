# The characteristic-two certificate forces arithmetic instability

## Outcome

The characteristic-two obstruction does **not** admit a stable-reduction
bridge of the usual kind.  At six sites its exact degree-nine certificate
has an integral lift which proves the opposite conditional statement:

> Every algebraic characteristic-zero point satisfying the ternary equality
> coefficient equations would have a negative color-balanced source
> monomial at every place above two.  Hence no target-stabilizing diagonal
> gauge, even after ramified extension and a global source scaling, could
> make all aggregate entries integral while retaining primitive GHZ output.

This conclusion is insensitive to signs and complex cancellation.  It is a
direct consequence of the nonarchimedean triangle inequality.  The integral
identity and its balanced multigrading are checked by
`computations/verify_degree9_integral_lift_obstruction.py`.

This is the relevant scope for a putative complex realization.  The fiber is
a finite-type affine scheme over `Q`; if it has a complex point, it has a
closed point over a number field.  One may then complete that number field
at any place above two.

For every even order at least six, the already proved characteristic-two
Schur reduction gives the corresponding global statement: if a
characteristic-zero realization exists, it cannot have potentially good
reduction with special output in the GHZ orbit.  This rules out automatic
good reduction as a route back from characteristic two; it does not by
itself exclude an arithmetically unstable complex realization.

## 1. Six-site coefficient polynomials

Write

\[
 z_{uv}^{ab}=A_{uv}^{ab}\qquad
 (0\leq u<v<6,\ 0\leq a,b<3)
\]

for the 135 aggregate entries.  For a coloring
`c=(c_0,...,c_5)`, let

\[
 F_c(z)=\sum_{M\in\operatorname {PM}([6])}
             \prod_{uv\in M}z_{uv}^{c_uc_v}.              \tag{1}
\]

Put

\[
 P=F_{000000}F_{111111}F_{222222}.                         \tag{2}
\]

The equality-tensor equations are

\[
 F_c=0\quad(c\text{ mixed}),\qquad
 F_{000000}=F_{111111}=F_{222222}=1.                       \tag{3}
\]

Give `z_uv^{ab}` degree one at each of the two modes `(u,a)` and
`(v,b)`.  Every term of `F_c` has degree one at `(v,c_v)` and zero at
the other two modes at `v`.  Consequently every term of `P` has degree
one at **all** 18 modes `(v,a)`.

## 2. The exact integral lift

Let

\[
 H=\langle(012),(345)\rangle
       \times\langle(012)_{\rm color}\rangle,
 \qquad |H|=27.                                           \tag{4}
\]

The complete `H`-invariant degree-nine Macaulay map used in the
characteristic-two audit has 430,206 row orbits and 162,672 column orbits.
A column is an `H`-orbit sum of

\[
                         m_DF_c,                            \tag{5}
\]

where `c` is mixed and `m_D` is a complementary degree-six monomial.
Thus every column belongs integrally to the ideal generated by the mixed
coefficient polynomials.

The saved GF(2) certificate selects 77,179 such column orbits.  Reconstruct
their unnormalized integer orbit sums.  If `C` denotes their sum, exact
coefficient comparison gives

\[
                         P-C=2R                            \tag{6}
\]

for an integral polynomial `R`.  There are 395,542 nonzero `H`-orbits in
its support; in the chosen orbit basis its coefficients range from `-12`
to `-1`.

There is a particularly useful structural fact about (6).  Index the 18
vertex/color modes by the stubs `(v,a)`.  Every row monomial in the complete
Macaulay map is a perfect matching of these 18 stubs, with no edge joining
two stubs at the same vertex.  Therefore each monomial of `R` has the form

\[
 z^\Gamma=\prod_{\{(u,a),(v,b)\}\in\Gamma}z_{uv}^{ab},     \tag{7}
\]

where `Gamma` is a nine-edge perfect matching of all 18 stubs.  In
particular,

\[
 \deg_{(v,a)}z^\Gamma=1
       \quad\text{for every }v\in[6],\ a\in[3].            \tag{8}
\]

The verifier independently checks the integer orbit normalization in (6),
the parity certificate, all 430,206 instances of (8), and the target count
`15^3`.  The orbit coefficient of a selected column at a row is reconstructed
as

\[
 \frac{\lvert\operatorname {Orb}(\text{column})\rvert}
       {\lvert\operatorname {Orb}(\text{row})\rvert}
       \times(\text{raw incidence count}),                 \tag{9}
\]

and exact divisibility is checked before (6) is accepted.  Thus reduction
modulo two is not being mistaken for an integral identity.

## 3. A forced negative balanced monomial

Let `K` be any discretely valued characteristic-zero field whose residue
field has characteristic two, and normalize its valuation only by requiring

\[
                         \nu(2)>0.                          \tag{10}
\]

Finite and ramified extensions are allowed.  Suppose, hypothetically, that
`A` satisfies (3) over `K`.  Every summand (5) vanishes at `A`, while
`P(A)=1`.  Evaluating (6) gives

\[
                         R(A)=\frac12,\qquad
                  \nu(R(A))=-\nu(2)<0.                    \tag{11}
\]

Write `R=sum_Gamma r_Gamma z^Gamma`, with
`r_Gamma in Z`.  The ultrametric inequality says

\[
 \nu(R(A))\geq
   \min_{r_\Gamma A^\Gamma\ne0}
      \bigl(\nu(r_\Gamma)+\nu(A^\Gamma)\bigr).             \tag{12}
\]

Since every nonzero integer coefficient has nonnegative valuation, (11)--
(12) force at least one `Gamma` in the support of `R` for which

\[
 \nu(A^\Gamma)=
   \sum_{\{(u,a),(v,b)\}\in\Gamma}\nu(A_{uv}^{ab})<0.      \tag{13}
\]

This argument makes no assertion that individual complex summands vanish
and uses no positivity.  Cancellation can only increase the valuation of a
sum, so it cannot invalidate (13).

The indicator of `Gamma` is exactly a color-balanced Farkas vector: by
(8), its incidence degree at `(v,a)` is one, independent of `v` for each
fixed color `a`.  Let a target-preserving diagonal gauge have valuation
parameters `t_{v,a}`.  They obey

\[
                         \sum_vt_{v,a}=0\qquad(a=0,1,2).    \tag{14}
\]

After the gauge, the valuation of the product (7) is

\[
 \nu(A^\Gamma)+\sum_{v,a}t_{v,a}=\nu(A^\Gamma)<0.          \tag{15}
\]

If every transformed aggregate entry were integral, the left side of (15)
would be nonnegative.  This contradiction proves:

For reference, this is also an exact necessary-and-sufficient test for an
arbitrary fixed point.  Put a nonnegative rational multiplicity `y_e` on
each nonzero colored source entry.  Call `y` balanced when, for every color
`a`, its incidence degree at `(v,a)` is independent of `v`.  Farkas' lemma
gives

\[
 \exists t:\quad \nu(A_{uv}^{ab})+t_{u,a}+t_{v,b}\geq0,
 \quad\sum_vt_{v,a}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_e y_e\nu(A_e)\geq0\text{ for every balanced }y\geq0. \tag{16}
\]

The matching `Gamma` forced above is an integral `0/1` witness violating
the right side.  Thus the conclusion is not based on a merely sufficient
normalization test.

**Theorem 3.1 (forced six-site instability).**  A six-site
characteristic-zero point satisfying (3), if one existed, would fail the
target-stabilizing integral-gauge linear program at every place above two.
It has a Farkas obstruction which can be chosen to be a nine-edge perfect
matching of the 18 vertex/color modes, with multiplicity exactly one at
each mode.

This is stronger than merely observing that reduction of a chosen model
might fail: the obstruction is forced by an exact integral consequence of
the full ternary coefficient equations.

## 4. Arbitrary local bases and all even orders

For `n>=3`, the projective stabilizer of the ternary GHZ tensor consists of
tuples

\[
                         D_vP,                              \tag{17}
\]

where `P` is one common color permutation and the `D_v` are diagonal, with
the product of their color-`a` diagonal entries independent of `a`.  As
proved in `notes/nonarchimedean-git-bridge.md`, if arbitrary local
`GL_3(K)` changes and a global source scaling produce a primitive integral
source whose special output is still in the GHZ orbit, an integral change
of basis moves the nonintegral part of that transformation into (16).
Primitivity of the output then turns its valuation constraints into exactly
(14).  Theorem 3.1 therefore also excludes arbitrary-local-basis good
reduction at six sites.

There is an even shorter all-order formulation.  The degree-nine
characteristic-two certificate excludes six-site equality over every
extension of `F_2`.  The transverse-Pfaffian Schur theorem in
`notes/char2-schur-reduction.md` reduces every even `n>=6` realization over
an infinite characteristic-two field to six sites.  Hence:

**Corollary 4.1 (no potentially good GHZ reduction).**  Let `n>=6` be
even.  If a characteristic-zero realization of `Delta_(n,3)` exists, then
there is no finite two-adic extension, arbitrary local `GL_3` change of
basis, and global source scaling which makes the source integral and whose
reduced matching tensor is a nonzero member of the ternary GHZ orbit.

Indeed, such a model would reduce to a characteristic-two realization;
applying the inverse reduced local bases would give `Delta_(n,3)`, contrary
to the Schur reduction and the six-site certificate.  If the residue field
is finite, first extend scalars to its infinite algebraic closure, where the
Schur theorem applies.

Thus the exact answer for this proposed bridge is negative in the only
sense useful to the conjecture: good GHZ reduction cannot occur for any
hypothetical counterexample.  Any successful arithmetic proof must extract
a contradiction directly from the forced instability (for example from a
minimal negative balanced monomial or a higher 2-adic filtration), rather
than assume that local gauges remove it.
