## Conditional sequential absorption theorem

The global C6 route is well founded under the following three source
statements.

1. **Spoke attachment:** the four-tail module acquires an endpoint component
   on a nonzero common tail, another-hole Hall mate, or a useful joint-kernel
   line hit.
2. **Exhaustivity:** its Koszul relation promotes either to proportional
   complete columns or to an isolated typed common-tail carrier.
3. **Landing:** every isolated carrier has an effective alternate pure
   matching restoring the deficient arm, or its anchor-contained Hall lock
   has a same-star kernel or complementary crossed wedge.

At fixed maximum-anchor count, order states lexicographically by

```text
(total endpoint support, number of unresolved typed base components).
```

A complete-column kernel move strictly lowers the first coordinate; a typed
exchange preserves it and lowers the second; a source unit or distinct-head
four-good pair is terminal.  This proves termination **provided** the three
statements above do not return merely by witness reselection or an unlanded
Fitting carrier.

The smallest unproved implication is statement 1: no committed theorem
forces a word-changed endpoint component to meet a nonzero selected tail.
Statements 2 and 3 remain independently necessary.  In particular, the
minimum-axis third-component guard shows that tail independence does not
restore local rank, and the full-five aggregate coloop guard shows that all
five tensor sums do not replace literal common-`q` exchange provenance.
The two sharp landing residuals are the target-coloop common-tail exchange
and the injective/no-complementary-wedge `M3` Hall lock.

The theorem needed to control this interface uniformly remains
spoke-to-hole synchronization: use the unary top and full response tensors
to force one of (3)--(4), a joint-kernel target-line move, a unit, or a free
carrier.  Only after a column exists can complete-column proportionality,
Fitting, and Hall landing be applied.
