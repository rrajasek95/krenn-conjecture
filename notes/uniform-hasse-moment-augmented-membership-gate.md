# The formal Hasse tower closes algebraically; physical carrier descent is the first gap

## Outcome

There is a positive source-faithful construction of the **formal** response
orders (2,ldots,h): take the complete principal-parts/Hasse--Schmidt
totalization of the complete source equations.  Hasse translation is an
algebra map,

\[
 D^{[j]}(fg)=\sum_{a+b=j}D^{[a]}f\,D^{[b]}g,             \tag{1}
\]

and the reduced Hasse coproduct has its canonical alternating cobar
totalization.  Thus no response layer has to be added as an unsupported
coefficient.  At the first two orders the resulting clean shadows are

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 h&\text{formal clean response layers}\\ \hline
 3&B_2,B_3,\\
 4&B_2,B_3,B_4,
 \end{array}
 \qquad B_j=q^{[h-j]}r^{[j]}.                            \tag{2}
\]

This does **not** yet produce the physical Hilbert--Cauchy moment tower.
The first missing physical statement is already

\[
 c_0=(r-2q)H_0=0,
 \qquad
 H_0=\int_0^1(q+tr)^{[h-2]},dt,                        \tag{3}
\]

in one physical degree-((h-1)) augmented module.  The committed normal
jet constructs (H_0), but does not prove (3).  At both (h=3) and
(h=4), the next missing moment is

\[
 c_1=(r-2q)H_1=0,
 \qquad
 H_1=\int_0^1t(q+tr)^{[h-2]},dt,                       \tag{4}
\]

with the same affine density, endpoint ordering, carrier, and physical
restriction map.  Consequently, going directly to third or fourth Hasse
response order does not bypass the gap: formal response order and moment
index are different filtrations.

After (3)--(4) are granted, the scalar Hilbert--Cauchy calculation closes
both (h=3) and (h=4) exactly.  The remaining terminal/(q) question is
a finite augmented column-membership test, recorded below.

## 1. Formal source provenance versus physical descent

Let (I) be the ideal generated by the complete source rows.  The
principal-parts resolution retains every coefficient of the translated
rows and every product-rule face.  Its alternating coproduct makes the
higher faces a chain, so (B_2,ldots,B_h) in (2) are source-labelled in
that resolution.

This statement has a precise boundary.  A principal-parts chain is not
automatically an ordinary physical correction.  To use the moment theorem,
one needs a comparison to one physical augmented module (Q) in which:

1. the clean row (u=\sum_{j=2}^hB_j) vanishes in (Q_h), while the
   adjacent class (x=B_0+B_1) remains represented;
2. both (3) and (4) vanish in (Q_{h-1});
3. ordinary multiplication by (q) and (r) defines chain maps
   (Q_{h-1}\to Q_h); and
4. protected target, word, fine, repeated-response, anchor, terminal, and
   physical (q)-cocycle readouts are all carried by that same comparison.

The existing Hasse totalization proves the source-labelled part before
this comparison.  It does not prove items 2--4.  In particular, integrating
a formal path coefficient or multiplying a coefficient-level relation by
(q,r) is not silently a physical chain operation.

## 2. Exact (h=3) coefficient calculation

Use the degree-three divided-power basis

\[
              (q^{[3]},q^{[2]}r,qr^{[2]},r^{[3]}).      \tag{5}
\]

In column order

\[
                         (u,qc_0,rc_0,qc_1,rc_1),       \tag{6}
\]

the scalar-shadow matrix has columns

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=(0,0,1,1),\\
qc_0&=(-12,0,1,0),&rc_0&=(0,-4,0,3),\\
qc_1&=(-6,-\tfrac13,\tfrac23,0),
&rc_1&=(0,-2,-\tfrac13,2).
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{7}
\]

For (x=(1,1,0,0)), one particular solution of (A_3z=x) is

\[
 z_3^0=
 \left(\frac7{20},\frac{43}{60},-\frac7{60},-\frac85,0\right). \tag{8}
\]

The complete affine freedom is

\[
 z_3(t)=z_3^0+t
       \left(0,-1,-\frac23,2,1\right).                  \tag{9}
\]

Thus the scalar moment theorem leaves exactly one adjustable parameter at
(h=3).  It is useful physical freedom, not an ambiguity to discard: the
terminal/(q) rows may choose (t), if their defect lies on this one
line.  If not, a physical correction column is required.

The initial moment (H_0) alone does not span (x).  Hence even after a
physical proof of (3), relation (4) is genuinely necessary at (h=3).

## 3. Exact (h=4) coefficient calculation

Use the degree-four basis ordered by increasing (r)-degree.  In the same
column order (6),

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=(0,0,1,1,1),\\
qc_0&=(-24,-3,\tfrac23,1,0),
&rc_0&=(0,-6,-2,1,4),\\
qc_1&=(-12,-\tfrac52,\tfrac13,\tfrac34,0),
&rc_1&=(0,-3,-\tfrac53,\tfrac12,3).
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{10}
\]

The matrix is square and invertible.  Its unique representation of
(x=(1,1,0,0,0)) is

\[
 z_4=
 \left(\frac18,-\frac5{12},-\frac34,
                   \frac34,\frac{23}{24}\right).       \tag{11}
\]

Therefore (h=4) has no scalar adjustment freedom.  Once candidate
physical lifts are chosen, their combined augmented defect is forced:

\[
 \delta_4=\widetilde x-left(
 \frac18\widetilde u-\frac5{12}\widetilde{qc_0}
 -\frac34\widetilde{rc_0}+\frac34\widetilde{qc_1}
 +\frac{23}{24}\widetilde{rc_1}\right).                \tag{12}
\]

The construction closes exactly when (delta_4) is zero or belongs to
the image of already physical zero-shadow correction columns.  Equation
(12) is the smallest exact (h=4) target for a source computation.

## 4. Finite augmented membership criterion

Let (P_h) be the finite physical augmented output space in the selected
word/fine/repeated grade.  Its rows must include the scalar binary shadow
and, literally rather than after aggregation:

\[
 \text{protected target},\ D/W,\ \text{anchor},\
 \eta/\sigma,\ \text{terminal},\ q\text{-cocycle}.      \tag{13}
\]

Let

\[
 A_h^{\rm aug}=
 [\widetilde u,\widetilde{qc_0},\widetilde{rc_0},
             \widetilde{qc_1},\widetilde{rc_1}]         \tag{14}
\]

and let (C_h) contain the available physical correction columns with
zero scalar shadow.  Then the whole positive replacement is equivalent to
the finite criterion

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname {rank}[A_h^{\rm aug}\ C_h]
 =\operatorname {rank}[A_h^{\rm aug}\ C_h\mid\widetilde x].} \tag{15}
\]

At (h=3), (15) is equivalently the existence of a scalar (t) for which
the augmented defect defined by (8)--(9) belongs to (operatorname{im}C_3).
At (h=4), it is exactly the membership of (12) in
(operatorname{im}C_4).

This formulation keeps physical (q) honest.  A scalar-shadow relation
with an undefined (q)-row is not a solution of (15).  If (q) is a
cocycle only on a protected kernel, that kernel is first imposed as rows
of (P_h); only then may the terminal generator/Fredholm alternative be
applied to a nonzero defect.  The algebraic moment theorem alone does not
promote such a defect.

## 5. Sharp next source lemma

The shortest constructive lemma is not a new all-order coefficient
identity.  It is the following two-moment comparison, uniform in (h=3,4):

> Construct physical lifts of (c_0,c_1) in one degree-((h-1)) augmented
> module, with legal (q,r) multiplication, such that the five columns in
> (14) have all readouts (13).  Then verify (15).

The first test should be (3).  Failure there produces a primitive dual to
the physical carrier relation before any (H_1) or higher response order
is relevant.  If (3) succeeds, (4) is the next and last new moment needed
at both (h=3) and (h=4).  The formal orders in (2) can then be imported
from the canonical principal-parts totalization.
