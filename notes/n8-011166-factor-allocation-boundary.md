# The crossed-double factor boundary on the `011166` row

## 1. Outcome

On the last five-witness row

\[
                         (0,1,1,1,6,6),                 \tag{1}
\]

the forced `p`- and `q`-anchors at the two exact `{1,2}` sites are
crossed.  Eliminating the common scalar from their two-hole binary identity
gives, after absorbing the three singleton cross-coordinate ratios into a
nonzero constant `kappa`,

\[
 \alpha _1\beta _1\gamma _{k,1}\,C B
   =\kappa\,\alpha _2\beta _2\gamma _{k,2}\,A D .       \tag{2}
\]

Here `A,C` are nonzero `alpha`-linear forms, `B,D` are nonzero
`beta`-linear forms, and `k` is the unique site with no zero-cross colour.

Factoriality of the incidence quadric gives a complete allocation
dichotomy:

1. `gamma_(k,1)` and `gamma_(k,2)` are associates.  Then necessarily
   \[
      A\sim\alpha _1,\quad C\sim\alpha _2,
      \quad B\sim\beta _2,\quad D\sim\beta _1.          \tag{3}
   \]
2. Otherwise both cross coordinates split,
   \[
       \gamma _{k,i}=\ell_i(\alpha)m_i(\beta),           \tag{4}
   \]
   and the two exact multiset identities are
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
    \{\alpha _1,C,\ell _1\}&=\{\alpha _2,A,\ell _2\},\\
    \{\beta _1,B,m_1\}&=\{\beta _2,D,m_2\},            \tag{5}
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   up to associates and with multiplicity.

Neither branch contradicts the nondegenerate cyclic staircase at `k` or
invertibility of `A_pq`.  Two exact integral models below realize the full
mask row (1), the staircase identity, and the uncancelled two-hole factor
identity.  Thus UFD allocation alone cannot finish the `011166` row; a
continuation must retain the shared five-hole cofactors (or another sector
of the full matching identity).

## 2. Derivation of the crossed identity

Let the two exact-double sites be `u,v`.  After possibly exchanging them,
the forced anchors have the form

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &p&q\\ \hline
u&Ae_1&Be_2\\
v&Ce_2&De_1,
\end{array}                                               \tag{6}
\]

where the table records the contracted star vectors.  Equivalently, for
constant column vectors `a,b,c,d`,

\[
 A_{pu}=ae_1^T,\quad A_{qu}=be_2^T,
 \quad A_{pv}=ce_2^T,\quad A_{qv}=de_1^T,                \tag{7}
\]

and

\[
 A=\alpha^Ta,\quad B=\beta^Tb,
 \quad C=\alpha^Tc,\quad D=\beta^Td.                    \tag{8}
\]

The two-hole correction on `u,v` is diagonal on the active plane:

\[
 R_{uv}|_{\langle e_1,e_2\rangle}
             =\operatorname {diag}(AD,BC).              \tag{9}
\]

Contract the three exact singleton-0 sites and `k` by their cross-product
covectors.  Both active target coefficients are nonzero.  If `h` is the
common residual scalar, the two diagonal equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \alpha _1\beta _1\gamma _{k,1}
       \prod_s\gamma _{s,1}&=hAD,\\
 \alpha _2\beta _2\gamma _{k,2}
       \prod_s\gamma _{s,2}&=hBC.                       \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]

Eliminating `h` gives (2), with

\[
       \kappa=\prod_s\gamma _{s,2}/\gamma _{s,1}.       \tag{11}
\]

In the anchor normalization used below every ratio in (11) is `-1`, so
`kappa=-1`.  More generally the preceding row-lock calculation makes the
singleton factors on the two sides associates; their nonzero scalar ratio
is all that is used in (2).

## 3. Factoriality and complete factor allocation

Put

\[
 S=\mathbb C[\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,
             \beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2]/(g),
 \qquad g=\alpha^TA_{pq}\beta,                           \tag{12}
\]

where `A_pq` is invertible.  Give `S` the bidegrees
`deg(alpha_i)=(1,0)` and `deg(beta_i)=(0,1)`.

**Lemma 3.1.**  The ring `S` is a UFD.  Its only units are nonzero
constants.  Every nonzero `alpha`- or `beta`-linear form is prime.  A
reducible nonzero homogeneous element of bidegree `(1,1)` is, up to a
constant, a product of one `alpha`-linear and one `beta`-linear form.

**Proof.**  Independent invertible changes in `alpha,beta` reduce `g` to
`sum_i alpha_i beta_i`.  This hypersurface is normal: it is
Cohen--Macaulay, and its singular locus is only the origin, of codimension
five.  After inverting `alpha_0`, solve for `beta_0`; the localization is a
Laurent polynomial ring and hence factorial.  The quotient by `alpha_0`
is

\[
 \mathbb C[\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2]
                  /(\alpha_1\beta_1+\alpha_2\beta_2),   \tag{13}
\]

a domain.  Thus `(alpha_0)` is the only height-one prime over
`alpha_0`.  Nagata's theorem says that the divisor class group of `S` is
generated by this principal prime, so it is zero.  Normality then makes
`S` a UFD.

The positive bigrading gives only constant units.  A form of bidegree
`(1,0)` or `(0,1)` has no nontrivial homogeneous factorization and hence is
prime in the UFD.  Finally, irreducible factors of a bihomogeneous element
may be chosen bihomogeneous; the only nontrivial decomposition of `(1,1)`
into nonnegative bidegrees is `(1,0)+(0,1)`. `QED`

**Lemma 3.2 (allocation dichotomy).**  Every instance of (2) has exactly
one of the following forms: the cross coordinates are associates and (3)
holds, or they are nonassociate, both split, and (4)--(5) hold.

**Proof.**  All displayed pure linear factors in (2) are prime.  If the
two cross coordinates are associates, cancel them.  This leaves

\[
 \{\alpha_1,C\}=\{\alpha_2,A\},\qquad
 \{\beta_1,B\}=\{\beta_2,D\}.                           \tag{14}
\]

Since `alpha_1` and `alpha_2` are not associates, and likewise for the two
`beta` coordinates, (14) is exactly (3).

Now suppose the two cross coordinates are not associates.  Neither can be
irreducible: an irreducible bidegree-`(1,1)` factor on one side could match
only the other bidegree-`(1,1)` factor, making them associates.  Thus both
are reducible.  Lemma 3.1 gives (4), and unique factorization separated by
bidegree gives precisely (5). `QED`

There is a convenient allocation test inside the split branch.  The first
line of (5) is soluble in the variable factors `A,C` if and only if the
fixed multisets

\[
             \{\alpha_1,\ell_1\},\qquad
             \{\alpha_2,\ell_2\}                        \tag{15}
\]

have a common associate.  After cancelling a chosen common factor, `A`
must equal the remaining left factor and `C` the remaining right factor.
The second line has the identical criterion for
`{beta_1,m_1}` and `{beta_2,m_2}`, with `D` equal to the remaining left
factor and `B` to the remaining right factor.  This also handles repeated
factors when interpreted as multiset cancellation.

## 4. Staircase models for both branches

Use the normalized staircase orientation

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q=A_{qk}&=E_{00}+e_1u^T+ve_2^T,\\
 P=A_{pk}&=E_{11}-e_0u^T+we_2^T,\\
 G=A_{pq}&=E_{22}-e_0v^T-we_1^T.                        \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
\]

It satisfies, coefficient by coefficient,

\[
 e_0^{(p)}\otimes Q+e_1^{(q)}\otimes P+e_2^{(k)}\otimes G
                       =\Delta_{\{p,q,k\},3},           \tag{17}
\]

and `det G=v_0w_1`.  Write

\[
 C_r=PK_rQ^T,\qquad
 \gamma_{k,r}=\alpha^TC_r\beta.                         \tag{18}
\]

For the associate branch take

\[
       u=0,\qquad v=e_0,\qquad w=e_1.                   \tag{19}
\]

Then

\[
 G=\operatorname {diag}(-1,-1,1),\qquad
 C_0=C_1=E_{10},\qquad C_2=-E_{10}.                     \tag{20}
\]

Thus `G` is invertible, `k` has no zero-cross colour, and
`gamma_(k,1)=-gamma_(k,2)=alpha_1 beta_0`.  Choose

\[
 A=\alpha_1,\quad C=\alpha_2,
 \quad B=\beta_2,\quad D=\beta_1.                       \tag{21}
\]

This is exactly (3), and (2) holds with `kappa=-1`.

For the split branch take instead

\[
       u=0,\qquad v=e_0,\qquad w=e_0+e_1.               \tag{22}
\]

Now

\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad C_0=E_{10},
 \quad C_1=(e_0+e_1)e_0^T,
 \quad C_2=-e_1e_0^T.                                  \tag{23}
\]

Again `det G=1` and every `C_r` is nonzero, while

\[
 \gamma_{k,1}=(\alpha_0+\alpha_1)\beta_0,
 \qquad \gamma_{k,2}=-\alpha_1\beta_0.                 \tag{24}
\]

The fixed alpha multisets share `alpha_1`, and the fixed beta multisets
share `beta_0`.  The allocation rule gives

\[
 A=\alpha_0+\alpha_1,\quad C=\alpha_2,
 \quad B=\beta_2,\quad D=\beta_1,                       \tag{25}
\]

and (2) again holds with `kappa=-1`.

To realize the three singleton masks and the value of `kappa`, use row
pairs

\[
 (e_0,(1,1,1)),\qquad ((1,1,1),e_0),
 \qquad ((1,1,1),(2,1,1)).                              \tag{26}
\]

Their cross products are respectively

\[
                  (0,-1,1),\quad(0,1,-1),\quad(0,1,-1), \tag{27}
\]

so all three masks are exactly `{0}` and every ratio
`gamma_(s,2)/gamma_(s,1)` is `-1`.  Use the row pairs `(e_1,e_2)` and
`(e_2,e_1)` at the two double sites, with left factors chosen from
(21) or (25).  Their masks are exactly `{1,2}` and their anchors are
crossed as in (6).  This realizes the entire mask multiset in (1) and
makes the unabsorbed identity in (10) hold as an ordinary polynomial
identity, not merely modulo `g`.

These are factor-boundary models, not full five-hole responses: no common
internal edge family or residual hafnian `h` has been supplied.  Their
purpose is exact and limited: neither UFD branch can be rejected from the
staircase determinant, nonwitness condition, crossed anchors, or pure-factor
bookkeeping alone.
