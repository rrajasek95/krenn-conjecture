# A uniform target-incidence invariant for all nine pair caps

## 1. Outcome

Let \(U\) be a ternary boundary of size \(2m\), with \(m\geq3\), and work
in the site-square-zero algebra

\[
 {\cal R}_U=\bigotimes_{u\in U}(\mathbb C\oplus V_u),
 \qquad V_u=\operatorname{span}\{e_0^{(u)},e_1^{(u)},e_2^{(u)}\}.
\]

Suppose a quadratic \(q=\sum_{u<v}q_{uv}\), two triples of arbitrary
linear forms \(p_i,s_j\), and an arbitrary matrix \(a=(a_{ij})\) satisfy
the full nine pair-cap equations

\[
 a_{ij}Q+p_i s_jF=\delta_{ij}X_i,
 \qquad
 F=q^{[m-1]},\quad Q=q^{[m]},\quad
 X_i=\bigotimes_{u\in U}e_i^{(u)}.                    \tag{1}
\]

This is equivalently the polarized normalization

\[
 (a_{ij}q+m p_i s_j)q^{[m-1]}=m\delta_{ij}X_i,        \tag{2}
\]

because \(q q^{[m-1]}=m q^{[m]}\).  Thus at eight boundary sites the
factor in (2) is exactly \(4\), and division by \(4\) gives (1).

For each site define the complete incident endpoint span

\[
 W_u=\sum_{v\ne u}\operatorname{im}_u(q_{uv})\subseteq V_u,
 \qquad
 D_i=\{u:e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\}.                           \tag{3}
\]

Then the following statements hold without any support, invertibility, or
noncancellation hypothesis:

\[
 \boxed{|D_i|\geq2m-2\quad(i=0,1,2),\qquad
        D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2=U.}                        \tag{4}
\]

Let \(n_r\) be the number of sites which belong to exactly \(r\) of the
sets \(D_0,D_1,D_2\).  The determinant part of (4) gives \(n_0=0\), and
the three ideal-order bounds give

\[
 \boxed{n_3\geq n_1+2m-6.}                             \tag{5}
\]

In particular, at least \(2m-6\) sites contain the complete target frame.
For the eight-site boundary this says more sharply

\[
                         n_3\geq n_1+2,                 \tag{6}
\]

so every survivor of all nine equations has at least two target-full
internal sites.  The assertion is about the full incident span at a site;
it does not assert that one physical block \(q_{uv}\) has rank three.

This uniformly extends the incidence portion of
[the six-site sitewise filtration](sitewise-common-power-response-filtration.md).
At \(m=3\), (4) is its four-cover and site-cover conclusion.  Here the
proof also permits the direct term \(a_{ij}Q\), and the same argument works
at every boundary order.  The six-site note's later pure-lift and
coordinate-plane conclusions are not claimed here.

## 2. Scalarization and the common cofactor matrix

Choose arbitrary covectors \(\ell_u\in V_u^*\).  Write

\[
 x_i=\prod_{u\in U}\ell_u(e_i^{(u)}),
 \qquad
 Q(\ell)=\left\langle\bigotimes_u\ell_u,Q\right\rangle. \tag{7}
\]

Resolve the two stars sitewise,

\[
 p_i=\sum_up_{i,u},\qquad s_j=\sum_us_{j,u},
 \qquad
 P_{iu}=\ell_u(p_{i,u}),\quad S_{ju}=\ell_u(s_{j,u}).   \tag{8}
\]

Define the symmetric zero-diagonal \(2m\) by \(2m\) cofactor matrix

\[
 C_{uv}(\ell)=
 \left\langle\bigotimes_{w\ne u,v}\ell_w,
       q^{[m-1]}\big|_{U\setminus\{u,v\}}\right\rangle
 \quad(u\ne v),\qquad C_{uu}=0.                         \tag{9}
\]

Both endpoint orders in \(p_i s_j\) are retained by ordinary matrix
multiplication:

\[
 [p_i s_jF]_{\ell}=\sum_{u\ne v}P_{iu}C_{uv}S_{jv},
 \qquad M(\ell)=PCS^{\mathsf T}.                       \tag{10}
\]

Consequently all nine tensor equations (1) become the single exact scalar
matrix identity

\[
                \boxed{M(\ell)=\operatorname{diag}(x_0,x_1,x_2)
                                  -Q(\ell)a.}           \tag{11}
\]

No coefficient of \(q\), \(F\), or a response is selected as a nonzero
term in this passage.

## 3. The cancellation-safe ideal orders

Regard all entries in (7)--(10) as polynomials in the covector
coordinates.  For each \(u\), let \(I_u\) be the linear ideal generated by
the functions

\[
                  \ell_u\longmapsto\ell_u(w),
                  \qquad w\in W_u,                     \tag{12}
\]

and put \(J=\sum_u I_u\).  These are ideals in a polynomial ring; in
particular every \(I_u\) and \(J\) is prime after a linear change of
coordinates.

Every monomial of an entry \(C_{uv}\) is a product of \(m-1\) edge-block
coefficients.  It therefore contains exactly \(2m-2\) endpoint factors
drawn from the corresponding spaces \(W_w\).  The two star factors in
(10) can only increase, never decrease, the \(J\)-adic order.  Hence

\[
                    M_{ij}\in J^{\,2m-2}.               \tag{13}
\]

Likewise every monomial of \(Q=q^{[m]}\) is a perfect matching on all of
\(U\), so

\[
                    Q\in J^{\,2m},\qquad Q\in I_u
                    \quad\hbox{for every }u.            \tag{14}
\]

The diagonal entries of (11) now give

\[
                    x_i=M_{ii}+a_{ii}Q\in J^{\,2m-2}.   \tag{15}
\]

The exact \(J\)-adic order of \(x_i\) is \(|D_i|\).  Indeed,
\(\ell_u(e_i)\in J\) exactly when \(e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\).  After choosing
coordinates adapted to all \(W_u\), the factors not in \(J\) remain
nonzero in the polynomial quotient by \(J\), while the associated graded
ring along the linear prime \(J\) is a domain.  Thus distinct terms in
(15) may cancel arbitrarily, but the ideal membership still forces

\[
                              |D_i|\geq2m-2.             \tag{16}
\]

This proves the first part of (4) without inferring that any matching term
or response summand is individually nonzero.

## 4. The full determinant forces the site cover

Cauchy--Binet applied to (10) gives

\[
 \det M=\sum_{\substack{I,K\subset U\\|I|=|K|=3}}
            \det P_I\,\det C_{I,K}\,\det S_K.          \tag{17}
\]

Fix a site \(u\).  Expand one determinant monomial in a \(3\) by \(3\)
minor \(C_{I,K}\), and then choose one matching monomial from each of its
three cofactor factors.  A factor \(C_{ik}\) contains a \(q\)-endpoint
factor at \(u\) unless \(u=i\) or \(u=k\).  Across the three factors, the
number whose internal matching covers \(u\) is therefore

\[
                  3-\mathbf1_{u\in I}-\mathbf1_{u\in K}\geq1. \tag{18}
\]

If the two exclusions occur in the same factor, that factor is
\(C_{uu}=0\), so the purported determinant monomial is zero; otherwise
(18) is literal.  Hence every nonzero fully expanded monomial of every
\(\det C_{I,K}\) contains a generator of \(I_u\).  It follows from (17),
without selecting any one minor, that

\[
                              \det M\in I_u              \tag{19}
\]

for every site \(u\), uniformly in \(m\).

Put \(D=\operatorname{diag}(x_0,x_1,x_2)\).  Direct determinant expansion
of (11) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \det(D-Qa)={}&x_0x_1x_2
 -Q(a_{00}x_1x_2+a_{11}x_0x_2+a_{22}x_0x_1)\\
 &+Q^2\sum_{i=0}^2\operatorname{Cof}_{ii}(a)x_i
 -Q^3\det a.                                           \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]

Every term after the first belongs to \(I_u\) by (14), while the left side
belongs to \(I_u\) by (19).  Therefore

\[
                              x_0x_1x_2\in I_u.          \tag{21}
\]

The quotient by the linear prime \(I_u\) is a polynomial domain, and
linear forms at sites different from \(u\) do not lie in \(I_u\).  Thus
(21) forces \(\ell_u(e_i)\in I_u\) for some \(i\), equivalently
\(e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\).  This proves the site cover in (4).

Finally, (16) gives

\[
 n_1+2n_2+3n_3=\sum_i|D_i|\geq6m-6,                    \tag{22}
\]

while the site cover gives \(n_1+n_2+n_3=2m\).  Subtracting twice the
second equality from (22) yields (5).

## 5. Translation to a hypothetical source and overlapping pairs

Let an exact ternary source have \(N=2m+2\) physical sites.  Delete any
ordered physical pair \(r,t\), orient all blocks toward their named
endpoints, and let \(q^{rt}\) be the quadratic on
\(U=B\setminus\{r,t\}\).  Sorting perfect matchings according to whether
they use \(rt\) gives (1) exactly.  Therefore, for every deleted pair:

* each target colour belongs to the internal incident span at all but at
  most two sites of \(U\);
* every internal site contains at least one target axis; and
* at least \(N-8\) internal sites contain all three target axes.

For \(N=10\), every pair deletion therefore exposes at least two complete
target-frame sites.  More generally the number grows linearly with the
order.

This has an exact overlap interpretation.  If \(u\in U\) is target-full,
the map

\[
 T_u:V_u^*\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in U\setminus\{u\}}V_v,
 \qquad
 \alpha\longmapsto
 \sum_{v\ne u}(\alpha\otimes\operatorname{id})q^{rt}_{uv} \tag{23}
\]

is injective.  In the cap for the overlapping physical pair \(r,u\), the
three \(u\)-star rows contain (23) as their component on
\(U\setminus\{u\}\); the additional component at \(t\) cannot destroy
injectivity because the site summands form a direct sum.  The same holds
for the pair \(t,u\).  Thus every target-full site forced by (5) produces
two overlapping pair-cap systems in which the newly deleted endpoint has
an aggregate rank-three star.

There is also a useful global count.  For distinct physical sites \(r,u\),
put

\[
 S_u^{(r)}=\sum_{v\notin\{r,u\}}\operatorname{im}_u(A_{uv}). \tag{24}
\]

For fixed \(r\), let \(b_r\) count the \(u\ne r\) for which
\(S_u^{(r)}\ne V_u\).  If such a pair \((r,u)\) is deficient, then \(u\)
is deficient in the internal span after deleting \(r,t\) for every
\(t\notin\{r,u\}\).  Since each deletion \(r,t\) has at most six
non-target-full internal sites, double counting \((t,u)\) gives

\[
                  b_r(N-2)\leq6(N-1).                  \tag{25}
\]

Hence \(b_r\leq6\) for every \(N\geq10\).  There are at most \(6N\)
directed deficient pairs.  If every unordered pair had a deficient side,
there would be at least \(N(N-1)/2\) such directed pairs.  Consequently:

\[
 \boxed{N\geq14\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{some physical pair has aggregate rank-three stars at both ends}.} \tag{26}
\]

The word **aggregate** is essential in (23)--(26).  Injectivity of the
three complete star rows does not say that every colour row of every
individual endpoint block is nonzero.  Therefore (26) does not directly
meet the stronger blockwise hypothesis of the connected rank-three
source-Hessian theorem.  Likewise, the closed six-site rank-budget-twelve
theorems concern a clean/base-locus response after the direct \(aQ\) term
has disappeared; (1) retains that term.  The precise remaining overlap
gate is to combine two caps sharing \(r\), whose other endpoint stars are
injective by (23), to force either a clean cap or the blockwise hypotheses
of an existing Hessian obstruction.  The present invariant supplies the
uniform input to that gate but does not silently claim the conversion.

## 6. Exact audit

The standalone checker
[verify_uniform_full_nine_target_incidence_invariant.py](../computations/verify_uniform_full_nine_target_incidence_invariant.py)

* verifies the divided-power normalization in (2);
* verifies the symbolic determinant expansion (20);
* enumerates every nonzero Cauchy--Binet cofactor-minor monomial for
  boundary sizes \(6,8,10,12\) and checks the uniform site multiplicity
  (18);
* exhausts the possible incidence-count quadruples and checks (5); and
* audits the directed-pair double count and threshold in (25)--(26).

The checker is a finite audit of the displayed algebra and combinatorial
ledger.  The proof above, rather than the finite range of the checker, is
the uniform argument.
