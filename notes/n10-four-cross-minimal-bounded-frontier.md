# A bounded four-cross frontier on the anchored N=10 lift

## Outcome

The first unresolved fixed-old support size is four cross coordinates.  The
complete topology and permanent-incidence census is now exact, but the
literal cylinder calculation is exhaustive only on the smallest structured
old-node families.

Modulo the forced-pair symmetry exchanging new vertices 8 and 9, the
four-cell support orbits are:

| support split | number of orbits | status |
|---|---:|---|
| \(4+0\) or \(0+4\) | 1,028,790 | permanent zero; excluded by the earlier arbitrary-support theorem |
| \(1+3\) or \(3+1\) | 4,294,080 | general determinantal family still open |
| \(2+2\) | 3,267,846 | general determinantal family still open |

There are 2,556 \(2+2\) supports fixed by the new-vertex swap.  The orbit
count is therefore

\[
 \frac{\binom{72}{2}^2+\binom{72}{2}}2=3,267,846.       \tag{1}
\]

For the tractable family in which all cells on each new vertex share one
old endpoint-colour node, the exact result is stronger:

1. all 1,512 old-node stars fail already in the enlarged affine cut-2
   quotient;
2. among 4,536 old-node rectangles, only 14 pass the enlarged quotient;
3. those 14 form seven forced-pair-swap orbits; and
4. a literal evaluated 21-column determinant excludes all seven for every
   nonzero choice of their four weights.

Thus this natural four-cell repair family is closed.  No N=10 source and no
Krenn counterexample was found.  The remaining problem is the unstructured
four-cell determinantal variety stated below.

## 1. Support topology and permanent incidence

Write the 72 coordinates incident to vertex 8 as \(X\)-coordinates and the
72 coordinates incident to vertex 9 as \(Y\)-coordinates.  A quadratic
permanent class requires one coordinate on each side and distinct old
endpoints.

For a \(1+3\) star, the singleton coordinate has 63 valid opposite
coordinates and nine which repeat its old endpoint.  Therefore the number
of forced-pair-swap orbits producing \(k\) distinct permanent classes is

\[
 72\binom{63}{k}\binom9{3-k}.                          \tag{2}
\]

The exact census is:

| permanent classes \(k\) | star orbits |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 6,048 |
| 1 | 163,296 |
| 2 | 1,265,544 |
| 3 | 2,859,192 |

The cases \(k=0\) are covered by permanent-zero exclusion, and \(k=1,2\)
were covered by the preceding one- and sharing-two-class theorem.  Only the
three-class star stratum is genuinely new at four cells.

For the \(2+2\) supports the checker enumerates all
\(\binom{72}{2}^2=6,533,136\) literal rectangles.  Endpoint collisions and
endpoint-swap recombination give:

| distinct permanent classes | literal rectangles |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 10,368 |
| 1 | 2,268 |
| 2 | 508,032 |
| 3 | 2,204,496 |
| 4 | 3,807,972 |

This census is support-theoretic.  It does not identify sources satisfying
the cut equations.

## 2. Exact affine necessary system

On fixed cut 2 retain the universal lower-degree space

\[
 {\cal U}^{\rm lin}_2
 =\operatorname{span}\{C_2^{(0)},L_{2,e}:e\text{ any cross coordinate}\},
 \qquad \dim{\cal U}^{\rm lin}_2=126.                  \tag{3}
\]

For permanent classes \(p_1,\ldots,p_s\) generated by a four-cell support,
enlarge this to

\[
 {\cal V}_{P}={\cal U}^{\rm lin}_2+
   \operatorname{span}\{Q_{2,p_j;h,i}:j,h,i\}.          \tag{4}
\]

If the evaluated source has permanent coefficients
\(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_s\), literal cylinder membership implies

\[
 R_2^{(0)}+\sum_j\pi_jD_{2,p_j}\in{\cal V}_{P},         \tag{5}
\]

together with the pure-anchor equations

\[
 \sum_j\pi_j(D_{p_j})_{c^{10}}=0,\qquad c=0,1,2.       \tag{6}
\]

The checker reduces (5)--(6) exactly over the rationals.  This is a
necessary relaxation: it lets every linear and quadratic labelled
cofactor direction vary independently, while an actual source has only 21
evaluated columns.

There are 1,805 exact individual grade-data signatures among the 2,268
permanent classes.  Their multiplicities are

\[
 1^{1770},\ 8^4,\ 9^{25},\ 26^1,\ 27^2,\ 53^1,\ 54^2. \tag{7}
\]

This records that the anchored old source has little residual symmetry; the
only support symmetry used in the orbit counts is the exact swap of the
forced pair.

## 3. Structured stars and rectangles

An old-node star fixes one old endpoint-colour node on its singleton side
and a second node on its three-cell side, using all three new-end colours.
There are

\[
                 24\cdot21\cdot3=1,512                \tag{8}
\]

such stars in one orientation.  Every one of their three-parameter systems
(5)--(6) is inconsistent.  Their new-vertex swaps therefore fail as well.

An old-node rectangle fixes distinct old endpoint-colour nodes
\(i=(v,\alpha)\), \(j=(w,\gamma)\), chooses two new colours on each side,
and uses the four cells

\[
 X_{i;\beta_1},X_{i;\beta_2},
 Y_{j;\delta_1},Y_{j;\delta_2}.                        \tag{9}
\]

There are

\[
                   24\cdot21\cdot3^2=4,536             \tag{10}
\]

literal rectangles.  Exact reduction of (5)--(6) leaves 14, paired into the
following seven forced-pair-swap orbit representatives:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
(0,2),(0,3),(0,5),(1,4),(3,5),(3,6),(5,7),
\end{array}                                             \tag{11}
\]

where every pair denotes its two old endpoints, both old colours are 1,
and both sides use new colours \(\{0,2\}\).

These are genuine survivors of the enlarged affine quotient, not actual
cylinder sources.

## 4. Literal determinant exclusion of all seven

Put weights \(a,b\) on the two \(X\)-cells and \(c,d\) on the two
\(Y\)-cells.  For each orbit representative the checker constructs the
literal 21 labelled cofactor columns

\[
 C_2(a,b,c,d)=C_2^{(0)}+
  aL_a+bL_b+cL_c+dL_d+
  acQ_{ac}+adQ_{ad}+bcQ_{bc}+bdQ_{bd}.                 \tag{12}
\]

It chooses an exact square column minor and augments the same rows by one
forced residual column.  In every orbit, the square minor and its augmented
minor are the same nonzero torus monomial, up to sign.  In the order (11)
the certificates are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{old endpoints}&\text{column rank}&
 \det C_{\rm pivot}=\det[C_{\rm pivot}\mid R]_{\rm aug}\\
\hline
(0,2)&17&-a^3\\
(0,3)&20&-a^9c^8d\\
(0,5)&21&-a^4c^3d\\
(1,4)&20&-a^6c^2d^4\\
(3,5)&21&a^4c^3d\\
(3,6)&17&a^3\\
(5,7)&17&-a^3
\end{array}                                             \tag{13}
\]

All four support weights are nonzero by definition, so every determinant in
(13) is nonzero.  The augmented residual cannot lie in the column span.
The \((1,4)\) orbit initially presents a generic pivot with a factor
\((ac+1)^3\); selecting the pivot at \(ac=-1\) produces the fourth monomial
in (13), which is nonzero on the whole coefficient torus and closes that
rank-drop divisor too.

The determinant expansions and factorizations are exact over
\(\mathbb Q[a,b,c,d]\), computed by Singular from literal matching columns.

## 5. The precise remaining four-cell problem

The bounded calculation does not exhaust the 4,294,080 star orbits or the
3,267,846 rectangle orbits.  Outside (8)--(10), cells on the same new vertex
may use different old endpoint-colour nodes.  Their permanent coefficients
remain constrained products:

For a star,

\[
                    \pi_j=a b_j,\qquad j=1,2,3.         \tag{14}
\]

For a four-class rectangle,

\[
 \pi_{11}=ac,\quad\pi_{12}=ad,\quad
 \pi_{21}=bc,\quad\pi_{22}=bd,\qquad
 \pi_{11}\pi_{22}-\pi_{12}\pi_{21}=0,                  \tag{15}
\]

before any endpoint-swap recombination.  The exact remaining variety is

\[
\begin{cases}
\text{the anchor equations (6)},\\
\operatorname{rank}[C_2(a,b,c,d)\mid R_2(a,b,c,d)]
 =\operatorname{rank}C_2(a,b,c,d),\\
abcd\ne0,
\end{cases}                                             \tag{16}
\]

followed, for a surviving cut-2 support, by the analogous equations on cuts
3,4 and a candidate cut 0,1 or 5.

Equation (16), indexed by the unstructured support orbits in the two census
tables, is the frozen frontier.  The next useful step is a finite-field
rank sieve followed by exact saturated determinant ideals only on the
surviving orbits.  Coefficient grids and further universal linear
enlargements should not be treated as proofs.

## Scope

The old N=8 source and isolated diagonal pair are fixed.  The theorem closes
the old-node star and rectangle families only.  It does not close all
four-cell additions, arbitrary old-source deformations, or all even orders.
Nothing here is a Krenn counterexample.
