# The Tate pairs are not Schreyer-complete; the first new class is target-locked

Exact promotion audit following `189791f`.  This identifies the first new
anchor-relevant source S-pair without enumerating arbitrary higher degrees.
It does not construct the missing relative anchor or prove Krenn's
conjecture.

## Outcome

The 239 Tate-top label relations computed in `189791f` do **not** generate
the full polynomial source syzygy module.  In the fixed binary C5 word
sector, the first unavoidable new anchor-relevant class already occurs in
edge-equivalent multiplier degree four and fine weight 16, below the
edge-only Tate top of weight 18.

Let

\[
 F_0=H_0-u
\]

be the pure full-nine row and let \(F_w=H_w\) be the Hamming-one mixed row
with

\[
                         w=01000000.                    \tag{1}
\]

Then the new class is the ordinary source Koszul relation

\[
            \boxed{H_w r_0-(H_0-u)r_w},\qquad
 d\bigl(H_w r_0-(H_0-u)r_w\bigr)=0.                   \tag{2}
\]

The complete fine component of (2) has 181 monomial columns:

* 90 perfect-matching terms of \(H_w\) multiplying \(r_0\);
* 90 perfect-matching terms of \(H_0\) multiplying \(r_w\); and
* the single homogenizer term \(u r_w\).

Exact sparse elimination gives 7200 polynomial rows, rank 180, and a
one-dimensional kernel.  The 181-entry vector displayed in (2) spans that
kernel.  Thus this is the complete first higher S-pair component, not a
hand-picked relation.

It does not repair the rootless anchor.  The coefficient on the pure row is
\(H_w\), so its typed readout is

\[
 (\operatorname {ainc},\widehat w,\operatorname {tgt},
   \operatorname {ores})=(-H_w,0,H_w,0).               \tag{3}
\]

On any open where \(H_w\) is normalized, (3) is exactly the old pure-row
signature \((-1,0,1,0)\), not the required \((-1,0,0,0)\).  Requiring
physical target zero kills \(H_w\) and therefore kills the anchor incidence
at the same time.

## 1. Why this is the first anchor-relevant higher degree

Give the homogenizer \(u\) the pure-word fine degree.  A syzygy with a
nonzero coefficient \(A\) on \(r_0\) contains the term \(-Au\) from
\(A(H_0-u)\).  Below multiplier degree four, no mixed-row coefficient can
contain \(u\), so the coefficient of \(u\) forces \(A=0\).  Hence no
anchor-relevant syzygy occurs earlier.

At multiplier degree four, \(u r_w\) first has the correct fine degree.
The other two faces in (2) are the quartic hafnians \(H_w r_0\) and
\(-H_0r_w\).  They cancel termwise, while \(-H_wu+uH_w=0\).  This proves
both existence and minimality without a search over later degrees.

The fine degree is

\[
                    \deg(0^8)+\deg(w),                 \tag{4}
\]

with two zero slots at seven sites and one zero plus one colour-one slot at
site 1.  Only the two rows \(r_0,r_w\) divide (4), which is why the complete
component has precisely the census above.

## 2. Why the Tate audit cannot generate it

The relations in `189791f` are edge-monomial label fibres in the natural
weight-18 Tate top.  They contain no homogenizer face.  Polynomial
multigrading is nonnegative, so those relations cannot generate the lower
weight-16 class (2).  Therefore the hoped-for Schreyer/Buchberger promotion
from the audited Tate critical pairs is false.

With two chart copies, the complete component has 362 columns and the same
rank 180.  Its 182-dimensional kernel is generated by the 181 pairwise
chart differences and one copy of (2).  Chart differences have zero typed
readout, while target zero removes the Koszul coefficient; the two-chart
extension also has zero anchor in its target-zero kernel.

There is nevertheless an all-degree statement for **polynomial full-nine
combinations**, but it comes from the monic target tag rather than
Schreyer completeness.  In every degree, the coefficient on a pure row
contributes the same polynomial with opposite signs to anchor incidence and
physical target.  Thus target-zero polynomial combinations have zero
anchor.  This does not cover a genuinely new relative/Tate generator whose
lower face is outside polynomial multiples of the old rows; constructing
exactly such a generator remains the proof task.
