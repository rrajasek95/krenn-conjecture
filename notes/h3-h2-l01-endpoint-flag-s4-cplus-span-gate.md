## Abstract direction choice versus one fixed chart

On the irreducible plane in (2), the abstract direction-vertex `S4` action
relations have rank two. Thus its coinvariants vanish. This is the
representation-theoretic content behind the raw endpoint-chart coefficient
contraction, but that `S4` includes maps exchanging an endpoint direction
with a residual direction.

The full matching stabilizer of `A` has order eight and its relations have
rank one on `[2,2]`. The smaller physical fixed-chart group generated by
endpoint transpose and root/residual transpose is `C2 x C2`; it has the same
rank-one relation and hence the same one-dimensional coinvariant, represented
by (1). Both transposes fix `v_A` while sending

\[
                         v_B-v_C\longmapsto-(v_B-v_C). \tag{3}
\]

Hence the endpoint-odd line in (3) is Maschke-contractible, while the
endpoint-even line (1) is not.

This also answers the physicality question sharply. Maschke contraction in
the same three-cap chart object does **not** remove (1). A permutation which
moves among `v_A,v_B,v_C` is an isomorphism between pointed response charts.
The fixed-chart reset theorem `d1b8ec4` proves that its presentation-preserving
graph cone retains the pointed scalar

\[

Its first principal-parts face is exactly the present `dL01` packet. Thus
the full-`S4` Maschke contraction is circular here: it leaves no *new*
