# The two-bad reciprocal packet has one common-hafnian coupling

## 1. Outcome

The cofactor-relaxed four-flag packet in
[`shared-reciprocal-lemma-e-flag-normal-form.md`](shared-reciprocal-lemma-e-flag-normal-form.md)
does not lift to an exact source.  Replacing its two formal half-target
cofactors by the literal hafnians of its displayed internal block family
produces two nonzero mixed output coefficients and no third-colour target.

More importantly, the exact source equation left after two bad diagonal
arms can be written over one common odd complement.  It couples the two
Lemma-E pure deleted tensors and the distributed shared-site remainder by
one lower common power.  This is the precise packet which a cubic/descent
proof must close; the cofactors cannot be chosen independently.

The general packet remains open.  The new result removes the existing
relaxed counterguard and identifies a sharp common-hole versus aggregate-
cancellation boundary without returning to support-face enumeration.

## 2. Common odd complement

Let the exact source have even order `N=2m`, let two bad reciprocal arms
`pq,pr` share `p`, and normalize their distinct bad colours to `a,c`.  Lemma
E makes the arms diagonal and peels their targets:

\[
 A_{pq}=\lambda E_{aa},\qquad
 A_{pr}=\mu E_{cc}.                                    \tag{1}
\]

Write `t` for the third colour and

\[
 C=B\setminus\{p,q,r\},\qquad |C|=2m-3.               \tag{2}
\]

In the site-square-zero algebra on `C`, let `q_C` be the internal quadratic
and put

\[
 K=q_C^{[m-2]},\qquad J=q_C^{[m-3]}.                   \tag{3}
\]

Thus `K` leaves one common site open and `J` leaves three.  Denote the
`p-C,q-C,r-C` star rows by `P_i,Q_j,R_k`, and write
`D_jk=A_qr[j,k]`.

Lemma E3 on the two bad arms gives the literal odd-star equations

\[
 Q_jK=\mu^{-1}\delta_{jc}X_c^C,
 \qquad
 R_kK=\lambda^{-1}\delta_{ka}X_a^C.                  \tag{4}
\]

If both bad flags occur at the shared endpoint, E1 kills the `a,c` rows of
every remaining `p`-port.  Hence the complete remainder uses only `P_t`.

## 3. The common-hafnian identity

Fix the output colours `j,k` at `q,r`.  After `p` uses a common-site port,
there are exactly two matching types on the remaining sites:

1. `q` is paired to `r`, giving `D_jk K`;
2. `q,r` use two distinct common sites, giving `Q_j R_k J`.

Therefore the full-output rows are

\[
 \boxed{
 P_t\bigl(D_{jk}K+Q_jR_kJ\bigr)
   =\delta_{jt}\delta_{kt}X_t^C
 }
 \qquad(0\le j,k\le2).                                \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) is cancellation-aware.  Each product is multiplication in the
site-square-zero algebra, so it already sums all choices of distinct holes
and all internal matchings.  In particular, the crucial mixed row is

\[
               P_t(D_{ca}K+Q_cR_aJ)=0.                \tag{6}
\]

The chord term in (6) cannot be silently dropped.  What is exact without
additional assumptions is that the image of `P_t Q_c R_a J` must lie in the
one-dimensional correction generated by `P_tK`, with scalar `-D_ca`.

For `N=8`, `C` has five sites, `K=q_C^[2]`, and `J=q_C` itself.  The checker
reconstructs all `1,458` odd-star coefficient rows and all `2,187` rows of
(5) from literal perfect matchings with independent symbolic cell names.

## 4. Why the relaxed four-flag guard fails

The prior relaxed guard uses the internal cells

```text
45:00, 67:00, 36:11, 57:11
```

and the actual odd-star routes

```text
R_0 at site 3,   Q_1 at site 4.
```

Its distributed third-colour ports are

```text
P_2 at site 5 with coefficient 1/2,
P_2 at site 6 with coefficient 1/2.
```

The guard formally assigned each port a half-`X_2` cofactor.  Literal
matching reconstruction instead gives exactly

```text
21001200 : 1/2
21001121 : 1/2
```

and gives coefficient zero at `22222222`.  These are the two
`P_2 Q_1 R_0 J` monomials in (6); for both active port holes the chord
cofactor `K` is zero, so no `D_10 K` term can cancel them.  The compact guard
therefore fails three source rows: two forbidden mixed coefficients and the
missing pure target.

This is a source-level failure, not merely a support objection.  The five
odd cofactors in the guard remain genuine hafnians of the displayed common
family; it is precisely the two formal `p`-cofactors that fail provenance.

## 5. Raw common-hole dichotomy

At five common sites a selected nonzero summand of `Q_cK` consists of a
`Q_c` hole and one of the three perfect matchings of the other four sites.
There are fifteen such witnesses, and likewise for `R_aK`.

Among the `15^2=225` witness pairs:

```text
same Q/R hole       45
distinct holes     180
```

With the same hole, the product `Q_cR_a` is zero by the site-square
relation.  With distinct holes, exactly one edge of either chosen
near-perfect matching avoids both holes, so a raw `Q_cR_aJ` summand is
forced.  The checker exhausts all 225 pairs.

This is the sharp provenance picture but not yet an aggregate theorem.
Different raw summands can have the same output word and cancel over
`C`.  Thus the remaining theorem cannot merely choose one nonzero matching
from each pure row.  It must either control that cancellation or show that
its vanishing forces all active `P_t` ports onto a common hole/flag.

## 6. Remaining proof target

The theorem-completing two-bad statement is now:

> **Common-hole cancellation lemma (open).**  Under (4)--(5), either the
> mixed common-power terms cannot all be absorbed by the chord multiples
> `D_jk P_tK`, or the three stars have a common-hole/line concentration
> which produces a literal cubic neighbour or an exact two-site descent.

The one-bad branch remains the unary-top/binary-response packet from
[`scalar-unit-binary-residual-target-branch.md`](scalar-unit-binary-residual-target-branch.md).
Its missing theorem is an anchor-preserving nine-row source modification,
not another binary-top support classification.
