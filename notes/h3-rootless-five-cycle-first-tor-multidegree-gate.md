# The first companion Tor has repeated-site degree and no literal Hasse carrier

## Outcome

On the five-cycle specialization of the primitive ridge cokernel from
c4cac60, the companion ideal is an odd-cycle edge ideal. Its minimal
resolution begins

\[
       5\text{ generators in degree }2,qquad
       5\text{ first syzygies in degree }3,qquad
       1\text{ second syzygy in degree }5.              \tag{1}
\]

The degree-three first syzygies are the first common-(q) operations which
pair nontrivially with the individual primitive functionals (lambda_v)
while cancelling their response companions. They have zero (w), target,
and ordinary-residue readouts. But every one has physical support type

\[
                              P_3\sqcup K_2,             \tag{2}
\]

so one of the five odd sites occurs twice. Every literal cofactor or Hasse
coefficient in the existing full-source inventory is a matching or a
submatching and has physical site degree at most one. Therefore **none of
the existing coefficients has the first-Tor fine multidegree**.

Moreover, the five first syzygies move only a rank-four subspace of the five
ridge classes. At the valid diagonal torus point all cycle cells equal one,
their cokernel is detected by (sum_v\lambda_v). Response cancellation of
a combination of the five formal relative tails requires the sum of its
coefficients to be zero, whereas anchor incidence (-1) requires the same
sum to be one. Thus the first Tor does not itself produce the source-valid
signature

\[
       (\operatorname{ainc},\widehat w,\operatorname{tgt},
          \operatorname{ores})=(-1,0,0,0).              \tag{3}
\]

This freezes the minimal missing source type: a new repeated-site relative
comparison, not another literal hafnian cofactor/Hasse face. A complete
family must also carry a primitive pure-anchor face and satisfy the unique
degree-five odd-cycle compatibility. The calculation specifies but does not
construct that source-resolution generator.

## 1. The specialized companion ideal

Write

\[
 (a,b,c,d,e)=(q_{12},q_{23},q_{34},q_{45},q_{15}),     \tag{4}
\]

with the endpoint colours fixed by `12112`. In the cycle order
((1,3,5,2,4)), the five companion sums specialize to

\[
 (h_1,h_3,h_5,h_2,h_4)=(bd,ad,ac,ce,be).              \tag{5}
\]

These are the five edges of the cycle

\[
              b-d-a-c-e-b.                             \tag{6}
\]

Their minimal first syzygies are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 a h_1-b h_3&=0,& c h_3-d h_5&=0,\\
 e h_5-a h_2&=0,& b h_2-c h_4&=0,\\
 d h_4-e h_1&=0.                                      \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]

For example, the first multidegree is (abd=q_{12}q_{23}q_{45}).
Its physical edges are (12,23,45), and its site profile on (1,ldots,5)
is

\[
                              (1,2,1,1,1).              \tag{8}
\]

The other four profiles move the unique `2` around the five sites. Hence
all five have exactly the nonmatching type (2).

The checker examines every one of the ten Taylor pairs. The five pairs in
(7) have degree three and are minimal. Each of the five nonadjacent pairs
has degree four, and its S-pair is generated by the cubic rows in (7).
Thus no first syzygy occurs earlier or in another multidegree.

There is one relation among (7). With the displayed order, multiply the
five rows respectively by

\[
                            ce, be, bd, ad, ac.       \tag{9}
\]

Their sum is zero. This has total multidegree (abcde); exact enumeration
of all lcms of subsets of (7) finds no second syzygy below it. This proves
(1) without a computer-algebra black box.

## 2. How first Tor sees the ridge cokernel

After the specialization, retain the unique surviving route
(-r_v+h_v) at each face. Combining two routes with a row of (7) cancels
the companion exactly. The first row, for example, projects to

\[
                         -a r_1+b r_3.                  \tag{10}
\]

Consequently

\[
             \lambda_1(10)=-a,\qquad
             \lambda_3(10)=b,                          \tag{11}
\]

and the other three (lambda)'s vanish. This is the first multidegree
which genuinely changes c4cac60's primitive classes. Since the complete
route columns have target zero and (7) cancels their common response
monomial, (10) has zero (w), target, and ordinary residue. It may be
added to the formal anchor-normalized tail without introducing one of those
three readouts.

But the five relations (10) have rank only four over the fraction field.
Their surviving covector has weights

\[
                        (h_1,h_3,h_5,h_2,h_4).          \tag{12}
\]

At (a=b=c=d=e=1), this is simply the all-ones covector. All five first-Tor
rows become differences of adjacent ridge classes. Thus they cannot kill
the diagonal aggregate.

There is an equivalent integral augmentation statement. Summing the two
coefficients in each row of (7) gives

\[
                    a-b, c-d, e-a, b-c, d-e.        \tag{13}
\]

These generate the diagonal ideal

\[
                        (a-b,b-c,c-d,d-e),              \tag{14}
\]

which is proper even in the Laurent ring: it vanishes at the torus point
((1,1,1,1,1)). Hence no polynomial or monomial-localized combination of
the first syzygies has primitive augmentation one. Inverting a difference
such as (a-b) would work only off its divisor and is not a universal
source-valid coefficient.

This is exactly the anchor conflict in (3). At the diagonal point, every
(h_v=1). For formal-tail coefficients (gamma_v), response zero says
(sum_vgamma_v=0), while total anchor incidence (-1) says
(sum_vgamma_v=1).

## 3. Audit of the literal higher-cofactor inventory

A monomial of an eight-site hafnian is a perfect matching. A nonzero Hasse
coefficient is obtained by selecting some of its edges, so it is a
submatching. In particular every physical site occurs at most once. The
checker enumerates all (105\cdot16=1680) subsets of the eight-site
matchings and verifies this bound.

That covers the literal coefficients in the named inventory:

* the four-site denominator companions and their first/second Euler
  coefficients are (2K_2);
* the genuine unary third-cofactor marking is (3K_2) on six sites;
* the full Hasse/Koszul top is (4K_2) on eight sites, and every proper
  face is a submatching;
* the degree-four pure/mixed Eq Koszul coefficients are sums of eight-site
  matching monomials; and
* Hamming-one/two and two-chart tags change endpoint/source labels but their
  internal hafnian coefficient remains a matching cofactor.

None can equal the repeated-site profile (8). The formal Hasse tail with
anchor incidence (-1) is therefore not the needed carrier: its matching
top is source-invalid for the already recorded unit reason, and none of its
proper faces realizes (7).

## 4. Minimal new generator specification

The first possible missing comparison must have all of the following data:

1. one of the five degree-three fine multidegrees in (7), equivalently a
   source-labelled (P_3\sqcup K_2) overlapping-edge grade;
2. boundary projecting to the corresponding response-cancelling ridge
   S-pair such as (10);
3. zero (w), target, and ordinary-residue readouts;
4. a primitive pure-anchor face making total anchor incidence (-1), not a
   coefficient in the proper ideal (14); and
5. for a five-face family, the degree-five compatibility (9), so that the
   proposed source differential still squares to zero.

No generator with these data appears in the complete typed inventory. A
future positive proof must construct it as genuinely new relative/Tor
source data. A future negative proof may instead show that source
multiaffinity forbids every repeated-site comparison satisfying items 2--5.
Either result would address the actual rootless gate; another matching
cofactor or ordinary Hasse face will not.
