# Independent audit of the sitewise common-power response filtration

## Verdict and one terminology correction

The four-cover and site-cover theorem in
[the primary filtration note](sitewise-common-power-response-filtration.md)
is correct. The proof remains valid for arbitrary-rank edge blocks,
arbitrary multi-site linear rows, parallel aggregate blocks, and complex
cancellation. The coordinate-plane equality case, uniqueness of the three
single-colour lifts, response orientation, complementary-minor frontier,
local pencil conclusions (33) and (35), sitewise chain rule, and scalar-cycle
sharpness model are also correct.

During the audit, one harmless terminology ambiguity in (8) was corrected:
the primary note now says **single-colour (monochromatic) four-site part**.
Under
(7), every occupied factor of \(F\), including a mixed-colour word, is
already in the target coordinate span. The proved assertion is that the
only single-colour words in \(F\) are

\[
                 \lambda_iE_i(B_i),\qquad i=0,1,2,
\]

and that all three coefficients are nonzero. The primary note correctly
allows mixed four-site words immediately afterward, so this is wording, not
a mathematical gap.

The clean-room checker
[audit_sitewise_common_power_response_filtration_independent.py](../computations/audit_sitewise_common_power_response_filtration_independent.py)
imports neither the primary verifier nor project code. It enumerates actual
ordered Cauchy--Binet terms, evaluates dense tensor blocks over
\(\mathbb F_{101}\), exhausts the local pencil claims over
\(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), and reconstructs the scalar six-cycle
in an exact sparse site-square-zero algebra over \(\mathbb Q\).

## 1. Scalar response and endpoint orientation

Write the multi-site linear rows as

\[
 p_i=\sum_{u\in U}p_{i,u},\qquad
 s_j=\sum_{u\in U}s_{j,u},\qquad p_{i,u},s_{j,u}\in V_u.       \tag{A1}
\]

For decomposable covectors \(\ell=(\ell_u)\), put

\[
 P_{iu}=\ell_u(p_{i,u}),\qquad
 S_{ju}=\ell_u(s_{j,u}).                                      \tag{A2}
\]

If the row factors occupy \(u\) and \(v\), the remaining four sites must
be occupied by \(F=q^{[2]}\). The two endpoint orders are separate ordered
choices. Direct contraction therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left\langle\bigotimes_w\ell_w,p_i s_jF\right\rangle
 &=\sum_{u\ne v}P_{iu}
   \left\langle\bigotimes_{w\ne u,v}\ell_w,
                   F|_{U\setminus\{u,v\}}\right\rangle S_{jv}\\
 &=\sum_{u,v}P_{iu}C_{uv}S_{jv}
  =(PCS^{\mathsf T})_{ij}.                                   \tag{A3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus there is neither a missing transpose nor a factor of two. This
derivation contracts the complete tensor sum and never selects an
individually nonzero matching.

The independent checker generates dense \(3\)-by-\(3\) blocks
\(q_{uv}\) on all fifteen pairs over \(\mathbb F_{101}\). It directly
enumerates the two ordered row sites and every matching of the remaining
four sites, and compares all nine values with \(PCS^{\mathsf T}\) in four
unrelated deterministic systems.

## 2. The \(J\)-adic four-cover

Let \(R\) be the polynomial ring in all covector coordinates, identifying
a vector in \(V_u\) with a linear polynomial in the \(u\)-site variables.
Put

\[
                  I_u=(W_u)\subset R,\qquad J=\sum_u I_u.     \tag{A4}
\]

Choose bases adapted to the \(W_u\). Then \(J\) is generated by a subset of
the polynomial variables, so \(\operatorname{gr}_J R\) is a polynomial
ring over \(R/J\) and is a domain. For nonzero \(e\in V_u\),

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_J\ell_u(e)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&e\in W_u,\\
 0,&e\notin W_u.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{A5}
\]

Every scalarized block \(q_{uv}\) belongs to \(I_uI_v\). Every monomial in
\(C_{uv}\) is a product of two disjoint scalarized blocks on
\(U\setminus\{u,v\}\). Hence the stronger multigraded containment

\[
             C_{uv}\in\prod_{w\ne u,v}I_w\subset J^4         \tag{A6}
\]

holds term by term. Multiplication by unrestricted row evaluations cannot
lower ideal order, so \(M_{ij}\in J^4\).

For the \(i\)-th target polynomial

\[
                     T_i=\prod_u\ell_u(e_i^{(u)}),
\]

the domain property makes orders additive:

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_JT_i
 =\#\{u:e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\}=|D_i|.                           \tag{A7}
\]

Since \(M_{ii}=T_i\in J^4\), this proves

\[
                              |D_i|\ge4.                    \tag{A8}
\]

Cancellation in a block expansion, in \(C_{uv}\), in the matrix product,
or between parallel descriptions cannot defeat this argument: every
summand already lies in the same ideal power.

## 3. Determinant exponents and the site cover

For three-subsets \(I,K\subset U\), expand a term of
\(\det C_{I,K}\) using a bijection \(\sigma:I\to K\). A nonzero term has
\(u\ne\sigma(u)\), since \(C_{uu}=0\). Each cofactor entry contributes a
\(W_t\)-origin factor at site \(t\), except when \(t\) is its omitted row
or column. Since rows and columns are separately distinct, the exact lower
exponent supplied by the cofactor part is

\[
 d_t(I,K)=3-\mathbf1_{t\in I}-\mathbf1_{t\in K}.           \tag{A9}
\]

Thus

\[
             d_t(I,K)\ge1,\qquad \sum_t d_t(I,K)=12.       \tag{A10}
\]

The row determinants may add ideal factors but cannot remove these.
Cauchy--Binet proves

\[
        \det M\in J^{12},\qquad \det M\in I_t\quad(t\in U). \tag{A11}
\]

Each \(I_t\) is prime because its quotient is a polynomial ring. Since

\[
       \det M=\prod_{t\in U}\prod_{i=0}^2\ell_t(e_i^{(t)}), \tag{A12}
\]

membership in \(I_t\) forces at least one of the three target factors at
site \(t\) into \(I_t\). Factors at other sites do not belong to this
site-variable ideal. Therefore

\[
                         D_0\cup D_1\cup D_2=U.            \tag{A13}
\]

The clean-room enumeration uses actual ordered determinant terms, not just
minor-support pairs. It finds exactly \(1420\) nonzero-diagonal terms, with
sorted site-exponent histogram

\[
\begin{array}{c|r}
 (2,2,2,2,2,2)&120\\
 (1,2,2,2,2,3)&720\\
 (1,1,2,2,3,3)&540\\
 (1,1,1,3,3,3)&40.
\end{array}                                                \tag{A14}
\]

Every term has total exponent twelve and positive exponent at all six
sites. The adjugate count is also correct: a \(2\)-by-\(2\) minor lies in
\(J^8\), giving only
\(|D_j|+|D_k|\ge8\), the sum of two four-cover inequalities.

## 4. Equality at local rank at most two

Assume \(\dim W_u\le2\). Since the three target axes at a site are
independent, at most two can lie in \(W_u\). The four-cover gives at least
twelve colour-site incidences:

\[
 12\le\sum_u\#\{i:e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\}\le6\cdot2=12.        \tag{A15}
\]

Equality holds throughout. Every colour occurs at exactly four sites,
every site contains exactly two target axes, and \(W_u\) is exactly their
coordinate plane. If \(B_i\) is the set of sites omitting colour \(i\),
the three \(B_i\) are disjoint pairs partitioning \(U\).

Decompose the degree-four tensor \(F\) by its missing pair \(P\). Every
occupied factor lies in its local \(W_u\). A single-colour word \(E_i(P)\)
can occur only if \(e_i^{(u)}\in W_u\) for every \(u\notin P\). Equivalently
\(B_i\subseteq P\), and both are pairs, so

\[
                              P=B_i.                       \tag{A16}
\]

This proves uniqueness of the possible single-colour lift. Moreover, the
\(X_i\)-coefficient in \(p_i s_iF\) can arise only from that lift: a row
factor cannot replace an occupied factor in the site-square-zero algebra.
The two row factors must fill \(B_i\). The required coefficient is one, so
\(\lambda_i\ne0\). Mixed-colour four-site words remain possible.

The checker separately enumerates all six-letter omission words. The
four-per-colour constraints leave exactly

\[
                        \frac{6!}{2!2!2!}=90              \tag{A17}
\]

assignments, all with two omissions of each colour.

## 5. Omission-pair response orientation

Choose local dual covectors \(e_i^{*(u)}\) extending the duals of the three
target axes. For \(B_i=\{r,t\}\), define

\[
 a_u=(e_i^{*(u)}(p_{0,u}),e_i^{*(u)}(p_{1,u}),
                         e_i^{*(u)}(p_{2,u}))^{\mathsf T},
\]

and define \(b_u\) analogously from the \(s\)-rows. Extracting the
\(X_i\)-coefficient in all nine equations gives

\[
 \lambda_i(a_rb_t^{\mathsf T}+a_tb_r^{\mathsf T})
                         =f_if_i^{\mathsf T}.              \tag{A18}
\]

Writing \(A=(a_r\ a_t)\) and \(B=(b_t\ b_r)\), the matrix in parentheses is
\(AB^{\mathsf T}\), whose second compound is

\[
              (a_r\wedge a_t)(b_t\wedge b_r)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{A19}
\]

The right side of (A18) is nonzero of rank one, so one wedge vanishes. If
the \(a\)-wedge vanishes, both \(a\)-vectors lie in the one-dimensional
column space \(\mathbb Cf_i\). If the \(b\)-wedge vanishes, both
\(b\)-vectors lie in the row space \(\mathbb Cf_i\). This includes zero
endpoint vectors and cancellation between the two summands.

The checker exhausts all four response vectors over \(\mathbb F_2^3\).
All \(42\) solutions of

\[
 a_rb_t^{\mathsf T}+a_tb_r^{\mathsf T}=f_0f_0^{\mathsf T}
\]

have at least one asserted orientation.

## 6. Complementary-minor leading multidegree

Use the six independent ideal filtrations \(I_u\). In the plane case,
two target factors at site \(u\) lie in \(I_u\), while the omitted factor
does not. The target determinant has exact sitewise order

\[
                              (2,2,2,2,2,2).               \tag{A20}
\]

The cofactor part of a Cauchy--Binet term has lower multidegree
\((d_u(I,K))_u\). If \(I\cap K\ne\varnothing\), then, because both sets
have size three, some site lies outside \(I\cup K\); its exponent is three.
That term has no component in multidegree (A20). The only surviving
possibility is

\[
             I\cap K=\varnothing,\qquad K=U\setminus I.   \tag{A21}
\]

There are \(20\) complementary support pairs and \(3!\) ordered cofactor
terms for each, producing the \(120\) leading terms in (A14). To survive,
the \(p\)-row factors on \(I\) and the \(s\)-row factors on its complement
are read in their quotients modulo the local plane ideals. This establishes
the stated frontier without asserting that an individual minor or summand
is nonzero.

## 7. The two local pencil conclusions

Fix \(u\), hold target-generic covectors fixed at the other sites, and vary
\(x=\ell_u\). The first row and column of the cofactor matrix are independent
of \(x\). Every entry in the remaining \(5\)-by-\(5\) block is a four-site
matching coefficient involving \(u\), so it vanishes for \(x\in W_u^\perp\).
Block multiplication gives

\[
 M(x)=p(x)\alpha^{\mathsf T}+\beta s(x)^{\mathsf T}.       \tag{A22}
\]

The adjugate orientation is

\[
 \operatorname{adj}M(x)
  =(\alpha\mathbin\times s(x))
     (p(x)\mathbin\times\beta)^{\mathsf T}.                \tag{A23}
\]

It follows from a two-column factorization or direct cofactors; there is no
missing sign.

### 7.1 Two target axes survive

Suppose exactly one target axis lies in \(W_u\), while the other two have
independent images modulo \(W_u\). After relabeling and harmless nonzero
scaling, the restricted target pencil is

\[
             \{\operatorname{diag}(0,y,z):y,z\in\mathbb C\}. \tag{A24}
\]

For generic \(y,z\ne0\), its row and column spaces are
\(H=\operatorname{span}(f_1,f_2)\). Equation (A22) forces
\(\alpha,\beta\in H\). Write \(\alpha=(A,B)\) in this plane. The vector
\((-B,A)\) annihilates \(\alpha^{\mathsf T}\), so every matrix in (A24)
sends it into the fixed line \(\mathbb C\beta\). Its images are

\[
                              (-By,Az).                    \tag{A25}
\]

Independent variation forces \(A=0\) or \(B=0\); a nonzero rank-two member
then puts \(\beta\) on the other coordinate line. Hence

\[
 \{\mathbb C\alpha,\mathbb C\beta\}
                         =\{\mathbb Cf_1,\mathbb Cf_2\}.   \tag{A26}
\]

The checker obtains the same classification differently. For every
\(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_3^3\), it constructs the six-column map

\[
                 (p,s)\longmapsto p\alpha^{\mathsf T}
                                      +\beta s^{\mathsf T}
\]

and tests whether both coordinate matrices lie in its image. Exactly eight
ordered pairs work: two orders and two nonzero scalings on each coordinate
line. Zero and off-plane candidates are exhausted.

### 7.2 Only one target axis survives

On the coordinate-plane boundary, \(W_u^\perp\) kills the two contained
target factors and leaves a nonzero rank-one pencil on the omitted colour
line \(\mathbb Cf_i\). The second compound of (A22) is

\[
 (p(x)\wedge\beta)(\alpha\wedge s(x))^{\mathsf T}=0
                         \quad(x\in W_u^\perp).            \tag{A27}
\]

Both wedge vectors have linear-polynomial coordinates in \(x\). The
coordinate ring of \(W_u^\perp\) is a domain, so one whole wedge vector
vanishes identically. If \(p(x)\wedge\beta\equiv0\), the column space of a
nonzero target member gives

\[
 p(W_u^\perp)\subseteq\mathbb Cf_i,\qquad
                         \beta\in\mathbb Cf_i.             \tag{A28}
\]

This remains true when \(\beta=0\): then
\(p(x)\alpha^{\mathsf T}=h(x)f_if_i^{\mathsf T}\); on the kernel of the
nonzero target functional \(h\), \(p(x)=0\), and off that kernel \(p(x)\)
lies on \(\mathbb Cf_i\). The other wedge symmetrically gives

\[
 s(W_u^\perp)\subseteq\mathbb Cf_i,\qquad
                         \alpha\in\mathbb Cf_i.            \tag{A29}
\]

An exhaustive two-parameter calculation over \(\mathbb F_2\) checks every
\(\alpha,\beta,p_0,p_1,s_0,s_1\in\mathbb F_2^3\), including
\(\alpha=0\) and \(\beta=0\). All \(180\) representations of one nonzero
rank-one coefficient and one zero kernel coefficient satisfy (A28) or
(A29).

## 8. Chain rule and arbitrary block rank

For fixed covectors away from \(u\), contract the \(v\)-endpoint of the
incident block:

\[
 z_{uv}=(\operatorname{id}_{V_u}\otimes\ell_v)(q_{uv})\in W_u. \tag{A30}
\]

Contract \(q^{[3]}\) at every site except \(u\). Every perfect matching has
a unique edge \(uv\) incident to \(u\). Once it is fixed, the contraction
of the other two edges is exactly \(C_{uv}\). Divided powers count every
matching once, so

\[
 \left\langle\bigotimes_{w\ne u}\ell_w,q^{[3]}\right\rangle
                    =\sum_{v\ne u}C_{uv}z_{uv}.           \tag{A31}
\]

This tensor identity holds before imposing vanishing and for blocks of
every rank. Thus \(q^{[3]}=0\) proves the chain rule. Cancellation is
harmless because (A31) groups a complete expansion by its unique incident
edge.

The finite-field audit computes both sides separately at every site in four
dense systems. One side enumerates all fifteen perfect matchings; the other
builds the five cofactors and contracted block vectors. All vector
coordinates agree.

## 9. Scalar-cycle sharpness

In the exact site-square-zero algebra, take

\[
 q=2e_2z_1+z_1z_5-z_5z_2+z_2z_4+z_4z_3+2z_3e_2.         \tag{A32}
\]

The two perfect matchings have weights \(-2\) and \(2\), and their local
tensors are identical. Hence direct divided-power expansion gives

\[
                              q^{[3]}=0.                   \tag{A33}
\]

With

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(p_0,p_1,p_2)=\left(e_0,\frac{-z_1+z_3}{2},z_4\right),\\
 &(s_0,s_1,s_2)=\left(\frac{-z_1+z_3}{2},e_1,
                              -\frac{z_1+z_3}{4}\right),
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{A34}
\]

the independent sparse-algebra expansion verifies all nine complete tensor
identities

\[
 p_i s_jq^{[2]}
 =\delta_{ij}\,e_i z_1z_2z_3z_4z_5.                      \tag{A35}
\]

It also reads the incident factors of \(q\) at site zero directly and finds
only \(e_2\):

\[
                              W_0=\mathbb Ce_2.            \tag{A36}
\]

Thus the sharpness statement is stronger than a scalar-matrix coincidence:
(A35) is a full tensor identity. It still is not a global ternary system,
because at sites \(1,\ldots,5\) all three target factors lie on the same
line. It is a valid local-boundary model, not a Krenn counterexample.

## 10. Scope

The audit proves the incidence filtration and its equality-case structure.
It does **not** eliminate the mixed-colour four-site part of \(q^{[2]}\),
and the complementary-minor statement does not make an individual minor
nonzero. It therefore does not by itself reduce the non-pure common-power
branch to the already closed uniform pure-lift branch.
